# Checks of the centrifugal deformation

Companion of `docs/background.md` (Centrifugal deformation) and `docs/centrifugal-equations.md`, for `centrifugal = 1`
(`bin/centrifugal.py`, NEW 2026-11-05; with a field, phase 1b; the dissipative terms and the differential rotation of the
deformed body, phase 2, NEW 2026-11-06). `run_all.sh [WORKDIR]` builds the runs it needs under
`tests/centrifugal/runs/` and prints one verdict per check and its time (about 13 minutes on 16 cores; the budgets need
petsc4py and slepc4py, `SKIP_SOLVE=1 run_all.sh` skips them: 9 minutes on 16 cores, 12 on four; with `OLD_KORE` set to a copy of
the commit before the change it also runs `tests/diff_rot/regress.sh`, 2 minutes more). The times of the budget
checks leave out their runs, which `mk_run.py` builds and solves first.

| script | what it checks |
| --- | --- |
| `derive.py` | the tool: from the definitions alone (the map X = α + ξ, ξ = ε(r)∇(r²P2/2), and w = J F⁻¹u), with sympy in Cartesian components, the closed forms that `centrifugal.py` implements: the level surfaces r(1 + εP2); the identities that hold for any map (mass conservation, the covariant Coriolis force (F⁻¹ẑ)×w, the advection w_r S0′/J, the buoyancy Φ′(r) r̂); the first-order (M − I)w, (z̃ − ẑ)×w and J − 1; the map of `to_physical`; the effective potential on the level surfaces, whose monopole gives g − (2/3)Ω²r (a few seconds) |
| `derive_mag.py [--quick]` | the same for a field: from b = F b̃/J and B0 = F B̃0/J alone (sympy), the covariant Lorentz force (j̃ × B̃0 + J̃0 × b̃)/J with j̃ = ∇×(M b̃), the induction ∂ₜb̃ = ∇×(w × B̃0/J), their first-order parts δ𝐆 and δ𝐄, and the flux and tangential field of a potential field on the deformed wall; then the couplings of `centrifugal.magnetic_build` against the projection of δ𝐆 and δ𝐄 on kore's rows, block by block, and the wall terms against closed forms (the blocks are derived in parallel on up to 16 cores: two and a half minutes on 16, eight on four, a quarter of an hour on one; `--quick` two of the seven cases) |
| `derive_diff.py [--quick]` | (phase 2) the dissipative terms from the map alone (sympy, Cartesian components): the covariant viscous force Fᵀ V(F w/J) = V(w) + δV, the traction on the level surface (the stress-free condition), the heat diffusion J ∇⋅(κ∇s) = ∇⋅(κ M⁻¹∇s) and the flux through the level surface, the electric field η M ∇×(M b̃) of the magnetic diffusion, and the force of a differential rotation constant on the level surfaces; then the couplings of `centrifugal.diffusion_build` and `rotation_build`, the stress-free and fixed-flux wall rows and the row of the electric field at a conducting inner core against the projections of these on kore's rows, with kore's spherical operators as the check of the script's own vector calculus (half a minute to a minute; `--quick` fewer cases) |
| `bc_check.py` | the insulating-wall rows of `centrifugal.wall_coefficients` against the exact matching on the deformed wall: a potential field with random coefficients outside the CMB (or inside an insulating inner core), its flux and tangential field in the reference space computed pointwise with the exact F; the rows leave a residual of second order in f (it falls by 4 when f is halved), those of the sphere one of first order; m = 0, 1, 2, 5 (a few seconds) |
| `algebra_check.py [--quick]` | the angular couplings of `centrifugal.primitives` (the recurrence algebra of `bin/angular.py`) against a projection of the closed forms by Gauss-Legendre quadrature, with harmonics from `scipy.special.lpmv`: every term, m = 0–6, l up to 36, and the P2 coupling of the heat rows (5e-14) |
| `clairaut_check.py` | the flattening of `'clairaut'`: a homogeneous body (f = 5q/4, η = 0), the polytrope of index 1 (f/q = 7.5/π²), `'Wu 2005'` against Clairaut's second-order equation integrated from ρ itself (7e-10), the profile codes against finite differences and their Chebyshev fits, and the Sun (`'model S hydrostatic'`: f/q = 0.519) |
| `eig_check.py` | eigenvalues of small runs (scipy shift-invert on A.npz, B.npz): f = 0 gives the matrices of `centrifugal = 0` entry by entry; the spin-over of a Maclaurin spheroid (inviscid, uniform f) has (Im λ − 1)/f → 1 (exact 1 + f + f²/2, first order 1 + f + f²/3); the relabelling test (f = c(1 − r²)², a sphere labelled differently inside: 13 resolved modes move at O(c²), their odd part is O(c³)); `'clairaut'` of a homogeneous body equals `'uniform'` f = 5q/4; the monopole equals Beyonce(1 − 2q/3) for g = r; the stratified, deformed ideal fluid has imaginary eigenvalues (1e-16). With a field: f = 0 gives the matrices of `centrifugal = 0` (a conducting inner core with the axial field, a full sphere with general poloidal and toroidal fields); the relabelling test with the axial and the Malkus field in a viscous fluid (Ek = Em = 1e-3), against the sphere with the field carried by the map (h = (1 + ε) r/2, and k1 = r(1 + η/5), k3 = −ηr/5): the parts of the shifts odd in c (c = ±1e-3; the even part is kore's error of second order) agree to 4e-6 (axial) and 3e-5 (Malkus) on the resolved modes, against 3 % to 60 % without the metric part of the magnetic diffusion; (phase 2) the free decay of a field in a sphere labelled as deformed (a full sphere, a conducting inner core of the fluid's conductivity and of 0.3) does not move at first order (its odd part is 2e-13 to 4e-11 of λ at c = 1e-3, of order c³) |
| `spheroid_modes.py [--ekman]` | (phase 2) the inertial modes of an inviscid homogeneous fluid in a spheroid, exact and independent of kore (the polynomial method, in the physical spheroid: no map, no spherical harmonics): kore's dω/df (`'uniform'`, runs at f = ±1e-4, ±2e-4 and Richardson's extrapolation) against the exact one for the 73 modes of degree ≤ 7 with m = 0, 1, 2 and both symmetries (1.4e-11); the exact dω/df by two routes, the differences of the exact spheroid modes and 2ω∫\|u_z\|²/∫\|u\|² from the sphere's mode alone (they agree to 9e-10). `--ekman` adds Greenspan's Ekman damping of a few modes and its derivative in f, as a reference only: kore approaches it as Ek^0.3, its derivative as Ek^0.1 (the critical latitudes) (half a minute on four cores, with `--ekman` under a minute) |
| `shape_check.py [NPROC]` | (phase 2) damped modes when the walls are deformed, at Ek = 1e-3: kore's dλ/df (`'uniform'`, f = ±5e-3, ±1e-2, Richardson) against the exact first-order shift computed from the sphere's mode alone, by Hadamard's formula (a wall integral of the mode and its adjoint, the mirror image) and by a volume formula (the weak form pulled back by the linear map), the formulas checked by a dilation of the sphere: no-slip walls (full sphere m = 1, 2 and a shell: 1e-10 to 2e-10; without the metric viscous terms kore is 5e-2 off), stress-free walls (5e-10 to 3e-8; the spin-over, a rigid rotation, moves as in the ideal fluid), fixed entropy and fixed flux (κ = 1e-2, a full sphere and a shell: 4e-10 to 3e-9) (two and a half minutes on four cores; NPROC cases in parallel, default 4) |
| `bin/ru_insane.py` | with `centrifugal = 1` also: the metric blocks (inertia, Coriolis, entropy) packed as `assemble.py` packs them, against direct (r, cos θ) quadratures of the closed forms on random fields, the inertia and entropy blocks Hermitian with I + Ic and E + Ec positive, the Coriolis block anti-Hermitian, and KE of the deformed body against `utils4pp.centrifugal_terms` (round-off: 1e-14); (phase 2) with viscosity the metric viscous block Hermitian with V + Vc negative, on fields regular at the centre in a full sphere (5e-14; 6e-11 with the profiles of `'Wu 2005'`, the level of the sphere's own viscous block there); `ru_wu_dr` adds a differential rotation with its thermal wind, which keeps the structure's g while the buoyancy takes the effective one |
| `budget_check.py [tol] [HALF]` | after `spin_doctor.py`: residσ, residω and resid𝑠 of every solution below tol (1e-8), also with a differential rotation (`bud_wu_dr`). spin_doctor weights KE with M, the Coriolis power with z̃ and TE with J by quadrature, apart from the matrices; without these weights the residuals are 1e-3 to 3e-2. With a field also resid𝐛 below tol, and with HALF (the same case at half the flattening) resid𝐄 of second order in f: the `bm_*` runs (uniform f, a conducting inner core, a full sphere with Luo_S2, the Malkus field, general fields with a differential rotation; residσ and residω below 2e-13, resid𝐛 below 1e-12, orders 1.7 to 2.2), and `bm_wu` (anelastic, `'clairaut'`, N 128: 2e-8, set by its resolution). Also solve.py's normalisation, KE = 1 and with a field KE + Hendrik (ME + MEc) = 1 (to the ten digits that the energies are printed with) |
| `fcn.sh [WORKDIR] [NP]` | the free core nutation, the spin-over of a fixed mantle, with a field: Ek 1e-7, Em 1e-5, Le 3e-3, the axial field and a conducting inner core, at the flattenings ±f and f/2 (f = 2.5e-3, N 160), at ±f without the field and at N 200. (Phase 2) the shift is taken between the deformed runs, at ±f: a run at f = 0 keeps the sphere's basis for the toroidal rows, and at N 160 that alone moves λ by 1.5e-6, 6e-4 of f (without the field). Im λ moves by f(1 + f/3) as in the first-order equations, plus the part of the Ekman layers, 5.8e-4 of f (Greenspan's boundary-layer theory gives 4.6e-4, which kore approaches only as Ek^0.1); the coefficient of f² is 1/3 to 7e-4, the field changes the shift by 3.6e-5 of f, N 200 by 5e-6. Not in `run_all.sh`: 15 ranks (NP 1, 3, 5 or 15), 15 GB, 5 minutes; MUMPS needs more workspace (`st_mat_mumps_icntl_14` = 1000, set in the script; 500 failed once at N 200). The budgets of these runs need more resolution (residσ 5e-4 at N 160, 1e-5 at N 200, as in the sphere) |
| `mk_run.py NAME 'par.x = ..' [solve]` | builds a run directory from the repo's `bin/` with the test defaults (the inviscid Boussinesq full sphere, m 1, symm −1, N 24, lmax 20, `'uniform'` f = 0, one rank) and runs submatrices and assemble (with `solve` also solve.py and spin_doctor.py) |

Results of 2026-11-06 are in `docs/centrifugal-equations.md` (Checks). What the checks do not reach: the terms of order
f ΔΩ of the thermal-wind balance (with `thermal_wind` 1 or 2 the entropy S_tw keeps the balance of the sphere), and the
second order in f, where kore's error is measured on the spin-over only (f²/6 in its frequency).
