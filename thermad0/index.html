    ThermaD = 0: runs without thermal diffusion

    With ThermaD = 0 the heat equation has no diffusion term. The entropy perturbation is only carried by the flow, so the motion is adiabatic:

    \[ p_0\left(\partial_t s + u_r\frac{dS}{dr}\right) = 0, \]

    plus, with a differential rotation, the advection terms of Differential rotation. This is the limit to use when thermal diffusion is negligible on the time scale of the modes. It works with every background and with every differential-rotation option, including the thermal wind.

    What kore does differently

    • No thermal boundary conditions. The heat equation is first order in \(r\) without diffusion, so bci_thermal and bco_thermal are not used.
    • Heat rows divided by \(p_0\). \(p_0\) is about \(10^{-7}\) near the surface of a solar model, and rows weighted by \(p_0\) are badly conditioned there. The heat rows are \(r/p_0\) times the equation, or \(r^2/p_0\) times it with a differential rotation and thermal_wind ≥ 1 (details).
    • No Ruiz prescaling. With the default prescale = None, solve.py turns the prescaling off for ThermaD = 0, where it degrades the solutions (Solver).

    Recommendations

    Prefer ThermaD = 0 to a very small ThermaD. As ThermaD → 0 the eigenvalues of a diffusive run approach the ThermaD = 0 ones linearly in ThermaD, as long as the diffusive run is resolved. With the thermal wind and walls that are both stress-free and at fixed entropy (bci_thermal = bco_thermal = 0), a small ThermaD forms a thermal boundary layer of width about \((\mathrm{ThermaD}/\lvert\lambda\rvert)^{1/2}\) at the walls, because the adiabatic entropy is not zero there. Below ThermaD ≈ \(10^{-7}\) that layer needs far more radial resolution than the bulk. The ThermaD = 0 run has no layer. Zero-flux or no-slip walls have no such layer either.

    Let \(dS/dr\) vanish at the inner wall. In a shell, a \(dS/dr \neq 0\) at the inner wall gives spurious modes trapped there. Their eigenvalues move with N and spin_doctor flags them (residσ ≈ 1). For an erf top hat, take \(x_1 \ge\) ricb \(+ 3w_1\). If the gradient must reach the wall, raise nev by one or two, because the spurious modes take some of the slots.

    Expect a band of eigenvalues near σ = 0 with a differential rotation. Without diffusion, the advection of \(s\) by the differential rotation has a continuous spectrum,

    \[ \lambda = -i\thinspace m\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega(r,\theta), \]

    which the discretization shows as many eigenvalues near \(\sigma = 0\) whose frequencies lie in the range of \(m\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega\). With the thermal wind some of them grow slowly. They peak at \(l \approx\) lmax and change with lmax: they are small-scale instabilities of the diffusionless thermal wind, not modes at the resolved scales. Discard any mode that fails the ℓ-convergence check of spin_doctor. They disappear with thermal_wind = 0 or with a moderate diffusivity (ThermaD ≈ \(10^{-3}\)).

    Put the target away from that band. The eigensolver converges slowly near it.

    Check the convergence in N. In a full sphere, eigenfunctions with ThermaD = 0 need more radial resolution than diffusive ones (N = 200 instead of 120 for a smooth solar model). Where \(\rho_0\) nearly vanishes at the outer wall (structure 'Wu 2005' with r_out close to 1), the thermal-wind rows converge slowly in N.

    Choose the thermal-wind option on purpose. With diff_rot = 1 the default thermal_wind = 1 includes the thermal-wind terms. For the advection of \(s\) by the differential rotation alone, set thermal_wind = 0.
