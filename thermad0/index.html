    ThermaD = 0 with the thermal wind (thermal_wind = 1, 2)

    ThermaD is kore's thermal-diffusion coefficient. With ThermaD = 0 the heat equation has no diffusion: entropy perturbations are only carried by the flow, so the perturbations are adiabatic. kore writes the heat equation in a special, lower-order form in that case, and in that form the thermal-wind terms of a differential rotation (thermal_wind = 1, 2) could not be represented, so kore used to stop. This change gives exactly this case its own radial row weight and basis, and leaves every other configuration bitwise unchanged. All operator, assembly, budget, MPI and regression checks pass, and as ThermaD → 0 the eigenvalues of diffusive runs converge linearly to the ThermaD = 0 ones wherever the resolution allows; the remaining limitations are listed under Caveats.

    Results of the change, 2026-10-12. The checks are in tests/heat_dr/; the analysis scripts named below (track.py, coupling.py, dense.py) are not part of the repository. The parameter names are those of that date: the 'user def' profiles are now structure 'Wu 2005' with stratification 'top hat', the smoothed sun is structure '8-term fit' with stratification 'odd top hat' and diffusivity 'step', aux0 is r_out (background.md), and diff_rot_amplitude is diff_rot_ampl.

    Background: how kore stores the heat equation

    This section introduces the few code concepts the rest of the page relies on. Broader notation is in the notation section of the companion page.

    kore solves the generalized eigenproblem \(A x = \lambda B x\), with \(\lambda = \sigma + i\omega\) (\(\sigma\) the growth rate, \(\omega\) the frequency). The unknowns are the radial Chebyshev coefficients of the poloidal and toroidal flow (\(P\), \(T\)) and of the entropy perturbation \(s\), for each spherical-harmonic degree \(l\). The matrix rows come in sections: u and v for the two momentum equations, and section h for the heat (entropy) equation.

    • Row weight. Before projection, each equation is multiplied by a power of \(r\) (and, for the heat equation, by a power of \(p_0 = \rho_0 T_0\)). This makes every coefficient a polynomial in \(r\), so the matrices are banded.
    • Gegenbauer basis. The rows of a section are projected onto a Gegenbauer polynomial family \(C^{(k)}\). The \(k\)-th radial derivative of a Chebyshev series is exactly a \(C^{(k)}\) series, so a section that contains \(k\)-th derivatives needs at least a \(C^{(k)}\) basis. \(C^{(0)}\) is the Chebyshev basis itself. The order per section is stored in gebasis in bin/submatrices.py; gebasis[2] is section h.
    • bc rows and chop. For each \(l\), the top rows of a section's block are replaced by boundary-condition (bc) rows. chop is the number of rows freed for them. Without diffusion the heat equation has no thermal bc, so section h has no bc rows.
    • Operator labels. Each radial operator is named by a short code string, decoded by utils.decode_label. For example h1tw20_D1 means: section h; digit 1, which lowers the row's power of \(r\) by one (rpower = base - rx, where the base is the row weight); profile tw2 differentiated 0 times; _D1, the first radial derivative of the unknown. The same label therefore means a different operator when the row weight of its section changes.
    • remroco. In the full sphere (ricb = 0, no inner core) the code removes rows and columns of the wrong radial parity. This requires each operator to have a definite parity in \(r\).

    The heat-equation rows exist in three variants. Here (eq) is the \(p_0\)-weighted heat equation of heat-equation.md §0, and the weight is applied to it:

    case row weight on (eq) basis of section h thermal bc rows
    ThermaD > 0 \(r^2\) \(C^{(2)}\) yes
    ThermaD = 0, old ("old rows") \(r/p_0\) \(C^{(0)}\) none
    ThermaD = 0 with the thermal wind ("new rows", this change) \(r^2/p_0\) \(C^{(1)}\) none

    The old rows are still used with ThermaD = 0 when thermal_wind = 0 or diff_rot = 0.

    Thermal wind. With differential rotation \(\Omega = \Omega_0(1 + \Delta\Omega)\), the background entropy gains a latitudinal part \(S_{tw}\) (the thermal-wind entropy) fixed by thermal-wind balance. It is expanded in Legendre polynomials, with coefficients \(\sigma_n\):

    \[ S_{tw}(r,\theta) = \sum_{n = 2, 4, 6} \sigma_n(r)\thinspace P_n(\cos\theta). \]

    The parameter thermal_wind selects it: 0 none, 1 the linear thermal wind, 2 the exact one (adds \(\sigma_6\)), 3 a prescribed \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\). Gaspard (\(G\)) is the frame rotation rate in units of the time unit. The differential rotation adds three kinds of terms to the heat equation (heat-equation.md §0–2):

    \[ p_0\left[\thinspace i\thinspace m\thinspace G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace s \mkern5mu +\mkern5mu u_r\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial r} \mkern5mu +\mkern5mu \frac{u_\theta}{r}\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta} \right]. \]
    • (a) is the advection of \(s\) by the differential rotation. It is present whenever diff_rot = 1.
    • (b) is the radial advection of \(S_{tw}\).
    • (c) and (c′) are the latitudinal advection of \(S_{tw}\) by the poloidal (\(P\)) and toroidal (\(T\)) parts of \(u_\theta\).

    (b), (c) and (c′) exist only with thermal_wind ≥ 1. In kore's profile names, tw2, tw4, tw6 hold \(\sigma_n\) and tp2, tp4, tp6 hold \(\sigma_n'\), each times \(p_0\) except in the new ThermaD = 0 mode below.

    Why ThermaD = 0 needed a separate treatment

    This section explains why the old ThermaD = 0 rows cannot hold the thermal-wind terms, and why the fix is to change the weight and the basis rather than drop the \(1/p_0\).

    With ThermaD = 0 kore writes the heat equation divided by \(p_0\), because \(p_0 \approx 10^{-7}\) near the surface made the \(p_0\)-weighted rows ill-conditioned. It also multiplies it by \(r\), projects it in a \(C^{(0)}\) Gegenbauer basis, and uses no thermal bc rows: decode_label gives section h rpower = 1 - rx, and submatrices.py sets gebasis[2] = 0.

    Term (c) of heat-equation.md §2, the advection of the thermal-wind entropy by \(u_\theta\), is, before the row weight,

    \[ p_0\thinspace \sigma_n\thinspace \frac{P + r P' + r\thinspace (\ln\rho)'\thinspace P}{r^2}\mkern5mu P_n'(X)\thinspace D , \]

    where \(X\) and \(D\) are the angular coupling matrices of heat-equation.md §2. Two things break:

    1. Basis. Its \(P'\) part (h1tw20_D1, h1tw40_D1, h1tw60_D1) is a first derivative, which lands in \(C^{(1)}\). In a \(C^{(0)}\) section, submatrices.py builds G[gbx][gbx-dx] = G[0][-1]. Python's negative index returns G0[-1] = 1 (the identity) instead of failing. The D1 output, a \(C^{(1)}\) series, would then be read as Chebyshev coefficients. Silently wrong.
    2. Row weight. With the \(r\) weight, the \(P\) part (h2tw20_D0) decodes to rpower = 1 - 2 = -1, a profile \(\sigma_n/r\). In the full sphere \(\sigma_2(0) \neq 0\) for the conical, Y20 and solar profiles (\(\sigma_2 \propto r f/g\) and \(g \propto r\)). So \(\sigma_n/r\) is singular at \(r = 0\) and has the wrong parity for remroco. At ricb > 0 it would be regular, but item 1 remains.

    The old code stopped in submatrices.py before reaching either problem. Dropping the \(1/p_0\) would fix item 2 but bring back the conditioning problem. The fix therefore keeps the \(1/p_0\) and, for this case only, changes the row weight from \(r\) to \(r^2\) and the basis from \(C^{(0)}\) to \(C^{(1)}\).

    What changed, file by file

    This section lists every file touched, so that a reader can find where the new mode is decided and which outputs it affects.

    The new mode is decided in one place, bin/utils.py:

    adiab_tw = thermal == 1 and ThermaD == 0 and diff_rot == 1 and thermal_wind >= 1

    In this mode the section-h rows are \(r^2\thinspace (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) in \(C^{(1)}\), still without bc rows, and they reuse the labels of both paths.

    • bin/utils.py: adiab_tw (the only place that decides the mode). decode_label gives section h rpower = 2 - rx when adiab_tw. So:
      • h0_D0 is \(r^2 s\);
      • h1dSd0_D0 is \(r^2 u_r S'\) (as \(r L P S'\));
      • h0abu0_D0/h0aub0_D0 is the \(s\) advection with the \(r^2\) weight;
      • the thermal-wind labels of the ThermaD > 0 path (h1tp20_D0, h2tw20_D0, h1tw20_D1, h1tw20lho1_D0, h1tw20_D0, and the 4, 6 ones) decode as they do there.
    • bin/submatrices.py:
      • gebasis[2] = 1 if ut.adiab_tw else 0;
      • the sys.exit for ThermaD = 0 with the thermal wind is removed;
      • G[gbx][gbx-dx] raises a ValueError (label, dx, basis) when dx > gbx;
      • chop = 0 for section h when ThermaD = 0 (at ricb > 0, chop = gbx = 1 would otherwise put one zero row on top of every section-h block);
      • comments.
    • bin/radial_profiles.py: ptw(r) returns \(p_0\), or 1 when adiab_tw. tw2/4/6 and tp2/4/6 use it, so in the new mode they are \(\sigma_n\) and \(\sigma_n'\) (bitwise the same as before when ThermaD > 0).
    • bin/operators.py: comments only. thermal_diffrot already used the same (b), (c), (c′) labels for both paths, and (a) uses h0abu0_D0/h0aub0_D0 whenever ThermaD = 0.
    • bin/params_default.py: thermal_wind comment: ThermaD > 0 is no longer required.
    • bin/assemble.py: unchanged. bc_theta_spherical returns nothing for ThermaD = 0, and section h is packed from the operators.
    • bin/ru_insane.py, tests/heat_dr/ebal.py: gb['h'] = 1 in the new mode; chop['h'] = 0 for every ThermaD = 0 run; entropy pairing weight wgt['h'] = p₀ (rows \(r^2\thinspace (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\)) instead of \(p_0 r\). The pairing weight is what the checks multiply the rows by when they pair them with \(s^\ast\) to form the entropy budget. heat_check.py, asm_check.py, terms.py and sd_check.py take these from ebal.py.
    • tests/heat_dr/asm_check.py: docstring. In the new mode the reference run is the same run with diff_rot_amplitude = 0, because diff_rot = 0 or thermal_wind = 0 gives the old \(r\thinspace (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) rows in \(C^{(0)}\). A wrong reference trips the existing assertion, which now hints at that.
    • tests/heat_dr/run_all.sh:
      • new-mode runs k1 km0 km2b ky20wb kshell kg3 ksun ksun_a with thermal_wind 1 and 2, and their z_* references (amplitude 0), in the heat_check and asm_check lists;
      • rigid configurations K1 (\(G = 1\)) and K13 (\(G = 3\)), whose baseline is rigid with amplitude 0 (the same heat rows);
      • terms.py and sd_check.py on k1_tw2.
    • tests/heat_dr/README.md, docs/heat-equation.md: the asm_check reference; §0 row weights, §3 the implementation, §5 the submatrices row, §7 the open item updated.

    Deviations from the proposed design:

    • chop = 0 for section h (one line in submatrices.py). It was needed: at ricb > 0, \(C^{(1)}\) would otherwise reserve one zero row per \(l\) for a bc that does not exist. Mutation M4 below shows what that does.
    • asm_check and the rigid test need a reference with the same heat rows, so they use the diff_rot_amplitude = 0 run instead of the diff_rot = 0 one. No code beyond comments and the run_all lines.

    Bitwise constraint: ThermaD > 0 runs, and ThermaD = 0 runs with thermal_wind = 0 or diff_rot = 0, never enter the new branches (adiab_tw is False; ptw returns prf.pressure(r, 0) as before; chop was already 0 for gbx = 0). Confirmed in test f.

    Results

    This section collects the tests: operator-level checks (a, b), eigenvalue identities and limits (c, d), energy budgets (e), regression and MPI (f, g), and independent reviews (h).

    \[ \rho \propto \left(1 - \mathrm{aux0}^2 r^2\right)^2, \qquad (\ln\rho)' = -\frac{4\thinspace \mathrm{aux0}^2\thinspace r}{1 - \mathrm{aux0}^2 r^2}, \]

    Reading. When N resolves the ThermaD > 0 run, the eigenvalues converge to the ThermaD = 0 ones linearly in ThermaD, with the same slope for both bcs:

    \[ \lambda(\mathrm{ThermaD}) - \lambda(0) \approx c\thinspace \mathrm{ThermaD}, \qquad c = 5 \text{ to } 40 . \]

    That holds at every ThermaD with zero-flux or no-slip walls, and down to 1e-7 (N 128) and 1e-8 (N 256) with fixed entropy and stress-free walls.

    The exception is the fixed-entropy, stress-free case at small ThermaD. There the ThermaD = 0 entropy is not zero at the wall: \(u_\theta \neq 0\) there, and so is the source \(u_\theta\thinspace \partial_\theta S_{tw}/r\). A ThermaD > 0 run must bring \(s\) to zero at the wall, so it has a boundary layer of width

    \[ \delta \sim \left(\frac{\mathrm{ThermaD}}{\lvert\lambda\rvert}\right)^{1/2}, \]

    about 1e-4 at 1e-8 and 1e-5 at 1e-10.

    • Below the resolved range its eigenvalue stalls 1e-6 to 1e-4 away, and the gap falls with N (T1 at 1e-10: 9.9e-6, 6.0e-6, 1.7e-6 at N 96, 128, 256; T5 at 1e-8: 1.0e-5, 6.2e-6, 2.2e-6, 1.6e-7 at N 96, 128, 192, 256).
    • The ThermaD = 0 runs have no layer and are converged at N 96.
    • thermal_wind = 0 has no such layer: \(u_r = 0\) and \(S' = 0\) at the walls make \(s = 0\) there anyway.

    e. Balances

    These check that the eigenvectors satisfy the energy and entropy budgets, with the new terms included.

    • sd_check.py compares the powers computed by spin_doctor (kore's post-processing diagnostics) with the matrix pairings: dWdr for the flow, dWdr_thm for the entropy.
    • resid1, resid2 (flow) and resid3 (entropy) are spin_doctor's budget residuals.
    • terms.py checks the entropy balance built from the matrix rows, whose residual is

    h. Independent reviews

    The reviews covered the discretisation and the numerics.

    Discretisation: no defect.

    • B matches only the claimed combination (\(C^{(1)}\), \(r^2\), first row) to 1e-15; every other basis, row or weight is off by ≥ 0.19.
    • 19 other configurations are byte-identical to the old code (every .mtx), MPI with 2 ranks too.
    • Its one finding, that the \(p_0\)-weighted checks cannot see the rows near the wall, gave row_check.py (a).

    Numerics.

    • With \(S_{tw} = 0\) the new rows reproduce the old-row spectrum (conical N 96: ≤ 4e-8; Y20-wall-bounded: 69 eigenvalues, median 1e-9, max 5e-7).
    • The smoothed sun with thermal_wind 2 converges in N: for the discrete modes, \(\lambda\) changes by about \(10^{-8}\) to \(2\times10^{-8}\) between N 160 and N 200.
    • ThermaD = 1e-10 at N 200 is within 8e-9 of ThermaD = 0 there.
    • The growing modes of the caveats (see Caveats).

    Caveats

    These are the limitations a user of ThermaD = 0 with the thermal wind should know about.

    Singular limit. With fixed-entropy, stress-free walls, ThermaD → 0 at fixed N is a singular limit (d). The ThermaD = 0 run is the well-resolved one. A "tiny ThermaD instead of 0" run, as the params comment suggests for the old ThermaD = 0, is not equivalent below ThermaD ~ 1e-7 at N 128.

    Continuous spectrum and small-scale growing modes. Term (a) without diffusion has a continuous spectrum,

    \[ \lambda = -i\thinspace m G\thinspace \Delta\Omega(r, \theta). \]

    ThermaD = 0 with thermal_wind = 0 has it too. The discretization shows it as hundreds of eigenvalues near \(\sigma = 0\). With the thermal wind some of them grow:

    • 22 (19 with thermal_wind 2) with \(\sigma \leq 0.011\) for k1 (N 96, lmax 20);
    • 6 with \(\sigma \leq 0.013\) for Y20-wall-bounded;
    • 26 up to \(\sigma = 0.10\) for the smoothed sun (ricb 0, N 160, lmax 30).

    They are converged in N but not in lmax: their eigenfunctions peak at \(l \approx \mathrm{lmax}\), and the sun has 43 and 57 of them, \(\sigma\) up to 0.12, at lmax 40 and 50. The ThermaD = 1e-10 runs have the same ones. They are absent with thermal_wind = 0, with \(S_{tw} = 0\) in the new rows, and with ThermaD = 1e-3. So they are small-scale instabilities of the diffusionless thermal wind, unresolved in \(l\), not an artefact of the new rows.

    Eigensolver near the band. ARPACK converges badly near it: csolve at 0 + 0.6i got 11/12 after 5000 iterations at ThermaD 0 and at 1e-10, and some shift-inverts at T2 took tens of minutes. Use shifts away from that band.

    Resolution. In the full sphere, ThermaD = 0 eigenfunctions need more N than ThermaD = 1e-3 ones (e: N 120 → 200 for the smoothed sun).

    Ruiz prescaling. solve.py turns Ruiz prescaling off automatically for ThermaD = 0. That was tuned on the \(r\thinspace (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) rows and is not tested here for the \(r^2\) rows (no PETSc/SLEPc in this container).

    Behaviour change. A run with thermal = 1, diff_rot = 1, ThermaD = 0 and the default thermal_wind = 1 used to stop and now runs. The old behaviour (advection of \(s\) only) needs thermal_wind = 0, as before.

    Coverage. The rigid test (c) does not exercise the thermal wind, since \(S_{tw} = 0\) for a rigid offset. The thermal-wind blocks are covered by a, b and d.
