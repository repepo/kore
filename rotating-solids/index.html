    Rotating solids

    An axisymmetric mode of the fluid pulls on the mantle and on the inner core, and their rotation changes in return. rotdyn = 1 lets both solids turn as rigid bodies about the rotation axis. They are coupled to the fluid by the viscous and magnetic torques at the walls, and to each other by gravity.

    setting default meaning
    rotdyn 0 1: the mantle and the inner core turn under the torques
    MoIZ_M, MoIZ_IC 11.16, 9.2e-3 axial moments of inertia of the mantle and of the inner core, in units of the density unit times the outer radius of the fluid to the fifth power
    gTorque 5e-8 gravitational coupling: the torque between inner core and mantle per radian of misalignment (dimensionless)
    OmgtauIC 2.3e3 the time over which the inner core relaxes viscously back into alignment, times the rotation rate; np.inf for no relaxation
    vtrq, mtrq 1, 1 0 switches off the viscous or the magnetic torque on the solids

    The defaults are Earth-like (2.3e3 corresponds to a relaxation time of one year). A run needs an axisymmetric, equatorially symmetric eigenvalue problem with an inner core and a no-slip inner wall. The outer wall is no-slip, or stress-free with or without a drag (Drag at the outer wall):

    par.m = 0
par.symm = 1
par.bci = 1
par.bco = 1
par.rotdyn = 1

    How the solids are coupled

    Three unknowns join the fields: the rotation rates of the mantle and of the inner core relative to the frame, and the angle of the inner core relative to the mantle.

    • The walls move with the solids. The fluid at each no-slip wall turns with the solid beyond it. At a stress-free outer wall the fluid slips along the mantle, and a drag (if any) pulls it towards the mantle's rotation.
    • Viscous torque. The stress of the fluid on each wall (at a stress-free outer wall, only that of the drag).
    • Magnetic torque. The magnetic stress on whatever conducts beyond each wall: a thin conducting layer, a resolved mantle or a conducting inner core. An insulating solid feels none.
    • Gravitational torque. It pulls the inner core and the mantle back into alignment, while the inner core relaxes viscously towards alignment over OmgtauIC.

    No torque comes from outside, so fluid and solids together conserve their angular momentum. The eigenvalue 0 is the rigid rotation of everything; ignore its residuals.

    Output

    • rotdyn.npy: the two rotation rates and the angle, one column per solution, normalized as the eigenvector.
    • rotdyn.dat: one row per solution with the same three quantities, the gravitational torque, and the viscous and magnetic torques on the mantle and on the inner core (Output files).
    • With a field, resid𝐄 also counts the energy of the solids (Magnetic field).

    The angular momentum check: resid𝐋

    The angular momentum of the fluid changes only through the torques on it. spin_doctor computes both from the solution and prints resid𝐋, the relative mismatch of this balance. Like residσ, it should be small for a resolved mode. It is computed:

    • for eigenvalue problems with m = 0, symm = 1 (axial angular momentum) or m = 1, symm = -1 (equatorial angular momentum, which includes the spin-over mode), with or without the rotating solids;
    • for forced runs with m = 0, symm = 1 whose force on the fluid is known: libration (forcings 7 and 8), rotating walls (forcing 12), thermal forcing (forcing 13), and the wave-like tide (forcing 1).

    It is not computed with centrifugal = 1, for m = 1 with differential rotation, or with walls of zero radial stress. flow.dat holds the angular momentum, the torques and resid𝐋 (Output files).

    What is supported

    Supported: any structure; with or without buoyancy; a no-slip or a stress-free outer wall, with or without a drag; with or without a magnetic field (with any poloidal B0, and a toroidal B0_tor that vanishes at both walls); insulating walls, a thin conducting layer, a resolved mantle or a conducting inner core ('conducting, Chebys').

    Not supported (kore stops with a message):

    • m other than 0, symm other than 1, a full sphere, forced runs, or no flow (hydro = 0);
    • an inviscid fluid, an inner wall that is not no-slip, or an outer wall with zero radial stress (bco = 2);
    • a non-rotating frame (Gaspard = 0);
    • differential rotation, a background flow or the centrifugal deformation;
    • a toroidal B0_tor that does not vanish at both walls.
