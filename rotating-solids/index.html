    Rotating solids

    An axisymmetric mode of the fluid pulls on the mantle and on the inner core, and their rotation changes in return. rotdyn = 1 lets both solids turn as rigid bodies about the rotation axis, coupled to the fluid by the viscous and magnetic torques at the walls and to each other by gravity, as in the conductive_IC branch.

    setting default meaning
    rotdyn 0 1: the mantle and the inner core turn under the torques
    MoIZ_M, MoIZ_IC 11.16, 9.2e-3 axial moments of inertia of the mantle and of the inner core, in units of the run's density unit times the outer radius of the fluid to the fifth power
    gTorque 5e-8 gravitational coupling: the torque between the inner core and the mantle per radian of misalignment, divided by that unit and by the rotation rate squared
    OmgtauIC 2.3e3 the time over which the inner core relaxes viscously back into alignment, times the rotation rate; np.inf for no relaxation
    vtrq, mtrq 1, 1 0 switches off the viscous or the magnetic torque on the solids; the fluid still follows the walls

    The defaults are those of the conductive_IC branch for the Earth (2.3e3 is a relaxation time of one year). A run needs an axisymmetric, equatorially symmetric eigenvalue problem with a no-slip inner wall; the outer wall is no-slip, as here, or stress-free, with or without a drag (Drag at the outer wall):

    par.m = 0
par.symm = 1
par.bci = 1
par.bco = 1
par.rotdyn = 1

    How the solids are coupled

    Three unknowns join the fields: the rotation rates of the mantle and of the inner core relative to the frame, and the angle of the inner core relative to the mantle.

    • The walls move with the solids. The fluid at each no-slip wall turns with the solid beyond it. At a stress-free outer wall the fluid slips along the mantle; a drag pulls it towards the mantle's rotation.
    • Viscous torque. The stress of the fluid on each wall: at a stress-free outer wall, that of the drag, or none.
    • Magnetic torque. The magnetic stress through each wall, which acts on whatever conducts beyond it: a thin conducting wall, a resolved mantle or a conducting inner core. An insulating solid feels none.
    • Gravitational torque. It pulls the inner core and the mantle back into alignment, in proportion to the angle between them, while the inner core relaxes viscously towards alignment over OmgtauIC.

    No torque comes from outside, so the fluid and the solids together conserve their angular momentum: every mode with a nonzero eigenvalue carries none in total. The eigenvalue 0 is the rigid rotation of everything, whose residuals mean nothing.

    Output

    • real_rotdyn.field, imag_rotdyn.field: the two rotation rates and the angle, one column per solution, in the normalisation of the eigenvector (the kinetic energy of the fluid, plus Hendrik times its magnetic energy, is 1).
    • rotdyn.dat: one row per solution with the same three, the gravitational torque, and the viscous and magnetic torques on the mantle and on the inner core (Output files).
    • With a field, resid𝐄 also counts the rotational energy of the solids, their gravitational energy and the power of the torques on them (Magnetic field), when both torques act (vtrq and mtrq 1).

    The angular momentum check: resid𝐋

    The angular momentum of the fluid changes only through the torques on it, which spin_doctor computes from the fields, apart from the matrices. It prints resid𝐋, the relative residual of that balance, for eigenvalue problems with the flow and either m = 0, symm = 1 (the axial angular momentum) or m = 1, symm = -1 (the equatorial one, which the Coriolis force turns; the spin-over mode is one of these), with or without the rotating solids. It is not computed with centrifugal = 1, nor for m = 1 with a differential rotation. Like residσ, it should be small for a resolved mode. flow.dat has the angular momentum, the torques and resid𝐋 (Output files).

    What is supported

    Supported: any structure; with or without buoyancy; with or without a field, with any poloidal B0 and a toroidal B0_tor that vanishes at both walls; insulating walls, a thin conducting layer, a resolved mantle or a conducting inner core.

    Not supported (the run stops at startup):

    • m other than 0, symm other than 1, a full sphere (ricb = 0), forced runs, or no flow (hydro = 0);
    • ViscosD = 0, an inner wall that is not no-slip (bci other than 1), or an outer wall that is neither no-slip nor stress-free (bco = 2);
    • Gaspard = 0;
    • a differential rotation (diff_rot = 1) or centrifugal = 1;
    • a toroidal B0_tor that does not vanish at a wall;
    • MoIZ_M or MoIZ_IC not positive and finite, gTorque negative or infinite, OmgtauIC not positive, vtrq or mtrq other than 0 or 1, rotdyn other than 0 or 1.
