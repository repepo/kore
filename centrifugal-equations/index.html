    Centrifugal deformation equations

    This page gives the mathematics behind centrifugal = 1, for readers who want to check the code or extend it: the coordinates that follow the level surfaces, the equations in them and their rows, the magnetic field, the dissipative terms, the differential rotation, the exact spheroid, the flattening of each shape, the effective gravity, the energy budgets, the plots, the accuracy, and the checks. How to set up a run is on Background model. The code is in bin/centrifugal.py, the checks in tests/centrifugal/.

    Coordinates that follow the level surfaces

    kore's flow conserves mass exactly only when the density depends on the radius alone. In a flattened body the density is constant on spheroids, so rather than give every profile a part that depends on latitude, kore maps the body onto a reference space in which the level surfaces are spheres.

    A point \(\boldsymbol\alpha\) of the reference space, at radius \(r\) and colatitude \(\theta\), is at \(\mathbf X = \boldsymbol\alpha + \boldsymbol\xi\) in the body, with

    \[ \boldsymbol\xi = \varepsilon(r)\thinspace\nabla\left(\tfrac12 r^2P_2(\cos\theta)\right) = \varepsilon(r)\thinspace r\left[P_2\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r} + \tfrac12\frac{dP_2}{d\theta}\thinspace\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\right], \qquad \varepsilon = -\tfrac23 f , \]

    where \(f(r)\) is the flattening of the level surface \(r\), positive for an oblate body. At first order that surface is at the distance \(r(1 + \varepsilon P_2)\) from the centre: its flattening is \(f\) and its volume that of the sphere of radius \(r\). In Cartesian components \(\boldsymbol\xi = \varepsilon(r)\thinspace(-\alpha_x/2, -\alpha_y/2, \alpha_z)\), an axisymmetric stretch at each radius, so the map is smooth at the centre when \(\varepsilon\) is even and smooth in \(r\); a purely radial \(\boldsymbol\xi\) would not be. Every background profile is a function of \(r\), and the walls are the level surfaces \(r\) = ricb and \(r = 1\).

    With the deformation gradient \(\mathsf F = \partial\mathbf X/\partial\boldsymbol\alpha = \mathsf I + \nabla\boldsymbol\xi\) and \(J = \det\mathsf F\), kore's poloidal and toroidal scalars describe the flow carried back to the reference space,

    \[ \mathbf w = J\thinspace\mathsf F^{-1}\mathbf u . \]

    This transform keeps what kore's formulation relies on. Mass: \(\nabla_\alpha\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf w) = J\thinspace\nabla_X\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf u)\), so the exact \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf w) = 0\) of kore's scalars is exact mass conservation in the body. Walls: \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n\) is proportional to \(w_r\) on a level surface, and \(\mathbf u = 0\) where \(\mathbf w = 0\), so the impenetrable and no-slip rows are unchanged.

    Equations

    kore removes the pressure by taking curls of the momentum equation. In the reference space this works when the equation is written with covariant components, \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\) times it: the pressure force stays a gradient, \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\nabla_X\Pi = \nabla_\alpha\Pi\). So does the buoyancy, which acts along the gradient of the effective potential, constant on the level surfaces. The dimensionless equations of Anelastic equations become

    \[ \mathsf M\thinspace\partial_t\mathbf w + 2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\tilde{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w = -\nabla\Pi + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g_{\rm eff}\thinspace s\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r} + \dots , \qquad \mathsf M = \frac{\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathsf F}{J}, \quad \tilde{\mathbf z} = \mathsf F^{-1}\hat{\mathbf z}, \]
    \[ p_0\left(J\thinspace\partial_t s + w_r\frac{dS}{dr}\right) = \dots , \]

    with the dots the viscous force and the thermal diffusion, which take covariant forms too (Dissipative terms). Three terms of the ideal fluid change: the inertia gains the metric \(\mathsf M\), the Coriolis force turns with the map (\(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathsf F\mathbf w)/J = \tilde{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w\)), and the rate of change of the entropy gains \(J\). The advection keeps its form, \(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla_X S = w_r S'/J\), and the buoyancy takes the effective gravity \(g_{\rm eff}\) (below).

    At first order. With \(\eta = r\varepsilon'\) and \(w_z = \hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\mathbf w\),

    \[ (\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\thinspace\mathbf w = \varepsilon\left(3w_z\hat{\mathbf z} - \mathbf w\right) + \eta\left[\tfrac32\cos\theta\left(w_z\hat{\mathbf r} + w_r\hat{\mathbf z}\right) - w_r\hat{\mathbf r} - P_2\thinspace\mathbf w\right], \]
    \[ (\tilde{\mathbf z} - \hat{\mathbf z})\times\mathbf w = -\varepsilon\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w - \eta\left[\tfrac32\cos^2\theta\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w - \tfrac12\cos\theta\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf w\right], \]
    \[ J - 1 = \eta\thinspace P_2 . \]

    A uniform flattening has \(\eta = 0\): the map is then a linear stretch, and \(J = 1\) at this order.

    Rows

    The new terms have the form of the force of a differential rotation, so kore builds them with the same tools. Each one multiplies the components of \(\mathbf w\) by polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) of degree 2 at most, with the radial factor \(\varepsilon\) or \(\eta\); kore builds their couplings with the recurrence algebra of bin/angular.py and projects them on its rows as it does the force (Spectral form), without the terms that come from the shear of the rotation.

    • Selection rules. A degree \(l\) couples to \(l\) and \(l \pm 2\) of the same kind (poloidal to poloidal, toroidal to toroidal, entropy to entropy), and to \(l \pm 1\) and \(l \pm 3\) of the other kind (poloidal to toroidal).
    • Matrices. \(A\) gains \(2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace(\tilde{\mathbf z} - \hat{\mathbf z})\times\mathbf w\) in the momentum rows. \(B\) gains \(-(\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\thinspace\mathbf w\) in the momentum rows and \((J - 1)\thinspace s = \eta P_2\thinspace s\) in the heat rows, with the signs of the inertia and of the entropy. The poloidal–toroidal couplings of the inertia are imaginary, so \(B\) is complex.
    • Radial operators. Their labels are those of the differential rotation with the profile code of \(\varepsilon\) (ce0) or \(\eta\) (ce1) in place of that of \(\Delta\Omega\), and that of \(r\varepsilon'\) (ce1) or \(r\eta'\) (ce2) for the derivative terms (centrifugal.MOMENTUM). The heat rows use the operator of the entropy times ce1. The three codes are Chebyshev fits on kore's grid, even in \(r\).

    Magnetic field

    A field is carried back to the reference space in the same way as the flow, so that kore's poloidal and toroidal scalars of the field keep their meaning and most of its rows keep the form they have in a sphere.

    The field as a flux. kore's scalars describe

    \[ \tilde{\mathbf b} = J\thinspace\mathsf F^{-1}\mathbf b, \qquad \tilde{\mathbf B}_0 = J\thinspace\mathsf F^{-1}\mathbf B_0 . \]

    The flux of \(\mathbf b\) through a surface of the body is that of \(\tilde{\mathbf b}\) through its image in the reference space, so \(\nabla\cdot\tilde{\mathbf b} = 0\) is exact and the field lines of \(\tilde{\mathbf b}\) are those of \(\mathbf b\), moved with the level surfaces. kore's formula for B0 and B0_tor gives \(\tilde{\mathbf B}_0\): the background field of the body is \(\mathsf F\tilde{\mathbf B}_0/J\), the field of the sphere stretched with the level surfaces.

    Equations. The Lorentz force is written with covariant components, as the rest of the momentum equation, and the induction equation for the flux:

    \[ \mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf F_L = \frac{\mathrm{Hendrik}}{\rho_0 J}\left[\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0 + \nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf B}_0)\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\right], \qquad \partial_t\tilde{\mathbf b} = \nabla\times\left(\frac{\mathbf w\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0}{J} - \mathrm{MagnetD}\thinspace\mathsf M\thinspace\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\right) . \]

    Two identities of the map give them. The current of the body, carried back as a flux, is \(\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\); and the cross product of two fields carried back as fluxes, \(\mathbf a = \mathsf F\tilde{\mathbf a}/J\) and \(\mathbf c = \mathsf F\tilde{\mathbf c}/J\), has the covariant components \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf a\times\mathbf c) = (\tilde{\mathbf a}\times\tilde{\mathbf c})/J\). The electric field of the diffusion is MagnetD times the current of the body, whose covariant components are \(\mathsf M\thinspace\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\) (Dissipative terms).

    At first order. With \(\mathsf M - \mathsf I\) and \(J - 1 = \eta P_2\) from above, and the currents \(\mathbf j = \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\) and \(\mathbf J_0 = \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0\) of the sphere, the force and the electric field of the induction gain

    \[ \delta\mathbf G = -\eta P_2\left(\mathbf J_0\times\tilde{\mathbf b} + \mathbf j\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0\right) + \left[\nabla\times\left((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf B}_0\right)\right]\times\tilde{\mathbf b} + \left[\nabla\times\left((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf b}\right)\right]\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0 , \qquad \delta\mathbf E = -\eta P_2\thinspace\mathbf w\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0 . \]

    A uniform flattening has \(\eta = 0\), and only the two currents of the metric remain. A flattening that varies with the radius gives the background field a current even where the spherical formula has none: the axial field, for one, is no longer uniform in the body.

    Rows. \(\delta\mathbf G\) enters the momentum rows as the Lorentz force of the sphere does (times Hendrik), and \(\nabla\times\delta\mathbf E\) the rows of \(f\) and \(g\) as the induction does; both are built with the recurrence algebra of bin/mag_algebra.py. A background of degrees up to \(n\) couples a degree \(l\) to \(l \pm (n + 3)\) at most, two more than in the sphere. The radial factors are products of the profile codes of the deformation and of the background: a label carries the amplitude E (\(\varepsilon\)), N (\(\eta\)), C (\(r\eta'\)) or Q (\(r(r\eta')'\)), then p or t and the degree of the poloidal or toroidal profile (centrifugal.product_code).

    Insulating walls. Outside an insulating wall the field is a potential field \(\nabla\phi\) of the body, and the wall is a level surface, so the matching happens on the spheroid. In the reference space, at the wall \(r = R\), the flux through the wall and the tangential field are continuous:

    \[ \tilde b_r = \left[J\thinspace\mathsf F^{-1}\nabla\phi(\mathbf X)\right]_r, \qquad \left[\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b}\right]_{\rm tan} = \nabla_{\rm tan}\thinspace\phi(\mathbf X(\boldsymbol\alpha)) . \]

    At first order, with \(m_s\) and \(m_t\) the spheroidal and toroidal parts of \((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf b}\) at the wall, \(\delta s = (\boldsymbol\xi\cdot\nabla\phi)/R\) and \(\delta q = -[\nabla\times(\boldsymbol\xi\times\nabla\phi)]_r\) (each of degree \(l\)), eliminating the potential gives the rows

    \[ (l+1)F + RF' + R(m_s - \delta s) - \frac{R\thinspace\delta q}{l+1} = 0, \qquad G + m_t = 0 \qquad \text{(outer wall)}, \]
    \[ lF - RF' - R(m_s - \delta s) - \frac{R\thinspace\delta q}{l} = 0, \qquad G + m_t = 0 \qquad \text{(insulating inner core)}, \]

    which couple \(F_l\) to \(F_{l\pm2}\), \(G_{l\pm1}\) and \(G_{l\pm3}\), and \(G_l\) to \(G_{l\pm2}\), \(F_{l\pm1}\) and \(F_{l\pm3}\).

    Conducting inner core. The inner core is deformed with the fluid, on the same map, and its field is a flux too. With the permeability of the fluid (mu_i2o = 1) the field \(\tilde{\mathbf b}\) is continuous at the inner boundary, since \(\mathbf b\) is and the map is smooth across it, so the rows that match the field keep their form. The tangential electric field is continuous too. The flow vanishes at the no-slip boundary, so on each side this field is that of the diffusion, and its row gains the metric part of the diffusion of the fluid and of the core (Dissipative terms).

    Dissipative terms

    The viscous force and the thermal and magnetic diffusion act in the body, so a damped mode feels the deformed walls and the deformed profiles through them. kore carries each back to the reference space as it does the inertia: it keeps its spherical form and gains a part of first order in \(f\).

    Viscous force. With \(\mathbf V(\mathbf u) = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\thinspace\mathsf S)\) the force per unit mass (Anelastic equations), its covariant form is, up to a gradient that the curl rows remove,

    \[ \mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathbf V\left(\frac{\mathsf F\mathbf w}{J}\right) = \mathbf V(\mathbf w) - \boldsymbol\xi\times\nabla\times\mathbf V(\mathbf w) + \mathbf V\left(\nabla\times(\boldsymbol\xi\times\mathbf w) - \boldsymbol\xi\thinspace\nabla\cdot\mathbf w\right) - \xi_r\frac{d\nu}{dr}\thinspace\nabla\cdot\mathsf S(\mathbf w) - \nabla\left(\frac{\xi_r}{\rho_0}\frac{d(\rho_0\nu)}{dr}\right)\cdot\mathsf S(\mathbf w) . \]

    The second and third terms carry the force of the sphere along the displacement \(\boldsymbol\xi\); the last two come from \(\nu\) and \(\rho_0\), which are constant on the level surfaces rather than on the spheres.

    Thermal diffusion. The heat rows are the equation times \(J\), and the diffusion becomes

    \[ J\thinspace\nabla_X\cdot\left(\kappa p_0\nabla_X s\right) = \nabla\cdot\left(\kappa p_0\thinspace\mathsf M^{-1}\nabla s\right), \]

    whose first-order part is \(-\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0(\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\nabla s)\).

    Magnetic diffusion. The current of the body, carried back as a flux, is \(\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\), and the covariant components of the electric field of the diffusion are MagnetD times \(\mathsf M\thinspace\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\). Its first-order part is MagnetD times \((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf b} + \nabla\times((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf b})\). A conducting inner core gains the same terms with its own diffusivity.

    Walls. The no-slip, impenetrable and fixed-entropy rows keep their form: \(\mathbf w = 0\), \(w_r = 0\) and \(s = 0\) on a level surface are \(\mathbf u = 0\), \(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf n = 0\) and \(s = 0\) in the body. The two other walls change:

    • a stress-free wall has no tangential stress on the deformed wall: the tangential part of \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathsf S_X\mathsf F^{-\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf r}\) vanishes, with \(\mathsf S_X\) the rate of strain of the body, \(\mathsf F^{-\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf r}\) the normal to the level surface and \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\) taking the components along its tangents;
    • a fixed-flux wall has no flux through the deformed wall: \(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathsf M^{-1}\nabla s = 0\).

    A drag at a stress-free outer wall acts on the slip along the deformed wall, per unit of its area; carried back to the reference sphere, its tensor joins the stress-free rows (Drag at the outer wall equations).

    Rows. The first-order parts multiply the fields and their derivatives by polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) of degree 2 at most, so they couple the degrees as the inertia does: \(l\) to \(l\) and \(l \pm 2\) of the same kind, and to \(l \pm 1\) and \(l \pm 3\) of the other. Their radial factors are products of the amplitudes of the map, of the diffusivity and its derivatives, and of the derivatives of \(\ln\rho_0\) (centrifugal.diffusion_build). When \(m \neq 0\) the toroidal rows of the viscous force and of the induction reach the third derivative of \(P\) and of \(F\), and kore builds those sections in the basis that has it. In a full sphere these rows have terms in \(P/r\) and \(F/r\), polynomials only once the rows are multiplied by \(r^4\) and \(r^3\): kore does it for the field (in a conducting inner core too), and asks for rpower_v ≥ 4 for the flow.

    Differential rotation

    A differential rotation is a profile like the others: \(\Delta\Omega(r, \theta)\) is given in the reference space, so the point of the body at \(\mathbf X(r, \theta)\) turns at \(\Delta\Omega(r, \theta)\), and a rotation that depends on \(r\) alone is constant on the level surfaces. The flow of the body is \(\mathbf U_0 = \mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf X\). The map commutes with the rotations about the axis, so the flux of this flow is \(J\) times the spherical formula \(\mathbf U_{00} = \mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\boldsymbol\alpha\), and its terms change as follows.

    • Momentum. The force \((\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U_0\) gains, up to a gradient, with \(\boldsymbol\omega_0 = \nabla\times\mathbf U_{00}\),

      \[ \delta\mathbf W = -\mathbf U_{00}\times\nabla\times\left((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\thinspace\mathbf w\right) + (\varepsilon + \eta P_2)\thinspace\mathbf w\times\boldsymbol\omega_0 - \eta\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega\left(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w - \cos\theta\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf w\right). \]

    • Heat. The advection of the entropy times \(J\) is \((1 + \eta P_2)\thinspace\mathbf U_{00}\cdot\nabla s\): the heat rows gain \(i m\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\eta P_2\thinspace\Delta\Omega\thinspace s\).

    • Induction. The electric field of the flow carries back exactly, \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b) = \mathbf U_{00}\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\): no new term.

    A degree \(n\) of \(\Delta\Omega\) couples \(l\) to \(l \pm (n + 2)\) of the same kind and to \(l \pm (n + 3)\) of the other, two more than in the sphere (centrifugal.rotation_build).

    The exact spheroid

    When every level surface has the same flattening, the body is a stretched sphere, and kore keeps the map exactly, at any flattening: centrifugal_shape = 'spheroid'. It serves a homogeneous fluid in a strongly flattened body or container, such as a fast-rotating planet with a rigid mantle (or, with \(f < 0\), an elongated one), and it can be checked against exact solutions at any \(f\).

    The map. The point \(\boldsymbol\alpha\) of the reference space is at

    \[ \mathbf X = \mathsf F\boldsymbol\alpha, \qquad \mathsf F = \mathrm{diag}(a, a, c), \qquad \frac{c}{a} = 1 - f, \qquad a^2c = 1, \]

    so the level surface \(r\) is the spheroid of equatorial radius \(ar\) and polar radius \(cr\), with the volume of the sphere of radius \(r\). At first order in \(f\) this is the map of 'uniform', \(a = 1 + f/3\) and \(c = 1 - 2f/3\). The metric is constant, \(J = 1\), \(\mathsf M = \mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathsf F = \mathrm{diag}(a^2, a^2, c^2)\) and \(\tilde{\mathbf z} = \hat{\mathbf z}/c\), and the Laplacian of the body, written in the reference space, is

    \[ D = \nabla\cdot\left(\mathsf M^{-1}\nabla\right) = \frac{1}{a^2}\nabla^2 + \left(\frac{1}{c^2} - \frac{1}{a^2}\right)\partial_z^2 . \]

    The terms. Each term that changes gains a part that kore keeps whole. With \(\mathsf Z\mathbf v = \hat{\mathbf z}(\hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\mathbf v)\), \(\partial_z = \hat{\mathbf z}\cdot\nabla\) (on each Cartesian component of a vector) and \(x = \cos\theta\), the parts beyond the sphere's are

    term its part beyond the sphere's
    inertia \((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\thinspace\mathbf w = (a^2 - 1)\thinspace\mathbf w + (c^2 - a^2)\thinspace\mathsf Z\mathbf w\)
    Coriolis force \((\tilde{\mathbf z} - \hat{\mathbf z})\times\mathbf w = (1/c - 1)\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w\)
    viscous force \(\nu\left[(a^2/c^2 - 1)\thinspace\partial_z^2\mathbf w + (c^2 - a^2)\thinspace\mathsf Z D\mathbf w\right]\)
    thermal diffusion \(\kappa p_0\left[(1/a^2 - 1)\thinspace\nabla^2 s + (1/c^2 - 1/a^2)\thinspace\partial_z^2 s\right]\)
    traction on a level surface \((a^2/c^2 - 1)\thinspace x\thinspace\partial_z\mathbf w + (c^2 - a^2)\thinspace\mathsf Z\left[\partial_r\mathbf w/a^2 + (1/c^2 - 1/a^2)\thinspace x\thinspace\partial_z\mathbf w\right]\)
    flux through a level surface \((1/a^2 - 1)\thinspace\partial_r s + (1/c^2 - 1/a^2)\thinspace x\thinspace\partial_z s\)

    The traction is that of the stress-free wall (Dissipative terms), \(\mathsf F^{\mathsf T}\mathsf S_X\mathsf F^{-\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf r} = \mathsf M(\nabla\mathbf w)\mathsf M^{-1}\hat{\mathbf r} + (\nabla\mathbf w)^{\mathsf T}\hat{\mathbf r}\), with \((\nabla\mathbf w)_{ik} = \partial_k w_i\), less the sphere's. The rest keeps its spherical form: the time derivative of the entropy and its advection (\(J = 1\)), the pressure, and the buoyancy. With the gravity \(g = r\) of 'Boussinesq', the buoyancy of the reference space acts in the body along \(\mathsf F^{-\mathsf T}\boldsymbol\alpha = \nabla_X(r^2/2)\): normal to the level surfaces, as the effective gravity of a homogeneous body in hydrostatic equilibrium (a Maclaurin spheroid), whose effective potential depends on \(r\) alone.

    Rows. \(\mathsf Z\) multiplies by polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) of degree 2 and \(\partial_z\) raises or lowers the degree by one, so the inertia couples a degree \(l\) to \(l\) and \(l \pm 2\) of the same kind and to \(l \pm 1\) of the other, the viscous force and the stress-free rows reach \(l \pm 4\) and \(l \pm 3\), and the heat rows \(l \pm 2\). The radial operators are kore's own, with no profile (centrifugal.spheroid_build, spheroid_walls), and the blocks reach \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert\) = 4, one more than at first order.

    The field. The field is carried back as a flux, as at first order (Magnetic field). With \(J = 1\) the flow induces the flux \(\tilde{\mathbf b} = \mathsf F^{-1}\mathbf b\) as it does in the sphere, while the Lorentz force and the magnetic diffusion keep \(\mathsf M\) whole. With the currents \(\mathbf j = \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\) and \(\mathbf J_0 = \nabla\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0\) of the sphere and \(\delta = c^2 - a^2\), so that \(\mathsf M - \mathsf I = (a^2 - 1)\thinspace\mathsf I + \delta\thinspace\mathsf Z\), their parts beyond the sphere's are

    term its part beyond the sphere's
    Lorentz force, per Hendrik \((a^2 - 1)\left(\mathbf j\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0 + \mathbf J_0\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\right) + \delta\left\{[\nabla\times(\mathsf Z\tilde{\mathbf b})]\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0 + [\nabla\times(\mathsf Z\tilde{\mathbf B}_0)]\times\tilde{\mathbf b}\right\}\)
    electric field of the diffusion, per MagnetD \((a^4 - 1)\thinspace\mathbf j + a^2\delta\left[\mathsf Z\mathbf j + \nabla\times(\mathsf Z\tilde{\mathbf b})\right] + \delta^2\thinspace\mathsf Z\nabla\times(\mathsf Z\tilde{\mathbf b})\)

    The force takes the labels of the sphere's Lorentz force (bin/mag_algebra.py) and couples \(l\) to \(l \pm (n + 3)\) through a background of degree \(n\); the diffusion takes kore's own labels (eta0) and reaches \(l \pm 4\) (centrifugal.spheroid_magnetic_build, spheroid_build).

    Insulating walls. Outside an insulating wall kore matches the exact potential field of the spheroid. In the spheroidal coordinates confocal with the wall, the wall is a surface \(\nu = \nu_0\), and on it the angle of these coordinates is the reference colatitude. A potential of degree \(l\) on the wall therefore meets the degree \(l\) of the field alone, and the matching keeps one row per degree, as in the sphere. The flux through the wall and the tangential field are those of the potential \(\Phi_l\) on it,

    \[ \tilde b_{r,l} = \frac{\lambda_l}{R}\thinspace\Phi_l, \qquad \left[\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b}\right]_{{\rm tan},\thinspace l} = \nabla_{\rm tan}\Phi_l, \qquad \lambda_l = \frac{a^2}{k}\thinspace\frac{d\ln R_l}{d\nu}(\nu_0), \qquad k = \sqrt{\lvert a^2 - c^2\rvert}, \qquad \nu_0 = \frac{c}{k} , \]

    with \(kR\) the focal distance of the wall \(r = R\) and \(R_l\) the radial function of the potential: \(Q_l^m(i\nu)\) outside an oblate wall, \(Q_l^m(\nu)\) outside a prolate one, and \(P_l^m\) of the same argument inside an insulating inner core (the level surfaces are similar, so \(\nu_0\) is the same). In the sphere \(\lambda_l\) is \(-(l + 1)\) outside and \(l\) inside. Eliminating \(\Phi_l\), with \(m_s\) and \(m_t\) the spheroidal and toroidal parts of \((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf b}\) at the wall, gives the rows

    \[ l(l + 1)\thinspace F = \lambda_l\left(F + RF' + Rm_s\right), \qquad G + m_t = 0 , \]

    which kore divides by \(l + 1\) outside and by \(l\) inside, as the rows of the sphere. They couple \(F_l\) to \(F_{l\pm2}\) and \(G_{l\pm1}\), and \(G_l\) to \(F_{l\pm1}\). kore computes the logarithmic derivatives by recurrence, forward for \(P_l^m\) and backward for \(Q_l^m\) (centrifugal.spheroid_lambda, spheroid_field_walls).

    Conducting inner core. With mu_i2o = 1 the field is continuous at the inner boundary, as at first order, and the row of the tangential electric field gains the part of the diffusion above on each side, with the conductivity of each (centrifugal.spheroid_icb).

    Conducting mantles. A resolved mantle (mantle = 'resolved') fills the level surfaces between the wall and \(r\) = r_surface, spheroids of the same flattening. Its field diffuses with the metric of the fluid's, and with \(\sigma(r)\) it gains the part of \((\ln\sigma)'\,\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf E\) beyond the sphere's. At the wall the tangential electric field is continuous, as at a conducting inner core, and at r_surface kore matches the potential field of the spheroid. The thin layer (mantle = 'TWA') is the limit of that mantle as its thickness \(h\) goes to zero with \(\sigma h = c_1 R\) fixed, so c_cmb = 0 and mu = 1. It carries a sheet of current along the wall,

    \[ \tilde{\mathbf K} = \frac{c_1 R}{a^2}\left[\mathbf E_H + V(x)\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}_H\left(\hat{\mathbf z}_H\cdot\mathbf E_H\right)\right], \qquad V(x) = \frac{a^2 - c^2}{c^2 + (a^2 - c^2)\thinspace x^2} , \]

    where \(\mathbf E_H\) is the tangential electric field of the fluid at the wall, \(\hat{\mathbf z}_H\) the tangential part of \(\hat{\mathbf z}\) and \(x = \cos\theta\); the term in \(V\) makes the current along the radius vanish inside the layer. The sheet makes the tangential field jump to the potential field outside, and kore adds its spheroidal and toroidal parts to the rows of the insulating wall. \(V\) is not a polynomial: its Legendre series, of degree 28 at \(f\) = 0.1, couples the rows of the wall over that many degrees. The layer lies between level surfaces, so its thickness in the body is \(ah\) at the equator and \(ch\) at the poles (centrifugal.spheroid_twa, mantle_rows).

    A fluid may also slip along either mantle (bco = 0, or an inviscid fluid). Its \(\mathbf E_H\) then includes \(-(\mathbf w\times\tilde{\mathbf B}_0)_H\), which is the body's \(-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) in the reference variables (with \(J\) = 1), and the layer at rest takes it on, as in the sphere (condition C.I). For the resolved mantle this is the sphere's slip term in the row of the electric field at the wall. For the thin layer it goes through the sheet, weight \(V\) included (centrifugal.spheroid_twa_slip).

    Scope. The fluid is homogeneous (structure = 'Boussinesq', diffusivity = 'uniform'), with centrifugal_q = 0 (the effective gravity is already kore's \(g\)), and any \(f < 1\); with a field, any background, insulating walls, an inner core that is insulating or conducting with mu_i2o = 1, and a resolved mantle or a thin layer at the outer wall. kore stops with a differential rotation. The budgets of spin_doctor and the plots use the exact metric and map, and every budget closes to round-off (Energy budgets), except the energy budget resid𝐄 with a conducting mantle, which kore does not compute.

    The flattening

    The flattening of each level surface follows from hydrostatic balance in the rotating body. 'clairaut' solves for it, 'uniform' and 'user' take it as given.

    'clairaut'. Clairaut's equation, in Radau's form for \(\eta_R = d\ln f/d\ln x\), with \(x\) the radius in units of the radius of the body:

    \[ x\frac{d\eta_R}{dx} = 6 - 6\thinspace\frac{\rho}{\bar\rho}\thinspace(\eta_R + 1) - \eta_R^2 + \eta_R, \qquad \eta_R(0) = 0, \]

    with \(\bar\rho\) the mean density inside \(x\). kore takes the density ratio from the gravity alone, by Poisson's equation,

    \[ \frac{\rho}{\bar\rho} = \frac{x g' + 2g}{3g}, \]

    so the structure's \(g\) must be its self-gravity. kore integrates from the centre and fixes the scale of \(f\) by the surface condition \(x f' + 2f = \tfrac52 q\), that is

    \[ f(x_t) = \frac{5\thinspace q_t}{2\left(\eta_R(x_t) + 2\right)}, \qquad q_t = q\thinspace x_t^3 , \]

    at the top \(x_t\) of the structure: the surface \(x = 1\), or the outer wall for 'model S hydrostatic', whose gravity ends there (the mass above it neglected). With centrifugal_f set, the scale instead makes \(f\) = centrifugal_f at the outer wall. A homogeneous body has \(\eta_R = 0\) and \(f = 5q/4\), the Maclaurin spheroid at first order; a polytrope of index 1 has \(f/q = 15/(2\pi^2)\) at the surface. Since \(\eta = \varepsilon\thinspace\eta_R\), the codes ce1 and ce2 follow from \(\eta_R\) without differentiating:

    \[ r\varepsilon' = \varepsilon\thinspace\eta_R, \qquad r\left(r\varepsilon'\right)' = \varepsilon\left[6 - 6\thinspace\frac{\rho}{\bar\rho}\thinspace(\eta_R + 1) + \eta_R\right]. \]

    'uniform' and 'user'. 'uniform' has \(f\) = centrifugal_f and \(\eta = 0\). 'user' evaluates centrifugal_profile at \(\lvert r\rvert\) and takes \(r\varepsilon'\) and \(r(r\varepsilon')'\) from the derivatives of the Chebyshev fit of \(\varepsilon\).

    Effective gravity

    The buoyancy acts along the gradient of the effective potential, the gravitational plus the centrifugal one, which is constant on each level surface. At first order its parts in \(P_2\) cancel there, and what remains is

    \[ \Phi_{\rm eff} = \Phi_0(r) - \tfrac13\Omega^2r^2, \qquad g_{\rm eff} = \frac{d\Phi_{\rm eff}}{dr} = g - \tfrac23\Omega^2 r . \]

    In kore's units the second term is \(-\tfrac23\thinspace q_t\thinspace g(x_t)\thinspace x/x_t\) (centrifugal.g_monopole), which radial_profiles.graviX adds to \(g\) when centrifugal_q > 0, with any shape.

    Energy budgets

    The energies of the deformed body are integrals over the body. Written in the reference space they gain the metric, so spin_doctor adds the metric parts to its budgets, computed from the fields on its own grid (utils4pp.centrifugal_terms), independently of the matrices. In the conventions of Energy balance:

    \[ K = \int\rho_0\thinspace\mathbf w^*\cdot\mathsf M\thinspace\mathbf w\thinspace dV, \qquad T = \int p_0\thinspace J\thinspace\lvert s\rvert^2\thinspace dV , \]

    and the power of the Coriolis force is \(2\int\rho_0\thinspace\mathbf w^*\cdot[2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\tilde{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf w]\thinspace dV\), imaginary: it still does no work. solve.py normalises the eigenvectors to this \(K\). spin_doctor adds the metric parts of the viscous power, of the power of a differential rotation and of the entropy diffusion in the same way (utils4pp.centrifugal_dissipation_terms), so the budgets close as well as without the deformation. It computes neither the enstrophy residual nor the viscous one, whose wall terms keep their spherical form (residens and residD are NaN).

    With a field. The magnetic energy of the deformed fluid is

    \[ \tfrac12\int\tilde{\mathbf b}^*\cdot\mathsf M\thinspace\tilde{\mathbf b}\thinspace dV = \mathrm{ME} + \mathrm{MEc}, \]

    with MEc the part of the metric (magnetic.dat, column 12), and solve.py normalises to \(K\) plus Hendrik times it. spin_doctor adds the metric parts of the Lorentz work (\(\delta\mathbf G\)) to residσ and residω, and those of the induction work (\(\delta\mathbf E\)) and of the magnetic diffusion to resid𝐛 (utils4pp.centrifugal_magnetic_terms), so these close to round-off. The full budget resid𝐄 also counts the Ohmic loss with the metric current \(\nabla\times((\mathsf M - \mathsf I)\tilde{\mathbf b})\), the energy of the potential field matched on each deformed wall, and the field of a conducting inner core with its metric. It closes at second order in \(f\) only: kore keeps the first order of \(1/J\) and of \(\mathsf M\) in the force, in the induction, in the diffusion and in the matching at the walls, and the energy does not cancel their products. In the exact spheroid, where kore keeps \(\mathsf M\) whole, it closes to round-off: the Ohmic loss there is MagnetD times \(\int\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})^*\cdot\mathsf M\thinspace\nabla\times(\mathsf M\tilde{\mathbf b})\thinspace dV\), and the energy of the potential field beyond each wall is a wall integral with the \(\lambda_l\) of its rows (centrifugal.spheroid_potential).

    Plots

    tools/plot_field.py draws the fields where they are in the body (centrifugal.to_physical). The reference point \((r, \theta)\) is at the distance \(\varpi\) from the axis and the height \(z\),

    \[ \varpi = r\sin\theta\thinspace(1 - \varepsilon/2), \qquad z = r\cos\theta\thinspace(1 + \varepsilon), \]

    and the flow there is \(\mathbf u = \mathsf F\mathbf w/J\), at first order

    \[ \mathbf u = \mathbf w + \varepsilon\left(\tfrac32 w_z\hat{\mathbf z} - \tfrac12\mathbf w\right) + \eta\thinspace w_r\left(\tfrac32\cos\theta\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z} - \tfrac12\hat{\mathbf r}\right) - \eta P_2\thinspace\mathbf w , \]

    given in the spherical basis of the body point, turned by \(-\tfrac32\varepsilon\sin\theta\cos\theta\) in colatitude from that of \((r, \theta)\). The field \(\mathbf b = \mathsf F\tilde{\mathbf b}/J\) maps in the same way. A scalar, such as \(s\), only moves.

    Accuracy

    The map, the transform of the flow and the covariant equations are exact. kore keeps them at first order in \(f\), except for the exact spheroid (The exact spheroid), where the only error is that of the discretization.

    • First order. The error is of order \(f^2\). For the spin-over of a Maclaurin spheroid it is \(f^2/6\) in the frequency (Checks): 0.2 % at Saturn's flattening, where the deformation moves the frequency by 10 %. submatrices.py warns above \(f = 0.1\).
    • Thermal wind. With thermal_wind 1 or 2 the entropy \(S_{tw}\) balances \(\Delta\Omega\) as in the sphere: the terms of order \(f\thinspace\Delta\Omega\) of that balance are missing, while its advection and the other terms of \(\Delta\Omega\) are those of the deformed body. submatrices.py warns.
    • What stops the run. The rows of a wall of zero radial stress (bco or bci = 2) would change on a deformed wall, so kore stops with them for a viscous flow. The matching rows of a conducting inner core change too when its permeability differs from the fluid's, so kore stops unless mu_i2o = 1, and so does the \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) of a fluid that slips along it under a poloidal B0, so kore stops unless bci = 1. The push of a current sheet on a wall the fluid slips along would change on a deformed wall too: kore stops where B0 ends in a sheet at such a wall. It also stops with a forcing, and for a viscous flow in a full sphere with \(m \neq 0\) unless rpower_v ≥ 4 (Dissipative terms).
    • Shifts measured from a sphere. A body that is not deformed keeps the bases and rows of the sphere (centrifugal.deformed), so that its matrices are those of centrifugal = 0. A deformed body with \(m \neq 0\) writes the rows of the toroidal flow in \(C^{(3)}\) when it is viscous, and those of the toroidal field when the field diffuses (Dissipative terms). A shift taken between a deformed run and one at \(f = 0\) therefore also holds the difference of their errors of discretization, which near the resolution limit can rival the part of the shift that the Ekman layers make: for the nutation of Checks without its field, 1.5e-6 in \(\lambda\) at N 160, 6e-4 of \(f\), as large as that part. Taken between deformed runs, at \(\pm f\), the shift does not carry it: it moves by 1e-6 of \(f\) from N 160 to N 200, 5e-6 with the field.

    Checks

    The deformation is checked in four ways, by the scripts in tests/centrifugal/ (see its README):

    • Symbolic derivation. Every closed form on this page, including the magnetic and dissipative terms, is derived again from the map with sympy, and kore's couplings and wall rows are compared with their projections.
    • Quadratures. The angular couplings, the metric blocks and the rows of an insulating wall are compared with direct numerical integration.
    • Exact results. The spin-over mode of a Maclaurin spheroid; the inertial modes of a homogeneous spheroid computed without the map; the first-order shift of damped modes computed from the sphere alone; a sphere relabelled as a deformed body, whose modes must not change at first order; the flattening of Clairaut's equation for known structures; and, for the exact spheroid, viscous and diffusive modes against exact Galerkin solutions.
    • Budgets. residσ, residω, resid𝑠 and, with a field, resid𝐛 and resid𝐄 close for resolved modes, as in the sphere.

    The conducting mantles on the exact spheroid are checked by tests/mantle/derive_spheroid.py (the rows of the thin layer against its sheet built point by point) and tests/mantle/spheroid_mantle_check.py: a mantle of the fluid's conductivity decays as a larger spheroid, thin resolved layers tend to the thin layer, and a small flattening gives the sphere.

    The drag at the outer wall on the deformed body is checked by tests/drag/deformed_check.py (Drag at the outer wall equations).
