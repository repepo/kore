    Magnetic field

    With magnetic = 1 kore adds an imposed background magnetic field \(\mathbf{B}_0\), the induction equation for the perturbation field \(\mathbf{b}\) and the Lorentz force on the flow. The operators, the background fields and the boundary conditions are ported from the conductive_IC branch (commit 39487bbf), formula by formula, into this branch's conventions (2026-10-28: step 1, insulating walls; step 2, a conducting inner core; step 3, any density profile; step 4, a toroidal background field; step 5, the full sphere, inviscid runs and the poloidal fields of conductive_IC of degree 2; 2026-10-30: the induction by a differential rotation, a confined field, fields of any degrees and the Ferraro field, with couplings built from the recurrence algebra).

    What works now: a spherical shell (ricb > 0) with an insulating mantle and an insulating or conducting ('conducting, Chebys') inner core, or a full sphere (ricb = 0, insulating mantle), any structure (variable density, the anelastic terms below), eigenvalue problems, a poloidal \(\mathbf{B}_0\) ('axial', 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'FDM' of degree 1 or 2, 'Luo_S2', 'confined', 'general' of any degrees, 'ferraro') and/or a toroidal one ('Malkus', 'wall' or 'user' of degree 1 or 2, 'general' of any degrees), uniform magnetic diffusivity, with or without a differential rotation (diff_rot = 1, any profile); ViscosD > 0, or ViscosD = 0 (Ek = 0) in a full sphere. background.check() stops on anything else.

    Equations

    The field is written as \(\mathbf{b} = \nabla\times\nabla\times(f\,\mathbf{r}) + \nabla\times(g\,\mathbf{r})\), with no \(\rho_0\) (it is solenoidal by itself). In units of the time scale \(\tau\) (timescale),

    \[ \frac{\partial \mathbf{b}}{\partial t} = \nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}_0) + \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla^2\mathbf{b}, \qquad \mathbf{F}_L = \frac{\mathrm{Hendrik}}{\rho_0}\left[(\nabla\times\mathbf{b})\times\mathbf{B}_0 + (\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_0)\times\mathbf{b}\right], \]

    \(\mathbf{F}_L\) per unit mass in the momentum equation, with

    factor meaning set_scales()
    Hendrik \((\tau B_0/r_0)^2/(\rho_0\mu_0)\) \((\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Le})^2\)
    MagnetD \(\eta_0\tau/r_0^2\) \(\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Em}\)

    The inputs are the Lehnert number Le \(= B_0/(\Omega r_0\sqrt{\rho_0\mu_0})\) and the magnetic Ekman number Em \(= \eta_0/(\Omega r_0^2)\), both defined with the rotation rate as Ek is: \(\mathrm{Em} = \mathrm{Ek}/\mathrm{Pm}\) and the Elsasser number is \(\Lambda = \mathrm{Le}^2/\mathrm{Em}\). Besides 'rotation' and 'viscous', set_scales() knows the time scales 'Alfven' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Le}\), so Hendrik \(= 1\)) and 'magnetic' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Em}\), so MagnetD \(= 1\)). Set Le and Em before calling set_scales(). The magnetic diffusivity \(\eta\) is uniform for now (background.diffusivity(..., 'eta') returns 1; the operators already have the \(\eta'\) terms).

    Background field

    \(\mathbf{B}_0 = c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h(r)\,Y_l^0\,\mathbf{r})\), axisymmetric and poloidal (bin/background_field.py):

    B0 \(h(r)\) degree, symmetry
    'axial' \(r/2\): the uniform field \(c_\mathrm{norm}\hat{\mathbf{z}}\), no current \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'FDM' the poloidal free decay mode of the shell, \(j_l(\beta r)\,y_{l-1}(\beta) - j_{l-1}(\beta)\,y_l(\beta r)\) (Zhang & Fearn 1995), \(\beta\) the root of findbeta near beta; with ricb = 0, \(j_l(\beta r)\) with \(j_{l-1}(\beta) = 0\) (Gubbins & Roberts 1987) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 (antisymmetric) or 2 (symmetric)
    'G21 dipole' \(r/6 - r^3/10\) (Gerick et al. 2021) \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S1' \(5r - 3r^3\) (Luo & Jackson 2022; the same field as 'G21 dipole') \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S2' \(157r^2 - 296r^4 + 143r^6\) (Luo & Jackson 2022) \(l = 2\), symmetric
    'confined' \(r^l(r_2^2 - r^2)^p\) (\(r_1 = 0\)) or \(r^l(r - r_1)^p(r_2 - r)^p\) in \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\), zero outside (\(r_1\), \(r_2\) = B0_conf_r1, B0_conf_r2, \(p\) = B0_conf_p, default 12) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 or 2
    'general' \(\sum_n h_n(r)\,Y_n^0\), B0_profiles = {n: 'numpy expression of r, p'} (\(p\) = B0_param) any degrees of one parity
    'ferraro' the field whose flux function is \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\) (below) odd degrees up to B0_ferraro_nmax, antisymmetric

    All match a potential field at the outer wall. 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are full-sphere fields: with an inner core, B0_ic = 'potential' (default) adds \(\beta_i r^{-(l+1)}\) so that \(rh' - lh = 0\) at the inner boundary (the field of the currents in the fluid, continued as a potential field into the core), and 'full sphere' keeps \(h\) as it is (conductive_IC's B0_beta_ic). cnorm is a number, 'rms_cmb' (rms of the radial field over the outer wall 1), 'mag_energy' (\(\int B_0^2\,dV = 1\) over the shell) or 'Schmitt2012' (\(= 4\)), computed as in conductive_IC. tol_B0 (default 1e-8) is the relative cut of the Chebyshev series of \(r^p h^{(k)}\): it sets the band width of the magnetic blocks.

    Confined field (2026-10-30). 'confined' is zero outside \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\), and \(h\) and its first \(p - 1\) derivatives vanish at the edges, so there are no surface currents (\(B_r \propto h\), \(B_\theta \propto h' + h/r\) and the current are continuous). Its purpose: a poloidal field inside a region where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform is a steady background with the differential rotation (Ferraro, below). With an inner core the region must lie in the fluid (\(r_1 \ge\) ricb). The edges are smooth only to order \(p\), so the Chebyshev series converge algebraically: with \(p = 12\) the budgets close to \(10^{-12}\) at N 96–128 in a shell, but a region that reaches the centre of a full sphere needs N 192 (degree 1) to 384 (degree 2) for \(10^{-9}\). \(h\) is evaluated in factored form (expanded into monomials, a polynomial of degree \(2p + l\) loses all its digits to cancellation near the edges).

    Any degrees (2026-10-30). 'general' takes \(h_n\) degree by degree as expressions; their derivatives come from Chebyshev interpolants (degree max(64, 2N), at \(|r|\) with the parity \((-1)^n\) in a full sphere, where \(h_n\) should go as \(r^n\) at the centre). The degrees must have one parity (one equatorial symmetry). cnorm = 'mag_energy' sums \(\int B_0^2 dV\) over the degrees. The couplings come from the recurrence algebra (mag_algebra.py, below).

    Ferraro field (2026-10-30, needs diff_rot = 1). An axisymmetric poloidal field is \(\mathbf{B}_0 = \nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi\); with \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\) its field lines lie on the surfaces of constant rotation, so \(\mathbf{B}_0\cdot\nabla\Omega = 0\) (Ferraro's law of isorotation) and the rotation and the field are a steady background together. In kore's form \(\psi = r\sum_n h_n(1 - x^2)P_n'(x)\), and the functions \((1 - x^2)P_n'\) are orthogonal with the weight \(1/(1 - x^2)\), so \(h_n(r) = \frac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}\frac{1}{r}\int_{-1}^{1}\psi(r, x)\,P_n'(x)\,dx\), computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature. \(\Delta\Omega\) has only even \(P_n\), so only odd \(n\) appear. \(G\) = B0_ferraro_G, a numpy expression of w = \(\Delta\Omega\) and p. The assumptions, which background.ferraro_report prints at the start of a run: \(\psi\) must vanish on the axis (it must be constant there for a regular field; for a rotation that varies along the axis, \(G\) has to vanish on the values \(\Delta\Omega\) takes there), and at the outer wall if the field is to stay inside (otherwise it meets the insulating mantle with a surface current, which the linear problem allows); the series is cut at B0_ferraro_nmax, which sets the residual \(\mathbf{B}_0\cdot\nabla\Omega\). Example: for a cylindrical rotation \(\Delta\Omega \propto s^2\), \(G(w) = w\) gives \(\psi \propto s^2\), the uniform axial field.

    Toroidal field (step 4). \(\mathbf{B}_0^{tor} = c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k(r)\,Y_n^0\,\mathbf{r})\), purely azimuthal, \(B_\phi = -c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\,\partial_\theta Y_n^0\): \(c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta\) for \(n\) = B0_tor_l = 1, \(3c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta\cos\theta\) for \(n = 2\); \(c_\mathrm{tor}\) = cnorm_tor (a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012'; \(\int B_0^2 dV = n_l L\int k^2 r^2 dr\)):

    B0_tor \(k(r)\)
    'Malkus' \(r\) \(\mathbf{B}_0 = c_\mathrm{tor}\,s\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\) (Malkus 1967), current \(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_0 = 2c_\mathrm{tor}\hat{\mathbf{z}}\)
    'wall' \(r(r - r_i)(1 - r)/(1 - r_i)^2\) zero at both walls
    'user' B0_tor_profile, a numpy expression of \(r\) and \(p\) = B0_tor_param its derivatives from a Chebyshev interpolant (degree max(64, 2N)); written to params.dat after #
    'general' \(\sum_n k_n Y_n^0\), B0_tor_profiles = {n: 'expression'} any degrees of one parity (2026-10-30)

    B0 = None gives a toroidal field alone; with both, they must have the same equatorial symmetry (a poloidal field of degree \(n\) has \((-1)^n\), a toroidal one \((-1)^{n+1}\)): the dipole goes with a degree-2 toroidal field, not with a degree-1 one. A toroidal field that does not vanish at a wall ('Malkus') carries a surface current there; the linear problem is unaffected (the walls' conditions on \(\mathbf{b}\) do not involve \(\mathbf{B}_0\), and \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}_0 = 0\) on a no-slip wall). Inside a conducting inner core \(\mathbf{B}_0\) does not enter: there is no flow there, and \(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}_0 = 0\) at the no-slip inner boundary, so the matching conditions do not involve it either.

    With an equatorially antisymmetric \(\mathbf{B}_0\) the field \(\mathbf{b}\) has the symmetry opposite to \(\mathbf{u}\) (ut.bsymm = symm * symmB0): \(f\) takes the degrees of the toroidal flow and \(g\) those of the poloidal flow. A degree-1 toroidal field is symmetric (\(B_\phi\propto\sin\theta\)), so \(f\) then has the degrees of the poloidal flow; a degree-2 one is antisymmetric, like the dipole.

    Implementation

    • Sections. f (rows \(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\) of the induction equation) and g (\(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\times\)), both in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis with two boundary-condition rows per block, are placed after the entropy section: ut.offset = {'u': 0, 'v': n, 'h': 2n, 'f': (2+thermal) n, 'g': (3+thermal) n} and sizmat = 2n + n*thermal + 2n*magnetic. Non-magnetic matrices are unchanged.
    • Labels. decode_label gives sections f and g the row power \(2 - X\) and no \(\rho_0\). The profile codes are bpo (\(c_\mathrm{norm} h^{(k)}\)) and eta (\(\eta\)). The Lorentz labels carry ohr1 (one power of \(\rho_0\) less), so the momentum rows hold \(r^p\) times the force \(\rho_0 \mathbf{F}_L\). submatrices.py lists each label next to its conductive_IC name.
    • Operators (operators.lorentz, induction, magnetic_diffusion, b): the conductive_IC operators divided by \(L^3\) of the row, as every operator of this branch, with Hendrik and MagnetD for \((\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Le})^2\) and \(\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Em}\). The sign convention is the branch's: A holds the right-hand side, B is \(-\mathbf{b}\) in the induction rows (as \(-\)inertia in the momentum rows).
    • Boundary conditions (assemble.bc_b_spherical): insulating walls, \((l+1)f + rf' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the outer wall, \(lf - r f' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the inner one.
    • Variable density (step 3). With \(u = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P\,\mathbf{r}) + \nabla\times(T\,\mathbf{r})\) the consoidal part of the flow is \(P' + P/r + \lambda_1 P\), \(\lambda_1 = (\ln\rho_0)'\), \(\lambda_2 = (\ln\rho_0)''\), and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u} = -\lambda_1 u_r\). Derived from the definitions with tests/magnetic/derive.py (sympy, exact projection on the harmonics), for \(\mathbf{B}_0\) of degree 1:
      • rows f, columns P: every \(d/dr\) on \(P\) becomes \(d/dr + \lambda_1\) (label f1bpo0lho1_D0);
      • rows g, columns P, diagonal in \(l\) only: \(2im[\,r h\lambda_1 P' + r h\lambda_2 P + (1 - L/2)\,r h'\lambda_1 P - (L/2)\,h\lambda_1 P\,]\) (in the scale of conductive_IC's rows; zero for m = 0), from \(-\mathbf{B}_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}\) and the \(\lambda_1 P\) above;
      • rows u: $\rho_0\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0^{-1}\mathbf{G}) = \hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf{G}
      • (\lambda_1/r)\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf{G}_H$, \(\mathbf{G}\) the Lorentz force per volume: the extra \(\lambda_1 r^4(\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf{G}_H)_l\), i.e. \(-im\lambda_1[(L+2)r^2hg + Lr^3h'g + 2r^3hg']\) for the toroidal field of the same degree and \((y/2)\lambda_1 r^3[2a\,h\,D_{l_c}f + L_c b\,(D_1 h) f]\) for the poloidal field of degree \(l_c = l\pm1\) (\(y = \langle Y_l|\cos\theta\,Y_{l_c}\rangle\), \(a = 2 - L_c - L\), \(b = L_c - 2 - L\), \(D_l f = f'' + 2f'/r - l(l+1)f/r^2\); operators.lorentz_rho);
      • rows v, the T columns, the diffusion, the walls and the inner core: no \(\rho_0\) terms. The new labels pair bpo with lho (two profiles, plus ohr1 in the rows u); they vanish exactly for 'Boussinesq', whose matrices are those of steps 1 and 2 bitwise. tol_B0 does not cut these products (they converge slowly near the surface). submatrices.py now counts odd profiles in both slots of a label for the parity at ricb = 0 (bitwise the same for every existing label).
    • Toroidal field (step 4, operators.induction_tor, lorentz_tor; profile code bto = \(c_\mathrm{tor} k^{(j)}\)). The rows were derived with tests/magnetic/derive.py (any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\): every coefficient is \(im\) (same degree) or the Coriolis factors \(C_- = \sqrt{l^2-m^2}/(2l-1)\), \(C_+ = \sqrt{(l+1)^2-m^2}/(2l+3)\) (columns \(l\mp1\)) times a polynomial in \(l\) (independent of \(m\)). Couplings: rows f from P at the same degree; rows g from P at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\) terms) and from T at \(l\); rows u from F at \(l\) (with \(\lambda_1\)) and G at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\)); rows v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\). Degree 2 (induction_tor2, lorentz_tor2): the quadrupole angular factors (operators.quad_factors, the same-degree terms with \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)) times polynomials in \(l\), couplings up to \(\Delta l = \pm2\): rows f from P at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); u from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); v from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\). operators.lorentz and induction add the poloidal and the toroidal parts (assemble.py loops over \(\Delta l = -2..2\) and skips absent couplings). Column offsets: operators.moff.
    • Poloidal field of degree 2 (step 5, operators.induction_pol2, lorentz_pol2, for 'Luo_S2' and 'FDM' with B0_l = 2). Derived with derive.py (\(b_0\) = ('pol', 2), any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\), \(m\) as for the toroidal field of degree 2: quad_factors times polynomials in \(l\) (the same-degree terms carry \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)), kept in two tables (row, column, \(\Delta l\)) of the label expressions. Couplings: rows f from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\pm1\) and T at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); u from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\); v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\). Two labels are added to those of the dipole (u2bpo2ohr1_D1, u2bpo2ohr1_D0). spin_doctor takes its Lorentz and induction powers from magnetic_powers_grid for these fields (utils4pp.use_grid).
    • Full sphere (step 5, ricb = 0). As for the flow, each block holds the Chebyshev polynomials of one parity on \([-1, 1]\) (ut.remroco): \(f\) has the vector parity vF = symmB0 * vP, \(g\) the opposite; \(h\) has the parity \((-1)^l\) of its degree and \(k\) that of \((-1)^n\) (utils.odd_profiles, both slots of a label). One boundary-condition row per block, the insulating condition at the outer wall (assemble.bc_b_spherical). The fields are regular at the centre: FDM \(j_l(\beta r)\) (analytic derivatives, background_field.jl_k), the polynomial fields, 'wall' \(= r^n(1 - r^2)\), 'Malkus' for \(n = 1\) only (k = r is not regular for \(n = 2\)), 'user' as \(f(|r|)\,\mathrm{sign}(r)^n\). cnorm and cnorm_tor are computed as with an inner core; spin_doctor's inner-wall terms are skipped.
    • Inviscid runs (step 5, ViscosD = 0, full sphere only). The Lorentz labels have \(D^3\) (rows u) and \(D^2\) (rows v), so the rows are in \(C^{(3)}\) (u) and \(C^{(2)}\) (v), with one boundary-condition row (\(u_r = 0\)) in u and none in v; without a field they stay in \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\). decode_label gives the u and v labels the row powers of the viscous rows. conductive_IC keeps \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\) and so converts these terms with the wrong matrices; its torsional test is barely affected (below).
    • Solver and output. solve.py writes real_magnetic.field, imag_magnetic.field and normalises the eigenvectors to \(\mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{Hendrik}\int\tfrac12|\mathbf{b}|^2dV = 1\) (over the shell, as conductive_IC).

    With a differential rotation (2026-10-30)

    With diff_rot = 1 the background flow \(\mathbf{U}_0 = G\,\Delta\Omega\,r\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\) also shears the field: the induction equation gains $$ \nabla\times(\mathbf{U}0\times\mathbf{b}) = G\left[-im\,\Delta\Omega\,\mathbf{b} + \sin\theta\,(r\,\partial_r\Delta\Omega\,b_r + \partial\theta\Delta\Omega\,b_\theta)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\right] $$ (the \(\Omega\)-effect, and the Doppler shift of the field). It is built for any \(\Delta\Omega = \sum_n f_n P_n\) with the recurrence algebra of the momentum terms (diff_rot.induction_block; Derivation tools, part B): F→f and G→g couple at even \(\Delta l\) up to \(n\), F→g (a poloidal field sheared into a toroidal one) at odd \(\Delta l\) up to \(n + 1\). The Lorentz force needs nothing new: it does not involve \(\mathbf{U}_0\).

    • Conducting inner core. It must co-rotate rigidly, \(\Delta\Omega(r_i, \theta) = c\) (\(f_n(r_i) = 0\) for \(n > 0\), else the run stops: a latitude-dependent rotation would shear a solid core). Its field then gains \(-imGc\,\mathbf{b}\); the matching conditions are unchanged, because only \(b_r\), which is continuous, enters the tangential part of \(\mathbf{U}_0\times\mathbf{b}\).
    • Steady background (Ferraro). \(\nabla\times(\mathbf{U}_0\times\mathbf{B}_0) = r\sin\theta\,(\mathbf{B}_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\) must vanish for the background to be steady: always for a toroidal \(\mathbf{B}_0\), for a poloidal one only if \(\Delta\Omega\) is constant along its field lines. The linear problem does not contain this term. background.ferraro_mismatch reports \(\max|\mathbf{B}_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega|\,r/(\max|\mathbf{B}_0|\max|r\nabla\Delta\Omega|)\) at the start of every run; above \(10^{-7}\) background.FERRARO_POLICY decides: 'warn' (default: a warning, the background is assumed to be maintained) or 'refuse'. Consistent backgrounds: a toroidal field, a 'confined' field where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform, or 'ferraro'.
    • Energy budgets. spin_doctor's induction work includes \(\mathbf{U}_0\times\mathbf{b}\) (on the \((r, \theta)\) grid). Because \(\mathbf{U}_0\times\mathbf{b}\) does not vanish at a wall where \(\Delta\Omega \ne 0\), it carries magnetic energy through the wall into the field outside: resid𝐛 (the fluid) uses the induction work with its wall terms, resid𝐄 (the full budget, which counts the energy outside) the volume work alone (utils4pp.INDU_WALLS), plus the power of the rotation's force on the flow.

    Couplings for any background (2026-10-30)

    bin/mag_algebra.py builds the couplings of the Lorentz force and of the induction for \(\mathbf{B}_0 = \sum_n c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h_n Y_n^0\mathbf{r}) + \sum_n c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k_n Y_n^0\mathbf{r})\), any degrees, from the recurrence algebra that the differential rotation uses, instead of the closed forms of operators.py (which exist for degrees 1 and 2 only). The background vectors are polynomials in \(x = \cos\theta\) times \(\hat{\mathbf{r}}\), \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\) or \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\); the products with the perturbation are sparse matrices on the degrees, times radial monomials; the rows take one radial derivative (product rule); each final monomial becomes a label with the profile codes hp + degree (\(c_\mathrm{norm}h_n\)) and kt + degree (\(c_\mathrm{tor}k_n\)). Derivation tools, part A, gives the method. mag_couplings = 'algebra' uses it for every type; 'closed' (default) keeps the closed forms, which 'general' and 'ferraro' cannot use. For the types that have closed forms both give the same eigenvalues to round-off.

    Conducting inner core

    innercore = 'conducting, Chebys' (eigenvalue problems, no-slip inner boundary bci = 1) adds the field inside the inner core to the unknowns, as conductive_IC does:

    • Unknowns. Sections fic, gic after f, g (ut.offset), with the degrees of f, g (up to lmax; conductive_IC's lmax_cic is not ported, as its ICB matching couples the degrees one by one). Each block is the Chebyshev series on \([-r_i, r_i]\) of one parity (that of the field at the centre), N_cic/2 coefficients per degree (ut.Nic, default N_cic = 64); ut.nic per section.
    • Equation. \(\partial\mathbf{b}/\partial t = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\eta_i\nabla^2\mathbf{b}\), \(\eta_i = 1/(\mu_{i2o}\sigma_{i2o})\) (mu_i2o, sigma_i2o: permeability and conductivity relative to the fluid), rows \(r^2\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\) and \(r^2\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\nabla\times\) divided by \(L^3\) as in the fluid, in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis. Its matrices are built apart from the labels (submatrices.py: r0_D0f_ic, r2_D0f_ic, r1_D1f_ic, r2_D2f_ic and the same for g), with ut.Dlam and bc_variables.Tk on the inner core's domain (their optional domain arguments).
    • Matching at the ICB (assemble.bc_b_cic), one row in each section, the inner one of f, g and the only one of fic, gic: \(f_o = f_i\) (\([b_r]\)), \((g + rg')_o = \eta_i (g + rg')_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf{E}\)), \(\mu_{i2o}(f + rf')_o = (f + rf')_i\) and \(\mu_{i2o}\,g_o = g_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf{H}\)). The continuity of \(\eta\nabla^2_l f\) is not imposed: with \(b_r\) continuous it follows from the radial induction equation on both sides, since \(\mathbf{u} = 0\) at the no-slip ICB.
    • Output. real_magnetic_ic.field, imag_magnetic_ic.field. The eigenvectors are normalised with the fluid's energies only (the inner-core field is scaled along), as in conductive_IC.

    Post-processing

    resid𝐄 (conductive_IC's, utils4pp.em_budget) closes \(2\sigma[\mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{Hendrik}(\mathrm{ME} + \mathrm{MEic} + \mathrm{MEext})] = \mathrm{Dkin} + \mathrm{Wthm} + \mathrm{Wlor} + \mathrm{Hendrik}(\mathrm{Indu} - \mathrm{Ohm\_f} - \mathrm{Ohm\_ic})\): unlike resid𝐛 it sees the wall and ICB conditions. The \(1/\mu_{i2o}\) of the inner core's energy and Ohmic loss is not in conductive_IC's em_budget (there resid𝐄 is \(10^{-2}\) at mu_i2o = 2).

    params.dat gains thirteen columns at the end: magnetic, B0 (index in background_field.B0_TYPES, −1 without a field), B0_l, cnorm (as a number), Hendrik, MagnetD, innercore (index in background.INNER_CORES), N_cic, mu_i2o, sigma_i2o, and (step 4) B0_tor (index in background_field.B0_TOR_TYPES, −1 without), B0_tor_l, cnorm_tor. spin_doctor's Lorentz force (utils4pp.lorentz4pp) acts per volume and its power \(\int\mathbf{u}^*\cdot\mathbf{G}\,dV\) has no \(\rho_0\); its induction term (induction4pp) takes the components of \(\mathbf{u}\) with their \(\lambda_1\) part, and holds for the non-solenoidal \(\mathbf{u}\) of a variable density (resid𝐛 closes to \(10^{-10}\) with 'Wu 2005', below). With a toroidal \(\mathbf{B}_0\) (or KORE_MAG_GRID=1 in the environment) both come instead from utils4pp.magnetic_powers_grid, computed on an \((r,\theta)\) grid from the vector fields \(\mathbf{u}\), \(\mathbf{b}\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf{b}\), \(\mathbf{B}_0\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf{B}_0\) (with the wall terms of \(\int\mathbf{b}^*\cdot\nabla\times(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{B}_0)\,dV\)); for a poloidal field it agrees with the spectral formulas to the 10 printed digits.
