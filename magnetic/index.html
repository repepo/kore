    Magnetic field

    With magnetic = 1, kore adds a background magnetic field that you choose, and solves for small perturbations of the flow and of the field together: the field's Lorentz force acts on the flow, and the flow induces a perturbation of the field. The background field is fixed, steady and axisymmetric. It is imposed, not computed: there is no dynamo, and the background flow does not react to it.

    kore does not include the field in the thermal-wind balance. The thermal-wind entropy is computed from the imposed rotation alone, as without a field, and the field's force acts only on the perturbations. This is exact for a field that carries no current, such as B0 = 'axial', and a good approximation when the field's force is weak compared with the Coriolis force of the differential rotation (Validity and caveats).

    This page shows how to set up a run and how to choose the background field. The equations, the formula of every field and the checks of the code are on Magnetic field equations.

    Parameters and a first run

    Two dimensionless numbers set how strong the field is and how fast it diffuses. Both are measured against the rotation, as the Ekman number Ek is:

    parameter meaning
    Le Lehnert number: the Alfvén speed of the field over the rotation speed of the outer wall
    Em magnetic Ekman number: the magnetic diffusivity over the rotation rate times the radius squared

    Two combinations are common: the magnetic Prandtl number Ek/Em and the Elsasser number Le**2/Em. The exact definitions are on Magnetic field equations.

    A first run. A uniform field along the rotation axis, in a shell with insulating walls, with Elsasser and magnetic Prandtl numbers both equal to one. Add these lines at the end of a bin/parameters.py like the checked-in one (which already imports numpy and sets m, symm, N, lmax and the target), so that no later line overrides them:

    par.Ek = 1e-3                # Ekman number
par.Em = 1e-3                # magnetic Ekman number: Pm = Ek/Em = 1
par.Le = np.sqrt(1e-3)       # Lehnert number: Elsasser number Le**2/Em = 1
par.set_scales()             # call it again: it sets Hendrik and MagnetD from Le and Em

par.structure = 'Boussinesq' # any structure works (Background model)
par.magnetic = 1
par.thermal = 0              # no entropy equation
par.B0 = 'axial'             # the poloidal background field (tables below)
par.cnorm = 'mag_energy'     # its amplitude: the integral of B0**2 over the fluid is 1
par.B0_tor = None            # no toroidal background field
par.innercore = 'insulator'
par.mantle = 'insulator'
par.ricb = 0.35; par.bci = 1; par.bco = 1   # no-slip walls

    set_scales() turns Le and Em into the factors of the equations, Hendrik (Lorentz force) and MagnetD (magnetic diffusion), so call it after setting them. Two values of timescale are magnetic: 'Alfven' (the Alfvén time) and 'magnetic' (the magnetic diffusion time). A run stops if Le or Em is zero.

    Choosing the background field

    The background field is one axisymmetric field for the whole domain. It is the sum of two parts: a poloidal part, whose field lines lie in meridional planes like those of a dipole, and a toroidal part, whose field lines are circles around the rotation axis. Choose a shape for each, with B0 and B0_tor, then set their amplitude. B0 defaults to 'axial' and B0_tor to None; for a toroidal field alone, set B0 = None.

    Equatorial symmetry

    The symmetry of the field about the equator decides which parts can be combined, and how the perturbations are paired.

    • A poloidal field of odd degree is dipolar (antisymmetric about the equator), one of even degree quadrupolar (symmetric). For a toroidal field it is the other way round: degree 1 is symmetric, degree 2 antisymmetric.
    • The two parts must have the same symmetry, or the run stops. A dipole therefore combines with a toroidal field of degree 2, not of degree 1.
    • symm still selects the symmetry of the flow. A symmetric background field gives the perturbation field the flow's symmetry; an antisymmetric one gives it the opposite.

    Poloidal field: B0

    Each option is a fixed radial shape (several for 'general'), multiplied by cnorm (Amplitude). The formulas are on Magnetic field equations.

    B0 the field extra parameters (default) geometry degree
    'axial' (default) uniform, along the rotation axis none shell or full sphere 1
    'FDM' the slowest free decay mode of the given degree, for insulating walls B0_l (1); beta, the first guess of the root finder shell or full sphere B0_l = 1 or 2
    'G21 dipole' a full-sphere dipole (Gerick et al. 2021) B0_ic shell or full sphere 1
    'Luo_S1' the shape of 'G21 dipole' (Luo & Jackson 2022), at a larger scale B0_ic shell or full sphere 1
    'Luo_S2' a full-sphere quadrupole (Luo & Jackson 2022) B0_ic shell or full sphere 2
    'confined' zero outside a spherical layer, going smoothly to zero at its edges B0_l (1), B0_conf_r1 (0.0), B0_conf_r2 (0.6), B0_conf_p (12) the layer must lie in the fluid B0_l = 1 or 2
    'general' your own radial profile for each degree B0_profiles = {degree: 'expression of r, p'}, B0_param (p) shell or full sphere any, of one parity
    'ferraro' field lines on the surfaces of constant rotation, so that the rotation does not wind it up (below) B0_ferraro_G ('w'), B0_ferraro_nmax (15, odd), B0_param needs diff_rot = 1 odd, up to B0_ferraro_nmax
    None no poloidal field

    Notes:

    • Full-sphere shapes with an inner core. 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are defined for a full sphere. With an inner core, B0_ic = 'potential' (default) continues the field into the core as a potential field; B0_ic = 'full sphere' keeps the full-sphere formula.
    • 'Luo_S1' and 'G21 dipole' give the same run when cnorm is a name ('mag_energy', 'Schmitt2012', 'rms_cmb'). With a numerical cnorm the 'Luo_S1' field is 30 times stronger.
    • 'confined' puts the field in the layer from B0_conf_r1 to B0_conf_r2 and nowhere else. The defaults reach the centre, which suits a full sphere; with an inner core, set B0_conf_r1 at or above ricb. B0_conf_p (at least 3) sets how smoothly the field vanishes at the edges, so that no current sheet forms there.

    • 'general' takes numpy expressions of r and p (sin, cos, exp, sqrt, where, erf, pi, ...). In a full sphere the profile of degree n should go as r**n at the centre. Example:

      par.B0_profiles = {1: 'r/6 - r**3/10', 3: '0.3*r**3*(1 - r**2)'}

    • 'ferraro' takes B0_ferraro_G, a numpy expression of w (the differential rotation) and p: see Four ways to a steady background.

    Toroidal field: B0_tor

    The toroidal part is purely azimuthal. A differential rotation does not wind it up, so it suits any rotation. Each option is one radial shape times cnorm_tor; the formulas are on Magnetic field equations.

    B0_tor the field extra parameters (default) geometry degree
    None (default) no toroidal field
    'Malkus' grows linearly with the distance to the axis (degree 1), the field of Malkus (1967) B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere; degree 2 shell only B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'wall' zero at both walls B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'user' a radial shape given as an expression of r and p B0_tor_profile, B0_tor_param (p), B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'general' your own radial profile for each degree B0_tor_profiles = {degree: 'expression of r, p'}, B0_tor_param (p) shell or full sphere any, of one parity

    A 'user' profile must be smooth, since its second derivative enters the equations. The expressions of 'user', 'general' (poloidal or toroidal) and 'ferraro' are written to params.dat after #, so they must not contain #.

    Amplitude

    The shapes above have arbitrary scales, so the size of the field is set separately. The field in the equations is Le times a dimensionless field, whose size is fixed by cnorm (poloidal part) and cnorm_tor (toroidal part). The Lorentz force goes as Le squared.

    value meaning for
    a number (default 1) multiplies the formula of the shape as it is all types
    'mag_energy' the integral of the squared field over the fluid is 1 (Luo & Jackson 2022) all types
    'Schmitt2012' the integral of the squared field over the fluid is 4 all types
    'rms_cmb' the rms radial field over the outer wall is 1 cnorm with 'axial', 'FDM', 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2' only

    Use 'mag_energy', as in the example: Le and the Elsasser number then refer to a field of fixed energy, whatever its shape. With a number, the field's size depends on the arbitrary scale of the formula, and Le alone does not say how strong the field is. 'confined', 'general' and 'ferraro' stop with 'rms_cmb'. Formulas: Magnetic field equations.

    With a differential rotation

    With diff_rot = 1 the fluid rotates differentially (Background model), and the shear acts on magnetic field lines in two ways:

    • It shears the perturbation field. kore includes this in the linear problem, for any rotation profile.
    • It winds up the background poloidal field: it stretches poloidal field lines around the axis into an azimuthal field, which grows until magnetic diffusion balances the winding. kore holds the background fixed and does not follow this growth. So the background is steady only if nothing is wound up, or if it already holds the azimuthal field at which the winding is balanced; otherwise kore can only assume that something outside the model keeps it steady.

    This section says when the background is steady, and gives four set-ups that make it so.

    The Ferraro condition

    A differential rotation winds up a poloidal field line unless the rotation rate is the same all along it. So nothing is wound up when the rotation rate is constant along every poloidal field line: this is Ferraro's law of isorotation. A toroidal field always satisfies it; a poloidal field usually does not.

    At the start of a run with a poloidal field and diff_rot = 1, kore prints how far the field and the rotation are from this condition, as a relative number (definition). Above 1e-7 it prints a warning and carries on; to make it stop instead, set ferraro_policy = 'refuse' (default 'warn'). With B0_wound = 1 it prints the residual of the wound field's balance instead.

    Four ways to a steady background

    Three set-ups avoid the winding by making the rotation rate the same along every field line: kore fits the field to the rotation (B0 = 'ferraro') or the rotation to the field (diff_rot_type = 'ferraro'), or you place the field where the rotation is uniform (B0 = 'confined'). The fourth, B0_wound = 1, accepts the winding: it keeps a field that the shear crosses, and adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up from it.

    set-up the winding what kore does other parameters (default) use it when
    B0 = 'ferraro' avoided uses a poloidal field whose field lines lie on the surfaces of constant rotation, and keeps the rotation B0_ferraro_G ('w'), B0_ferraro_nmax (15, odd) the rotation is given, and any field that it does not wind up will do
    diff_rot_type = 'ferraro' avoided keeps the field, and replaces the rotation by one that is constant along each field line diff_rot_F ('psi'), diff_rot_param, diff_rot_nmax (8, even) the field is given (e.g. a model of an observed or a dynamo field), and the rotation only has to fit it
    B0 = 'confined' avoided nothing: the field sits in a layer where the rotation is uniform B0_conf_r1 (0.0), B0_conf_r2 (0.6) the field lives away from the shear (e.g. below the tachocline)
    B0_wound = 1 balanced by diffusion keeps the field and the rotation, and adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up from the poloidal field B0_wound_eta (1.0) both are given and do not match (e.g. a tachocline)

    B0 = 'ferraro': field lines on the surfaces of constant rotation. This set-up avoids the winding. The rotation winds up any poloidal field line that crosses its surfaces of constant rotation, so kore uses a poloidal field whose field lines lie on these surfaces. Nothing is wound up, no azimuthal field is created, and by itself the field puts no torque on the rotation. The rotation is kept as given. The field lines are set by the rotation, and B0_ferraro_G, a function of the rotation rate w, sets the magnetic flux that passes between the axis and each of these surfaces. Where the rotation is uniform the field vanishes, so this field lives only where the rotation varies. B0_ferraro_G should vanish on the values the rotation takes on the axis and, for a field that stays inside the fluid, at the outer wall; kore prints how well these hold. A toroidal part of even degree can be added. cnorm must be a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012'.

    diff_rot_type = 'ferraro': rotation constant along the field lines. This set-up also avoids the winding, the other way round: kore keeps the poloidal field (any B0 type) and replaces the rotation profile by one that is constant along each of its field lines. diff_rot_F gives the rotation rate of each field line (times diff_rot_ampl, as for every profile), as a function of the field's flux function psi, which labels the field lines (and of p = diff_rot_param); the default 'psi' is the simplest. It needs a poloidal field and cannot be combined with B0 = 'ferraro'. kore's rotation is symmetric about the equator, so with a quadrupolar field diff_rot_F must be even (e.g. a function of psi**2); the run stops otherwise.

    B0 = 'confined': a field where the rotation is uniform. The poloidal field fills a spherical layer and nothing else. If the rotation is uniform in that layer, as below the solar tachocline, nothing is wound up.

    B0_wound = 1: the field wound up by the shear. This set-up accepts the winding. kore keeps the poloidal field (any type) and the rotation as given, although the shear crosses the field lines. It adds to the background the azimuthal field that the shear winds up from the poloidal field, at the strength where magnetic diffusion balances the winding. This wound field is computed at the start of the run and added to any B0_tor. Its strength follows from the physics, not from cnorm, which normalises the poloidal part alone: it grows with the shear, is inversely proportional to the diffusivity, and can exceed the poloidal field many times. B0_wound_eta (positive, default 1) is that diffusivity in units of MagnetD; the perturbation still diffuses with MagnetD. The wound field's torque would brake the shear; kore leaves it out, so this background holds only while something maintains the rotation. It needs a poloidal B0 and diff_rot = 1. Read Validity and caveats before using it.

    B0 = 'ferraro' or B0_wound = 1? They describe different states. B0 = 'ferraro', like diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', describes a field and a rotation that have reached isorotation: the rotation rate is the same along every field line, and nothing is wound up. B0_wound = 1 describes a shear that something maintains across the field lines: the shear keeps winding them up, and diffusion keeps the wound field steady.

    A conducting inner core must rotate rigidly with the fluid at its surface: the rotation at ricb must not depend on latitude, or the run stops. The method of each set-up is on Magnetic field equations.

    Inner core and walls

    The perturbation field is matched at each wall to the field outside the fluid. The mantle is always an electrical insulator, so the field outside the outer wall is a potential field. The inner core is an insulator too, or a conductor whose field kore then solves for together with the rest.

    setting values (default) notes
    mantle 'insulator' the only option: the field matches a potential field outside
    innercore 'insulator' (default) or 'conducting, Chebys' conducting: needs ricb > 0 and a no-slip inner wall (bci = 1)
    N_cic 64 Chebyshev polynomials of the inner-core field (even, at least 4)
    mu_i2o 1.0 magnetic permeability, inner core over fluid
    sigma_i2o 1.0 electrical conductivity, inner core over fluid
    B0_ic 'potential' for 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2' with an inner core (above)

    The magnetic diffusivity of the inner core is MagnetD divided by mu_i2o and sigma_i2o. Matching conditions: Magnetic field equations.

    What is supported

    kore checks the combination of options at startup and stops on any it cannot handle.

    Supported: a shell or a full sphere (ricb = 0); any structure; eigenvalue problems; with or without a differential rotation; ViscosD > 0, or ViscosD = 0 in a full sphere. The magnetic diffusivity is uniform.

    Not supported (the run stops at startup):

    • no background field at all (B0 = None and B0_tor = None), or Le or Em zero;
    • forced runs (forcing other than 0);
    • ViscosD = 0 with an inner core (ricb > 0);
    • a conducting inner core in a full sphere, without a no-slip inner wall (bci = 1), with an odd N_cic or one below 4, or with a rotation that varies with latitude at its surface;
    • a poloidal and a toroidal part of opposite equatorial symmetry;
    • the limits of the options above:
      • degrees other than 1 or 2 for 'FDM', 'confined' and B0_tor_l, and 'Malkus' of degree 2 in a full sphere;
      • a 'confined' layer that is empty or does not lie in the fluid, or B0_conf_p below 3;
      • B0_tor = 'user' without B0_tor_profile, or 'general' without profiles or with degrees that are not positive integers of one parity;
      • an expression (B0_tor_profile, B0_profiles, B0_tor_profiles, B0_ferraro_G, diff_rot_F) that is empty or contains #;
      • a cnorm other than a number, 'rms_cmb', 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012', or 'rms_cmb' with 'confined', 'general' or 'ferraro'; a cnorm_tor other than a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012';
      • B0 = 'ferraro' without diff_rot = 1, with diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', or with B0_ferraro_nmax even or below 1;
      • diff_rot_type = 'ferraro' without a poloidal field, with a diff_rot_F of the wrong equatorial symmetry, or with a negative or odd diff_rot_nmax;
      • B0_wound = 1 without a poloidal field or without diff_rot = 1, or with B0_wound_eta not positive;
    • with ferraro_policy = 'refuse', a field and a rotation further than 1e-7 from the Ferraro condition;
    • a value of B0, B0_tor, mantle, innercore, mag_couplings, B0_ic or ferraro_policy other than those listed on this page.

    Output

    A magnetic run writes the perturbation field next to the flow, and spin_doctor checks each mode with the magnetic energy budgets.

    solve.py writes real_magnetic.field and imag_magnetic.field (and real_magnetic_ic.field, imag_magnetic_ic.field for a conducting inner core). Each eigenvector is normalised so that the kinetic energy plus Hendrik times the magnetic energy is 1 in the fluid.

    spin_doctor adds the Lorentz work to residσ and residω, and prints two more residuals: resid𝐛, the energy budget of the induction equation in the fluid, and resid𝐄, the full energy budget including the field outside the fluid (no-slip walls only: bco = 1, and bci = 1 with an inner core). Like residσ (Solver), they should be small for a resolved mode. It also prints Mag/Kin, the ratio of magnetic to kinetic energy (without the factor Hendrik), and writes magnetic.dat and 25 magnetic columns of params.dat (Output files).

    Practical tips

    The field, more than the flow, usually sets the resolution and the memory a run needs.

    • Resolution. Where a poloidal field crosses a no-slip wall, a thin magnetic boundary layer (a Hartmann layer) forms. It, not the bulk flow, usually sets N, and it gets thinner at lower Ekman numbers. A toroidal field alone has no Hartmann layer and converges faster. A full sphere needs about twice the N of a shell. Always compare the residuals at two values of N; examples are in the checks.
    • 'confined' converges more slowly than the other fields, since it is smooth only to a finite order at its edges, and most slowly when its layer reaches the centre of a full sphere.
    • tol_B0 (default 1e-8) is the relative cut of the radial series of the background field. It sets the band width of the magnetic blocks and the fill-in of the LU factors. Lower it for more accurate operators.
    • mag_couplings chooses how the couplings are built. 'closed' (default) uses closed formulas, which exist for fields of degree 1 and 2. 'algebra' builds them for any degree (bin/mag_algebra.py); it is used automatically for 'general' (poloidal or toroidal), 'ferraro' and B0_wound = 1. Both give the same eigenvalues to round-off.
    • Memory. Fields of high degree, and wound fields with a high-degree rotation, couple more degrees and need more LU memory. If MUMPS stops with INFOG(1) = −9, raise st_mat_mumps_icntl_14 in petsc_opts (Solver).

    Validity and caveats

    kore holds the background field and the background flow fixed. This section says what that leaves out and when it matters. Two effects that would change the background are left out:

    • Ohmic decay. A field that carries electric current would decay by diffusion; kore holds it fixed, with or without rotation. Only the wound field is steady by itself, since the winding balances its diffusion.
    • The Lorentz force of the background field on the background flow. The rotation is imposed and is not changed by the field, and the thermal-wind entropy is computed from the rotation alone. With B0_wound = 1 this force is dominated by the wound field, which can be large.

    When the field leaves the thermal wind unchanged

    With thermal_wind 1 or 2, the latitudinal entropy of the background balances the differential rotation alone (Background model). The force of the background field would enter the same balance. The derivations and the estimates are in Magnetic field equations.

    Exact: a field without current. A poloidal field that carries no electric current in the fluid, with no toroidal part, exerts no force, so the thermal wind is exact. If the rotation also satisfies the Ferraro condition, the field does not wind up or decay, and the whole background is steady. B0 = 'axial' is such a field, and so is a 'general' field whose profile of each degree n combines r**n and, in a shell, r**-(n+1).

    Approximate: any other field. All other fields carry current: the other poloidal types, any B0_tor, and the wound field. The background is then a good approximation under three conditions.

    1. The meridional force is weak. The field's force in the meridional planes (magnetic pressure, the hoop stress of the azimuthal field, the tension of curved poloidal lines) adds to the thermal-wind balance. It is negligible when

      \[ \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2\,\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2}{\rho_0} \ll 2\,\lvert\Delta\Omega\rvert , \]

      with \(\rho_0\) the background density and \(\mathbf B_0\) the whole dimensionless field, wound part included. A field that varies on a shorter scale than the rotation makes this stricter. Where it fails, the thermal-wind entropy is wrong by about the ratio of the two sides.

    2. The torque is slow. A poloidal field crossed by the shear brakes it (Ferraro's mechanism), at a rate set by the Elsasser number of the poloidal field built on the background diffusivity:

      \[ \Lambda_p = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}, \]

      with \(\eta_w\) = B0_wound_eta. The thermal wind follows the imposed rotation, so the torque does not change it directly, but the rotation must be maintained against it. The torque is negligible when this rate is small compared with the mode frequencies, their damping rates, and the rate of whatever maintains the rotation. If viscous or turbulent stresses hold the rotation, it is tenable only for a Hartmann number of order one or less:

      \[ Ha = \frac{\mathrm{Le}}{\sqrt{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}} \lesssim 1 . \]

    3. The field is steady. The Ferraro condition holds, or B0_wound = 1 balances the winding. The wound field can be much stronger than the poloidal field that makes it, so it must meet condition 1 too. The Ohmic decay of the field must be slow compared with the modes.

    Where the rotation is uniform there is no thermal wind. In a stably stratified region the field's force is then balanced by a small change of the stratification, which kore leaves out. It is negligible when the Alfvén frequency is far below the buoyancy frequency.

    What condition 1 means for the modes. The error it leaves in the frequencies is about half the left side of condition 1, in units of the rotation rate, for modes that feel the baroclinicity (more for slower modes). The field's direct effect on a mode, which kore includes in full, is larger by a factor that grows as the square of the number of the mode's wavelengths along the field lines. So a field can change the modes while leaving the thermal wind unchanged.

    Wound fields and the tachocline

    A B0_wound background is a snapshot: it holds only for modes faster than the braking and the decay above. This is the tachocline confinement problem (Gough & McIntyre 1998; MacGregor & Charbonneau 1999). With molecular diffusivities, even a weak poloidal field in the radiative interior has a large Hartmann number, and its torque would impose isorotation along its field lines and spread the shear of the convection zone inward. A wound field that strong would brake the shear that makes it, as self-consistent tachocline models include.

    A field below the tachocline is better represented by a 'confined' field whose layer lies there (B0_conf_r2 below the tachocline): the rotation is uniform there, so the Ferraro condition holds. A B0 = 'ferraro' field cannot be put there, since it follows the surfaces of constant rotation and vanishes where the rotation is uniform. A toroidal B0_tor can be added for the tachocline and the convection zone, since a toroidal field is never wound up.

    The checks of this code, with a Hartmann number near 30 and a braking time shorter than a rotation period, test the mathematics (closed form, residuals, budgets), not the physical validity of such a background.

    The Sun, Jupiter and Saturn

    Orders of magnitude with published values, where the field and the shear meet. The numbers and references are on Magnetic field equations.

    region condition 1: meridional force condition 2: torque the background
    Sun, convection zone holds: a mean field of a few thousand G in the lower half gives a ratio of 10⁻³ or less, and the mean field is weaker higher up the weak mean poloidal field (about 10 G) brakes the rotation over a century or more; Hartmann number of order one with turbulent values consistent; the rotation does not follow poloidal field lines, so use a toroidal B0_tor, or B0_wound from a weak poloidal field
    Sun, tachocline holds for 10⁴ G (ratio about 0.005); fails near 10⁵ G (ratio about 0.5) set by the poloidal field and the diffusivity (above) consistent up to about 2×10⁴ G
    Sun, radiative interior no thermal wind: the rotation is uniform the Ferraro condition holds consistent with a 'confined' field whose layer lies below the tachocline
    Jupiter, winds (above 0.96 R) holds: ratio about 10⁻⁹ about a century consistent
    Saturn, winds (above 0.86 R) holds: ratio about 10⁻¹² about 10⁴ years consistent

    The Sun is the case for magnetic effects on inertial modes. At the base of its convection zone a 10⁴ G field has a Lehnert number of a few hundredths: enough to matter for modes with several wavelengths along the field, while condition 1 still holds. In Jupiter and Saturn the Lehnert number is 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷. Their fields change neither the thermal wind nor the large-scale inertial modes measurably: kore's background is consistent there, and the field's effect on the modes is negligible.

    Checklist for solar inertial modes

    Solar inertial modes have periods longer than a rotation (Löptien et al. 2018; Gizon et al. 2021). This makes the criteria above stricter: the effect of the field on a mode grows as its frequency falls, and the background must stay steady over the lifetime of the modes.

    • Time scales. The braking rate and the Ohmic decay rate of the poloidal field must be small compared with the damping rate of the modes, not only with their frequency. The wound field needs a diffusion time across its layer to build up (months to years for a thin tachocline with a turbulent diffusivity): on solar-cycle time scales it is a snapshot.
    • Sensitivity. The effect of the field grows roughly as the square of the Alfvén frequency over the mode frequency, so low-frequency modes feel it most. Scan B0_wound_eta and Le rather than trusting one value.
    • Critical layers and continua. Where the mode frequency matches the local advection by the rotation (the critical latitudes) and at Alfvén resonances, the spectrum has continuous parts and the eigenfunctions thin layers. Check convergence in N and lmax, and tell discrete modes from the continuum.
    • Thermal wind. Keep the thermal-wind entropy on (thermal_wind 1 or 2) and check condition 1 above: the background baroclinicity does not include the forces of the field.
    • Consistent diffusivities. The observed line widths constrain the turbulent viscosity (Ek). Take a turbulent magnetic Prandtl number near one (Em close to Ek), and build the wound field with the same turbulent diffusivity (B0_wound_eta = 1). A wound field built with a molecular diffusivity under modes damped by turbulence is inconsistent.
    • Radiative interior. With molecular values the Hartmann number there is huge, so a field there must not cross a shear: its torque would remove the shear (Ferraro's mechanism). Keep the field below the tachocline, where the rotation is uniform: use 'confined' with its layer there, not B0_wound.
    • Where the modes live. Most observed modes are concentrated in the convection zone (many near the surface or the equator), so a tachocline field reaches them only through their tails. Test the high-latitude and deep modes first.
    • Measurability. A frequency shift matters only above about 1 nHz, a few thousandths of the rotation rate.
    • Symmetry. A dipolar poloidal field with kore's equatorially symmetric rotation gives a wound field of the same family, so the two mode classes stay separate, as without the field.
    • Practical. The wound field has degrees up to the sum of those of the rotation and of the poloidal field, plus one, so a high-degree 'tabulated' rotation means wide couplings and more memory (see Practical tips). In each run read the printed residual of the induction balance.
    • Field geometry. The field inside the Sun is not known, and each set-up imposes its own geometry. Compute the same modes with more than one (for example a toroidal B0_tor, and B0_wound from a weak poloidal field) at a similar field strength: the spread shows how much the results depend on the field assumed.
