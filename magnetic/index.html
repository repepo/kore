    Magnetic field

    With magnetic = 1, kore adds an imposed background magnetic field to the problem. It solves for a small perturbation of the field together with the flow: the induction equation for the perturbation, and the Lorentz force in the momentum equation. The background field is steady and axisymmetric. It is given, not computed: there is no dynamo, and the background flow does not react to the field. The equations and their derivation are on Magnetic field equations.

    This page shows how to set up a run and how to choose the background field.

    Parameters and a first run

    Two numbers set the strength of the field and of its diffusion:

    parameter meaning
    Le Lehnert number: the Alfvén speed of the field over the rotation speed of the outer wall
    Em magnetic Ekman number: the magnetic diffusivity over the rotation rate times the radius squared

    Both use the rotation rate, as Ek does. Two ratios are common: the magnetic Prandtl number Ek/Em and the Elsasser number Le**2/Em. The exact definitions are on Magnetic field equations.

    An example: a uniform field along the rotation axis, in a shell with insulating walls, with Elsasser and magnetic Prandtl numbers both equal to one. Add these lines to a bin/parameters.py like the checked-in one (which already imports numpy and sets m, symm, N, lmax and the target), after its call to set_scales():

    par.Ek = 1e-3                # Ekman number
par.Em = 1e-3                # magnetic Ekman number: Pm = Ek/Em = 1
par.Le = np.sqrt(1e-3)       # Lehnert number: Elsasser number Le**2/Em = 1
par.set_scales()             # call it again: it sets Hendrik and MagnetD from Le and Em

par.structure = 'Boussinesq' # any structure works (Background model)
par.magnetic = 1
par.B0 = 'axial'             # the poloidal background field (tables below)
par.cnorm = 'mag_energy'     # its normalisation: unit magnetic energy in the fluid
par.B0_tor = None            # no toroidal background field
par.innercore = 'insulator'
par.mantle = 'insulator'
par.ricb = 0.35; par.bci = 1; par.bco = 1   # no-slip walls

    set_scales() turns Le and Em into the factors Hendrik (Lorentz force) and MagnetD (magnetic diffusion), so it must run after they are set. Besides 'rotation', the time scales 'Alfven' (the Alfvén time) and 'magnetic' (the magnetic diffusion time) are available. A run stops if Le or Em is zero.

    Choosing the background field

    The background field has a poloidal part (B0, with field lines in meridional planes) and a toroidal part (B0_tor, azimuthal). B0 defaults to 'axial' and B0_tor to None: for a toroidal field alone, set B0 = None.

    Equatorial symmetry

    A poloidal field of odd degree is dipolar (antisymmetric about the equator); one of even degree is quadrupolar (symmetric). For a toroidal field it is the other way round: a toroidal field of degree 1 is symmetric.

    • The two parts can be combined only if they have the same symmetry; the run stops otherwise. So the dipole combines with a toroidal field of degree 2, not of degree 1.
    • symm still selects the symmetry of the flow. With a symmetric background field the perturbation field has the flow's symmetry; with an antisymmetric one, the opposite.

    Poloidal field: B0

    Each poloidal option is one radial shape (or several, for 'general'), times cnorm. The formulas are on Magnetic field equations.

    B0 the field extra parameters (default) geometry degree
    'axial' (default) uniform field along the rotation axis none shell or full sphere 1
    'FDM' the slowest free decay mode of the given degree, for insulating walls B0_l (1); beta, only the first guess of the root finder shell or full sphere B0_l = 1 or 2
    'G21 dipole' a full-sphere dipole (Gerick et al. 2021) B0_ic shell or full sphere 1
    'Luo_S1' the shape of 'G21 dipole' (Luo & Jackson 2022), at a larger scale B0_ic shell or full sphere 1
    'Luo_S2' a full-sphere quadrupole (Luo & Jackson 2022) B0_ic shell or full sphere 2
    'confined' zero outside a spherical layer, going smoothly to zero at its edges B0_l (1), B0_conf_r1 (0.0), B0_conf_r2 (0.6), B0_conf_p (12) the layer must lie in the fluid B0_l = 1 or 2
    'general' any sum of degrees, one expression per degree B0_profiles = {degree: 'expression of r, p'}, B0_param (p) shell or full sphere any, of one parity
    'ferraro' field lines along the surfaces of constant rotation (below) B0_ferraro_G ('w'), B0_ferraro_nmax (15, odd), B0_param needs diff_rot = 1 odd degrees up to B0_ferraro_nmax
    None no poloidal field

    Notes:

    • 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are defined for a full sphere. With an inner core, B0_ic = 'potential' (default) adds a potential field so that the field continues into the core; B0_ic = 'full sphere' keeps the full-sphere formula.
    • 'Luo_S1' and 'G21 dipole' give the same run when cnorm is a name ('mag_energy', 'Schmitt2012', 'rms_cmb'). With a numerical cnorm the 'Luo_S1' field is 30 times stronger.
    • 'confined': the layer runs from B0_conf_r1 to B0_conf_r2, inside the outer wall. The defaults reach the centre, which suits a full sphere; with an inner core, set B0_conf_r1 at or above ricb. B0_conf_p (at least 3) is how smoothly the field vanishes at the edges, so that no current sheet forms there.

    • 'general': the expressions are numpy expressions of r and p (sin, cos, exp, sqrt, where, erf, pi, ...). In a full sphere the profile of degree n should go as r**n at the centre. Example:

      par.B0_profiles = {1: 'r/6 - r**3/10', 3: '0.3*r**3*(1 - r**2)'}

    • 'ferraro': B0_ferraro_G is a numpy expression of w (the differential rotation) and p.

    Toroidal field: B0_tor

    Each toroidal option is a purely azimuthal field: one radial shape times cnorm_tor. Formulas: Magnetic field equations.

    B0_tor the field extra parameters (default) geometry degree
    None (default) no toroidal field
    'Malkus' grows linearly with the distance to the axis (degree 1), the field of Malkus (1967) B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere; degree 2 shell only B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'wall' zero at both walls B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'user' a radial shape given as an expression of r and p B0_tor_profile, B0_tor_param (p), B0_tor_l (1) shell or full sphere B0_tor_l = 1 or 2
    'general' any sum of degrees B0_tor_profiles = {degree: 'expression of r, p'}, B0_tor_param (p) shell or full sphere any, of one parity

    A 'user' profile must be smooth, since its second derivative enters the equations. It is written to params.dat after #; the 'general' and 'ferraro' expressions are not.

    Amplitude

    The field in the equations is Le times a dimensionless field, and the Lorentz force goes as Le squared. The size of the dimensionless field is set by cnorm (poloidal) and cnorm_tor (toroidal):

    value meaning for
    a number (default 1) multiplies the formula of the shape as it is all types
    'mag_energy' unit magnetic energy in the fluid (the integral of the squared field is 1) all types
    'Schmitt2012' the integral of the squared field is 4 all types
    'rms_cmb' the rms radial field over the outer wall is 1 cnorm with 'axial', 'FDM', 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2' only

    With a number, the field's size depends on the arbitrary scale of the formula, so Le alone does not say how strong the field is. Use 'mag_energy' (as in the example) so that Le and the Elsasser number refer to a field of unit energy. 'confined', 'general' and 'ferraro' stop with 'rms_cmb'. Formulas: Magnetic field equations.

    With a differential rotation

    With diff_rot = 1 the fluid rotates differentially, with a profile set by diff_rot_type (Background model). The background flow then shears the perturbation field, and kore adds that induction term for any rotation profile.

    The Ferraro condition. The rotation also shears the background field itself: it stretches poloidal field lines into an azimuthal field, a process called winding. The background is steady only if the rotation rate is constant along each poloidal field line (Ferraro's law of isorotation). A toroidal field always satisfies this; a poloidal one usually does not. kore does not include the winding of the background field in the linear problem: if the condition fails, the background is assumed to be held steady by something outside the model.

    With a poloidal field (and B0_wound = 0), kore prints at the start of the run how far the field and the rotation are from this condition, as a relative number (definition). Above 1e-7 it prints a warning. To make it stop instead, set ferraro_policy = 'refuse' (default 'warn').

    Steady backgrounds. Four ways to have a poloidal field and a differential rotation that are consistent:

    route set use it when
    field from the rotation B0 = 'ferraro', B0_ferraro_G the rotation is given, and any field that follows it will do
    rotation from the field diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', diff_rot_F ('psi'), diff_rot_param, diff_rot_nmax (8, even) the field is given (any poloidal type, e.g. a model of an observed or a dynamo field), and the rotation only has to fit it
    wound toroidal field B0_wound = 1, B0_wound_eta (1.0) both are given and do not match (e.g. a tachocline)
    confined field B0 = 'confined' inside a layer where the rotation is uniform the field lives where there is no shear
    • B0 = 'ferraro' builds the poloidal field from the rotation: its flux function is a function G of the rotation, given by B0_ferraro_G. G should vanish on the values the rotation takes on the axis and, for a field kept inside the fluid, at the outer wall; kore prints how well these hold. It accepts a toroidal part of even degree. cnorm must be a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012'.
    • diff_rot_type = 'ferraro' builds the rotation from the field: the rotation is a function F of the field's flux function psi, given by diff_rot_F (an expression of psi and p = diff_rot_param). It needs a poloidal field and cannot be combined with B0 = 'ferraro'. kore's rotation is symmetric about the equator, so with a quadrupolar field F must be even (e.g. a function of psi**2); the run stops otherwise.
    • B0_wound = 1 keeps both and adds the wound field: the steady toroidal field at which the winding is balanced by magnetic diffusion. It needs a poloidal B0 and diff_rot = 1. B0_wound_eta (positive) is the diffusivity of that balance in units of MagnetD; a turbulent value above 1 gives a weaker wound field, while the perturbation still diffuses with MagnetD. The wound field is added to any B0_tor. Its strength is set by the physics: it grows with the shear and falls with the diffusivity, and can exceed the poloidal field many times. cnorm normalises the poloidal part alone. The run prints the residual of the steady balance and the degrees used, instead of the Ferraro check. Read the caveats before using it.

    A conducting inner core must co-rotate rigidly with the fluid at its surface: the rotation at ricb may not depend on latitude, or the run stops. Details of each route: Magnetic field equations.

    Inner core and walls

    setting values (default) notes
    mantle 'insulator' the only option: the field matches a potential field outside
    innercore 'insulator' (default) or 'conducting, Chebys' conducting: needs ricb > 0 and a no-slip inner wall (bci = 1)
    N_cic 64 Chebyshev polynomials of the inner-core field (even, at least 4)
    mu_i2o 1.0 magnetic permeability, inner core over fluid
    sigma_i2o 1.0 electrical conductivity, inner core over fluid
    B0_ic 'potential' for 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2' with an inner core (above)

    The magnetic diffusivity of the inner core is MagnetD divided by mu_i2o and sigma_i2o. Matching conditions: Magnetic field equations.

    What is supported

    Supported: a shell or a full sphere (ricb = 0); any structure; eigenvalue problems; with or without a differential rotation; ViscosD > 0, or ViscosD = 0 in a full sphere. The magnetic diffusivity is uniform.

    Not supported (the run stops at startup):

    • no background field at all (B0 = None and B0_tor = None), or Le or Em zero;
    • forced runs (forcing > 0);
    • ViscosD = 0 with an inner core (ricb > 0);
    • a mantle other than 'insulator';
    • a conducting inner core in a full sphere, or without a no-slip inner wall (bci = 1), or with a rotation that varies with latitude at its surface;
    • a poloidal and a toroidal part of opposite equatorial symmetry;
    • the per-option limits of the tables above: degrees other than 1 or 2 for 'FDM', 'confined' and B0_tor_l, 'Malkus' of degree 2 in a full sphere, 'ferraro' without diff_rot = 1, B0 = 'ferraro' with diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', B0_wound = 1 without a poloidal field or without diff_rot = 1, B0_conf_p below 3, and cnorm = 'rms_cmb' with 'confined', 'general' or 'ferraro';
    • a value of mag_couplings, B0_ic or ferraro_policy other than those listed on this page.

    Output

    solve.py writes real_magnetic.field, imag_magnetic.field (and real_magnetic_ic.field, imag_magnetic_ic.field for a conducting inner core). Each eigenvector is normalised so that the kinetic energy plus Hendrik times the magnetic energy is 1 in the fluid.

    spin_doctor adds the Lorentz work to residσ and residω and prints two more residuals: resid𝐛, the energy budget of the induction equation in the fluid, and resid𝐄, the full energy budget including the field outside the fluid (no-slip walls only: bco = 1, and bci = 1 with an inner core). Like residσ (Solver), they should be small for a resolved mode. It also prints Mag/Kin, the ratio of magnetic to kinetic energy (without the factor Hendrik), and writes magnetic.dat and 25 magnetic columns of params.dat (Output files).

    Practical tips

    • Resolution. Where a poloidal field crosses a no-slip wall, a thin magnetic boundary layer (a Hartmann layer) forms; it, not the bulk flow, usually sets N. It gets thinner at lower Ekman numbers, so N must grow. A toroidal field alone has no Hartmann layer and converges faster. A full sphere needs about twice the N of a shell. Always compare the residuals at two values of N; examples are in the checks.
    • 'confined' converges more slowly than the other fields, since it is smooth only to a finite order at its edges, and most slowly when its layer reaches the centre of a full sphere.
    • tol_B0 (default 1e-8): the relative cut of the radial series of the background field. It sets the band width of the magnetic blocks and the fill-in of the LU factors. Lower it for more accurate operators.
    • mag_couplings: 'closed' (default) uses the closed formulas, which exist for fields of degree 1 and 2. 'algebra' builds the couplings for any degree (bin/mag_algebra.py); it is used automatically for 'general', 'ferraro' and B0_wound = 1. Both give the same eigenvalues to round-off.
    • Memory. Fields of high degree, and wound fields with a high-degree rotation, couple more degrees and need more LU memory. If MUMPS stops with INFOG(1) = −9, raise st_mat_mumps_icntl_14 in petsc_opts (Solver).

    Validity and caveats

    The background field is held fixed. Two effects that would change the background are left out:

    • Ohmic decay of the poloidal field. A real field would decay by diffusion; here it does not, with or without rotation.
    • The Lorentz force of the background field on the background flow. The rotation is imposed and is not modified by the field. With B0_wound = 1 this force is dominated by the wound field, which can be large. For the tachocline this is a significant approximation: a wound field that strong would brake the shear that makes it, as self-consistent tachocline models include.

    When the ignored force matters. It has two parts, with three dimensionless numbers to watch. Their definitions and derivation are in Magnetic field equations.

    1. Azimuthal torque (Ferraro's mechanism). It brakes the shear along the field lines at a rate set by the Elsasser number of the poloidal field built on the background diffusivity:

      \[ \Lambda_p = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}, \]

      with \(\rho_0\) the background density and \(\eta_w\) = B0_wound_eta. It matters unless this rate is small compared with the mode frequencies, their damping rates, and the rate of whatever maintains the rotation. If viscous or turbulent stresses hold the rotation, it is tenable only for a Hartmann number of order one or less:

      \[ Ha = \frac{\mathrm{Le}}{\sqrt{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}} \lesssim 1 . \]

    2. Meridional force (hoop stress and magnetic pressure of the wound field). It changes the thermal-wind balance and drives magnetic buoyancy. It matters when the Lehnert number of the wound field, squared, is comparable with the differential rotation:

      \[ \mathrm{Le}_\phi^2 \gtrsim \Delta\Omega, \qquad \mathrm{Le}_\phi = \mathrm{Le}\,\frac{\max\lvert B_\phi\rvert}{\max\lvert\mathbf B_p\rvert} . \]

      It can matter even for a weak poloidal field, because the wound field can be much stronger.

    A B0_wound background is therefore a snapshot, valid for modes faster than these effects. This is the tachocline confinement problem (Gough & McIntyre 1998; MacGregor & Charbonneau 1999): with molecular diffusivities a weak poloidal field in the radiative interior has a large Hartmann number, and its torque would impose isorotation along the field lines and spread the shear of the convection zone inward. Below the tachocline a strong field is better represented by a Ferraro-consistent background (B0 = 'ferraro', diff_rot_type = 'ferraro') or a 'confined' one. The checks of this code, with a Hartmann number near 30 and a braking time of about one rotation, test the mathematics (closed form, residuals, budgets), not the physical validity of such a background.

    Checklist for solar inertial modes. Their periods are longer than a rotation and their damping is weak (Löptien et al. 2018; Gizon et al. 2021), which makes every criterion above stricter.

    • Time scales. The braking rate and the Ohmic decay rate of the poloidal field must be small compared with the damping rate of the modes, not only with their frequency. The wound field needs a diffusion time across its layer to build up (years for a thin tachocline with a turbulent diffusivity): on solar-cycle time scales it is a snapshot.
    • Sensitivity. The effect of the field grows roughly as the square of the Alfvén frequency over the mode frequency, so low-frequency modes feel it most. Scan B0_wound_eta and Le rather than trusting one value.
    • Critical layers and continua. Where the mode frequency matches the local advection by the rotation (the critical latitudes) and at Alfvén resonances, the spectrum has continuous parts and the eigenfunctions thin layers. Check convergence in N and lmax, and tell discrete modes from the continuum.
    • Thermal wind. Keep the thermal-wind heat rows on (thermal_wind) and check the meridional criterion above: the background baroclinicity does not include the forces of the wound field.
    • Consistent diffusivities. The observed line widths set the turbulent viscosity (ViscosD). Take a turbulent magnetic Prandtl number near one (Em close to Ek) and set B0_wound_eta to the background's turbulent diffusivity. A wound field built with a molecular diffusivity under modes damped by turbulence is inconsistent.
    • Radiative interior. With molecular values the Hartmann number is huge: use 'confined' or 'ferraro' fields below the tachocline, not B0_wound.
    • Where the modes live. Most observed modes are concentrated in the convection zone (many near the surface or the equator), so a tachocline field reaches them only through their tails. Test the high-latitude and deep modes first.
    • Measurability. A frequency shift matters only above about 1 nHz, a few thousandths of the rotation rate.
    • Symmetry. A dipolar poloidal field with kore's equatorially symmetric rotation gives a wound field of the same family, so the two mode classes stay separate, as without the field.
    • Practical. The wound field has degrees up to the sum of those of the rotation and of the poloidal field, plus one, so a high-degree 'tabulated' rotation means wide couplings and more memory (see Practical tips). In each run read the printed residual of the induction balance and the ratio of the wound to the poloidal field.
    • Bracketing. Compute the same modes with B0_wound and with a 'ferraro' background (the field the torque would relax to). The spread measures what the steady-background assumption costs.
