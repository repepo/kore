    Magnetic field

    With magnetic = 1, kore adds a background magnetic field of your choice and solves for small perturbations of the flow and of the field together: the Lorentz force acts on the flow, and the flow induces a perturbation of the field. The background field is fixed, steady and axisymmetric. It is imposed, not computed: there is no dynamo, and the background flow does not react to it.

    The field does not enter the thermal-wind balance. The thermal-wind entropy is computed from the rotation alone, as without a field, and the Lorentz force acts only on the perturbations. This is exact for a current-free field, such as B0 = 'axial' or 'dipole', and a good approximation when the magnetic force is weak compared with the Coriolis force of the differential rotation (Validity and caveats).

    This page shows how to set up a run and choose the background field. The equations and the formula of every field are on Magnetic field equations.

    Parameters and a first run

    Two dimensionless numbers set how strong the field is and how fast it diffuses, both measured against the rotation:

    parameter meaning
    Le Lehnert number: the Alfvén speed of the field over the rotation speed of the outer wall
    Em magnetic Ekman number: the magnetic diffusivity over the rotation rate times the radius squared

    Two combinations are common: the magnetic Prandtl number Ek/Em and the Elsasser number Le**2/Em. The exact definitions are on Magnetic field equations.

    A first run. A uniform field along the rotation axis, in a shell with insulating walls, with Elsasser and magnetic Prandtl numbers both equal to one. Add these lines at the end of a bin/parameters.py like the default one (which already imports numpy and sets m, symm, N, lmax and the target):

    par.Ek = 1e-3                # Ekman number
par.Em = 1e-3                # magnetic Ekman number: Pm = Ek/Em = 1
par.Le = np.sqrt(1e-3)       # Lehnert number: Elsasser number Le**2/Em = 1
par.set_scales()             # call it again: it sets Hendrik and MagnetD from Le and Em

par.structure = 'Boussinesq' # any structure works (Background model)
par.magnetic = 1
par.thermal = 0              # no entropy equation
par.B0 = 'axial'             # the poloidal background field (tables below)
par.cnorm = 'mag_energy'     # its amplitude: the integral of B0**2 over the fluid is 1
par.B0_tor = None            # no toroidal background field
par.innercore = 'insulator'
par.mantle = 'insulator'
par.ricb = 0.35; par.bci = 1; par.bco = 1   # no-slip walls

    set_scales() converts Le and Em into the factors that multiply the Lorentz force (Hendrik) and the magnetic diffusion (MagnetD), so call it after setting them. Two values of timescale are magnetic: 'Alfven' (the Alfvén time) and 'magnetic' (the magnetic diffusion time).

    Choosing the background field

    The background field is the sum of two parts: a poloidal part, whose field lines lie in meridional planes like those of a dipole, and a toroidal part, whose field lines are circles around the rotation axis. Choose a shape for each with B0 and B0_tor, then set their amplitude. B0 defaults to 'axial' and B0_tor to None; for a toroidal field alone, set B0 = None.

    Equatorial symmetry

    • A poloidal field of odd degree is dipolar (antisymmetric about the equator), one of even degree quadrupolar (symmetric). For a toroidal field it is the other way round: degree 1 is symmetric, degree 2 antisymmetric.
    • The two parts must have the same symmetry. A dipole therefore combines with a toroidal field of degree 2, not 1.
    • symm still selects the symmetry of the flow.

    Poloidal field: B0

    The formulas are on Magnetic field equations.

    B0 the field extra parameters (default) geometry
    'axial' (default) uniform, along the rotation axis – shell or full sphere
    'dipole' the field of a dipole at the centre – shell only
    'FDM' the slowest free-decay mode of degree B0_l (1 or 2) B0_l (1) shell or full sphere
    'G21 dipole' a full-sphere dipole (Gerick et al. 2021) B0_ic shell or full sphere
    'Luo_S1' the same shape (Luo & Jackson 2022) B0_ic shell or full sphere
    'Luo_S2' a full-sphere quadrupole (Luo & Jackson 2022) B0_ic shell or full sphere
    'confined' a field that is zero outside a spherical layer B0_l (1), B0_conf_r1 (0.0), B0_conf_r2 (0.6), B0_conf_p (12) the layer must lie in the fluid
    'general' your own radial profile for each degree B0_profiles, B0_param shell or full sphere
    'ferraro' field lines along the surfaces of constant rotation (below) B0_ferraro_G ('w'), B0_ferraro_nmax (15) needs diff_rot = 1
    None no poloidal field
    • Full-sphere shapes with an inner core. 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are defined for a full sphere. With an inner core, B0_ic = 'potential' (default) continues the field into the core as a current-free field; B0_ic = 'frozen currents' keeps the full-sphere formula.
    • 'confined' fills the layer from B0_conf_r1 to B0_conf_r2. The defaults reach the centre, which suits a full sphere; with an inner core, set B0_conf_r1 at or above ricb. B0_conf_p (at least 3) sets how smoothly the field vanishes at the edges.

    • 'general' takes a numpy expression of r and p for each degree. In a full sphere the profile of degree n should behave as r**n near the centre. Example:

      par.B0_profiles = {1: 'r/6 - r**3/10', 3: '0.3*r**3*(1 - r**2)'}

    • At the walls. 'general' and 'ferraro' are treated as fields produced by currents inside the fluid. If they do not match a current-free field at an insulating wall, a current sheet forms there (Current sheets at slipping walls). Use 'axial' for a uniform field and 'dipole' for a dipole at the centre, rather than writing them as 'general'.

    Toroidal field: B0_tor

    A toroidal field is purely azimuthal, so differential rotation does not wind it up; it suits any rotation. The formulas are on Magnetic field equations.

    B0_tor the field extra parameters (default)
    None (default) no toroidal field
    'Malkus' grows linearly with distance from the axis (Malkus 1967) B0_tor_l (1)
    'wall' zero at both walls B0_tor_l (1)
    'user' a radial shape given as an expression of r and p B0_tor_profile, B0_tor_param, B0_tor_l (1)
    'general' your own radial profile for each degree B0_tor_profiles, B0_tor_param

    B0_tor_l is 1 or 2. A 'user' profile must be smooth, since its second derivative enters the equations. Expressions must not contain #.

    A toroidal field that is not zero at a wall ends there in a current sheet, unless a conductor beyond the wall carries it on (Current sheets at slipping walls). With the rotating solids it must vanish at both walls.

    Amplitude

    The shapes above have arbitrary scales, so the size of the field is set separately: the field is Le times a dimensionless field whose size is set by cnorm (poloidal part) and cnorm_tor (toroidal part).

    value meaning
    a number (default 1) multiplies the formula as it is
    'mag_energy' the integral of the squared field over the fluid is 1 (Luo & Jackson 2022). Recommended
    'Schmitt2012' the integral of the squared field over the fluid is 4
    'rms_cmb' the rms radial field at the outer wall is 1 (cnorm only, not for 'confined', 'general', 'ferraro')
    'rms_Bs' the volume rms of the cylindrical radial field \(B_s\) over the fluid is 1 (cnorm only, not for 'axial', whose \(B_s\) is zero). The field of torsional oscillations

    With 'mag_energy', Le and the Elsasser number refer to a field of fixed energy, whatever its shape. With a number, the strength of the field depends on the arbitrary scale of the formula.

    With a differential rotation

    With diff_rot = 1 (Background model), the shear acts on the field in two ways:

    • It shears the perturbation field. kore includes this for any rotation profile.
    • It winds up the background poloidal field into an azimuthal field, which grows until magnetic diffusion balances it. kore keeps the background fixed and does not follow this growth. The background is therefore steady only if nothing is wound up, or if it already contains the balanced azimuthal field.

    The Ferraro condition

    Differential rotation does not wind up a poloidal field line if the rotation rate is the same all along it (Ferraro's law of isorotation). A toroidal field always satisfies it; a poloidal field usually does not.

    At the start of a run with a poloidal field and diff_rot = 1, kore prints how far the field and the rotation are from this condition (definition) and warns if the mismatch is significant. Set ferraro_policy = 'refuse' to make it stop instead.

    Four ways to a steady background

    set-up what kore does other parameters (default) use it when
    B0 = 'ferraro' keeps the rotation, and uses a poloidal field whose lines lie along the surfaces of constant rotation B0_ferraro_G ('w'), B0_ferraro_nmax (15, odd) the rotation is given, and any field that it does not wind up will do
    diff_rot_type = 'ferraro' keeps the field, and uses a rotation that is constant along each field line diff_rot_F ('psi'), diff_rot_nmax (8) the field is given, and the rotation only has to fit it
    B0 = 'confined' nothing: you put the field in a layer where the rotation is uniform B0_conf_r1, B0_conf_r2 the field lives away from the shear (e.g. below the tachocline)
    B0_wound = 1 keeps both, and adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up, balanced by diffusion B0_wound_eta (1.0) both are given and do not match

    B0 = 'ferraro'. The field lines follow the surfaces of constant rotation, so nothing is wound up. B0_ferraro_G, a function of the rotation rate w, sets how much magnetic flux passes between the axis and each surface. Where the rotation is uniform the field vanishes. kore prints how well the field stays inside the fluid. cnorm must be a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012'.

    diff_rot_type = 'ferraro'. kore keeps the poloidal field and replaces the rotation by one that is constant along each field line. diff_rot_F gives the rotation rate of each field line as a function of the flux function psi. With a quadrupolar field, diff_rot_F must be even in psi.

    B0 = 'confined'. If the rotation is uniform in the layer of the field, as below the solar tachocline, nothing is wound up.

    B0_wound = 1. kore keeps the poloidal field and the rotation as given, and adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up, at the strength where diffusion balances the winding. Its strength follows from the physics, not from cnorm: it grows with the shear and with the inverse of the diffusivity, and can be many times stronger than the poloidal field. B0_wound_eta is the diffusivity used for this balance, in units of MagnetD. This background holds only while something maintains the rotation against the magnetic braking. Read Validity and caveats before using it.

    B0 = 'ferraro' and diff_rot_type = 'ferraro' describe a field and a rotation that have reached isorotation. B0_wound = 1 describes a shear that something maintains across the field lines.

    A conducting inner core must rotate rigidly with the fluid at its surface, so the rotation at ricb must not depend on latitude.

    Inner core and walls

    The perturbation field is matched at each wall to the field beyond it. The mantle is an insulator, possibly with a conducting layer at its base: either a thin one, described by boundary conditions alone, or one of any thickness whose field kore solves for. The inner core is an insulator (again possibly with a thin conducting layer), or a conductor whose field kore solves for.

    setting values (default) notes
    mantle 'insulator' (default), 'TWA' or 'resolved' 'TWA': a thin conducting layer; 'resolved': a conducting layer of any thickness (both below)
    innercore 'insulator' (default), 'conducting, Chebys', 'conducting, Bessel' or 'TWA' need ricb > 0; 'conducting, Bessel' in forced runs only (Forced runs)
    N_cic 64 number of Chebyshev polynomials for the inner-core field with 'conducting, Chebys' (even, at least 4)
    mu_i2o 1.0 magnetic permeability, inner core over fluid
    sigma_i2o 1.0 electrical conductivity, inner core over fluid
    B0_ic 'potential' for 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2' with an inner core (above)

    Matching conditions: Magnetic field equations.

    Thin conducting layer

    A thin conducting layer, such as the base of the Earth's mantle, carries currents that couple the field across it. 'TWA' (the thin-wall approximation of Roberts, Glatzmaier & Clune 2010) models such a layer, at the base of the mantle or on an insulating inner core, through boundary conditions alone, without extra unknowns.

    setting default meaning
    c_cmb, c_icb 1e-5, 0 thickness times permeability of the layer, relative to the wall radius and to the fluid's permeability
    c1_cmb, c1_icb 1e-5, 0 conductance of the layer: thickness times conductivity, relative to the wall radius and to the fluid's conductivity
    muw_cmb, muw_icb 1.0 permeability of the layer relative to vacuum
    mu 1.0 permeability of the fluid relative to vacuum (used only by the thin-wall conditions)
    B0_cmb 'wall' how the background field meets a thin layer at the outer wall: 'wall' adjusts it to the layer, 'plain' keeps it as given
    • With the default permeabilities the conductance c1 sets nearly all of the effect of the layer.
    • Validity. The layer must be thin compared with its magnetic skin depth at the frequency of the mode. spin_doctor prints the estimated relative error, εTWA; keep it below about 1 %.
    • With differential rotation, a conducting layer turns with the fluid next to it, so the rotation must be uniform in latitude at that wall.
    • On the exact spheroid (centrifugal_shape = 'spheroid') the layer at the outer wall works in its thin limit, c_cmb = 0 and mu = 1, with a no-slip or a slipping fluid at the wall. The layer lies between two level surfaces, so in an oblate body it is a little thicker at the equator than at the poles (The exact spheroid).

    The conditions: Magnetic field equations.

    Resolved conducting mantle

    When the conducting layer is too thick, too conducting, or the mode too slow for the thin-wall approximation, use mantle = 'resolved'. kore then puts a conducting layer between the outer wall and r_surface, with an insulator beyond, and solves for the field inside it. The layer is solid and at rest, and its conductivity may vary with radius.

    setting default meaning
    r_surface 1.83 outer radius of the layer, the outer wall being at 1 (1.83 is the whole mantle of the Earth)
    N_mantle 64 number of Chebyshev polynomials for the field in the layer (at least 4)
    sigma_mantle 1.0 conductivity of the layer relative to the fluid's: a number, or an expression of r and p such as '1e-2*exp(-p*(r - 1))'
    sigma_mantle_param 0.0 the p of that expression
    • Resolution. The conductivity profile must be smooth; kore stops if N_mantle does not resolve it. Check the field in the layer with a second run at a larger N_mantle.
    • With differential rotation the rotation must be uniform in latitude at the outer wall.
    • The layer and the fluid have the permeability of vacuum (mu = 1).
    • On the exact spheroid (centrifugal_shape = 'spheroid') the layer ends at the level surface r_surface, a spheroid of the same flattening, and with the flow the outer wall must be no-slip (bco = 1) (The exact spheroid).

    The equations: Magnetic field equations.

    Current sheets at slipping walls

    A background field that does not match the field beyond an insulating wall ends there in a sheet of electric current. This happens for a toroidal field that is not zero at the wall, or a poloidal field that does not match the current-free field outside. A conductor beyond the wall (a conducting inner core, a resolved mantle or a conducting thin layer) carries the field on, with no sheet. The sheet pushes the fluid along the wall. A no-slip wall takes that push; along a stress-free wall, kore includes it in the boundary conditions (Magnetic field equations).

    An inviscid fluid cannot take this push, so a current sheet stops an inviscid run. Use a field that matches at the wall (such as B0_tor = 'wall'), a viscous fluid, or a conductor beyond the wall.

    The field without the flow

    Free decay: hydro = 0. Without the momentum equation the field only diffuses (and is carried by the differential rotation, if any). Each mode decays at a rate set by the conductivity, the walls and the inner core, which for uniform conductivity is known exactly: a useful check of the boundary conditions. B0 still sets the equatorial symmetry of the perturbation. With thermal = 1 the same switch gives the free decay of the entropy. This works for eigenvalue problems only.

    Kinematic induction. bin/just_induction.py and bin/solve_ind.py give the field that a given flow induces, without the force of that field on the flow. Build a magnetic eigenvalue problem (submatrices.py, assemble.py), put the flow in flow.npy (Output files) from a run with the same N, lmax, m, symm and ricb, then run

    ./bin/just_induction.py [lambda]
mpiexec -n 4 ./bin/solve_ind.py

    The optional lambda is the time dependence of the flow, a complex number such as the eigenvalue of a mode; by default it is \(i\) times forcing_frequency. The field is written to magnetic.npy.

    Forced runs

    With a field, every forcing except the wave-like tide (forcing 1) works as without one: the field is solved together with the flow at the forcing frequency, and spin_doctor checks the budgets (Tidal forcing, Libration, Thermal forcing, Spherical Couette flow, Convective forcing).

    • A conducting inner core at one frequency. innercore = 'conducting, Bessel' uses the exact solution inside a uniform conducting core at the forcing frequency, so it adds no unknowns. It gives the same result as 'conducting, Chebys', but only for forced runs at forcing_frequency (not for frequency scans).
    • Walls that turn. In the libration (forcing 7 with m = 0) and the rotating walls (forcing 12), a conducting solid behind a turning wall turns with it.

    A boundary that moves

    Forcings 6, 9, 10 and 14, and forcing 7 with m = 2, represent a boundary that moves or is not spherical. The walls stay spherical and the forcing prescribes the radial flow at the wall. A conducting solid behind the wall moves too, and forced_walls sets how:

    forced_walls the solid behind the wall suits
    'rigid' stays at rest; only its shape moves past the sphere a triaxial mantle that librates (7 with m = 2), the flattened inner core of forcing 6
    'deforming' deforms with the boundary a boundary that moves (9, 10), a tide (14)
    None (default) 'rigid' for forcings 6 and 7, 'deforming' for 9, 10 and 14

    With insulating solids forced_walls changes nothing. A moving wall that the background field reaches needs the same magnetic permeability on both sides (mu, mu_i2o and the thin layer's muw equal to 1); kore stops otherwise. The eccentricity tide (forcing 2) needs insulating walls.

    The equations: Magnetic field equations.

    What is supported

    kore checks the combination of options at startup and stops, with a message, on any it cannot handle.

    Supported: a shell or a full sphere; any structure; eigenvalue problems and forced runs; with or without differential rotation; a viscous fluid, or an inviscid one in a full sphere; with or without the flow (hydro = 0); insulating walls, a thin conducting layer, a resolved conducting mantle or a conducting inner core; rotating solids (Rotating solids). The magnetic diffusivity of the fluid is uniform.

    The main restrictions:

    • a background field is required, and Le and Em must not be zero;
    • a conducting inner core, or a conducting layer at a wall, needs the rotation to be uniform in latitude at that wall;
    • walls with zero radial stress (bci or bco = 2) do not work with a conducting solid behind them;
    • the poloidal and toroidal parts must have the same equatorial symmetry;
    • 'conducting, Bessel' works only in forced runs at a single frequency;
    • a current sheet at the wall of an inviscid fluid stops the run;
    • the wave-like tide (forcing 1) does not work with a field.

    Combining features shows how the field combines with the other features.

    Output

    solve.py writes the perturbation field to magnetic.npy, plus magnetic_ic.npy for a conducting inner core and magnetic_mantle.npy for a resolved mantle (Output files). Each eigenvector is normalised so that its total (kinetic plus magnetic) energy is 1.

    spin_doctor includes the Lorentz force in its budgets and prints two more residuals: resid𝐛, the energy budget of the induction equation, and resid𝐄, the full energy budget including the field outside the fluid. Like residσ (Solver), they should be small for a resolved mode; with a thin conducting layer of non-zero thickness, resid𝐄 is only as small as the approximation error εTWA. It also prints Mag/Kin, the ratio of magnetic to kinetic energy, and writes magnetic.dat (Output files).

    Practical tips

    The field, more than the flow, usually sets the resolution and memory a run needs.

    • Resolution. Where a poloidal field crosses a no-slip wall, a thin magnetic boundary layer (a Hartmann layer) forms. It usually sets N, and it gets thinner at lower Ekman numbers. A toroidal field alone has no Hartmann layer and converges faster. A full sphere needs about twice the N of a shell. Always compare the residuals at two values of N.
    • 'confined' converges more slowly than the other fields.
    • tol_B0 (default 1e-8) is the relative truncation of the background field's radial series. Lower it for more accurate operators, at some cost in memory.
    • mag_couplings. 'closed' (default) uses closed formulas, which exist for fields of degree 1 and 2. 'algebra' works for any degree and is used automatically for 'general', 'ferraro' and B0_wound = 1. Both give the same eigenvalues.
    • Memory. Fields of high degree couple more harmonics and need more memory. If MUMPS stops with INFOG(1) = −9, raise st_mat_mumps_icntl_14 in petsc_opts (Solver).

    Validity and caveats

    kore holds the background field and the background flow fixed. This leaves out two effects:

    • Ohmic decay. A field that carries electric current would slowly decay. Only the wound field is steady by itself.
    • The Lorentz force of the background field on the background flow. The rotation is imposed, and the thermal wind is computed from the rotation alone.

    When the field leaves the thermal wind unchanged

    Exactly, for a current-free field. A poloidal field that carries no current in the fluid exerts no force, so the thermal wind is exact. B0 = 'axial' and 'dipole' are such fields.

    Approximately, for any other field, under three conditions (the derivations are in Magnetic field equations):

    1. The magnetic force in the meridional plane is weak compared with the Coriolis force of the differential rotation:

      \[ \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2\,\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2}{\rho_0} \ll 2\,\lvert\Delta\Omega\rvert , \]

      with \(\rho_0\) the background density, \(\mathbf B_0\) the whole dimensionless field (wound part included) and \(\Delta\Omega\) the relative differential rotation.

    2. The magnetic braking is slow. A poloidal field crossed by the shear brakes it. The braking must be slow compared with the mode frequencies and damping rates, and with whatever maintains the rotation.

    3. The field is steady: the Ferraro condition holds, or B0_wound = 1 balances the winding.

    The direct effect of the field on a mode, which kore includes in full, can be much larger than its effect on the thermal wind. So a field can change the modes noticeably while leaving the thermal wind unchanged.

    Wound fields and the tachocline

    A B0_wound background is a snapshot: it holds only for modes faster than the magnetic braking and the Ohmic decay. With molecular diffusivities, even a weak poloidal field in the solar radiative interior would quickly enforce isorotation along its field lines (the tachocline confinement problem: Gough & McIntyre 1998). A field below the tachocline is better represented by a 'confined' field whose layer lies there, where the rotation is uniform. A toroidal B0_tor can be added in the tachocline and the convection zone, since a toroidal field is never wound up.

    The Sun, Jupiter and Saturn

    Estimates with published values (details):

    • Sun, convection zone and tachocline. The approximation holds for fields up to about \(2\times10^4\) G. At the base of the convection zone a \(10^4\) G field can noticeably affect modes with several wavelengths along the field.
    • Sun, radiative interior. The rotation is uniform; use a 'confined' field there.
    • Jupiter and Saturn. The approximation holds easily, and the fields have a negligible effect on the large-scale inertial modes.

    Checklist for solar inertial modes

    Solar inertial modes have periods longer than a rotation, so the field's effect on them is relatively large and the background must stay steady over their lifetime.

    • Time scales. The magnetic braking and decay must be slow compared with the damping of the modes.
    • Sensitivity. Low-frequency modes feel the field most. Scan Le and B0_wound_eta rather than trusting one value.
    • Critical layers and continua. Check convergence in N and lmax, and distinguish discrete modes from continuous parts of the spectrum.
    • Thermal wind. Keep it on (thermal_wind 1 or 2) and check condition 1 above.
    • Consistent diffusivities. Take a turbulent magnetic Prandtl number near one (Em close to Ek), and build the wound field with the same diffusivity (B0_wound_eta = 1).
    • Radiative interior. Keep the field where the rotation is uniform: use 'confined', not B0_wound.
    • Measurability. A frequency shift matters only above about 1 nHz.
    • Field geometry. The field inside the Sun is not known. Compute the same modes with more than one geometry at a similar strength; the spread shows how much the results depend on the assumed field.
