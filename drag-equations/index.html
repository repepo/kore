    Drag at the outer wall equations

    This page gives the condition of a drag at a stress-free outer wall (Drag at the outer wall), the drag of the topography, how the condition enters the rows of the wall, and the power and damping rate that spin_doctor reports. They are those of the conductive_IC branch (its topo.py, the drag rows of its assemble.py and utils4pp.cmb_slip_K), written for any density and viscosity profile in bin/topo.py.

    The condition

    The wall pulls on the fluid along it with a stress in proportion to the slip, the flow along the wall relative to the mantle. The tangential viscous stress of the fluid at the outer wall (\(u_r = 0\) there) then meets

    \[ \rho_0\,\nu\,\mathrm{ViscosD}\;r\,\partial_r\!\left(\frac{\mathbf u_t}{r}\right) = -\,\mathrm{Gaspard}\;\rho_w\,\mathsf K\,(\mathbf u_t - \mathbf v_m) \qquad (r = 1), \]

    with \(\mathsf K\) the drag coefficient in units of \(\rho_w\Omega r_0\) (\(\rho_w\) the fluid's density at the wall), \(\nu\) the viscosity profile of the structure, and \(\mathbf v_m = \omega_M\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r\) the velocity of the mantle (rotdyn = 1, 0 otherwise). \(\mathsf K = 0\) is the stress-free wall and \(\mathsf K\to\infty\) the no-slip one. \(\rho_0 = \rho_w\) cancels, and the condition is

    \[ r\,\partial_r\!\left(\frac{\mathbf u_t}{r}\right) + q\,\mathsf K\,(\mathbf u_t - \mathbf v_m) = 0, \qquad q = \frac{\mathrm{Gaspard}}{\mathrm{ViscosD}\,\nu(1)} . \]

    kappa_cmb is \(\mathsf K\) times the unit tensor. For a dimensional drag of stress \(\kappa\,\mathbf u_t\), kappa_cmb \(= \kappa/(\rho_w\Omega r_0)\).

    The drag of the topography

    A flow of amplitudes \(U_x\) (zonal) and \(U_y\) (meridional) and frequency \(\omega_0\) along a topography of spectrum \(S(k)\) radiates internal waves of mean power per unit area

    \[ P = \frac{\rho\,I_k}{2\lvert\omega_0\rvert}\int_0^{2\pi}\left(U_x^2\cos^2\alpha + U_y^2\sin^2\alpha\right) g\;d\alpha, \qquad g = \sqrt{\max\left[0,\ (\omega_0^2 - N^2)(f^2 - \omega_0^2) + \omega_0^2\,\tilde f^2\sin^2\alpha\right]}, \]
    \[ f = 2\Omega\cos\theta, \qquad \tilde f = 2\Omega\sin\theta, \qquad I_k = \int_0^\infty S\,k^2\,dk = \frac{h_\text{rms}^2\,k_0\,\Gamma(H - 1/2)}{4\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(H)}, \qquad k_0 = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda_0}, \]

    with \(\alpha\) the direction of a wave vector of the topography from east, \(\theta\) the colatitude and \(N\) the buoyancy frequency at the wall (Rekier et al. 2025, eqs. B1 and B2). Their eq. (B1) as printed (version 4 of the preprint) lacks a factor \(4\pi^2\), lost between their eqs. (A19) and (10); with it, a sinusoidal ridge gives the classical \(\rho U^2h^2k\sqrt{N^2 - \omega^2}/4\) of Bell (1975), and kore keeps it. The power has no term that mixes the zonal and the meridional flow, so the linear drag of the same mean power is a diagonal tensor on \((u_\theta, u_\phi)\), in units of \(\rho\Omega r_0\):

    \[ \mathsf K_{\theta\theta} = \frac{I_k/r_0}{\lvert\omega_0/\Omega\rvert}\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\alpha\,\frac{g}{\Omega^2}\,d\alpha, \qquad \mathsf K_{\phi\phi} = \frac{I_k/r_0}{\lvert\omega_0/\Omega\rvert}\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\alpha\,\frac{g}{\Omega^2}\,d\alpha . \]

    The inputs are topo_hrms \(= h_\text{rms}/r_0\), topo_lambda0 \(= \lambda_0/r_0\) and topo_H \(= H\), with \(N/\Omega\) from the stratification, \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Beyonce}\;g\,dS/dr}/\mathrm{Gaspard}\) at the wall (0 without buoyancy or where it is unstable), or topo_N, and \(\omega_0/\Omega\) = topo_omega, or \(\lvert\)itau\(\rvert\)/Gaspard (eigenvalue problems), or \(\lvert\)forcing_frequency\(\rvert\)/Gaspard (forced runs). The integral over \(\alpha\) is a midpoint rule of 2048 points (topo.kappa). Where \(g\) vanishes for every \(\alpha\) no wave can be radiated and the drag vanishes: near the poles when \(N\) exceeds \(\omega_0\).

    The rows

    At the wall a degree \(l\) of the flow slips along it as

    \[ \mathbf u_t = s_l\,\nabla_1 Y_l + t_l\,\nabla_1 Y_l\times\hat{\mathbf r}, \qquad s_l = P_l' + \frac{P_l}{r} + (\ln\rho_0)'P_l, \qquad t_l = T_l , \]

    with \(\nabla_1\) the gradient on the unit sphere (Numerical method). The stress-free rows of assemble.bc_u_spherical are \(r^2\) (section u, the second row of the outer wall) and \(r\) (section v, its first row) times the parts of \(r\,\partial_r(\mathbf u_t/r)\) on \(\nabla_1 Y_l\) and on \(\nabla_1 Y_l\times\hat{\mathbf r}\). The drag adds the same parts of \(q\,\mathsf K\,\mathbf u_t\), which couple the degree \(l\) to \(s\) and \(t\) of every degree \(l'\):

    \[ (\text{row } \texttt u)_l + r^2 q\sum_{l'}\left(A_{ss}^{ll'}s_{l'} + A_{st}^{ll'}t_{l'}\right) = 0, \qquad (\text{row } \texttt v)_l + r\,q\sum_{l'}\left(A_{ts}^{ll'}s_{l'} + A_{tt}^{ll'}t_{l'}\right) = 0, \]
    \[ A_{xy}^{ll'} = \frac{\oint x_l^*\cdot\mathsf K\,y_{l'}\,dS}{\oint\lvert x_l\rvert^2dS}, \qquad x, y \in \{\,s: \nabla_1 Y,\ \ t: \nabla_1 Y\times\hat{\mathbf r}\,\} , \]

    on the unit sphere (topo.couplings, topo.rows). A constant drag gives $A_{ss} = A_{tt} = $ kappa_cmb and no coupling between degrees. The drag of the topography is even about the equator, so it couples degrees of the same symmetry: \(l\) to \(l\pm2, \dots\) within \(P\) and within \(T\), and for \(m \neq 0\) \(P_l\) to \(T_{l\pm1}, \dots\). The integrals are Gauss–Legendre quadratures in \(\cos\theta\) with \(\max(4(l_\text{max} + 2), 2000)\) nodes, many since \(\mathsf K\) has kinks where \(g\) starts to vanish. With the rotating mantle the toroidal flow of degree 1 slips by \(T_1 - r\,\omega_M\), which gives the rows a column of \(\omega_M\) (Rotating solids equations).

    Power and damping rate

    spin_doctor computes the power the drag dissipates from the flow at the wall (topo.dissipation),

    \[ D_\text{topo} = 2\,\mathrm{Gaspard}\;\rho_w\oint(\mathbf u_t - \mathbf v_m)^*\cdot\mathsf K\,(\mathbf u_t - \mathbf v_m)\,dS , \]

    in the units of its energies (the integral of \(\rho_0\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^2\) of the complex amplitude, whose rate of change is \(2\sigma\) times it), and the damping rate it implies, \(\delta\sigma_\text{topo} = -D_\text{topo}/(2E)\), with \(E\) the energy of the mode: the kinetic energy, plus Hendrik times the magnetic energy inside and outside the fluid and that stored in the conducting walls, plus the energy of the rotating solids. With a stress-free wall and no drag, both use \(\mathsf K = 1\): per unit kappa_cmb.

    The power through the wall. The viscous stress at the wall works on the fluid at the rate \(W_\nu\) (flow.dat column 25), and on the mantle at the rate \(2\,\mathrm{Re}(\omega_M^*\Gamma^\nu_M)\) of its viscous torque. The condition above makes their sum minus the drag's dissipation:

    \[ D_\text{topo} = -W_\nu - 2\,\mathrm{Re}\left(\omega_M^*\,\Gamma^\nu_M\right) . \]

    So the budgets need no new term: residσ and resid𝐄 already count the power of the stress at the walls, and the energy budget of the rotating solids the power of their torques. \(\delta\sigma_\text{topo}\) is the drag's share of the damping rate, one term of that budget.

    Checks (2026-11-06)

    tests/drag/drag_check.py (run_all.sh, about 2 min on 2 ranks):

    • Against conductive_IC (2d84351), eleven runs: a constant drag on the spin-over mode in a shell and in a full sphere; the drag of the topography on the spin-over mode, on an \(m = 2\) mode and in a full sphere; the rotating solids with a stress-free outer wall without a drag, with each drag, with thin walls and a dipole, and with a conducting inner core, a resolved mantle and a field of degree 2; the drag of the topography with a thin wall and a dipole. Eigenvalues to \(6\cdot10^{-14}\), and the rotation of the solids and the torques on the mantle in rotdyn.dat to \(3\cdot10^{-10}\), its precision.
    • Conservation with 'Wu 2005' (r_out 0.9, N 64) and the rotating solids: every mode but \(\lambda = 0\) carries no total angular momentum (\(2\cdot10^{-9}\)) and closes resid𝐋 (\(10^{-9}\)); with kappa_cmb the energy budget of the fluid and the solids closes (\(10^{-10}\)), with the drag of the topography, a dipole, a conducting inner core and a resolved mantle resid𝐄 does (\(10^{-10}\)).
    • Equivalences. A constant tensor given to the drag of the topography (the test hook topo_K) gives the eigenvalues of kappa_cmb (\(2\cdot10^{-14}\)). A rigid differential rotation \(a = 0.3\) with kappa_cmb gives the eigenvalues of a frame turning \(1 + a\) times faster with kappa_cmb\(/(1 + a)\), shifted by \(-i\,m\,\mathrm{Gaspard}\,a\) (\(2\cdot10^{-16}\)): the drag acts on the slip relative to the mantle, in units of the frame's rotation rate.
    • Limits. kappa_cmb = \(10^8\) gives the eigenvalues of a no-slip wall (\(7\cdot10^{-12}\); with the rotating solids \(10^{-10}\), and their rotdyn.dat). A weak drag (kappa_cmb = \(10^{-6}\)) dissipates kappa_cmb times the Dtopo/κ of the stress-free run (\(6\cdot10^{-5}\); with the rotating solids \(2\cdot10^{-5}\)), and with the mantle at rest changes the damping rate of the spin-over mode by kappa_cmb times its δσtopo/κ (\(5\cdot10^{-4}\)). With the rotating solids the change is 0.49 times it for their axial mode: the drag turns the mantle, which changes the other losses at the same order.
    • Frequencies. \(N/\Omega\) and \(\omega_0/\Omega\) in params.dat: topo_N and topo_omega when set, \(\sqrt{10}\) for the stratification 'linear' with Beyonce 10, \(\lvert\)itau\(\rvert\) and the forcing frequency otherwise (\(10^{-9}\)). The same run with a time unit twice as long (Gaspard 2, Beyonce 40, ViscosD, ThermaD and itau doubled) has its eigenvalues doubled (\(10^{-15}\)).
    • The power through the wall closes in every run with a drag but the stiff ones (whose slip is round-off), to \(2\cdot10^{-9}\), and in a forced run (forcing 6) to the printed digits.
    • Stops of background.check_drag.

    Also checked once:

    • Calibration. At Ek \(10^{-4}\) (N 64) the Earth's topography (topo_hrms 4.35e-4, topo_lambda0 0.431, topo_H 0.8, \(N = 0\)) adds to the damping of the spin-over mode (\(\omega_0/\Omega\) = 0.995) \(-2.3100\cdot10^{-5}\), and kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6 adds \(-2.3038\cdot10^{-5}\).
    • Weak and strong drag (the spin-over mode, Ek \(10^{-3}\), \(\sqrt{E} = 0.032\)). The change of the damping rate over kappa_cmb times δσtopo/κ is 0.97 at kappa_cmb = \(10^{-3}\), 0.76 at \(10^{-2}\) and 0.47 at \(3\cdot10^{-2}\); the share of the no-slip wall's added damping is 0.72 at \(3\cdot10^{-2}\), 0.94 at 0.1 and 0.998 at 1. With kappa_cmb = \(10^{-4}\) the added damping is \(-5.042\cdot10^{-4}\) at Ek \(10^{-3}\) and \(-5.063\cdot10^{-4}\) at \(10^{-4}\).
    • Quadrature. For the spin-over test (\(\omega_0/\Omega\) = 0.98, \(N/\Omega\) = 0.5) the couplings change by \(6\cdot10^{-7}\) of the largest from 2000 to 16000 nodes, the error of the kinks. The nodes are those of conductive_IC (computed by scipy.special.roots_legendre instead of numpy's leggauss, ten times faster; the couplings agree to \(5\cdot10^{-12}\)).
    • Other forcings. The random Reynolds stress (forcing 11) with kappa_cmb and with the drag of the topography, and the entropy at the inner wall (forcing 13) with kappa_cmb: Dtopo is minus the power of the stress at the wall, to the printed digits.
    • Operators. The drag and the rotating solids change only rows of assemble.py: submatrices.npz is bitwise the same with and without them, so their parameters are not stored in it.
