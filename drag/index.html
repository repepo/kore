    Drag at the outer wall

    A stress-free outer wall lets the fluid slip freely, and a no-slip wall holds it. A drag lies between the two: the wall pulls back on the slipping fluid in proportion to the slip, which damps the flow without a viscous boundary layer. kore has two drags: a constant drag coefficient, and the drag of the wall's topography, which depends on latitude and on the direction of the flow (Rekier et al. 2025). Either one applies to a stress-free outer wall:

    par.bco = 0
par.kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6
    setting default meaning
    kappa_cmb 0.0 the constant drag coefficient, in units of the fluid's density at the wall times the rotation rate times the outer radius; 0: no drag
    topo_drag None 'rekier': the drag of the topography, instead of kappa_cmb
    topo_hrms 0.0 the rms height of the topography, over the outer radius
    topo_lambda0 0.431 the wavelength below which the spectrum of the topography falls off, over the outer radius
    topo_H 0.8 the Hurst exponent of the topography, which sets how fast its spectrum falls off (above 1/2)
    topo_N None the buoyancy frequency at the wall, over the rotation rate; None: that of the stratification
    topo_omega None the frequency of the flow relative to the mantle, over the rotation rate; None: from the run

    Constant drag: kappa_cmb

    kappa_cmb sets a drag that is the same everywhere on the wall and in every direction. The slip is the flow along the wall relative to the mantle, which is at rest or, with the rotating solids, turns.

    For the Earth's core-mantle boundary, Rekier et al. (2025) give a drag coefficient that corresponds to kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6.

    Drag of the topography: topo_drag = 'rekier'

    A flow along a wall with topography radiates internal waves into a rotating or stratified fluid, and these waves carry momentum away. The resulting drag depends on latitude, on the direction of the flow (zonal or meridional), on its frequency, and on the buoyancy frequency at the wall. topo_drag = 'rekier' uses the drag that Rekier et al. (2025) derive for a topography described by its rms height, the wavelength where its spectrum rolls off, and its Hurst exponent. For the Earth's core-mantle boundary:

    par.bco = 0
par.topo_drag = 'rekier'
par.topo_hrms = 4.35e-4
par.topo_lambda0 = 0.431
par.topo_H = 0.8

    Without buoyancy these values damp the spin-over mode about as much as kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6.

    • Buoyancy frequency. By default, that of the stratification at the wall (0 without buoyancy or where the stratification is unstable). topo_N sets it instead.
    • Frequency of the flow. The drag depends on the frequency of the flow, which an eigenvalue problem does not know in advance. kore uses the frequency of the solver's target (itau), or the forcing frequency in a forced run, unless topo_omega sets it. For a mode, iterate until the two agree (below).
    • Coupled degrees. The drag varies with latitude, so it couples spherical-harmonic degrees. Check the convergence in lmax as usual.

    The frequency of the drag

    tools/topo_iterate.sh repeats the solve until the frequency used by the drag matches that of the mode. Each pass builds the drag at the solver's target, solves, and moves the target to the nearest eigenvalue through the file track_target (Following a mode). A few passes are usually enough. It needs track_target = 1, topo_omega = None, and a first guess in the file. From kore's top folder:

    echo "-0.02 0.99" > track_target    # the guess: Re λ and Im λ
tools/topo_iterate.sh 1e-10         # stops when λ changes by less than 1e-10 (relative); at most 20 passes

    Each pass appends its results to the .dat files, so their last rows belong to the converged mode. The iteration follows one mode at a time.

    Weak and strong drag

    As long as kappa_cmb is small compared with the square root of the Ekman number (ViscosD/Gaspard), the fluid slips nearly freely and the drag adds a damping of its own that does not depend on the Ekman number. This is the regime the drag is meant for. As kappa_cmb approaches the square root of the Ekman number, the drag holds the fluid at the wall, a viscous layer forms, and the wall behaves more and more like a no-slip wall. The Earth value of 4.5e-6 is a weak drag down to Ekman numbers of about 1e-8.

    With the rotating solids

    With rotdyn = 1 the drag acts on the slip relative to the turning mantle, and pulls the mantle in return; its torque appears as the viscous torque on the mantle in rotdyn.dat.

    Output

    For a viscous run with a stress-free outer wall, spin_doctor prints two columns:

    • Dtopo, the power the drag dissipates;
    • δσtopo, the drag's contribution to the damping rate of the mode.

    Without a drag, both are given per unit kappa_cmb (columns Dtopo/κ and δσtopo/κ). Multiplied by a small kappa_cmb, δσtopo/κ estimates the extra damping a weak drag would add, without a new run. To get the full effect of the drag, run with it.

    flow.dat holds both (columns 40–41), and params.dat the drag settings (Output files).

    What is supported

    Supported: any structure; a full sphere; any m; with or without buoyancy; with or without a magnetic field; the rotating solids; a constant drag together with differential rotation or a background flow; eigenvalue problems, and the forced runs whose outer wall stays in place and may slip (forcings 6, 8, 11, 13, and 9 and 12 with the outer wall at rest).

    Not supported (kore stops with a message):

    • a drag without the flow (hydro = 0), without viscosity, or at an outer wall that is not stress-free;
    • a non-rotating frame, the centrifugal deformation, or a forcing that moves the outer wall radially;
    • the wave-like tide (forcing 1);
    • kappa_cmb and topo_drag together;
    • the drag of the topography with differential rotation or a background flow, with a flow frequency of 0, or in a frequency scan of forcing 11 without topo_omega.
