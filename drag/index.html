    Drag at the outer wall

    A stress-free outer wall lets the fluid slip along it freely, and a no-slip wall holds it. A drag lies between the two: the wall pulls back on the slipping fluid in proportion to the slip, and so damps the flow without a viscous layer. kore has two drags, as the conductive_IC branch: a constant drag coefficient, and the drag of the wall's topography, which depends on latitude and on the direction of the flow (Rekier et al. 2025). Either one changes the condition of a stress-free outer wall:

    par.bco = 0
par.kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6
    setting default meaning
    kappa_cmb 0.0 the constant drag coefficient, in units of the fluid's density at the wall times the rotation rate times the outer radius; 0: no drag
    topo_drag None 'rekier': the drag of the topography, instead of kappa_cmb
    topo_hrms 0.0 the rms height of the topography, over the outer radius
    topo_lambda0 0.431 the wavelength below which the spectrum of the topography falls off, over the outer radius
    topo_H 0.8 the Hurst exponent of the topography, which sets how fast its spectrum falls off (above 1/2)
    topo_N None the buoyancy frequency at the wall, over the rotation rate; None: that of the stratification
    topo_omega None the frequency of the flow relative to the mantle, over the rotation rate; None: from the run

    Constant drag: kappa_cmb

    kappa_cmb sets a drag that is the same everywhere on the wall and in every direction: the wall pulls on the fluid with a stress of kappa_cmb times the slip, in the units above. The slip is the flow along the wall relative to the mantle, which is at rest, or turns with the rotating solids (Rotating solids).

    For the Earth's core-mantle boundary, Rekier et al. (2025) give a drag coefficient of 11.3 kg m⁻² s⁻¹ at the diurnal frequency: kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6, with a density of 9.9e3 kg m⁻³ at the wall, the Earth's rotation rate and a radius of 3480 km.

    Drag of the topography: topo_drag = 'rekier'

    A flow along a wall with topography radiates internal waves into a rotating or stratified fluid, and the waves carry momentum away from the flow. The drag this exerts depends on the latitude, through the Coriolis force, on the direction of the flow, zonal or meridional, on its frequency, and on the buoyancy frequency at the wall. topo_drag = 'rekier' uses the drag that Rekier et al. (2025) derive for a topography with a power-law spectrum, described by its rms height, the wavelength where the spectrum rolls off, and its Hurst exponent. The Earth's core-mantle boundary (an rms height of 1.51 km, a roll-off at 1500 km and a Hurst exponent of 0.8, at a radius of 3480 km):

    par.bco = 0
par.topo_drag = 'rekier'
par.topo_hrms = 4.35e-4
par.topo_lambda0 = 0.431
par.topo_H = 0.8

    Without buoyancy these values damp the spin-over mode as kappa_cmb = 4.5e-6 does, to 0.3 %.

    • The buoyancy frequency at the wall is that of the stratification (thermal = 1), or 0 without buoyancy or where the stratification is unstable. topo_N sets it instead.
    • The frequency of the flow. The drag depends on the frequency of the flow relative to the mantle, which an eigenvalue problem does not know in advance: kore takes the frequency of the solver's target (itau), or the forcing frequency in a forced run, unless topo_omega sets it. For a mode found away from the target, set itau to its frequency and solve again, until the two agree. The run stops if this frequency is 0.
    • Coupled degrees. The drag varies with latitude, so the wall couples each degree of the flow to the others of the same symmetry, and for m other than 0 the poloidal flow to the toroidal one. Check the convergence in lmax as usual.

    Weak and strong drag

    kore applies the drag at a stress-free wall, where the fluid slips. As long as kappa_cmb is small against the square root of the Ekman number (ViscosD/Gaspard), the fluid slips nearly freely, and the drag adds a damping of its own that does not depend on the Ekman number: the regime of a drag on the flow outside the thin viscous layer of a real wall, the one to run in. As kappa_cmb approaches the square root of the Ekman number, the drag holds the fluid at the wall, a viscous layer forms, and the wall acts more and more as a no-slip wall. The Earth's 4.5e-6 is a weak drag down to an Ekman number of about 1e-8. The same holds for each direction of the drag of the topography.

    With the rotating solids

    With rotdyn = 1 the outer wall may be stress-free, with or without a drag. The drag then acts on the slip relative to the turning mantle, and pulls the mantle in return: its torque is the viscous torque on the mantle in rotdyn.dat. Without a drag the mantle feels only the magnetic torque and gravity. A fluid slipping along a conducting outer wall (a thin conducting layer or a resolved mantle, Magnetic field) induces its field through the slip relative to the turning mantle too.

    Output

    spin_doctor prints two columns for a viscous run with a stress-free outer wall (not with centrifugal = 1):

    • Dtopo, the power the drag dissipates, in the units of the energies;
    • δσtopo, minus Dtopo over twice the energy of the mode (its kinetic energy, its magnetic energy inside and outside the fluid, and the energy of the rotating solids): the drag's share of the damping rate.

    Without a drag both are per unit kappa_cmb (the columns Dtopo/κ and δσtopo/κ): the drag a constant kappa_cmb of 1 would exert on the free slip. For a weak drag this estimates the damping it would add, without a run: with the mantle at rest, kappa_cmb times δσtopo/κ is the change of the damping rate of the spin-over mode to 5e-4 of it (Ek 1e-3). A drag also changes the flow, and with it the other losses: with the rotating solids it turns the mantle too, and the damping rate of a mode of the solids changed by about half of the estimate. Run with the drag for its effect.

    flow.dat has both (columns 40–41), and params.dat the settings, with the buoyancy frequency and the frequency of the flow that the drag of the topography used (columns 135–141, Output files).

    What is supported

    Supported: any structure (the density and the viscosity at the wall enter); a full sphere; any m; with or without buoyancy; with or without a field, with any magnetic walls; the rotating solids; a constant drag with a differential rotation; eigenvalue problems, and the forced runs whose outer wall does not move (forcings 6, 11 and 13, and 12 with the outer wall at rest).

    Not supported (the run stops at startup):

    • a drag without the flow (hydro = 0), without viscosity (ViscosD = 0), or at an outer wall that is not stress-free (bco other than 0);
    • Gaspard = 0, centrifugal = 1, or forcings 9 and 10, which move the outer wall;
    • kappa_cmb and topo_drag together;
    • the drag of the topography with a differential rotation, with a frequency of the flow of 0, or in a frequency scan of forcing 11 (rsf_frequencies) without topo_omega, since it is built for one frequency;
    • kappa_cmb negative or not finite, topo_drag other than None and 'rekier', topo_hrms or topo_lambda0 not positive, topo_H not above 1/2, topo_N or topo_omega negative.
