    Benchmarks

    kore reproduces published eigenvalues of differentially rotating fluids, from an incompressible fluid to the solar convection zone with a radiative interior. This page gives the set-up of each benchmark, the script that runs it, and the result.

    benchmark physics result
    Baruteau & Rieutord 2013 incompressible, shellular and cylindrical rotation reproduced
    Guenel et al. 2016 incompressible, conical rotation reproduced, including an unstable mode
    Mirouh et al. 2016 Boussinesq, stably stratified, shellular rotation reproduced
    Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025a solar convection zone, observed rotation, thermal wind reproduced, including an unstable mode
    Bekki et al. 2024 onset of the high-latitude instability reproduced
    Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b solar convection zone with a radiative interior reproduced, except two high-latitude modes

    The scripts are in tests/diff_rot/. Each one builds a run directory with a copy of bin/ and its own parameters.py, runs submatrices.py and assemble.py, and solves near the published eigenvalue. Getting started runs one of these benchmarks, fig. 10 of Baruteau & Rieutord, step by step.

    Conventions. kore's eigenvalue is \(\lambda = \sigma - i\omega\) for fields \(\propto e^{\lambda t + im\phi}\) in the frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) (Anelastic equations). The three fluid-dynamics papers work in the inertial frame, with fields \(\propto e^{i\Omega_p t + im\phi}\) (Baruteau & Rieutord, Guenel et al.) or \(e^{(\tau + i\omega)t + im\phi}\) (Mirouh et al.), so their frequency is \(\mathrm{Im}\thinspace\lambda - m\) and their damping rate \(\tau = \mathrm{Re}\thinspace\lambda\), in units of \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\). The solar papers give frequencies in nHz in the frame rotating at 456 nHz, negative for retrograde modes, and growth rates in nHz.

    Incompressible and Boussinesq fluids

    These three papers use a viscous fluid in a spherical shell with stress-free walls. Their Ekman number is \(E = \nu/(\Omega_{\rm ref}R^2)\), which is ViscosD with timescale = 'rotation'. The common settings are

    par.timescale = 'rotation'; par.set_scales()
par.structure = 'Boussinesq'                 # uniform density
par.bci = par.bco = 0                        # stress-free
par.diff_rot = 1; par.thermal_wind = 0
par.ricb = 0.35                              # their η, unless stated
par.ViscosD = 1e-8                           # their E

    tests/diff_rot/lit_bench.py WORKDIR CASE builds and solves one published mode; lit_bench.py WORKDIR list prints the cases (br*, gu*, mi*). The runs take minutes on 8 ranks; the largest needs 26 GB.

    Baruteau & Rieutord 2013

    JFM 719, 47. Shellular rotation \(\Omega = \Omega_{\rm ref}(r/R)^\sigma\), with \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) the surface rate, and cylindrical rotation \(\Omega = \Omega_{\rm ref}(1 + \varepsilon s^2)\), with \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) the rate on the axis:

    par.thermal = 0
par.diff_rot_type = 'shellular';   par.diff_rot_param = -0.5   # σ: ΔΩ = r^σ - 1
par.diff_rot_type = 'cylindrical'; par.diff_rot_ampl = -0.4    # ε: ΔΩ = ε r² sin²θ

    Result. For the 12 modes compared, the frequencies agree to \(2\times10^{-4}\thinspace\Omega_{\rm ref}\) and the damping rates to 1 %, once kore is resolved (some modes need more resolution than the paper used). Two modes differ in the damping rate: an \(m = 3\) mode in a cluster that does not settle with resolution, and the growing \(m = -6\) mode, whose growth rate is 6 % lower, a shift of 0.4 % in its critical Ekman number. Two modes at \(E \le 5\times10^{-9}\) need about 190 GB and were not computed.

    Guenel et al. 2016

    A&A 589, A22. Conical rotation \(\Omega = \Omega_{\rm ref}(1 + \varepsilon\sin^2\theta)\), in the frame rotating at the pole:

    par.thermal = 0
par.diff_rot_type = 'conical'; par.diff_rot_ampl = -0.25      # ε
par.ricb = 0.71

    Result. For the 7 modes compared, the frequencies agree to \(10^{-4}\thinspace\Omega_{\rm ref}\) (\(5\times10^{-4}\) for one) and six damping or growth rates to 1 %, including the unstable mode with a corotation layer. The seventh, at \(\eta = 0.10\), sits in a cluster and is 6 % off in damping rate.

    Mirouh et al. 2016

    JFM 800, 213. A stably stratified Boussinesq fluid with \(N^2\) uniform, \(g = r\), the temperature fixed at both walls, and the rotation \(\Omega = \Omega_{\rm ref}\left[1 + \tfrac{N^2}{2}(1 - r^2)\right]\):

    par.thermal = 1; par.stratification = 'linear'               # dS/dr = r, so the buoyancy frequency is N² r² with g = r
par.bci_thermal = par.bco_thermal = 0
par.Beyonce = 1.5                                            # their N²
par.ThermaD = 1e-7                                           # their E/P
par.diff_rot_type = 'shellular_boussinesq'; par.diff_rot_ampl = 1.5   # N² again

    thermal_wind = 0 is required: their background is balanced by centrifugal buoyancy, which kore does not carry.

    Result. Of the 14 modes, 13 are identified. Their frequencies agree to \(2\times10^{-4}\thinspace\Omega_{\rm ref}\), and the damping or growth rates of 9 to 1 % and of 3 more to 2–4 %. One mode at \(E = 10^{-10}\) needs more resolution than fits in memory. A growing mode in a dense cluster of corotation modes is not identified.

    The Sun

    These papers solve the fully compressible equations about Model S; kore is anelastic. The common set-up: the Model S density, \(\nu = \kappa = 10^{12}\) cm² s⁻¹, an adiabatic convection zone, stress-free walls, the frame rotating at 456 nHz and their rotation profile. "With the thermal wind" means thermal_wind = 2 without \(u_r\thinspace\partial_r S_{tw}\) (thermal_wind_radial = 0), as in the papers.

    Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025a

    A&A, arXiv 2501.16797. The convection zone, 0.71–0.985 R☉, with their tabulated rotation, the file data/diff_rot.npz of their data set (doi:10.17617/3.NP26AI):

    import units
par.timescale = 'rotation'; par.set_scales()
par.structure = 'old model S prototype'                  # the Model S density
par.r_out = 0.985; par.ricb = 0.71/0.985
par.thermal = 1
par.stratification = 'odd top hat'                       # with aux5 = 0: dS/dr = 0
par.aux1, par.aux2, par.aux3, par.aux4, par.aux5 = 0.0, 0.1, 0.68, 0.06, 0.0
par.bci = par.bco = 0; par.bci_thermal = par.bco_thermal = 1
par.diff_rot = 1; par.diff_rot_type = 'tabulated'; par.diff_rot_file = 'diff_rot.npz'
par.diff_rot_nmax = 12
par.thermal_wind = 2; par.thermal_wind_radial = 0
units.set_physical(par, frame_nHz=456.0, nu=1e12, kappa=1e12)   # Gaspard, Beyonce, ViscosD, ThermaD, diff_rot_frame
par.N = 64; par.lmax = par.m + 63

    tests/diff_rot/mk_bekki.py NAME m=… symm=… file=… builds this run for any \(m\) and symmetry, and solve_bekki.py NAME FREQ GROWTH solves it near a frequency and growth rate in nHz. run_bekki.py DIFF_ROT_NPZ runs the five modes below (about a minute each, one core).

    Result. For the \(m = 3\) Rossby modes (\(n = 0, 1\)), the \(m = 3\) columnar mode, the \(m = 10\) high-frequency retrograde mode and the unstable \(m = 1\) high-latitude mode, kore agrees with their compressible values to 0.2 nHz in frequency and 0.4 nHz in growth rate. The high-latitude mode is at −95.76 nHz with growth rate +11.30 nHz (theirs: −95.70, +11.69). This needs the thermal wind: without it the frequencies are off by up to 11 nHz and the high-latitude mode is stable.

    Bekki et al. 2024

    The high-latitude mode of the previous set-up becomes baroclinically unstable when the poles are warm enough. With thermal_wind = 3 the latitudinal entropy gradient is prescribed, \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\), with dSdtheta \(= \Delta_\theta s/c_p\) (negative: warm poles):

    tests/diff_rot/mk_bekki.py RUN tw=3 ds=-1000 file=diff_rot.npz m=1 symm=-1
tests/diff_rot/solve_bekki.py RUN -93 5

    with ds = \(\Delta_\theta s\) in erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹.

    Result. The \(m = 1\) high-latitude mode becomes unstable at \(\lvert\Delta_\theta s\rvert\) = 425 erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹ with the rotation of Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025, and at 546 with the Larson & Schou 2018 table, for \(c_p = 3.46\times10^{8}\). The published value is about 500. The radial shear of the rotation in the lower convection zone sets the onset: a rotation profile uniform in radius needs about four times more.

    Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b

    A&A 708, A253. The convection zone extended down to 0.5 R☉ with their stably stratified, rigidly rotating radiative interior:

    par.ricb = 0.5/0.985
par.solar_density_window = 'sun'                                # one Model S fit over the whole Sun
par.stratification = 'solar_rz'; par.diffusivity = 'solar_rz'   # their eqs. (8)-(10)
par.visc0, par.therm0 = 0.01, 0.04                              # ν and κ below 0.70 R☉, relative to the convection zone
par.diff_rot_type = 'solar_P4'                                  # their eqs. (5)-(6)
par.bci_thermal = 1; par.bco_thermal = 0                        # zero entropy flux at the bottom, s = 0 at the top
par.N = 128

    the rest as above. tests/diff_rot/mk_rz.py NAME rin=0.5 m=… symm=… builds it for any inner radius rin (in R☉, 0 for the full sphere). tests/diff_rot/solar_checks.py DIFF_ROT_NPZ runs the modes of both Mukhopadhyay papers, with and without the thermal wind (about 40 minutes on one core).

    Result. Without the thermal wind, every convection-zone and mixed mode whose counterpart can be identified (8 modes) agrees with theirs to 0.13 nHz, and the two published growth rates to 0.006 nHz. With the thermal wind the convection-zone modes are 2–10 nHz off, so their radiative-interior runs appear to have no latitudinal entropy gradient, unlike those of 2025a. Their two high-latitude convection-zone modes (\(m = 1\) and 2) are not matched: without the thermal wind kore's nearest convection-zone modes are strongly damped, and the paper gives no growth rates to compare.
