    Differential rotation in kore: physics, equations and implementation

    This page is a self-contained reference for the differential rotation of kore's anelastic_log branch. It covers:

    • the physics: what a differentially rotating background does to a linear perturbation (§1);
    • the anelastic (LBR) equations with differential rotation, and the derivation of every term that the differential rotation adds to the momentum and heat equations (§2);
    • the spectral form of these terms: how they couple spherical-harmonic degrees, and how the angular coupling coefficients are obtained (§3);
    • the implementation, term by term: radial profiles, operator labels, the operators, the assembly of the matrices and the diagnostics (§4 to §8);
    • the internal consistency checks built into the code, and the known limitations (§9, §10);
    • benchmarks against published eigenvalues: Baruteau & Rieutord 2013, Guenel et al. 2016, Mirouh et al. 2016, Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025a and 2025b (§11);
    • the cost in time and memory against the highest degree of \(\Delta\Omega\) (§12).

    It describes the code after the differential rotation was unified in bin/diff_rot.py on the angular algebra of bin/angular.py (2026-10-23, on top of commit b4598010). The code references give a file and a function, and a line number in that version (line numbers drift; the function names do not).

    0. Notation and code names

    Everything is dimensionless. The length unit is the outer radius \(r_0\) (r = 1 at the outer boundary), and the time unit is \(\tau\), chosen by par.timescale ('rotation' gives \(\tau = 1/\Omega_{\rm ref}\)).

    Two code conventions recur everywhere:

    • Sections. The unknowns and the matrix rows are stacked in blocks: section u holds the rows of the radial component of the double curl of the momentum equation (one row block per poloidal degree), section v the rows of the radial component of its curl (one per toroidal degree), and section h the heat equation (one per degree of \(s\), which follows the poloidal degrees).
    • Row weights. Each row is multiplied by a power of \(r\) and of \(\rho_0\), chosen so that every radial coefficient is a polynomial, and divided by \(L'^3\), where \(l'\) is the row degree.

    1. Physics

    1.1 The background

    kore solves in a frame rotating at the constant rate \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\). The background rotates at

    \[ \Omega(r,\theta) = \Omega_{\rm ref}\left[1 + \Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\right], \qquad \Delta\Omega = \sum_{n = 0, 2, 4, \dots} f_n(r) P_n(\cos\theta) . \]

    Only even \(n\) appear, so the rotation is symmetric about the equator, and the equatorial symmetry of the modes (par.symm) still separates the problem into two classes. In the rotating frame the differential rotation is a steady azimuthal flow

    \[ \mathbf U_0 = G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace r\sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi = G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r . \qquad (1.1) \]

    The factor \(G\) = Gaspard converts the rotation rate \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\Delta\Omega\) to the time unit \(\tau\). It is 1 when \(\tau = 1/\Omega_{\rm ref}\). \(G\) does not choose the frame: \(\Delta\Omega\) is defined relative to the frame, and for the solar profiles the frame rate is set by diff_rot_frame (or fixed at 435 nHz for 'solar').

    The spherical background (\(\rho_0\), \(T_0\), \(g\), \(S_0\), \(\nu\), \(\kappa\)) is the same as without differential rotation. The differential rotation adds only two things: the flow \(\mathbf U_0\), and a latitudinal entropy \(S_{tw}(r,\theta)\) (§1.3).

    1.2 What the differential rotation does to a perturbation

    A perturbation \(\mathbf u\) is carried by the background flow and sheared by it. Linearising the advection \((\mathbf U_0 + \mathbf u)\cdot\nabla(\mathbf U_0 + \mathbf u)\) gives (§2.3) three effects:

    1. Advection at the local rotation rate. A pattern \(e^{i m\phi}\) is carried round at the local rate \(G\Delta\Omega\), which shifts its frequency by \(-m G\Delta\Omega\) locally. With uniform \(\Delta\Omega\) this is just a Doppler shift.
    2. Coriolis force of the local rotation. The Coriolis parameter becomes \(2G(1 + \Delta\Omega)\) instead of \(2G\). Together with (1), a uniform \(\Delta\Omega\) is exactly a change of frame (§2.6).
    3. Transport across the shear. A perturbation that moves across the gradient of \(\Omega\) carries its angular momentum to a place where the background rotates at a different rate. This is the term \(r\sin\theta\thinspace (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\). It is the only term through which the perturbation can exchange kinetic energy with the background flow (§2.3), so it is what drives shear instabilities.

    In the heat equation the entropy perturbation is advected by \(\mathbf U_0\) (the same Doppler shift for \(s\)), and the perturbation velocity advects the latitudinal background entropy \(S_{tw}\).

    1.3 Thermal-wind balance

    A rotation rate that varies along the rotation axis cannot be balanced by pressure alone. The centrifugal force of the differential rotation then has a curl, and in a stratified fluid only a buoyancy force that varies with latitude, that is a latitudinal entropy gradient, can balance it. This is thermal-wind balance. A differentially rotating star in equilibrium therefore has a background entropy \(S_0(r) + S_{tw}(r,\theta)\). kore computes \(S_{tw}\) from \(\Delta\Omega\) (thermal_wind 1 or 2), prescribes it (thermal_wind = 3), or omits it (thermal_wind = 0).

    \(S_{tw}\) is small in amplitude, since it scales as \(G^2/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\). But the buoyancy it produces through a perturbation scales as \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\). The product does not depend on Beyonce: the feedback of \(S_{tw}\) on the motion is of order \(\Delta\Omega\) relative to the Coriolis force, the same order as the momentum terms of §1.2. In a convection zone, where \(S_0' \approx 0\), \(S_{tw}\) is the only background entropy gradient, and the meridional flow of a perturbation across it (the baroclinic coupling) can destabilise modes.

    1.4 What is not modelled

    • The background is imposed and fixed: nothing maintains \(\Omega\) or \(S_{tw}\) against viscosity or diffusion, and the perturbation equations have no source terms for them.
    • \(\rho_0\), \(T_0\) and \(g\) stay spherical: no centrifugal distortion, no latitudinal variation of \(\rho_0 T_0\) or \(g\) induced by the thermal wind, and no thermal-wind density in the mass flux. These effects are of relative size \(\Delta\Omega/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\).
    • The thermal wind is the one consistent with LBR buoyancy. The full anelastic balance would add a term \(p_b S_0'/(\rho_0 g)\) to \(S_{tw}\) (\(p_b\) the background pressure perturbation), the analogue of the \(p_1 S_0'\) term that LBR drops for the perturbations. It matters only where \(S_0' \neq 0\).
    • There is no magnetic field and no viscous heating.

    2. Equations

    2.1 The linear anelastic (LBR) equations

    In the frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\), with the background of §1.1, a perturbation \((\mathbf u, s)\) obeys, per unit mass (Lantz–Braginsky–Roberts form, with \(\varpi'\) the reduced pressure \(p'/\rho_0\)):

    \[ \frac{\partial\mathbf u}{\partial t} + 2G\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u + \mathbf W(\mathbf u) = -\nabla\varpi' + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r} + \mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace \mathbf F_\nu(\mathbf u), \qquad (2.1) \]
    \[ \nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf u) = 0, \qquad (2.2) \]
    \[ p_0\left[\frac{\partial s}{\partial t} + \mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla s + u_r S_0' + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_{tw}\right] = \mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace \nabla\cdot(\kappa\thinspace p_0\nabla s). \qquad (2.3) \]

    Here \(\mathbf F_\nu = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\thinspace \mathsf S)\) is the viscous force of the anelastic stress tensor \(\mathsf S\), and

    \[ \mathbf W(\mathbf u) = (\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U_0 \qquad (2.4) \]

    is the linearised advection by the background flow. Without differential rotation, \(\mathbf W = 0\), \(\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla s = 0\) and \(S_{tw} = 0\), and (2.1)–(2.3) are kore's standard equations. The differential rotation adds exactly three terms: \(\mathbf W\) in (2.1), and \(p_0\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla s\) and \(p_0\mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_{tw}\) in (2.3).

    Remarks:

    • In LBR form the buoyancy depends on \(s\) only. The reduced pressure is a gradient, so it disappears when the curl of (2.1) is taken (§3.2): kore never solves for it.
    • The viscous force, the buoyancy and the diffusion are not changed by the differential rotation.
    • (2.2) is satisfied exactly by the poloidal–toroidal form of \(\mathbf u\) (§3.1).

    kore solves the eigenvalue problem for fields \(\propto e^{\lambda t + i m\phi}\), so \(\partial_t \to \lambda\) and \(\partial_\phi \to i m\).

    2.2 The background flow as a local rotation

    Write the background rotation in time units as \(\Omega_d(r,\theta) = G\Delta\Omega\), so that \(\mathbf U_0 = \Omega_d\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r\). Two identities are used below:

    \[ \mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla = \Omega_d\thinspace \partial_\phi, \qquad (\mathbf v\cdot\nabla)(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r) = \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf v , \]

    for any vector \(\mathbf v\). In components, with \(\hat{\mathbf z} = \cos\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r} - \sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\),

    \[ \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u = \left(-\sin\theta\thinspace u_\phi,\ -\cos\theta\thinspace u_\phi,\ \sin\theta\thinspace u_r + \cos\theta\thinspace u_\theta\right) \qquad (2.5) \]

    in the \((\hat{\mathbf r}, \hat{\boldsymbol\theta}, \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi)\) basis.

    2.3 Derivation of the momentum term

    Advection of the perturbation by \(\mathbf U_0\). \((\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u = \Omega_d\thinspace \partial_\phi\mathbf u\), where \(\partial_\phi\) also acts on the unit vectors: \(\partial_\phi\hat{\mathbf r} = \sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\), \(\partial_\phi\hat{\boldsymbol\theta} = \cos\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\), \(\partial_\phi\hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi = -\sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r} - \cos\theta\thinspace \hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\). With (2.5) this is

    \[ (\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u = \Omega_d\left[i m\thinspace \mathbf u + \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\right], \]

    where \(i m\thinspace \mathbf u\) means \(i m\) times each component.

    Advection of \(\mathbf U_0\) by the perturbation.

    \[ (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U_0 = (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\Omega_d)\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r + \Omega_d\thinspace (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r) = r\sin\theta\thinspace (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\Omega_d)\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi + \Omega_d\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u . \]

    Sum. With \(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega = u_r\partial_r\Delta\Omega + (u_\theta/r)\partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\),

    \[ \mathbf W = G\left[\Delta\Omega\left(i m\thinspace \mathbf u + 2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\right) + \sin\theta\left(r\thinspace \partial_r\Delta\Omega\thinspace u_r + \partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\thinspace u_\theta\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\right]. \qquad (2.6) \]

    This is the force that operators.differential_rotation discretises. The three parts are those of §1.2: the Doppler term \(i m\Delta\Omega\thinspace \mathbf u\); the Coriolis force of the local rotation \(2\Delta\Omega\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\), which adds to the frame's \(2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\); and the shear term.

    For \(\Delta\Omega = f(r)P_n(x)\), using \(\partial_\theta P_n(\cos\theta) = -\sin\theta\thinspace P_n'(x)\), the components are

    \[ \begin{aligned} W_r/G &= f P_n\left[i m\thinspace u_r - 2\sin\theta\thinspace u_\phi\right],\cr W_\theta/G &= f P_n\left[i m\thinspace u_\theta - 2x\thinspace u_\phi\right],\cr W_\phi/G &= f P_n\left[i m\thinspace u_\phi + 2\sin\theta\thinspace u_r + 2x\thinspace u_\theta\right] + \sin\theta\left[(r f')\thinspace P_n\thinspace u_r - f\sin\theta\thinspace P_n'\thinspace u_\theta\right]. \end{aligned} \qquad (2.7) \]

    The profile enters only as \(f\) and \(r f'\); the second derivative \(r^2 f''\) appears later, when the double curl differentiates \(W\) in \(r\) (§3.4).

    Energy. For a mode, \(2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace \mathbf u^\ast\cdot\mathbf W\thinspace dV\) is the rate at which \(\mathbf W\) removes kinetic energy. The Doppler part gives \(i m\int\rho_0\Delta\Omega\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^2 dV\), which is imaginary. The Coriolis part gives \(\mathbf u^\ast\cdot(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u) = \hat{\mathbf z}\cdot(\mathbf u\times\mathbf u^\ast)\), which is also imaginary. Only the shear term, \(2G\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace r\sin\theta\thinspace u_\phi^\ast\thinspace (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\thinspace dV\), exchanges energy with the background. These parts shift the frequency (residω) or change the growth rate (residσ) in spin_doctor (§8).

    2.4 Thermal-wind entropy

    The balance. The steady background satisfies, in the rotating frame and per unit mass,

    \[ (\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U_0 + 2G\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf U_0 = -\nabla\varpi_b + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace S_{tw}\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r}, \]

    where \(\varpi_b\) collects the pressure and gravity potential terms. With \(\mathbf U_0 = \Omega_d\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf r\), the left side is \(-(\Omega_d^2 + 2G\Omega_d)\thinspace \varpi\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \varpi = -G^2\left[(1 + \Delta\Omega)^2 - 1\right]\varpi\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \varpi\), the centrifugal and Coriolis forces of the differential rotation (\(\hat{\mathbf e}_ \varpi\) the cylindrical radial unit vector). The \(\phi\)-component of the curl removes \(\varpi_b\). Using \((\nabla\times F\hat{\mathbf e}_ \varpi)_ \phi = \partial_z F\) and \((\nabla\times F\hat{\mathbf r})_ \phi = -r^{-1}\partial_\theta F\),

    \[ \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta} = \frac{G^2\thinspace r\sin\theta}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\thinspace \frac{\partial (1 + \Delta\Omega)^2}{\partial z}, \qquad \frac{\partial}{\partial z} = \cos\theta\thinspace \frac{\partial}{\partial r} - \frac{\sin\theta}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}. \qquad (2.8) \]

    In the variable \(x\). With \(\partial_\theta = -\sin\theta\thinspace \partial_x\) and \(\partial_z = x\partial_r + r^{-1}(1 - x^2)\partial_x\), (2.8) becomes

    \[ \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial x} = -\frac{r^2 G^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[x\thinspace \frac{\partial F}{\partial r} + \frac{1 - x^2}{r}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}\right], \qquad F = \begin{cases} 2\Delta\Omega & \text{(linear)} \cr (1 + \Delta\Omega)^2 - 1 & \text{(exact)} \end{cases} \qquad (2.9) \]

    thermal_wind = 1 uses the linear form, thermal_wind = 2 the exact one. Only the exact form is independent of the frame: \(G^2(1 + \Delta\Omega)^2\) is the square of the physical rotation rate, the same whatever \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) the profile is written against, whereas \(G^2\cdot 2\Delta\Omega\) is \(2\Omega_{\rm ref}(\Omega - \Omega_{\rm ref})\tau^2\) and changes with the frame. With thermal_wind = 1 the modes therefore move with the choice of frame, at second order in \(\Delta\Omega\).

    The right side of (2.9) is a polynomial in \(x\) of degree \(n_{\max} + 1\) (linear) or \(2n_{\max} + 1\) (exact), so \(S_{tw} = \sum_n \sigma_n(r) P_n(x)\) has even degrees up to \(n_{\max} + 2\) or \(2n_{\max} + 2\). The \(l = 0\) part \(\sigma_0\) is a spherical entropy, absorbed in \(S_0\), and set to zero.

    Closed forms for \(\Delta\Omega = f_0 + f_2 P_2\). Writing the bracket of (2.9) on \(P_1\) and \(P_3\) (with \(x P_2 = \tfrac{3}{5}P_3 + \tfrac{2}{5}P_1\), \(3x(1 - x^2) = \tfrac{6}{5}(P_1 - P_3)\), \(P_2' = 3P_1\), \(P_4' = 7P_3 + 3P_1\)) and matching coefficients gives, with \(e = 0\) for thermal_wind = 1 and \(e = 1\) for thermal_wind = 2,

    \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_2 &= -\frac{2r^2G^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[(1 + e f_0)\left(\frac{f_0'}{3} + \frac{f_2'}{21} + \frac{4}{7}\frac{f_2}{r}\right) + \frac{e\thinspace f_2 (f_0' + f_2')}{21}\right],\cr \sigma_4 &= -\frac{6r^2G^2}{35\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[(1 + e f_0)\left(f_2' - \frac{2f_2}{r}\right) + e\left(f_2 f_0' + \frac{4}{11}f_2 f_2' + \frac{14}{11}\frac{f_2^2}{r}\right)\right],\cr \sigma_6 &= -e\thinspace \frac{4r^2G^2}{77\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\thinspace f_2\left(f_2' - \frac{2f_2}{r}\right). \end{aligned} \qquad (2.10) \]

    kore computes the \(\sigma_n\) from (2.9) by the Legendre-series algebra of §5.4, for any degrees of \(\Delta\Omega\); (2.10) is kept as a validation (tests/diff_rot/gen_check.py, §9). Two analytic checks:

    • Cylindrical rotation, \(\Delta\Omega = \varepsilon\varpi^2\), i.e. \(f_0 = \tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon r^2\), \(f_2 = -\tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon r^2\): \(\sigma_2 = \sigma_4 = \sigma_6 = 0\). A rotation constant on cylinders is barotropic and needs no thermal wind.
    • Conical rotation, \(\Delta\Omega = \varepsilon\sin^2\theta\), i.e. \(f_0 = \tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon\), \(f_2 = -\tfrac{2}{3}\varepsilon\) (linear form): \(\sigma_2 = 16\varepsilon r/(21\thinspace\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\), \(\sigma_4 = -8\varepsilon r/(35\thinspace\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\), and \(S_{tw}(\text{pole}) - S_{tw}(\text{equator}) = \varepsilon r/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\): an equator rotating faster (\(\varepsilon > 0\)) needs warmer poles.

    Prescribed latitudinal entropy (thermal_wind = 3). \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\), independent of \(\Omega\) and of \(r\). Since \(\sin 2\theta = -\tfrac{2}{3}\thinspace dP_2/d\theta\),

    \[ \sigma_2 = -\tfrac{2}{3}\thinspace \texttt{dSdtheta}, \qquad \sigma_n = 0 \quad (n \ge 4). \]

    dSdtheta is in the units of \(s\) (\(\delta s/c_p\)); negative values give warm poles. \(\Omega\) still enters through \(\mathbf W\) and the advection of \(s\). For a uniformly rotating background with a prescribed \(S_{tw}\), use diff_rot_type = 'rigid' with diff_rot_ampl = 0.

    2.5 The heat-equation terms

    \(S_{tw}\) does not depend on \(\phi\), so \(\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla S_{tw} = 0\), and \(\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla s = G\Delta\Omega\thinspace \partial_\phi s\). The left side of (2.3) gains

    \[ p_0\left[\thinspace \underbrace{i m G\Delta\Omega\thinspace s}_ {\text{(a) advection}} + \underbrace{u_r\frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial r}}_ {\text{(b) radial}} + \underbrace{\frac{u_\theta}{r}\frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta}}_ {\text{(c) latitudinal}}\thinspace \right]. \qquad (2.11) \]

    (a) is always included. (b) and (c) are included for thermal_wind 1, 2, 3; thermal_wind_radial = 0 drops (b). (a) is anti-Hermitian under the entropy pairing \(\int s^\ast(\cdot)\thinspace dV\), so it only shifts the frequency; (b) and (c) exchange entropy variance with the flow.

    2.6 An exact result: the rigid offset

    For a uniform \(\Delta\Omega = a\) (diff_rot_type = 'rigid'), \(\partial\Delta\Omega = 0\), \(S_{tw} = 0\), and (2.6) and (2.11) reduce to \(G a(i m\mathbf u + 2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u)\) and \(i m G a\thinspace p_0 s\). Equations (2.1) and (2.3) become those of uniform rotation at rate \(G(1 + a)\), with \(\lambda\) replaced by \(\lambda + i m G a\). So

    \[ \lambda(a, G) = \lambda_0\big(G(1 + a)\big) - i m G a , \qquad (2.12) \]

    where \(\lambda_0(G)\) is the eigenvalue without differential rotation. This identity tests the Doppler and Coriolis parts of the operators and their signs (§9).

    3. Spectral form

    3.1 Fields

    At fixed \(m\) and equatorial symmetry, each field is a sum over degrees,

    \[ \mathbf u = \sum_l\left[\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P_l Y_l\thinspace \mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T_l Y_l\thinspace \mathbf r)\right], \qquad s = \sum_l s_l Y_l , \]

    where \(P\) and \(s\) use the poloidal degrees and \(T\) the toroidal ones (utils.ell(m, lmax, symm) returns both lists). This form satisfies (2.2) exactly. For one degree, with \(Z_l = i m Y_l/\sin\theta\) and

    \[ a_l = \frac{(r P_l)'}{r} + h' P_l = \frac{P_l}{r} + P_l' + h' P_l , \qquad (3.1) \]

    the components are

    \[ u_r = \frac{L P_l}{r} Y_l, \qquad u_\theta = a_l\thinspace \partial_\theta Y_l + T_l Z_l, \qquad u_\phi = a_l Z_l - T_l\thinspace \partial_\theta Y_l . \qquad (3.2) \]

    These expressions underlie diff_rot._primitives (through the basis fields of §3.3), utils4pp.diff_rot_setup and ru_insane.py.

    3.2 Projection of a vector field, and kore's rows

    Any vector field \(\mathbf F\) is projected on vector spherical harmonics as

    \[ \mathbf F = \sum_l\left[F^q_l\thinspace Y_l\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r} + F^s_l\thinspace r\nabla Y_l + F^t_l\thinspace r\nabla Y_l\times\hat{\mathbf r}\right], \qquad r\nabla Y = \partial_\theta Y\thinspace \hat{\boldsymbol\theta} + Z\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi, \quad r\nabla Y\times\hat{\mathbf r} = Z\thinspace \hat{\boldsymbol\theta} - \partial_\theta Y\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi , \qquad (3.3) \]

    with, since \(\int(\lvert\partial_\theta Y_l\rvert^2 + \lvert Z_l\rvert^2)\thinspace d\Omega = L n_l\),

    \[ F^q_l = \frac{1}{n_l}\int F_r Y_l^\ast\thinspace d\Omega, \qquad F^s_l = \frac{1}{L n_l}\int\left(F_\theta\thinspace \partial_\theta Y_l^\ast + F_\phi Z_l^\ast\right)d\Omega, \qquad F^t_l = \frac{1}{L n_l}\int\left(F_\theta Z_l^\ast - F_\phi\thinspace \partial_\theta Y_l^\ast\right)d\Omega . \qquad (3.4) \]

    By (3.2) the velocity has \(u^q = L P/r\), \(u^s = a\), \(u^t = T\).

    Taking curls removes the pressure:

    \[ \hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\mathbf F = \sum_l\frac{L}{r}F^t_l Y_l, \qquad \hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf F = \sum_l\frac{L}{r^2}\left[F^q_l - \partial_r(r F^s_l)\right]Y_l . \qquad (3.5) \]

    (The toroidal part \(F^t r\nabla Y\times\hat{\mathbf r} = \nabla\times(F^t Y\mathbf r)\) has no radial curl-curl, and the spheroidal part \(F^q Y\hat{\mathbf r} + F^s r\nabla Y\) has the curl \(r^{-1}\left[F^q - \partial_r(r F^s)\right] r\nabla Y\times\hat{\mathbf r}\).)

    kore's rows of degree \(l'\) are (3.5) multiplied by \(r^{\rm rpower}\rho_0^{\rm rhopower}\) and divided by \(L'^3\). With the defaults rpower_u = 5, rpower_v = 3, rhopower_u = rhopower_v = 1:

    \[ \mathrm{row}_ u(\mathbf F) = \frac{r^3\rho_0}{L'^2}\left[F^q_{l'} - \partial_r(r F^s_{l'})\right], \qquad \mathrm{row}_ v(\mathbf F) = \frac{r^2\rho_0}{L'^2}F^t_{l'} . \qquad (3.6) \]

    For example, \(\mathbf F = \mathbf u\) gives \(\mathrm{row}_ u = \left[L r^2\rho_0 P - r^3\rho_0(2P' + rP'' + h'P + r h'' P + r h' P')\right]/L^2\) and \(\mathrm{row}_ v = r^2\rho_0 T/L^2\), which is operators.inertia (line 26). The diff-rot operator is (3.6) applied to \(\mathbf W\).

    3.3 Angular primitives

    Insert one source degree \(l\) of (3.2) into (2.7), with \(\Delta\Omega = f(r)P_n(x)\). Each component of \(\mathbf W\) is a sum of four radial "shapes" times angular functions:

    shape radial factor from
    q \(f\thinspace L P_l/r\) \(u_r\) with \(f\)
    a \(f\thinspace a_l\) the consoidal part of \(u_\theta\), \(u_\phi\)
    T \(f\thinspace T_l\) the toroidal part
    dq \((r f')\thinspace L P_l/r\) \(u_r\) in the shear term

    From (2.7) and (3.2), the angular functions, per unit \(G\) and per unit shape, are (\(Y = Y_l\), \(Z = Z_l\)):

    shape \(W_r\) \(W_\theta\) \(W_\phi\)
    q \(i m P_n Y\) 0 \(2\sin\theta\thinspace P_n Y\)
    a \(-2 i m P_n Y\) \(P_n(i m\thinspace \partial_\theta Y - 2xZ)\) \(P_n(i m Z + 2x\thinspace \partial_\theta Y) - \sin^2\theta\thinspace P_n'\thinspace \partial_\theta Y\)
    T \(2\sin\theta\thinspace P_n\thinspace \partial_\theta Y\) \(P_n(i m Z + 2x\thinspace \partial_\theta Y)\) \(P_n(-i m\thinspace \partial_\theta Y + 2xZ) - \sin^2\theta\thinspace P_n' Z\)

    Projecting each entry on the target degree \(l'\) with (3.4) defines twelve matrices \(C^k_{\rm sh}[l', l]\), with \(k \in \lbrace q, s, t\rbrace\) the projection and sh the shape. Then, for the target degree \(l'\),

    \[ W^k_{l'} = G\sum_l\left[C^k_q\thinspace f\frac{L_l P_l}{r} + C^k_a\thinspace f a_l + C^k_T\thinspace f T_l + C^k_{dq}\thinspace (r f')\frac{L_l P_l}{r}\right]. \qquad (3.7) \]

    The angular algebra. The \(C^k_{\rm sh}\) are not computed by integration but built exactly, as sparse products of a few matrices on the degree index (bin/angular.py). Multiplying a field by a function of \(x\) maps degree \(l\) to a few neighbouring degrees, by two exact recurrences of the Schmidt harmonics (\(p_l\), \(q_l\) as in (3.8) below):

    \[ x\thinspace Y_l = p_l Y_{l+1} + q_l Y_{l-1} \quad\to X, \qquad (1 - x^2)\frac{dY_l}{dx} = -\sin\theta\thinspace \partial_\theta Y_l = (l+1)q_l Y_{l-1} - l p_l Y_{l+1} \quad\to D , \]

    with rows the target degree and columns the source degree. Their adjoints under \(\int d\Omega\) (with \(N = \mathrm{diag}(n_l)\)) are \(X^\dagger = N^{-1}X^{\rm T}N = X\) and \(D^\dagger = N^{-1}D^{\rm T}N = 2X - D\) (integration by parts). \(P_n(X)\) and \(P_n'(X)\) follow from the three-term recurrences \((k+1)P_{k+1} = (2k+1)XP_k - kP_{k-1}\), \(P_{k+1}' = (k+1)P_k + XP_k'\). Write \(R_l = Y_l\hat{\mathbf r}\), \(S_l = r\nabla Y_l\), \(T_l = r\nabla Y_l\times\hat{\mathbf r}\) for the basis fields of (3.3), \(L\) for \(\mathrm{diag}(l(l+1))\) acting on the source and \(1/L'\) for the division of each row by its \(L'\) (0 for \(l' = 0\)). The operations needed by (2.7) are then:

    operation result on \((q, s, t)\) from
    \(g(x)\thinspace R_l\) \(q\): \(g(X)\)
    \(g(x)\thinspace S_l\) \(s\): \((g(X)L - g'(X)D)/L'\), \(t\): \(-i m\thinspace g'(X)/L'\) \(\int g\nabla Y_l\cdot\nabla Y_{l'}^\ast = \int Y_{l'}^\ast(L_l g Y_l - g' DY_l)\)
    \(g(x)\thinspace T_l\) \(s\): \(i m\thinspace g'(X)/L'\), \(t\): \((g(X)L - g'(X)D)/L'\) the same, rotated by \(\hat{\mathbf r}\times\)
    \(\hat{\mathbf z}\times(c\thinspace Y\hat{\mathbf r})\) \(s\): \(-i m\thinspace c/L'\), \(t\): \(D^\dagger c/L'\) \(\hat{\mathbf z}\times\hat{\mathbf r} = \sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\)
    \(\hat{\mathbf z}\times S_l\) \(-i m R_l - x T_l\) (2.5)
    \(\hat{\mathbf z}\times T_l\) \(-(DY_l)\hat{\mathbf r} + x S_l\) (2.5)
    \(\sin\theta\thinspace F_\theta\) (a scalar) \(-D s + i m\thinspace t\) \(\sin\theta\thinspace \partial_\theta Y = -DY\)

    diff_rot._primitives (line 324) composes them, per unit \(G\) and per unit shape:

    \[ \mathbf W_{\rm sh} = P_n(x)\left[i m\thinspace \mathbf u_{\rm sh} + 2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u_{\rm sh}\right] + \hat{\mathbf z}\times\left[-P_n'(x)\sin\theta\thinspace u_{\theta,{\rm sh}}\thinspace \hat{\mathbf r}\right], \qquad \mathbf W_{dq} = \hat{\mathbf z}\times\left[P_n(x)\thinspace Y\hat{\mathbf r}\right], \]

    with \(\mathbf u_q = R_l\), \(\mathbf u_a = S_l\), \(\mathbf u_T = T_l\) (the second term, the \(u_\theta\) part of the shear term, vanishes for the shape q). Every product is a sparse product of banded matrices, so the cost is \(O(l_{\max})\) and the result is exact to round-off at any degree. The matrices are built up to \(l_w\) = lmax + n + 6, because a product of matrices truncated at \(l_w\) is wrong only in its last few degrees.

    The primitives were first computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature of the table above; that builder is kept as an independent reference in tests/diff_rot/quad_reference.py. It cost \(O(l_{\max}^3)\) (177 s at lmax 1025) and failed silently from \(l = 646\) on, where scipy.special.sph_harm_y returns NaN; the two agree to \(\sim 10^{-12}\) below that (§9).

    3.5 Selection rules

    • The angular factors of (2.7) are \(P_n\), \(xP_n\), \(\sin\theta P_n\) and \(\sin^2\theta\thinspace P_n'\), polynomials in \(x\) (up to the \(\sin\theta\) factors absorbed by \(\partial_\theta Y\) and \(Z\)) of degree at most \(n + 1\). So \(W\) from degree \(l\) reaches the target degrees \(\lvert l' - l\rvert \le n + 1\).
    • \(\Delta\Omega\) is even in \(x\), so \(\mathbf W\) keeps the equatorial symmetry of \(\mathbf u\). In kore's ordering the poloidal and toroidal degrees of one symmetry class alternate, so same-type couplings (\(P \to P\), \(T \to T\)) have even \(\Delta l\) and mixed ones (\(P \leftrightarrow T\)) odd \(\Delta l\).
    • Together: \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le n\) for \(P \to P\) and \(T \to T\), and \(\le n + 1\) for \(P \leftrightarrow T\) (diff_rot.max_dl, line 425). For \(\Delta\Omega = f_0 + f_2P_2\): \(\Delta l \in \lbrace 0, \pm 2\rbrace\) and \(\lbrace\pm 1, \pm 3\rbrace\); the \(f_0\) terms alone have \(\Delta l = 0\) and \(\pm 1\), like the Coriolis force.
    • For \(m = 0\) the Doppler term vanishes, but the Coriolis and shear terms do not.

    The coupled problem is therefore wider than with uniform rotation: the row block of degree \(l'\) has entries in the column blocks of up to \(n_{\max} + 1\) neighbouring degrees of each type.

    3.6 Closed forms for \(P_0\) and \(P_2\)

    For \(n = 0\) and \(n = 2\) the coefficients of the table of §3.4 were first derived in closed form. kore now builds every degree with the algebra of §3.3, and the closed forms are kept as its validation in tests/diff_rot/diff_rot_coefficients.py (44 functions, \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le 3\), and the \(\sigma_n\) of (2.10)): f_* and df_*, d2f_* for \(f_2\) (\(P_2\), unnormalised, despite "Y20" in the comments), f0_*, df0_*, d2f0_* for \(f_0\). Each function takes the target degree l, m and offdiag = \(l - l'\) (source minus target) and returns the coefficient, already multiplied by \(L'\), with explicit conditions where a degree would fall below \(m\) or below 1.

    Two worked examples show the structure.

    • \(T \to T\), \(P_0\), \(\Delta l = 0\). With \(P_n = 1\), the T entries of the table of §3.3 give \(C^t_T = \left[i m L n_l + 2\int x(\partial_\theta Y Z^\ast - Z\partial_\theta Y^\ast)d\Omega\right]/(L n_l)\). For real \(Y\) and \(\partial_\theta Y\) the integral is \(-2im\int x Y\partial_\theta Y\thinspace d\theta\thinspace d\phi = -i m n_l\), so \(L C^t_T = i m (L - 2)\), which is f0_1C_D0T = 1j*(-2 + l + l**2)*m. The \(i m L\) is the Doppler term, and \(-2im\) is the local Coriolis force: compare operators.coriolis, whose \(T \to T\) row is \(-2 i m G\thinspace v_1/L^3\).
    • \(P \to T\), \(P_0\), \(\Delta l = -1\). For constant \(f_0 = a\), f0_1C_D0P = \(2(l-1)^2(l+1)\sqrt{l^2 - m^2}/(2l - 1)\) and f0_1C_lho1_D0P = f0_1C_D1P = \(-2(l^2 - 1)\sqrt{l^2 - m^2}/(2l - 1)\) are \(2L\) times the coefficients \(C(l - 1)\) and \(-C\) of the \(\Delta l = -1\) Coriolis row, \(C = (l-1)\sqrt{l^2 - m^2}/(l(2l - 1))\) (operators.coriolis, line 90). So a uniform \(f_0 = a\) gives exactly \(a\) times the Coriolis operator plus \(i m G a\) times the inertia operator, which is (2.12) at the matrix level.

    The algebra (§3.3) and the closed forms agree to round-off, \(\le 1.3\times 10^{-15}\) of the largest coefficient of the same row block, up to lmax 1025 (tests/diff_rot/gen_check.py, §9). Entry by entry, the closed forms are the more accurate: a few coefficients are small differences of terms of size \(O(L)\) in the algebra of §3.4 (for example df0_2C_D1P = \(2im\) exactly, \(-L(C^s_a + L_l C^s_{dq})\) in the algebra), so their own relative error grows like \(L\epsilon\), about \(10^{-10}\) at \(l \approx 1000\), while staying at round-off on the scale of the row, which is the scale at which they enter \(A\). Moving the profile \(f\) through the curls analytically before projecting would remove these cancellations; it has not been needed.

    3.7 The heat-equation terms in harmonic form

    Multiplication by a function of \(x\) is a matrix on the degree index. With Schmidt harmonics and \(p_l = \sqrt{(l+1)^2 - m^2}/(2l+1)\), \(q_l = \sqrt{l^2 - m^2}/(2l+1)\):

    \[ x\thinspace Y_l = p_l Y_{l+1} + q_l Y_{l-1} \quad\to X, \qquad (1 - x^2)\frac{dY_l}{dx} = (l+1)q_l Y_{l-1} - l p_l Y_{l+1} \quad\to D , \qquad (3.8) \]

    with rows the target degree and columns the source degree. Multiplying by \(P_n(x)\) is then the matrix \(P_n(X)\), and by \(P_n'(x)\) the matrix \(P_n'(X)\). These are the matrices of the angular algebra of §3.3 (angular.Algebra), shared by the momentum and heat terms.

    For one source degree, \(\partial_\theta S_{tw} = -\sin\theta\sum_n\sigma_n P_n'(x)\), and \(\sin\theta\thinspace \partial_\theta Y = -(1 - x^2)\thinspace dY/dx = -DY\). With (3.2), the three terms of (2.11) projected on degree \(l'\) are, before the row weight and the \(p_0\) factor,

    \[ \begin{aligned} \text{(a)}\quad & i m G\sum_n f_n\thinspace \left[P_n(X)\right]_ {l'l}\thinspace s_l ,\cr \text{(b)}\quad & \frac{L_l P_l}{r}\sum_n\sigma_n'\thinspace \left[P_n(X)\right]_ {l'l} ,\cr \text{(c)}\quad & \frac{a_l}{r}\sum_n\sigma_n\thinspace \left[P_n'(X)\thinspace D\right]_ {l'l} ,\cr \text{(c}')\quad & -\frac{i m T_l}{r}\sum_n\sigma_n\thinspace \left[P_n'(X)\right]_ {l'l} . \end{aligned} \qquad (3.9) \]

    (c) and (c′) are the poloidal and toroidal parts of the latitudinal term. The couplings are \(\Delta l \in \lbrace 0, \pm 2, \dots, \pm n_{\max}\rbrace\) for (a) (\(s \to s\)), \(\lbrace 0, \pm 2, \dots, \pm n_{tw}\rbrace\) for (b), (c) (\(P \to s\)) and \(\lbrace\pm 1, \dots, \pm(n_{tw} - 1)\rbrace\) for (c′) (\(T \to s\)), with \(n_{tw}\) the highest degree of \(S_{tw}\) (diff_rot.tw_nmax).

    5. Radial profiles

    5.1 The profiles \(f_n\) of each type

    diff_rot.shapes (line 111) defines the shape of each degree; \(f_n\) = diff_rot_ampl × \(a_n\) × shape, with \(a_n\) = diff_rot_term_amp[n/2] (1 in the table below). With \(a\) = diff_rot_ampl, \(p\) = diff_rot_param, and \(x\) the fractional solar radius (diff_rot.solar_x: \(x = r\cdot\)r_out with a solar structure (background.md), \(x = r\) otherwise):

    diff_rot_type \(f_0/a\) \(f_2/a\) \(f_n/a\), \(n \ge 4\)
    'rigid' 1
    'Y20' 1
    'Y20-wall-bounded' \((1 - r)(r - r_i)\), \(r_i\) = ricb
    'shellular' \(r^p - 1\)
    'shellular_boussinesq' \(\tfrac12(1 - r^2)\)
    'cylindrical' (\(a\varpi^2\)) \(\tfrac{2}{3}r^2\) \(-\tfrac{2}{3}r^2\)
    'conical' (\(a\sin^2\theta\)) \(\tfrac{2}{3}\) \(-\tfrac{2}{3}\)
    'solar' \((-7.695/435)\thinspace H(x)\) \((-86.04/435)\thinspace H(x)\)
    'solar_P4' \((\Omega_{RZ} + (O_0 - \Omega_{RZ})E)/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1\) \(O_2 E/\Omega_{\rm ref}\) \(O_4 E/\Omega_{\rm ref}\) (\(n = 4\))
    'tabulated' fit fit fit, up to diff_rot_nmax
    'user' diff_rot_profiles[0] diff_rot_profiles[2] diff_rot_profiles[n]

    Here \(H(x) = \tfrac12\left[1 + \tanh(2(x - 0.71)/0.1)\right]\) (the 'solar' frame is 435 nHz), and for 'solar_P4' \(E(x) = \tfrac12\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}((x - r_{tc})/d_{tc})\right]\) and \(\Omega_{CZ} = c_0 + c_2\cos^2\theta + c_4\cos^4\theta\) projected on Legendre polynomials (\(\cos^2\theta = (1 + 2P_2)/3\), \(\cos^4\theta = 1/5 + 4P_2/7 + 8P_4/35\)): \(O_0 = c_0 + c_2/3 + c_4/5\), \(O_2 = 2c_2/3 + 4c_4/7\), \(O_4 = 8c_4/35\).

    'user'. diff_rot_profiles maps each even degree to a string, a numpy expression of r (kore radius), x (fractional solar radius) and p (diff_rot_param), evaluated in a namespace with only sin, cos, tan, arcsin, arccos, arctan, sinh, cosh, tanh, exp, log, log10, sqrt, abs, sign, pi, where, minimum, maximum, erf, erfc (no builtins). For example a cylindrical rotation:

    par.diff_rot = 1; par.diff_rot_type = 'user'
par.diff_rot_profiles = {0: '(2/3)*r**2', 2: '-(2/3)*r**2'}
par.diff_rot_ampl = -0.25

    builds matrices bitwise identical to diff_rot_type = 'cylindrical' with the same amplitude. The degrees must be even integers, and the strings must not contain #: spin_doctor.py ends each params.dat row of a 'user' run with them, after a # (diff_rot.description), so that every row carries its profile and np.loadtxt still reads the numeric columns.

    'tabulated' (diff_rot._tabulated_fits, line 43). The file holds ome[θ, r] (nHz), r (R☉) and theta (rad). At each table radius, \(\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1\) is interpolated in \(\cos\theta\) by a cubic spline and projected on \(P_n\) with 64 Gauss–Legendre nodes, \(f_n = \tfrac{2n+1}{2}\int(\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1)P_n\thinspace dx\). Each \(f_n(r)\) is then fitted by least squares with a Chebyshev series:

    • by default (diff_rot_window = None with ricb > 0), on the table radii within 0.01 of the shell, of degree min(diff_rot_rdeg, half the number of radii);
    • with diff_rot_window = 'sun' (the default at ricb = 0), once over the whole Sun, of degree diff_rot_sun_deg, even in \(r\): the data are mirrored to \(-r\), continued below the first table radius by its value, and the odd coefficients are set to zero.

    diff_rot_hold = \(x_h\), which held each \(f_n\) at \(f_n(x_h)\) below \(x_h\), was removed on 2026-10-26, and a run that sets it stops. It held every degree, so the latitudinal shear of the table at \(x_h\) filled the whole core. In bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz that shear is about 4 nHz at 0.6 R☉, an artefact of the inversion; held from there, it gave the core spurious modes that mixed with the HFR modes. Its params.dat column stays, always nan.

    The fit residuals and, for the shell fit, the truncation error of the expansion up to diff_rot_nmax are printed in nHz.

    5.2 Derivatives and the full sphere

    The operators need \(f_n\), \(r f_n'\) and \(r^2 f_n''\) on the Chebyshev nodes. diff_rot.fX (line 224) transforms the shape to \(N\) Chebyshev coefficients on the radial domain (utils.chebco_f, coefficients below \(10^{-14}\) of the largest dropped; cached per degree), evaluates the series or its derivatives (utils.funcheb) and multiplies by diff_rot_ampl and \(a_n\). So \(f'\) and \(f''\) are always consistent with the resolved \(f\), also for tabulated, step-like or 'user' profiles.

    With ricb = 0 the radial coordinate is \(r \in [-1, 1]\) and each operator keeps only one parity of Chebyshev polynomials (§6.3). The shapes are therefore evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\), which makes \(f_n\) even, \(f_n'\) odd, and \(rf_n'\), \(r^2f_n''\) even. A shape with a kink at \(r = 0\) ('Y20-wall-bounded', 'shellular' unless \(p\) is even and positive, or a 'user' expression such as r) then converges slowly in \(N\); submatrices.py prints a WARNING for the predefined ones (line 245).

    5.3 Profile codes

    Each profile used in an operator has a three-letter code: two letters and the degree character diff_rot.DIGITS[n] (0–9, then a–z for 10–35):

    code profile function (diff_rot.profile_functions)
    zf0, zf2, … \(f_n\) (zonal flow) fX(n, r, Dorder)
    zd0, zd2, … \(r f_n'\) Chebyshev fit of \(r\thinspace\)fX(n, r, 1)
    zs0, zs2, … \(r^2 f_n''\) Chebyshev fit of \(r^2\thinspace\)fX(n, r, 2)
    tw2, tw4, … \(p_0\sigma_n\) Chebyshev fit of \(p_0\thinspace\)sigmaX(n, r, 0)
    tp2, tp4, … \(p_0\sigma_n'\) (odd in \(r\), in utils.odd_profiles) Chebyshev fit of \(p_0\thinspace\)sigmaX(n, r, 1)

    They are not in radial_profiles.proffdir until burrito first meets one and adds diff_rot.profile_functions(). With adiab_tw (ThermaD = 0 with the thermal wind) the tw, tp profiles are \(\sigma_n\), \(\sigma_n'\) without \(p_0\) (diff_rot._ptw), because those heat rows are divided by \(p_0\) (§7.2). A separate tp code is needed: a label such as h1tw21_D0 would mean \(r(p_0\sigma_2)'P\), the derivative of the product, not \(r p_0\sigma_2' P\).

    5.4 The thermal-wind coefficients \(\sigma_n\)

    diff_rot.sigma_coefficients(r) (line 251) solves (2.9) in Legendre space at all the radii at once, with the \(m = 0\) matrices of angular.py (the Schmidt harmonics of order 0 are the Legendre polynomials):

    • the Legendre coefficients of \(\Delta\Omega\) and \(\partial_r\Delta\Omega\) are the \(f_n\) and \(f_n'\) (fX); a product of two series is \(\sum_n f_n\thinspace P_n(X)\) applied to the other one, so \(F = 2\Delta\Omega + \Delta\Omega^2\) and \(\partial_r F = 2(1 + \Delta\Omega)\partial_r\Delta\Omega\) (thermal_wind = 2), or \(2\Delta\Omega\), \(2\partial_r\Delta\Omega\) (1);
    • multiplication by \(x\) is \(X\) and \((1 - x^2)\thinspace d/dx\) is \(D\), so the bracket of (2.9) is \(X\thinspace\partial_rF + DF/r\), and \((1 - x^2)\thinspace\partial_x S_{tw} = -\tfrac{r^2G^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}(I - X^2)(X\partial_rF + DF/r)\);
    • that is \(D\sigma\), which is solved for \(\sigma\) (with \(\sigma_0 = 0\), the \(l = 0\) part belonging to \(S_0\)) by forward substitution of \(h_k = (k+2)q_{k+1}\sigma_{k+1} - (k-1)p_{k-1}\sigma_{k-1}\) (_solve_D).

    This is exact (no quadrature), for any degrees of \(\Delta\Omega\), and matches the closed forms (2.10) to \(2\times 10^{-16}\) of the natural scale \(r^2G^2\lvert\partial\Delta\Omega\rvert/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\) (§9). The result is multiplied by thermal_wind_amplitude; thermal_wind = 3 gives \(\sigma_2 = -\tfrac23\)dSdtheta. sigmaX differentiates the Chebyshev series of \(\sigma_n(r)\) for \(\sigma_n'\). At ricb = 0, \(\sigma_n\) is even (\(f\) even, \(f'\) and \(g\) odd) and \(\sigma_n'\) odd, hence tp in odd_profiles.

    6. Radial operators and labels

    6.1 Label grammar

    A label names one radial operator: sX[fu1D1][fu2D2]_DY (utils.decode_label, line 68).

    • s: the section, u, v or h.
    • X: lowers the row's power of \(r\): the operator carries \(r^{p - X}\), with \(p\) = rpower_u (5) in u, rpower_v (3) in v, and 2 in h (1 when ThermaD = 0 without adiab_tw). Sections u and v also carry \(\rho_0\) to the power rhopower_u, rhopower_v (1 by default), section h no \(\rho_0\).
    • fu1D1, fu2D2: up to two profiles (§5.3) and their derivative orders. lho1, lho2 are \(h'\) and \(h''\).
    • _DY: the radial derivative order applied to the unknown.

    For example, with the defaults, u2zd20lho1_D0 is \(r^3\rho_0\thinspace (r f_2')\thinspace h'\) acting on \(P\), and h1tw20lho1_D0 is \(r\thinspace p_0\sigma_2\thinspace h'\) acting on \(P\). rap.burrito evaluates \(r^{\rm rpower}\rho_0^{\rm rhopower} f_1 f_2\) on the Chebyshev nodes, utils.chebco_f transforms it, and utils.Mlam builds the multiplication matrix in the Gegenbauer basis \(C^{(\lambda)}\) of the section; submatrices.py then multiplies on the right by the derivative matrix and changes the basis: u is \(C^{(4)}\), v \(C^{(2)}\), h \(C^{(2)}\) (\(C^{(0)}\) if ThermaD = 0, \(C^{(1)}\) with adiab_tw). The diff-rot labels need at most \(P''\) in u, \(P'\) in v and \(P'\) in h, so they fit those bases. An operator that several terms share is computed only once.

    6.2 The labels

    diff_rot.labels() (line 443) lists every radial operator of the terms, and submatrices.py (line 191) adds them with the vector parity of their column (vP for \(P\) and \(s\), vT for \(T\)), after diff_rot.check():

    • for each degree \(n\) of \(\Delta\Omega\), the 18 momentum labels of the table of §3.4 (diff_rot.MOMENTUM with the codes of \(n\));
    • with thermal = 1 (_heat_labels, line 429): (a) h0pss0zf{n}0_D0 (\(r^2 p_0 f_n\); h0zf{n}0_D0 without pss if ThermaD = 0) for each degree of \(\Delta\Omega\); and for each degree \(n\) of \(S_{tw}\): (b) h1tp{n}0_D0 (\(r p_0\sigma_n'\), only if thermal_wind_radial = 1), (c) h2tw{n}0_D0, h1tw{n}0_D1, h1tw{n}0lho1_D0 (\(p_0\sigma_n\) times \(P\), \(rP'\), \(rh'P\)), (c′) h1tw{n}0_D0 (\(r p_0\sigma_n\) acting on \(T\), parity vT).

    Like every other operator, each diff-rot matrix has its last chop rows dropped and that many zero rows added at the top, to make room for the boundary-condition rows of its row block, so the boundary conditions are unchanged by the differential rotation.

    6.3 Parity in a full sphere

    At ricb = 0 the vector parity of the unknowns is \(v_P = (-1)^m\)symm for \(P\) and \(s\), and \(v_T = -v_P\) for \(T\). An operator has the parity \((-1)^{\text{rpower} + \text{dorder}_ 1 + \text{dorder}_ 2 + \text{DY} + \text{adj}}\), with adj = 1 for a first profile in utils.odd_profiles. utils.remroco keeps the rows of parity \(v\times\)(operator parity) and the columns of parity \(v\). This is correct only if every profile has the parity assumed: \(f_n\), \(rf_n'\), \(r^2f_n''\), \(\sigma_n\) even, \(\sigma_n'\) odd, \(h^{(k)}\) of parity \((-1)^k\); §5.2 explains how the profiles are made so. If a profile's Chebyshev coefficients of the wrong parity exceed \(10^{-8}\) of the largest, submatrices.py prints a WARNING: that part of the profile is dropped.

    7. The operators and the assembly

    7.1 Momentum: diff_rot.momentum_block

    operators.differential_rotation(l, section, component, offdiag) (line 113) calls diff_rot.momentum_block(l, section, component, offdiag, M) (line 470) with M the operators' namespace, where the .mtx matrices are loaded under their labels. For the row degree l (= \(l'\)), the section ('u' or 'v'), the column type ('upol' for \(P\), 'utor' for \(T\)) and offdiag = \(l - l'\), it sums over the degrees \(n\) of \(\Delta\Omega\) that couple this Δl (§3.5): \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le\) max_dl(n, …) and the parity of Δl; for each, the coefficient momentum_coef(name, n, l, m, offdiag) times the matrix of each label of momentum_labels(n, …). The result is multiplied by Gaspard and divided by \(L'^3\). Since each stored coefficient carries a factor \(L'\), the block is

    \[ \frac{G}{L'^3}\sum(L'\times\text{coefficient})\times(\text{radial operator}) = \mathrm{row}(\mathbf W) \quad\text{of (3.6)} . \]

    The \(1/L'^3\) makes these rows consistent with all the other momentum operators (§3.2). It returns [matrix, offd], offd the column block offset (utils.dr_offd, §7.3), or [0, 0] if no degree couples this Δl.

    momentum_coefficients(n, m) (line 348) builds the coefficients once per \((n, m)\), for degrees up to lmax + n + 6 (building beyond lmax keeps the entries near lmax free of truncation errors), and stores them both as sparse matrices and by diagonal (angular.band, \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le n + 3\)), so that momentum_coef is an array lookup. The whole set takes milliseconds at lmax 1025.

    8. Diagnostics

    spin_doctor.py checks every solution against the energy, frequency and entropy budgets. With differential rotation, the terms couple different degrees, so a per-degree worker cannot evaluate them from its own degree alone. utils4pp.diff_rot_setup (line 569), called once per solution by utils4pp.diagnose, therefore:

    1. builds \(\mathbf u\) and \(\partial_r\mathbf u\) on the \((r_k, \cos\theta)\) grid from the per-degree components (velocity), with lmax + 10 + max(\(n_{tw}\), \(n_{\max}\)) Gauss–Legendre nodes in \(\cos\theta\). The harmonics \(Y_l\), \(\partial_\theta Y_l\) and \(Z_l\) come from angular.ylm_grid: the recurrence of \(X\) started at \(Y_m \propto \sin^m\theta\), differentiated in \(\theta\) for \(\partial_\theta Y_l\), and started at \(\sin^{m-1}\theta\) for \(Y_l/\sin\theta\), so that nothing is divided by \(\sin\theta\). The recurrence is linear, so it is carried as a mantissa times a power of two per node: for large \(m\) the start \(\sin^{m-1}\theta\) would underflow near the poles (from \(m \approx 330\) at \(l\) of a few thousand), and the mantissas are rescaled by \(2^{-600}\) when they grow past \(2^{600}\), which is exact. It is valid at any \(l\) and \(m\) (it matches exact rational values of \(P_l\) to \(\sim 10^{-13}\) up to \(l = 2000\), and keeps the norms to \(\sim 10^{-13}\) at \(m = 1425\), \(l = 3000\)), where scipy.special.sph_harm_y is NaN from \(l = 646\) on; utils.Ylm and utils.Ylm_full use it too;
    2. evaluates \(\Delta\Omega\), \(\partial_r\Delta\Omega\), \(\partial_r^2\Delta\Omega\), \(\partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\) there, as numpy Legendre series of the \(f_n\) (diff_rot.fX);
    3. forms the force \(\mathbf F = -\mathbf W\) of (2.6) and its radial derivative (the force closure, line 610), and projects them back on \((q, s, t)\) with (3.4): drforce[l];
    4. with thermal = 1, forms \(p_0[i m G\Delta\Omega s + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_{tw}]\) and projects it on \(Y_l\): drheat[l].

    Only the profiles \(f_n\) come from diff_rot.py; the angular part here is independent of its algebra. The \(\sigma_n\) used here are computed by utils4pp.thermal_wind_entropy (line 489), which integrates (2.9) in \(x\) with numpy Legendre series at each radius and writes out its \(r\) derivative, not with diff_rot.sigma_coefficients. So the budgets test the assembled operators against a second implementation of the same equations.

    The per-degree workers then add:

    • flow_worker (lines 761–769): Wdr = \(2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\mathbf u^\ast\cdot\mathbf F\thinspace dV\), Wdr_im its imaginary part, and Ensdr the enstrophy term \((\nabla\times\mathbf u)\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\);
    • thermal_worker (lines 855–857): Wdr_thm = \(-2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int p_0 s^\ast[i m G\Delta\Omega s + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_{tw}]\thinspace dV\).

    spin_doctor (lines 389–395) includes them in the residuals:

    \[ \begin{aligned} \text{resid}\sigma &: \quad 2\sigma\thinspace KE - D_{kin} - W_{thm} - W_{dr} = 0,\cr \text{resid}\omega &: \quad 2\omega\thinspace KE + W_{cor,im} - D_{kin,im} - W_{thm,im} - W_{dr,im} = 0,\cr \text{resid}s &: \quad 2\sigma\thinspace TE - D_{thm} - W_{adv} - W_{dr,thm} = 0 , \end{aligned} \]

    and the enstrophy residual includes Ensdr. By §2.3, only the shear part of \(\mathbf W\) contributes to Wdr; the Doppler and local-Coriolis parts appear in Wdr_im. The diff-rot options are written to params.dat (spin_doctor.param_table: diff_rot_ampl, the \(a_n\), diff_rot_param, and diff_rot_type as its index in diff_rot.TYPES, with a 'user' run's profile definitions at the end of the row after #), and Wdr, Wdr_im, Ensdr, Wdr_thm to flow.dat and thermal.dat.

    10. Limitations

    • The profiles do not depend on the background model, except that solar_x takes \(x = r\cdot\)r_out with a solar structure (background.md).
    • The degree character of the profile codes limits the degrees to 35: \(n_{\max} \le 16\) with thermal_wind = 2 (\(S_{tw}\) up to \(2n_{\max} + 2 = 34\)), \(n_{\max} \le 32\) with thermal_wind = 1; diff_rot.check stops otherwise.
    • 'user' expressions are evaluated with Python's eval in a namespace without builtins: enough to stop accidents, not a sandbox for untrusted input.
    • The inviscid formulation (ViscosD = 0, rows times \(\rho_0^2\)) builds the same labels with different weights, but is untested with differential rotation, and ru_insane.py does not cover it.
    • With thermal_wind = 1 the eigenvalues depend on the choice of frame at second order in \(\Delta\Omega\); thermal_wind = 2 is frame independent.
    • The \(p_b S_0'\) term of the thermal wind is omitted (§1.4); it matters where \(S_0' \neq 0\), e.g. in a radiative interior (stratification 'solar_rz').
    • With ThermaD = 0 and the thermal wind, the boundary layer that a small ThermaD would form at fixed-entropy walls is absent, and small-scale growing modes peaking at \(l \approx\) lmax can appear that do not converge in lmax.
    • The matrices widen with the highest degree: the momentum rows couple \(n_{\max} + 1\) degrees on each side, the heat rows \(n_{tw}\) = \(2n_{\max} + 2\) with thermal_wind = 2. 'tabulated' with a large diff_rot_nmax is the most expensive case (§12). Sharp profiles (the 'solar' tachocline at ricb = 0) need large N (about 400).
