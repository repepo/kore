2026-10-29

Fix: D3 and D2 Lorentz terms in an inviscid full sphere; new parameter inviscid_basis (bin/submatrices.py,
bin/params_default.py, docs/method.md, docs/setup.md, docs/solver.md, tests/test_torsional_s2.py,
tests/torsional_s2/reference.eig)
- With Ek = 0 and ricb = 0 the rows of section u are in C^(2) and those of section v in C^(1), but the Lorentz force
  has the third derivative of f in section u ('u303') and the second in section v ('v102'). G[gbx-1][gbx-dx] then
  indexed from the end of the list and applied S10 (S0) to coefficients in C^(3) (C^(2)): a wrong operator, which
  moved the eigenvalues by 6e-8 (torsional mode, Lu = 1e3) to 8e-7 (tests/torsional_s2, Lu = 2e4) at any N.
- Now any term with dx > gbx is brought back from C^(dx) to C^(gbx) with the inverse of the conversion
  G[dx-1][dx-gbx]. It is upper triangular and its inverse dense, so these blocks of A are dense: nnz(A) +53% at
  N = 128 and +87% at N = 192 for tests/torsional_s2, solve time +15% and +35%. The unused viscous terms of an
  inviscid run go the same way. Viscous runs are unchanged (no term has dx > gbx there).
- New parameter inviscid_basis: 'low' (default) is the above; 'high' puts the rows of section u in C^(3) and of
  section v in C^(2) (no bc rows in v), as anelastic_log does. A then stays banded, but the eigenvalues converge only
  as N^-3 (the unresolved magnetic boundary layer enters through f'''): tests/torsional_s2 is 1.3e-5 and 1.2e-4 from
  Luo & Jackson (2022) at N = 128 with 'high', against 8.4e-7 and 5.2e-6 with 'low'.
- Cost at high N (tests/torsional_s2, lmax = N - 1, 8 ranks; mode-2 error, peak memory, time of one solve):
  'low' 4.5e-8, 16 GB, 60 s at N = 512 and 1.5e-8, 95 GB, 490 s at N = 1024; 'high' 1.5e-6, 8.2 GB, 21 s at
  N = 512 and 1.0e-7, 37 GB, 120 s at N = 1024. 'low' is 13-35x more accurate at the same N and cheaper for a given
  accuracy down to ~1e-8, but its overhead grows with N (memory x1.9 to x2.6, time x2.8 to x4.1 from N = 512 to
  1024); 'high' suits runs that need N >= ~1000 anyway. Documented in docs/solver.md.
- Checked: torsional mode 2 of tests/torsional_s2 at Lu = 1e3, N = 64, 128, 192: the new eigenvalues equal those of
  the C^(3)/C^(2) rows in the limit (difference 1.5e-4, 1.8e-5, 3.4e-6, falling as N^-4), while the old ones stay
  6e-8 away at every N. A and B are bitwise unchanged for the viscous cases checked (conducting inner core, spin-over).
  tests/torsional_s2/reference.eig recomputed (modes 1 and 2 moved by 2.1e-7 and 8.1e-7, beyond the test's 1e-7).
  inviscid_basis = 'high' gives A and B bitwise equal to a copy with the C^(3)/C^(2) rows hard-coded; any other
  value stops submatrices.py.

2026-10-23

Axisymmetric background flow (bin/bgflow.py new, bin/assemble.py, bin/params_default.py, bin/spin_doctor.py,
docs/bgflow.md new, docs/output.md, mkdocs.yml)
- bgflow = 1 adds the linearised advection by an axisymmetric flow U(r,theta), F = (U.grad)u + (u.grad)U, to the
  momentum equation (sections u and v, with the Coriolis terms), for example for the modes on top of the steady
  flow of forcing = 12. U is bgflow_Ro times the m = 0 flow with the poloidal and toroidal scalars P_n(r), T_n(r)
  in bgflow_file. Hydro only, ricb > 0.
- bgflow.py: r F is a sum of products of a radial factor of U and one of u (P/r, (rP)'/r, T, or their r d/dr)
  times angular functions, which are projected by Gauss-Legendre quadrature in cos(theta); the radial operators
  use ut.Mlam and ut.Dlam as in submatrices.py. A term of U of degree n couples |l - l'| <= n, so A is much denser
  than without the background flow.
- bgflow.py as a script in a forcing = 12 folder writes bgflow.npz with the P_n, T_n (n <= nmax) of its steady
  flow: python3 bin/bgflow.py nmax for the linear flow (from the solution, per unit forcing_amplitude_icb), or
  python3 bin/bgflow.py nmax nonlinear for the nonlinear flow at Ro = forcing_amplitude_icb, by Newton's method,
  (A + B_k) x_k+1 = b + B_k x_k/2 with B_k the bgflow matrix of the current flow 2 x_k, and continuation in Ro.
  A nonlinear file holds its Ro and can only be used with bgflow_Ro = Ro.
- spin_doctor: the work of the background flow, -2 Re int u^*.(u.grad)U dV, enters resid u and is column 20 of
  flow.dat; bgflow and bgflow_Ro are the last two columns of params.dat.
- Tests: a rigid rotation (T_1 = r) gives lambda0(OmgTau = 1+Ro, Ek/(1+Ro)) - i m Ro to 7e-14 (m = 1, symm = 1
  and m = 2, symm = -1); with the forcing = 12 flow at Ek = 1e-4, resid u <= 5e-7 at N = 64. Onsets at Ek = 1e-4,
  ricb = 1/3 (N = 48, lmax = m + 95, nmax = 40) on the nonlinear flow: m = 1 at Ro = -0.647, m = 3 at +0.419 and
  m = 4 at +0.446 (Hollerbach 2003, JFM 492: -0.651, 0.414, 0.453). On the linear flow: -0.644 and +0.452.

  + c.c., so u_phi = DeltaOmega_b r_b sin(theta)). Forcing 7 (m = 0 libration) uses the same rows, but its
  amplitude is i*forcing_frequency*(libration angle) and vanishes at zero frequency. With forcing_frequency = 0
  the forced matrix is A alone: the linear spherical Couette (Proudman-Stewartson) flow.
- spin_doctor: resid0 includes the power of the rotating walls, -2 Re(Gamma_cmb DeltaOmega_cmb/2 + Gamma_icb
  DeltaOmega_icb/2)/(OmgTau Ek); resid u is |int u.lap(u) dV| / viscous dissipation (zero for a steady flow without
  body force; the eigenvalue-style form is 1 by construction here); resid L is computed (m = 0, symm = 1): the
  torques on the mantle and the inner core cancel.
- resid E stays nan for forcing 12, as for all forced problems (em_budget is not set up for a forcing). resid0 and
  resid u cover its energy balance.
- Checked (hydro only, Ek = 1e-4 and 1e-5, ricb = 0.35): with rotation = 0 the exact Stokes solution
  u_phi = DeltaOmega ri^3 (1/r^2 - r) sin(theta) / (1 - ri^3) to 5e-16; boundary values to 1e-14; N = 64,
  lmax = 127 converged to 1e-12 at Ek = 1e-4; the interior tends to Proudman's (1956) geostrophic rotation as
  Ek decreases (1e-3 at Ek = 1e-6). resid0, resid u and resid L <= 7e-13 for the inner core, the mantle or both
  rotating. solve_nopp.py (MUMPS) agrees with a scipy LU solve to 3e-12.
- Not done: the 'conducting, Bessel' ICB condition uses k = (1-i) sqrt(omega/2 E_eta) and dlogjl(l, k ri), which
  is 0*inf at omega = 0; the static limit (k j_l'/j_l -> l/r) is not coded, so forcing 12 with a magnetic field
  needs 'insulator', 'TWA' or 'conducting, Chebys'.


2026-10-21

Thin walls: wall permeability muw_cmb, muw_icb = mu_w/mu0 (default 1) and the O(h) terms that go with it
(bin/assemble.py, bin/utils.py, bin/utils4pp.py, bin/params_default.py, docs)
- The poloidal thin-wall conditions (Guervilly, Wood & Brummell 2013, app. A) are the limit h -> 0 with h*mu_w
  and h*sigma_w fixed, which needs mu_w >> mu0. At finite h they drop O(h) terms from the wall's curvature, from
  matching the potential field at r +- h, and from the l(l+1)/r^2 part of lap_l in the wall, terms of the same
  order as the c term when mu_w ~ mu0. With p = mu_w/mu0 and h/r = c*mu/p, bc_b_thinlayer now adds
  + (c*mu*l/(p*r))*((l+1)/p - 1)*F (CMB) and - (c*mu*(l+1)/(p*r))*(l/p + 1)*F (ICB). For p = 1 they cancel the
  c term at leading order (a static field crosses a wall with the permeability of vacuum unchanged), for p = inf
  they vanish (the former conditions, bitwise identical matrices). The toroidal conditions are unchanged.
- Checked against an exact conducting layer (layer equation integrated across the wall; CMB and ICB; p = 1 to
  100, mu = 1 to 3, steady and oscillating fields): error O(h) relative to the wall's own effect, against O(1)
  for small p without the terms (e.g. 8% at l = 5, ICB, p = 1). With c1 = 0 and p = mu = 1 a wall now gives the
  insulating eigenvalues to 1e-14 (Ek = 1e-5, c = 1e-2; 1e-3 to 1e-2 off before).
- B0_cmb = 'wall': the steady condition gets the same term; for p = 1 and mu = 1 it is the insulating condition
  and B0 is unchanged.
- em_budget (resid E): wall energy includes the radial field, (h/p) int |b_r|^2, and the potential field is
  matched at r + h (r - h at the ICB); resid E closes to O(c*c1) as before (4e-6 at c = c1 = 1e-3, Ek = 1e-5).
- Changes results of existing TWA runs with c != 0 by O(l*c) unless muw_cmb = np.inf is set.


2026-10-18

Conducting inner core: radial projection; consoidal projection and perfect conductors removed (bin/utils.py,
bin/submatrices.py, bin/operators.py, bin/assemble.py, bin/params_default.py)
- With innercore = 'conducting, Chebys' the poloidal induction equation in the fluid used its consoidal
  component (C^(3) basis), with the continuity of eta*lap_l f as an extra ICB row. The consoidal component
  is the radial derivative of the radial one, so its tau error was integrated across the shell into a
  spurious c/r term of the radial equation (r*e constant to 3 digits from the ICB to the CMB). A torsional
  mode at Ek = 1e-9, sigma_i2o = 1e-6, N = 688 had spin_doctor resid b = 1e-3 (1e-5 with an insulating
  inner core) and a low-l poloidal field wrong by factors up to 30; the error decays with N, but needed
  about 20% more N.
- Section f now always uses the radial component (C^(2) basis), as for an insulating inner core, with [n.b]
  (section f) and [n x b/mu] (section fic) as the ICB conditions of the poloidal field. The radial induction
  equation on both sides then makes (curl E)_r, i.e. the poloidal part of [n x E], continuous. Removed:
  induction_consoidal, the consoidal branches of b and magnetic_diffusion, the C^(3) basis of section f, the
  [n x E] row of bc_b_cic and the extra CMB row offsets of bc_b_cmb and bc_b_thinlayer.
- Removed the 'perfect conductor, material' and 'perfect conductor, spatial' inner cores (bc_b_icb), the only
  other users of the consoidal projection, already unreachable (not in ic_bc_list). A perfectly conducting
  inner core is pending a rewrite: with the radial projection f = 0 at the ICB is enough for the poloidal field.
- Checked at Ek = 1e-5 (sigma_i2o = 1e-6, 1e-2, 1; N = 96, 128): same eigenvalues as the consoidal version to
  1e-9. At Ek = 1e-9, sigma_i2o = 1e-6, the torsional modes T1-T5 have resid b = 1.0-1.2e-5 at the N an
  insulating inner core needs (688-1024), against 1e-4 to 1e-3 before, and the poloidal field equals the
  insulating-core one to 4 digits. At sigma_i2o = 1 (N = 1024) the torsional modes have all residuals below
  1e-8. A and B are unchanged for insulating and thin-wall inner cores.
- spin_doctor's residuals cover the fluid volume only: they do not test the inner-core field or the ICB
  conditions. Check N_cic by the change of the eigenvalue.

Full energy budget, resid E (bin/spin_doctor.py, bin/utils4pp.py)
- New residual resid E: 2 sigma (KE + ME of the fluid, of the inner core, of thin walls and of the potential
  field outside) against the viscous dissipation, the work of the background currents (J0 x b) and buoyancy,
  and the Ohmic losses in the fluid, a conducting inner core and thin walls, as eta*int |curl b|^2. It tests
  the ICB and CMB matching conditions, which resid b (fluid only, with b.lap(b)) cannot see. New function
  utils4pp.em_budget; the inner-core field is read from the magnetic_ic files. The terms are written to the
  new file budget.dat (8 columns). Needs no-slip boundaries; nan for forced problems, 'conducting, Bessel',
  thin walls with mu != 1, or rotdyn with vtrq or mtrq = 0.
- With rotdyn = 1 the budget adds the rotational energies I|omega|^2 of the mantle and inner core and the
  gravitational energy K|phi|^2 (spin_doctor's energies are int |u|^2, twice the energy of the complex
  amplitude), the inner core's viscous relaxation 2K(OmgTau/OmgtauIC)|phi|^2, and the work of the magnetic
  torques 2 Re(Gamma conj(omega)); the viscous term is then Dint. resid0 now includes the work of the moving
  walls, -2 Re(Gamma_visc conj(omega)): it was 1e-4 to 1e-1 with rotdyn and is now 1e-13.
- Thin walls: Ohmic loss int |K|^2 dS/(c1 r) from the wall current K (|g|^2/c1 toroidal, c1 |r lap_l f|^2
  poloidal), the field beyond the wall from the wall condition, and the energy c r/2 int |b_t|^2 dS of the
  poloidal b_t that goes with the c term.
- Checked on torsional modes (Luo_S2, Ek = 1e-4, N = 64): resid E = 1e-10 for insulating, conducting
  (sigma_i2o = 1e-2, 1) and thin-wall inner cores with insulating or thin-wall mantles, and 1e-13 for a full
  sphere. With c and c1 both nonzero it closes to about c*c1 times the wall's share (7e-6 for c = c1 = 1e-2),
  the order of the thin-wall model. With rotdyn (I_M = 0.5, gTorque = 1e-3, OmgtauIC = 1e3; insulating,
  conducting and thin-wall boundaries) resid E = 1e-10. At Ek = 1e-9, sigma_i2o = 1 (N = 832) resid E = 8e-9
  where resid b = 6e-7.

Background-field series (bin/submatrices.py, bin/params_default.py)
- The Chebyshev series of the profiles r**p * h^(j) (p = -1..5, j = 0..3) that build the magnetic coupling
  blocks were cut where a coefficient fell below an absolute 1e-9. With B0_ic = 'potential' the
  beta*r**-(l+1) term is not a polynomial, so these series ran to 31 terms (Luo_S2, ricb = 0.35), against
  at most 12 for the polynomial full-sphere field; the series length sets the band width of those blocks
  and with it the fill-in of the LU factors.
- New parameter tol_B0 (default 1e-8): each series is now cut relative to its largest coefficient.
- Checked on a torsional mode (Luo_S2, ricb = 0.35, insulating, Ek = 10**-8.5, N = 768, lmax = 767),
  against a relative cut of 1e-12 (same memory as the old absolute cut): 1e-8 used 9% less memory in the
  solve, with the same eigenvalue to 1e-9 and the same spin_doctor residuals; 1e-10 saved 4%; 1e-6 saved
  13% but moved the eigenvalue by 4e-8 and raised resid L to 2e-5.


2026-10-16
----------
Postprocessing speed (bin/utils4pp.py, bin/spin_doctor.py, bin/utils.py)
- spin_doctor.py was as slow as the solve at high resolution: 1126 s for 10 solutions at N = 1008,
  lmax = 1007 on 8 processes. diagnose sent each l to the pool as a separate task together with the
  full Chebyshev coefficients of u and b (32 MB per task, 2016 tasks per solution), so the workers
  waited on the transfers (143 s per solution, of which ~19 s was arithmetic), and each worker
  re-evaluated every radial function about five times (lorentz4pp, induction4pp and pressure4pp also
  use the neighbouring l's), with chebval, a Python loop over N.
- New cheb2space_table in utils4pp.py: the radial functions of all l and their r-derivatives at the
  quadrature points, with one matrix product per derivative (chebvander) instead of one chebval per
  row. diagnose builds these tables once per solution and runs the workers serially on them
  (cheb2space_pol, cheb2space_tor and buoyancy4pp read from a table through the new radial(), and
  still take Chebyshev coefficients as before). ncpus and pool are no longer used by diagnose; the
  worker functions and their arguments are unchanged.
- New b0_profiles in utils4pp.py: h0, h1, h2 and B0_norm() at the grid points, computed once per grid
  instead of in lorentz4pp and induction4pp for every l (B0_norm alone takes 10 ms with
  cnorm = 'mag_energy').
- geostrophic_fraction builds P_l^1(cos theta) by the upward recurrence in l instead of calling
  scipy.special.lpmv for each l (17 s at lmax = 1007, now 0.03 s; same values to 2e-12), and splits
  the complex product into two real ones.
- spin_doctor.py reads the solution files with up to ncpus processes (2.5 s per 250 MB file) and no
  longer creates a pool for diagnose. ut.Dcheb uses len() instead of np.size(), so it also works on
  2D arrays, one expansion per column; unchanged for 1D input.
- Result: 27 s instead of 1126 s at N = 1008 (10 solutions), peak memory 0.9 GB instead of 4.3 GB.
- Checked: on that run and on eight small cases (spin-over, S2 torsional full sphere, Jones 2000
  thermal, Silva 2019 thermocompositional, no rotation, a G21 dipole with conducting IC and thin-wall
  mantle, an m = 2 G21 dipole, an m = 1 full-sphere S2), old and new spin_doctor run on the same
  solutions give the same eigenvalues and the same summary table, apart from the last printed digit
  of some residuals below 1e-12. In the .dat files, energies, dissipation and work terms agree to
  1e-11 relative or better, torques, angular momentum and geo to 4e-16, and the residuals change by
  at most 4e-13 in absolute value. press0 in full-sphere cases (evaluated at the innermost quadrature
  point, close to r = 0) is dominated by rounding in both versions and changes by up to 1e-11.
  Against an 80-bit long double evaluation, the new radial values are 1.5 to 3 times more accurate
  than chebval's.

- [10-07] New B0_alpha_cmb. With mantle = 'TWA', B0_cmb = 'wall' and c_cmb != 0 (or mu != 1), h0..h3
          add alpha*r**l to h, with alpha set by the wall's steady matching condition at the CMB,
          (1/mu + l*c_cmb)*(r*h)' + l*h = 0.
          Why: the wall condition is linear in B, so B0 has to satisfy it too. For a steady field the
          wall's eddy currents vanish (Guervilly et al. 2013, eq. 44 with d/dt = 0), so c1_cmb drops out
          but c_cmb stays. The full-sphere fields, the FDMs and 'dipole' were built for a vacuum outside,
          i.e. for c_cmb = 0 and mu = 1.
          What the added term does:
            - it carries no current, so the currents in the fluid are unchanged;
            - r**l satisfies r*h' - l*h = 0, so the ICB matching of B0_ic is kept;
            - for l = 1 it is a uniform axial field.
          'axial' (imposed from outside the core) is left alone. h0..h3 take a new argument cmb_pot (like
          ic_pot) so that beta and alpha can be computed without recursion.
          Checked:
            - every B0, ricb = 0 and 0.35, c_cmb = 0.01 and 0.3, mu = 1 and 2: the wall condition holds
              to 7e-16, the current (h'' + 2h'/r - l(l+1)h/r^2) is unchanged to 8e-16 (relative; 'dipole'
              stays current-free), the ICB matching holds ('dipole' never had it), and h1..h3 match finite
              differences of h0;
            - the change alpha makes to the matrix A (Luo_S1, c_cmb = 0.3) equals 2*alpha times the
              matrix of B0 = 'axial' to 4e-15, so the operators treat the added term like a supported field;
            - S2 torsional mode (setup of the bin/assemble.py entry, c1_cmb = 0.015): alpha moves it by
              2.6e-3 at c_cmb = 1e-2 (frequency +0.27%) and 5.6e-2 at c_cmb = 0.3 (+5.8%). FDM (l = 1)
              torsional mode at c_cmb = 1e-2: 8e-4. All spin_doctor residuals < 6e-6;
            - rotdyn = 1, c_cmb = 0.3: resid L = 5.8e-6, so the mantle torque (gamma_magnetic, which uses
              h0 at the CMB) is consistent with the new B0.

              (relative); after it they agree to 1e-8.
- [10-07] New B0_beta_ic. With B0_ic = 'potential' and ricb > 0, h0..h3 add beta*r**-(l+1) to h,
          where beta = ricb**(l+1)*(ricb*h' - l*h)/(2l+1) is evaluated at the ICB.
          Why: the full-sphere fields (G21 dipole, Luo_S1, Luo_S2) do not match a potential field at
          the ICB. With an inner core, part of their current then has to flow in the IC, or in a
          current sheet on the ICB. A steady B0 cannot keep such currents in an IC of finite
          conductivity, whatever its value: with no EMF in the IC, curl(eta*J0) = 0 there, and an
          axisymmetric azimuthal J0 has to vanish.
          What the added term does:
            - it carries no current, so the currents in the fluid are unchanged;
            - it keeps the CMB matching;
            - B0 continues into the IC as the potential field h(ricb)*(r/ricb)**l.
          The result is the field of the fluid part of the original currents. 'axial' and the FDMs
          already satisfy the ICB condition (to 1e-14). 'dipole' is a point source at the centre. Both
          are left alone.
          Checked for Luo_S2 at ricb = 0.35:
            - beta = -0.06716; r*h' - 2h = 0 at the ICB; h' + 3h = 2e-15 at the CMB;
            - h0..h3 match finite differences of each other;
            - the current (h'' + 2h'/r - 6h/r^2) is unchanged to 3e-16.
          Size of the change: 10% of B0 at the ICB, 1.7% at the CMB, 2.7% rms. cnorm = 'mag_energy'
          gives 1.0568x the full-sphere value (1.050x with 'full sphere').
          A full sphere (ricb = 0) is unaffected: both CI tests pass.
          Effect on a torsional mode (Luo_S2, ricb = 0.35, Ek = 1e-7, Lambda = 10, Pm = 0.1, no-slip,
          insulating mantle, cnorm = 0.005061567359972097, Alfven time units, N = 232, N_cic = 48 and 64):
            - insulating IC: -0.45521 + 1.00258i with 'full sphere', -0.45657 + 0.99090i with
              'potential' (frequency 1.2% lower);
            - conducting IC, sigma_i2o = 1: -0.47390 + 1.08496i and -0.47616 + 1.06118i (2.2% lower);
            - conducting IC with 'potential', sigma_i2o = 1e-4 and 1e-3: the eigenvalue differs from
              the insulating one by 1.8e-4 and 1.8e-3 (relative), so it converges linearly to the
              insulating limit.
          All these runs have every spin_doctor residual < 2e-5.
- [10-07] B0_norm and B0_norm2 (cnorm = 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012') integrate B0**2 with Gauss-Legendre
          quadrature instead of Chebyshev-Gauss times sqrt(1-x^2). The old rule was only second order:
          for Luo & Jackson's S2 field cnorm was off by 1.1e-7 (0.005061566802 instead of the exact
          0.005061567360). Gauss-Legendre on the same 240 points is exact for these polynomial fields.
- [10-01] packit appends each block to lists, and the new unpackit joins them once. assemble.py uses
          both (see above).
- [10-01] Mlam (the Chebyshev multiplication matrix for lambda > 0) is vectorised:
            - it computes all entries with |j-k| <= bandwidth at once;
            - it keeps only the terms where the profile a0 is nonzero;
            - it starts the recursion from a closed-form value that costs O(lambda);
            - it builds the CSR matrix directly, instead of filling a dok_matrix entry by entry.
          csl0 and csl were no longer needed and were removed. The sparsity is unchanged and entries
          agree to 1.6e-15, slightly closer to exact rational arithmetic than before.
          submatrices.py at N = 270: hydro 1.7 s -> 0.5 s, conducting IC 7.2 s -> 0.9 s, thermal
          1.3 s -> 0.6 s.
- [10-01] Mlam takes the parity of the profile a0 from its largest coefficient instead of its last
          nonzero one. This gives the same result when a0 has a definite parity, and is more robust
          when a tiny wrong-parity coefficient survives at the tail.
- [10-01] New Ylm_full_vec(lmax, m, theta, phi) computes Kore's seminormalized spherical harmonics
          for many theta at once, from the stable three-term recurrence in l (with the
          Condon-Shortley phase, as in scipy).
          It was needed because scipy 1.15's sph_harm returns NaN for every l >= 646, so the plotting
          tools crashed at Ek <= 1e-8. The recurrence works to at least l = 1500 and matches scipy
          to about 4e-14 where scipy works.
          Ylm_full now calls it, so plot_field.py, plot_poltor.py and shear_layer_profile.py all work
          at high lmax (plot_poltor also went from 12.3 s to 2.6 s).
          Ylm and Ylm_symm were removed: nothing used them, Ylm_symm could not run on Python 3, and
          sph_harm disappears in scipy 1.17.
- [10-01] Dlam builds its sparse matrix with dtype=float.
- [10-02] FIX: gamma_visc (the m = 0 part, for the mantle) and gamma_visc_icb used the coefficient
          4*pi/3. The viscous torque, the boundary integral of s*sigma_rphi, is
          (8*pi/3)*Ek*R^2*(R*t' - t). This was found with angrymom_z: at Ek = 1e-3 the fluid's
          lambda*L came out exactly 2.000 times the viscous torques, for every mode and every N.
- [10-02] FIX: gamma_magnetic_ic was missing a factor ricb^2, in the dipole line and both quadrupole
          lines. After the viscous fix, the remaining imbalance implied a factor of 0.12, against
          ricb^2 = 0.1225.
          Both fixes carry "# FIX (2026-10-02)" comments with the derivation and the check.
          They only enter the rotdyn rows, so matrices without rotdyn are byte-identical. The torques
          spin_doctor reports for m = 0 do change: viscous by x2, IC magnetic by x8 at ricb = 0.35.
          With the fixes, total angular momentum (fluid + mantle + IC) is conserved to 1e-4 at N = 48
          and 6e-5 at N = 72, improving with resolution. Before, it was off by 50%.
- [10-02] New load_npz_mmap(filename) memory-maps the arrays inside an .npz file, so solve_nopp.py
          can read each rank's rows without loading the whole matrix.

bin/utils4pp.py
---------------
- [10-06] The radial quadrature (setup_grid / cg_quad) uses Gauss-Legendre nodes and weights instead
          of Chebyshev-Gauss nodes with the integrand multiplied by sqrt(1-x^2). That product is not
          smooth at the ends, so the old quadrature was only second order. spin_doctor's resid0 (the
          Dkin + Dint = 0 identity) measured the quadrature, not the solution: in the torsional case
          at Ek = 1e-7 it fell as 1/N^2 (1.5e-2 at N = 200, 6.1e-4 at N = 976), and at fixed N = 200
          it fell the same way when only the number of quadrature points was raised. Part of resid u
          was quadrature error too.
          With Gauss-Legendre on the same N points (same cost) at N = 200: resid0 ~ 1e-11 and resid u
          2e-6 instead of 7e-5. resid b, residL, Tor/Pol, Mag/Kin and the torques are unchanged to
          the printed digits. Also checked for m = 1 and for a conducting IC.
          With rotdyn = 1, resid0 stays at ~5e-4. This is not a quadrature effect: the rotating
          mantle and IC do work through the boundary stresses, and spin_doctor sets that term (pss)
          to 0 for eigenvalue problems. With rotdyn = 0 the same case gives resid0 ~ 1e-10.
- [10-06] New kin_mag_energy(usol2, bsol2, Ra, Rb, ls=None) returns [KE, ME] of one solution
          with diagnose's definitions, but computes only the energies (no dissipation or power
          terms). solve_nopp.py uses it to normalise the eigenvectors. ls restricts the sum to some
          l values, so that the work can be split among MPI ranks.
- [10-01] New angrymom_z(u_sol2) returns the fluid's axial angular momentum,
          L_z = (8*pi/3) * integral of r^3 t_10 dr (with Kore's Y_1^0 = cos(theta)).
          It applies to m = 0, symm = 1 and returns 0 otherwise. Checked against an analytic profile:
          1.3944 against an exact 1.3937 at N = 40.
- [10-01] New setup_grid(Ra, Rb), and diagnose(..., pool=None) now accepts an existing process pool.
          This lets spin_doctor create one pool for all solutions instead of one per solution.
