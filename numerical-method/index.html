    Numerical method

    This page shows how kore turns the anelastic equations into the sparse matrices of an eigenvalue problem: the unknowns, the radial basis, the rows, the boundary conditions and the checks of a solution. The terms of a differential rotation are built in the same way (Differential rotation, §3 to §7).

    Unknowns

    At fixed \(m\) and equatorial symmetry, the velocity is written with a poloidal scalar \(P\) and a toroidal scalar \(T\),

    \[ \mathbf u = \frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla\times\nabla\times\left(\rho_0 P\thinspace\mathbf r\right) + \nabla\times\left(T\thinspace\mathbf r\right), \]

    so that \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf u) = 0\) holds exactly and the mass equation is never solved. For one harmonic \(Y = Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\), with \(P = P_l(r)\thinspace Y\), \(T = T_l(r)\thinspace Y\) and \(\mathcal L = l(l+1)\),

    \[ u_r = \frac{\mathcal L\thinspace P_l}{r}\thinspace Y, \qquad \mathbf u_h = \left(P_l' + \frac{P_l}{r} + \frac{d\ln\rho_0}{dr}P_l\right) r\nabla Y + T_l\thinspace\nabla Y\times\mathbf r , \]

    with a prime for \(d/dr\) (utils4pp.cheb2space_pol, cheb2space_tor). With thermal = 1 the third unknown is \(s = s_l(r)\thinspace Y\).

    Radial basis

    Each radial function \(P_l\), \(T_l\), \(s_l\) is expanded in Chebyshev polynomials: N of them on the shell \([\)ricb\(, 1]\), or, in a full sphere (ricb = 0), the N/2 polynomials on \([-1, 1]\) that have the parity of the field. A radial derivative of order \(k\) maps Chebyshev coefficients to coefficients in the Gegenbauer (ultraspherical) basis \(C^{(k)}\), where it is a sparse matrix, and so is the product with a profile expanded in Chebyshev polynomials. The rows of each section are written in the Gegenbauer basis of its highest derivative: \(C^{(4)}\) for u, \(C^{(2)}\) for v and h (\(C^{(0)}\) for h with ThermaD = 0, \(C^{(1)}\) if a thermal wind is included). This makes \(A\) and \(B\) sparse.

    Rows

    The pressure is removed by taking the radial components of the curl and of the double curl of the momentum equation; \(\nabla\Pi\) drops out of both because it is a gradient. Each term becomes a radial operator acting on \(P_l\), \(T_l\) or \(s_l\):

    section rows equation used
    u of \(P_l\) \(r^5\rho_0\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times(\text{momentum})/\mathcal L^3\)
    v of \(T_l\) \(r^3\rho_0\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times(\text{momentum})/\mathcal L^3\)
    h of \(s_l\) \(r^2\thinspace(\text{heat equation})/\mathcal L^3\)

    All rows are projected on \(Y_l^m\) and divided by \(\mathcal L^3\), with \(l\) the degree of the row. The powers of \(r\) are rpower_u (5) and rpower_v (3), the smallest that keep every operator a polynomial in \(r\). The factor \(\rho_0\) (rhopower_u = rhopower_v = 1) improves the accuracy of the viscous rows near a surface where \(\rho_0\) is small. Without viscosity (ViscosD = 0) the momentum rows are \(r^3\rho_0^2\) and \(r^2\rho_0\) times the equations. With ThermaD = 0 the heat rows are divided by \(p_0\), \(r\thinspace(\partial_t s + u_r\thinspace dS/dr)/\mathcal L^3\), which is better conditioned where \(p_0\) is small (and \(r^2/p_0\) times the equation with the thermal wind). The Coriolis force couples \(l\) to \(l\pm1\); the other terms keep \(l\).

    term function in bin/operators.py profiles
    \(\partial_t\mathbf u\) inertia \(\ln\rho_0\)
    \(2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\) coriolis \(\ln\rho_0\)
    \(\mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\mathsf S)\) viscous_diffusion \(\ln\rho_0\) (up to its 4th derivative), \(\nu\) (up to its 2nd)
    \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\) buoyancy \(g\)
    \(p_0\thinspace\partial_t s\) entropy \(p_0\)
    \(p_0\thinspace u_r\thinspace dS/dr\) thermal_advection \(p_0\thinspace dS/dr\) (\(dS/dr\) if ThermaD = 0)
    \(\mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s)\) thermal_diffusion \(\kappa p_0\)

    submatrices.py writes one sparse matrix per radial operator, for example u1lho2_D0 for \(r^4\rho_0\thinspace(\ln\rho_0)''\) times the unknown (the label grammar and the profile codes are in utils.decode_label and radial_profiles.proffdir). For the section h row with ThermaD > 0, for example, the diffusion term is

    \[ r^2\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s) = \left[r^2\kappa p_0\thinspace s_l'' + \left(2r\kappa p_0 + r^2(\kappa p_0)'\right)s_l' - \mathcal L\thinspace\kappa p_0\thinspace s_l\right] Y. \]

    Eigenproblem

    assemble.py puts the blocks into \(A\mathbf x = \lambda B\mathbf x\). \(B\) holds \(-\)inertia in the momentum rows and entropy in the heat rows. \(A\) holds coriolis \(-\) viscous_diffusion \(-\) buoyancy in the momentum rows and \(-\)thermal_advection \(+\) thermal_diffusion in the heat rows, so each row reads

    \[ \lambda\thinspace\mathbf u + 2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u = \mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\mathsf S) + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}, \]
    \[ \lambda\thinspace p_0 s = -p_0 u_r\frac{dS}{dr} + \mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s), \]

    projected as in the table above. solve.py finds the eigenvalues nearest a target \(\tau\) by shift-and-invert with SLEPc, factoring \(A - \tau B\) once with MUMPS (Solver). Forced runs solve \((A - i\thinspace\mathrm{wf}\thinspace B)\mathbf x = \mathbf b\), with wf the forcing frequency; for the random force of forcing 11, \(\mathbf b\) is derived on Reynolds-stress forcing.

    Boundary conditions

    The conditions (assemble.bc_u_spherical, bc_theta_spherical) are applied at the outer wall (bco) and at the inner one (bci, when ricb > 0); the full sphere has no condition at the centre:

    setting condition rows
    bco = 0 stress-free \(u_r = 0\), and zero tangential stress \(\partial_r(u_\theta/r) = \partial_r(u_\phi/r) = 0\) \(P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace r^2P_l'' + r^2(\ln\rho_0)'P_l' + \left[r^2(\ln\rho_0)'' + \mathcal L - 2 - r(\ln\rho_0)'\right]P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace rT_l' - T_l = 0\)
    bco = 0 with a drag (kappa_cmb, topo_drag) \(u_r = 0\), and the tangential stress balances the drag of the wall on the slip the stress-free rows plus the terms of the drag (Drag at the outer wall equations)
    bco 0 or 2 where the background field ends in a current sheet the tangential stress balances the push of the sheet on the fluid the stress-free rows plus the terms of the push (Magnetic field equations)
    bco = 1 no-slip \(\mathbf u = 0\) \(P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace P_l' + \left[(\ln\rho_0)' + 1/r\right]P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace T_l = 0\)
    bco_thermal = 0 fixed entropy \(s_l = 0\)
    bco_thermal = 1 zero diffusive entropy flux \(s_l' = 0\)

    The density enters the stress-free and no-slip rows through \(u_\theta\) and \(u_\phi\). The thermal conditions are used only when ThermaD > 0. Without viscosity only \(P_l = 0\) is imposed. bco = 2 (and bci = 2) replaces \(u_r = 0\) by a zero viscous radial stress, \(\left[r(\ln\rho_0)' - 3\right]P_l + 3rP_l' = 0\), which does not contain the pressure.

    Regularity at the centre

    The centre of a full sphere is not a boundary, but the fields must be smooth there, and kore imposes no condition to make them so. This section says why none is needed, when a solution is still not smooth at the centre, and how to check it.

    A field that is smooth at the centre has radial functions that go as \(r^l\) there, for a component of degree \(l\), times a series in \(r^2\). The basis gives \(P_l\), \(T_l\), \(s_l\) (and the \(f_l\), \(g_l\) of a magnetic field) the parity of \(l\), and the equations exclude the lower powers of that parity, as long as the background is smooth at the centre too. Each background profile follows the same rule with its own degree \(n\): the \(f_n\) of a differential rotation, the \(h_n\), \(k_n\) of a magnetic field and the \(\sigma_n\) of a thermal wind go as \(r^n\), \(dS/dr\) and \(g\) as \(r\), and \(\ln\rho_0\), \(p_0\), \(\nu\), \(\kappa\) are series in \(r^2\). kore evaluates every profile at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity of its degree, which gives the parity but neither the power nor the smoothness. A profile can fail in two ways:

    • It lacks the power. The \(P_2\) term of 'Y20' and 'conical', and the entropy of thermal_wind 3, keep their value at the centre. The modes converge in N, but to solutions that are not smooth there either.
    • It has a kink or a jump. Its even or odd continuation to \(r < 0\) is not smooth: 'Y20-wall-bounded', 'shellular' unless \(\sigma\) is even and positive, a 'user' expression in \(\lvert r\rvert\), a \(dS/dr\) that changes abruptly near the centre. The modes then converge slowly in N.

    With ricb = 0, submatrices.py checks every profile near the centre and prints a WARNING for each failure (background.centre_check).

    Spurious modes. Without viscosity (ViscosD = 0) or with ThermaD = 0, the spectrum of a full sphere also holds modes that do not converge when N or lmax is raised: without viscosity some of them grow, with ThermaD = 0 they crowd near zero frequency. Many of them sit at the centre, where they are not smooth; others sit at the wall. Target the frequency of the mode you want, and keep the modes that stay put in N and lmax.

    irreg₀. In a full sphere spin_doctor prints irreg₀ for each solution (column 28 of flow.dat): the largest term \(a_p r^p\) with \(p < l\) of the flow and of the field, at \(r = \pi/\mathrm N\) (about the spacing of the Chebyshev points at the centre), relative to their largest value (utils4pp.centre_irregularity). It is at round-off for a resolved smooth mode and near 1 for a spurious mode at the centre. Values in between come from a background that is not smooth at the centre, or from a mode that is not resolved there. After the table spin_doctor counts the solutions above \(10^{-6}\).

    Checks

    spin_doctor.py evaluates the budgets of Energy balance for each solution, from the fields and independently of the matrices. The radial integrals use Gauss–Legendre quadrature with \(3N/2\) nodes, the angular ones the orthogonality of the harmonics (\(\int\lvert Y_l\rvert^2\thinspace d\Omega = 4\pi/(2l+1)\)). The force of a differential rotation and the dissipation \(D_\nu\) are computed on an \((r, \cos\theta)\) grid, with Gauss–Legendre quadrature in \(\cos\theta\), exact for these polynomials. Each residual is relative to the largest of its terms:

    residual budget in spin_doctor's names
    residσ \(2\sigma K - W_\nu - W_b - W_\Omega - W_p = 0\) (and the Lorentz work) 2σ KE - Dkin - Wthm - Wdr - Wp
    residω its imaginary part, with the Coriolis force 2w KE + Wcor_im - Dkin_im - Wthm_im - Wdr_im - Wp_im, \(w = \mathrm{Im}\thinspace\lambda\)
    resid\(s\) \(2\sigma T - W_{\rm adv} + D_{\rm th} - W_{\Omega,\rm th} = 0\) 2σ TE - Dthm - Wadv - Wdr_thm (Dthm \(= -D_{\rm th}\))
    residD \(W_\nu - W_\nu^{\rm wall} + D_\nu = 0\) Dkin - (Wv_icb + Wv_cmb) + Dvisc
    resid𝐋 \(\lambda L\) = the torques on the fluid (Rotating solids equations) lam*angmom - (-vtorq - vtorq_ic - mtorq - mtorq_ic [+ i Gaspard angmom])

    residσ and resid\(s\) judge whether a solution is resolved (Solver gives the thresholds), also for forced runs, where \(\sigma = 0\). Where the walls drive the flow along themselves (the libration, forcing 7 with \(m\) = 0, and the steady rotation of the walls, forcing 12), \(W_\nu = W_\nu^{\rm wall} - D_\nu\) is a small difference of two large terms, so residσ, and resid𝐄 with a field, are relative to \(D_\nu\) too. A residual whose terms all vanish, such as residω of a steady flow with \(m\) = 0, is 0. residD compares two independent computations of the viscous terms: \(W_\nu\) from the viscous force of each degree (utils4pp.visforce), and \(D_\nu\) from the strain rate on the grid (utils4pp.viscous_dissipation). It is \(10^{-15}\) to \(10^{-12}\).

    The work at the walls (utils4pp.wall_powers). kore does not solve for the pressure. spin_doctor recovers it from the momentum equation: \(\nabla\Pi = \mathbf F - \lambda\mathbf u\), with \(\mathbf F\) every force but the pressure, and the consoidal component of a gradient is \(\Pi_l/r\), so that for each degree \(l\) of \(P\)

    \[ \Pi_l = r\left[\mathbf F - \lambda\mathbf u\right]_{s,l} , \]

    the consoidal component of the Coriolis, viscous and differential-rotation forces and of \(\lambda\mathbf u\) (utils4pp.pressure_force). The buoyancy is radial and does not enter. With \(q_l = \mathcal L P_l/r\) the radial velocity, \(s_l\) and \(t_l\) the consoidal and toroidal components of \(\mathbf u\),

    \[ W_p = -2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\sum_l \frac{4\pi}{2l+1}\Big[\rho_0\thinspace r^2\thinspace q_l^*\thinspace\Pi_l\Big]_{\rm inner}^{\rm outer}, \]

    and the stress on a sphere is

    \[ \boldsymbol\sigma\cdot\hat{\mathbf r} = \mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace\rho_0\nu\left[\left(2q_l' + \tfrac23(\ln\rho_0)'q_l\right)Y\hat{\mathbf r} + \left(s_l' - \frac{s_l}{r} + \frac{q_l}{r}\right)r\nabla Y + \left(t_l' - \frac{t_l}{r}\right)r\nabla Y\times\hat{\mathbf r}\right], \]

    dotted with \(\mathbf u^*\) and integrated over each wall for \(W_\nu^{\rm wall}\). flow.dat has these terms for each wall (Output files).

    \(\Pi_l\) needs the radial derivatives of \(P_l\) up to the third at the walls. That is where the truncation error of the tau method is largest, and near a surface of low density the \((\ln\rho_0)'\) terms of the viscous force amplify it. The wall pressure therefore converges with N more slowly than the volume integrals. For 'Wu 2005' (r_out 0.99) at Ek \(10^{-3}\), residσ of a forced run is \(5\times10^{-2}\) at N 96 and \(2\times10^{-10}\) at N 192 in a shell, and 0.36, \(6\times10^{-6}\) and \(10^{-8}\) at N 192, 288 and 384 in a full sphere, while the kinetic energy and the dissipation have converged at the lowest N. Where \(u_r = 0\) at the walls, \(W_p = 0\) whatever the resolution.

    ru_insane.py checks the same pairings on the operators themselves: inertia Hermitian and positive, Coriolis anti-Hermitian, viscous Hermitian and negative, the entropy and diffusion blocks, the buoyancy and the advection against quadratures, and the differential-rotation blocks against an \((r, \theta)\) grid. tests/tidal checks the budgets with moving walls, against an exact solution of the forced flow and on runs with a variable density, buoyancy and a differential rotation.
