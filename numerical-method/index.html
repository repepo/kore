    Numerical method

    This page shows how kore turns the anelastic equations into the sparse matrices of an eigenvalue problem: the unknowns, the radial basis, the rows, the boundary conditions and the checks of a solution. The terms of a differential rotation are built in the same way (Differential rotation, §3 to §7).

    Unknowns

    At fixed \(m\) and equatorial symmetry, the velocity is written with a poloidal scalar \(P\) and a toroidal scalar \(T\),

    \[ \mathbf u = \frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla\times\nabla\times\left(\rho_0 P\thinspace\mathbf r\right) + \nabla\times\left(T\thinspace\mathbf r\right), \]

    so that \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf u) = 0\) holds exactly and the mass equation is never solved. For one harmonic \(Y = Y_l^m(\theta,\phi)\), with \(P = P_l(r)\thinspace Y\), \(T = T_l(r)\thinspace Y\) and \(\mathcal L = l(l+1)\),

    \[ u_r = \frac{\mathcal L\thinspace P_l}{r}\thinspace Y, \qquad \mathbf u_h = \left(P_l' + \frac{P_l}{r} + \frac{d\ln\rho_0}{dr}P_l\right) r\nabla Y + T_l\thinspace\nabla Y\times\mathbf r , \]

    with a prime for \(d/dr\) (utils4pp.cheb2space_pol, cheb2space_tor). With thermal = 1 the third unknown is \(s = s_l(r)\thinspace Y\).

    Radial basis

    Each radial function \(P_l\), \(T_l\), \(s_l\) is expanded in Chebyshev polynomials: N of them on the shell \([\)ricb\(, 1]\), or, in a full sphere (ricb = 0), the N/2 polynomials on \([-1, 1]\) that have the parity of the field. A radial derivative of order \(k\) maps Chebyshev coefficients to coefficients in the Gegenbauer (ultraspherical) basis \(C^{(k)}\), where it is a sparse matrix, and so is the product with a profile expanded in Chebyshev polynomials. The rows of each section are written in the Gegenbauer basis of its highest derivative: \(C^{(4)}\) for u, \(C^{(2)}\) for v and h (\(C^{(0)}\) for h with ThermaD = 0, \(C^{(1)}\) if a thermal wind is included). This makes \(A\) and \(B\) sparse.

    Rows

    The pressure is removed by taking the radial components of the curl and of the double curl of the momentum equation; \(\nabla\Pi\) drops out of both because it is a gradient. Each term becomes a radial operator acting on \(P_l\), \(T_l\) or \(s_l\):

    section rows equation used
    u of \(P_l\) \(r^5\rho_0\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times(\text{momentum})/\mathcal L^3\)
    v of \(T_l\) \(r^3\rho_0\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times(\text{momentum})/\mathcal L^3\)
    h of \(s_l\) \(r^2\thinspace(\text{heat equation})/\mathcal L^3\)

    All rows are projected on \(Y_l^m\) and divided by \(\mathcal L^3\), with \(l\) the degree of the row. The powers of \(r\) are rpower_u (5) and rpower_v (3), the smallest that keep every operator a polynomial in \(r\). The factor \(\rho_0\) (rhopower_u = rhopower_v = 1) improves the accuracy of the viscous rows near a surface where \(\rho_0\) is small. Without viscosity (ViscosD = 0) the momentum rows are \(r^3\rho_0^2\) and \(r^2\rho_0\) times the equations. With ThermaD = 0 the heat rows are divided by \(p_0\), \(r\thinspace(\partial_t s + u_r\thinspace dS/dr)/\mathcal L^3\), which is better conditioned where \(p_0\) is small (and \(r^2/p_0\) times the equation with the thermal wind). The Coriolis force couples \(l\) to \(l\pm1\); the other terms keep \(l\).

    term function in bin/operators.py profiles
    \(\partial_t\mathbf u\) inertia \(\ln\rho_0\)
    \(2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u\) coriolis \(\ln\rho_0\)
    \(\mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\mathsf S)\) viscous_diffusion \(\ln\rho_0\) (up to its 4th derivative), \(\nu\) (up to its 2nd)
    \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}\) buoyancy \(g\)
    \(p_0\thinspace\partial_t s\) entropy \(p_0\)
    \(p_0\thinspace u_r\thinspace dS/dr\) thermal_advection \(p_0\thinspace dS/dr\) (\(dS/dr\) if ThermaD = 0)
    \(\mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s)\) thermal_diffusion \(\kappa p_0\)

    submatrices.py writes one sparse matrix per radial operator, for example u1lho2_D0 for \(r^4\rho_0\thinspace(\ln\rho_0)''\) times the unknown (the label grammar and the profile codes are in utils.decode_label and radial_profiles.proffdir). For the section h row with ThermaD > 0, for example, the diffusion term is

    \[ r^2\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s) = \left[r^2\kappa p_0\thinspace s_l'' + \left(2r\kappa p_0 + r^2(\kappa p_0)'\right)s_l' - \mathcal L\thinspace\kappa p_0\thinspace s_l\right] Y. \]

    Eigenproblem

    assemble.py puts the blocks into \(A\mathbf x = \lambda B\mathbf x\). \(B\) holds \(-\)inertia in the momentum rows and entropy in the heat rows. \(A\) holds coriolis \(-\) viscous_diffusion \(-\) buoyancy in the momentum rows and \(-\)thermal_advection \(+\) thermal_diffusion in the heat rows, so each row reads

    \[ \lambda\thinspace\mathbf u + 2\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u = \mathrm{ViscosD}\thinspace\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\nu\mathsf S) + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace\hat{\mathbf r}, \]
    \[ \lambda\thinspace p_0 s = -p_0 u_r\frac{dS}{dr} + \mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace\nabla\cdot(\kappa p_0\nabla s), \]

    projected as in the table above. solve.py finds the eigenvalues nearest a target \(\tau\) by shift-and-invert with SLEPc, factoring \(A - \tau B\) once with MUMPS (Solver). Forced runs solve \((A - i\thinspace\mathrm{wf}\thinspace B)\mathbf x = \mathbf b\), with wf the forcing frequency; for the random force of forcing 11, \(\mathbf b\) is derived on Reynolds-stress forcing.

    Boundary conditions

    The conditions (assemble.bc_u_spherical, bc_theta_spherical) are applied at the outer wall (bco) and at the inner one (bci, when ricb > 0); the full sphere has no condition at the centre:

    setting condition rows
    bco = 0 stress-free \(u_r = 0\), and zero tangential stress \(\partial_r(u_\theta/r) = \partial_r(u_\phi/r) = 0\) \(P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace r^2P_l'' + r^2(\ln\rho_0)'P_l' + \left[r^2(\ln\rho_0)'' + \mathcal L - 2 - r(\ln\rho_0)'\right]P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace rT_l' - T_l = 0\)
    bco = 1 no-slip \(\mathbf u = 0\) \(P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace P_l' + \left[(\ln\rho_0)' + 1/r\right]P_l = 0\), \(\thinspace T_l = 0\)
    bco_thermal = 0 fixed entropy \(s_l = 0\)
    bco_thermal = 1 zero diffusive entropy flux \(s_l' = 0\)

    The density enters the stress-free and no-slip rows through \(u_\theta\) and \(u_\phi\). The thermal conditions are used only when ThermaD > 0. Without viscosity only \(P_l = 0\) is imposed. bco = 2 (and bci = 2) replaces \(u_r = 0\) by a zero viscous radial stress, \(\left[r(\ln\rho_0)' - 3\right]P_l + 3rP_l' = 0\), which does not contain the pressure.

    Checks

    spin_doctor.py evaluates the energy balance of each solution, built from the fields independently of the matrices: with \(K = \int\rho_0\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^2\thinspace dV\),

    \[ \sigma K = -\frac{\mathrm{ViscosD}}{2}\int\rho_0\nu\thinspace\lvert\mathsf S\rvert^2\thinspace dV + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace g\thinspace u_r^* s\thinspace dV , \]

    and the entropy balance, the heat equation times \(s^*\) integrated:

    \[ \sigma\int p_0\lvert s\rvert^2\thinspace dV = -\mathrm{Re}\int p_0\frac{dS}{dr}u_r s^*\thinspace dV - \mathrm{ThermaD}\int\kappa p_0\lvert\nabla s\rvert^2\thinspace dV . \]
