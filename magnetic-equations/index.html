    Magnetic field equations

    This page gives the equations kore solves with magnetic = 1: the induction equation and the Lorentz force in the anelastic setting, the background fields \(\mathbf B_0\) and their normalisation, the rows and boundary conditions, the coupling with a differential rotation (the Ferraro constructions and the wound field), the energy budgets, and the checks. How to set up a run is on Magnetic field. How the rows were derived and fitted is on Derivation tools, part A.

    The operators, the background fields and the boundary conditions were ported from the conductive_IC branch (commit 39487bbf), formula by formula, into this branch's conventions: on 2026-10-28 insulating walls (step 1), a conducting inner core (step 2), any density profile (step 3), a toroidal background field (step 4), the full sphere, inviscid runs and the poloidal fields of degree 2 (step 5); on 2026-10-30 the induction by a differential rotation, a confined field, fields of any degrees and the Ferraro field, with couplings built from the recurrence algebra.

    Equations

    The perturbation field is written as

    \[ \mathbf b = \nabla\times\nabla\times(f\,\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(g\,\mathbf r), \]

    with no \(\rho_0\): it is solenoidal by itself. In units of the time scale \(\tau\) (timescale),

    \[ \frac{\partial \mathbf b}{\partial t} = \nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0) + \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla^2\mathbf b, \]
    \[ \mathbf F_L = \frac{\mathrm{Hendrik}}{\rho_0}\left[(\nabla\times\mathbf b)\times\mathbf B_0 + (\nabla\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf b\right], \]

    with \(\mathbf F_L\) the Lorentz force per unit mass in the momentum equation (Anelastic equations). The factors are

    factor definition set_scales()
    Hendrik \((\tau B_0/r_0)^2/(\rho_0\mu_0)\) \((\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Le})^2\)
    MagnetD \(\eta_0\tau/r_0^2\) \(\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Em}\)

    with the Lehnert number and the magnetic Ekman number, both defined with the rotation rate as Ek is,

    \[ \mathrm{Le} = \frac{B_0}{\Omega r_0\sqrt{\rho_0\mu_0}}, \qquad \mathrm{Em} = \frac{\eta_0}{\Omega r_0^2} = \frac{\mathrm{Ek}}{Pm}, \qquad \Lambda = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\mathrm{Em}} \]

    (\(\Lambda\) the Elsasser number, \(Pm\) the magnetic Prandtl number). Besides 'rotation' and 'viscous', set_scales() knows the time scales 'Alfven' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Le}\), so Hendrik \(= 1\)) and 'magnetic' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Em}\), so MagnetD \(= 1\)). Le and Em must be set before set_scales() is called.

    The magnetic diffusivity \(\eta\) is uniform (background.diffusivity(..., 'eta') returns 1; the operators already have the \(\eta'\) terms).

    Equatorial symmetry. With an equatorially antisymmetric \(\mathbf B_0\) the field \(\mathbf b\) has the symmetry opposite to \(\mathbf u\) (ut.bsymm = symm * symmB0): \(f\) takes the degrees of the toroidal flow and \(g\) those of the poloidal flow. A poloidal field of degree \(n\) has the symmetry \((-1)^n\), a toroidal one \((-1)^{n+1}\). A degree-1 toroidal field is symmetric (\(B_\phi\propto\sin\theta\)), so \(f\) then has the degrees of the poloidal flow; a degree-2 one is antisymmetric, like the dipole. A poloidal and a toroidal part can be combined only if they have the same symmetry.

    Background fields

    Poloidal fields

    \[ \mathbf B_0 = c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h(r)\,Y_l^0\,\mathbf r), \]

    axisymmetric, with \(Y_l^0\) the Schmidt harmonic \(P_l(\cos\theta)\) (bin/background_field.py). Then

    \[ B_{0r} = c_\mathrm{norm}\,\frac{l(l+1)\,h}{r}\,Y_l^0, \qquad \nabla\times\mathbf B_0 = c_\mathrm{norm}(D_l h)\,\partial_\theta Y_l^0\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}, \qquad D_l h = h'' + \frac{2h'}{r} - \frac{l(l+1)h}{r^2} . \]
    B0 \(h(r)\) degree, symmetry
    'axial' \(r/2\): the uniform field \(c_\mathrm{norm}\hat{\mathbf z}\), no current \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'FDM' the poloidal free decay mode (equation below) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 (antisymmetric) or 2 (symmetric)
    'G21 dipole' \(r/6 - r^3/10\) (Gerick et al. 2021) \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S1' \(5r - 3r^3\) (Luo & Jackson 2022): 30 times the \(h\) of 'G21 dipole', so the same field with cnorm 'mag_energy', 'Schmitt2012' or 'rms_cmb', and 30 times stronger with a numerical cnorm \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S2' \(157r^2 - 296r^4 + 143r^6\) (Luo & Jackson 2022) \(l = 2\), symmetric
    'confined' \(r^l(r_2^2 - r^2)^p\) (\(r_1 = 0\)) or \(r^l(r - r_1)^p(r_2 - r)^p\) in \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\), zero outside (\(r_1\), \(r_2\) = B0_conf_r1, B0_conf_r2, \(p\) = B0_conf_p, default 12) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 or 2
    'general' \(\sum_n h_n(r)\,Y_n^0\), B0_profiles = {n: 'numpy expression of r, p'} (\(p\) = B0_param) any degrees of one parity
    'ferraro' the field whose flux function is \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\) (below) odd degrees up to B0_ferraro_nmax, antisymmetric

    Free decay mode. In a shell (Zhang & Fearn 1995), with \(\beta\) the root of findbeta near beta, and in a full sphere (Gubbins & Roberts 1987),

    \[ h = j_l(\beta r)\,y_{l-1}(\beta) - j_{l-1}(\beta)\,y_l(\beta r) \quad (r_i > 0), \qquad h = j_l(\beta r) \ \text{with}\ j_{l-1}(\beta) = 0 \quad (r_i = 0). \]

    The FDM, 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' match a potential field at the outer wall (\(rh' + (l+1)h = 0\) at \(r = 1\)); the FDM matches one at both walls, so it suits insulating walls. 'axial' is the uniform field, potential everywhere. 'confined' is zero at the wall. 'general' and 'ferraro' need not match: where they do not, the field meets the insulating mantle with a surface current, which the linear problem allows.

    Full-sphere fields with an inner core. 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are full-sphere fields. With an inner core, B0_ic = 'potential' (default) adds \(\beta_i r^{-(l+1)}\), \(\beta_i = r_i^{l+1}(r_i h' - l h)/(2l+1)\) at \(r_i\), so that \(rh' - lh = 0\) at the inner boundary: the field of the currents in the fluid, continued as a potential field into the core. 'full sphere' keeps \(h\) as it is (conductive_IC's B0_beta_ic). B0_ic has no effect on the other types or with ricb = 0.

    Confined field. 'confined' is zero outside \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\), and \(h\) and its first \(p - 1\) derivatives vanish at the edges, so there are no surface currents (\(B_r \propto h\) and \(B_\theta \propto h' + h/r\) are continuous for \(p \ge 2\); the volume current, which involves \(h''\), is continuous for \(p \ge 3\), the smallest value accepted, because the rows of the momentum equation hold \(h'''\), which would carry a delta at the edges for \(p = 2\); \(p = 4\) makes \(h'''\) continuous). In a full sphere with \(r_1 > 0\), \(h\) is evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity \((-1)^l\). Its purpose: a poloidal field inside a region where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform is a steady background with the differential rotation (Ferraro, below). With an inner core the region must lie in the fluid (\(r_1 \ge\) ricb). The edges are smooth only to order \(p\), so the Chebyshev series converge algebraically (coefficients \(\sim k^{-(p+1)}\)): with \(p = 12\) the budgets close to \(10^{-12}\) at N 96–128 in a shell, but a region that reaches the centre of a full sphere needs N 192 (degree 1) to 384 (degree 2) for \(10^{-9}\). \(h\) is evaluated in factored form: expanded into monomials, a polynomial of degree \(2p + l\) loses all its digits to cancellation near the edges.

    Any degrees. 'general' takes \(h_n\) degree by degree as expressions. Their derivatives come from Chebyshev interpolants (degree max(64, 2N)); in a full sphere they are evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity \((-1)^n\), and \(h_n\) should go as \(r^n\) at the centre. The degrees must have one parity (one equatorial symmetry); the lowest sets it. cnorm = 'mag_energy' sums \(\int B_0^2 dV\) over the degrees. The couplings come from the recurrence algebra (Couplings for any background).

    Toroidal fields

    \[ \mathbf B_0^{tor} = c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k(r)\,Y_n^0\,\mathbf r), \]

    purely azimuthal, with \(c_\mathrm{tor}\) = cnorm_tor and \(n\) = B0_tor_l:

    \[ B_\phi = -c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\,\partial_\theta Y_n^0 = \begin{cases} c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta, & n = 1,\\ 3c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta\cos\theta, & n = 2. \end{cases} \]
    B0_tor \(k(r)\)
    'Malkus' \(r\) for \(n = 1\), \(B_\phi = c_\mathrm{tor}\,s\) (Malkus 1967), with a uniform axial current \(2c_\mathrm{tor}\)
    'wall' \(r(r - r_i)(1 - r)/(1 - r_i)^2\); with ricb = 0, \(r^n(1 - r^2)\) zero at both walls
    'user' B0_tor_profile, a numpy expression of \(r\) and \(p\) = B0_tor_param its derivatives from a Chebyshev interpolant (degree max(64, 2N)); written to params.dat after #
    'general' \(\sum_n k_n Y_n^0\), B0_tor_profiles = {n: 'numpy expression of r, p'}, with \(p\) = B0_tor_param any degrees of one parity; derivatives as for the poloidal 'general'

    B0 = None gives a toroidal field alone. A toroidal field that does not vanish at a wall ('Malkus') carries a surface current there. The linear problem is unaffected: the walls' conditions on \(\mathbf b\) do not involve \(\mathbf B_0\), and \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\) on a no-slip wall. Inside a conducting inner core \(\mathbf B_0\) does not enter: there is no flow there, and \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\) at the no-slip inner boundary, so the matching conditions do not involve it either.

    Normalisation

    cnorm is a number, or computed as in conductive_IC (background_field.B0_norm, Gauss–Legendre quadrature with 240 nodes):

    • 'rms_cmb': the rms of the radial field over the outer wall is 1, \(c_\mathrm{norm} = \sqrt{2l+1}/(L\,h(1))\), \(L = l(l+1)\). Single-degree types only: 'confined', 'general' and 'ferraro' stop with a message ('confined' is zero at the wall).
    • 'mag_energy': \(\int B_0^2\,dV = 1\) over the fluid (Luo & Jackson 2022); 'Schmitt2012': \(\int B_0^2\,dV = 4\). For one degree,
    \[ \int B_0^2\,dV = \frac{4\pi L}{2l+1}\int\left[(L + 1)h^2 + 2rhh' + r^2h'^2\right]dr , \]

    summed over the degrees for 'general' and 'ferraro' (they are orthogonal), and taken over the region only for 'confined'.

    cnorm_tor is a number, 'mag_energy' or 'Schmitt2012', with \(\int B_0^2\,dV = n_l L\int k^2 r^2\,dr\), \(n_l = 4\pi/(2n+1)\), \(L = n(n+1)\) (summed over the degrees for 'general').

    Variable density

    With the flow written as

    \[ \mathbf u = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P\,\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T\,\mathbf r), \]

    its consoidal part is \(P' + P/r + \lambda_1 P\), with \(\lambda_1 = (\ln\rho_0)'\), \(\lambda_2 = (\ln\rho_0)''\), and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u = -\lambda_1 u_r\). The rows were derived from the definitions with tests/magnetic/derive.py (sympy, exact projection on the harmonics). For \(\mathbf B_0\) of degree 1:

    • rows f, columns P: every \(d/dr\) on \(P\) becomes \(d/dr + \lambda_1\) (label f1bpo0lho1_D0);

    • rows g, columns P, diagonal in \(l\) only (in the scale of conductive_IC's rows; zero for m = 0), from \(-\mathbf B_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\) and the \(\lambda_1 P\) above:

      \[ 2im\left[r h\lambda_1 P' + r h\lambda_2 P + \left(1 - \tfrac{L}{2}\right) r h'\lambda_1 P - \tfrac{L}{2}\,h\lambda_1 P\right]; \]

    • rows u, with \(\mathbf G\) the Lorentz force per volume:

      \[ \rho_0\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0^{-1}\mathbf G) = \hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf G - \frac{\lambda_1}{r}\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf G_H . \]

      The extra term, \(\lambda_1 r^4\,\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf G_H\) projected on \(Y_l\), is, for the toroidal field of the same degree and for the poloidal field of degree \(l_c = l\pm1\) (operators.lorentz_rho),

      \[ -im\lambda_1\left[(L+2)r^2hg + Lr^3h'g + 2r^3hg'\right], \qquad \frac{y}{2}\lambda_1 r^3\left[2a\,h\,D_{l_c}f + L_c b\,(D_1 h) f\right], \]

      with \(D_l\) as above and

      \[ y = \langle Y_l\,\vert\,\cos\theta\,Y_{l_c}\rangle, \qquad a = 2 - L_c - L, \qquad b = L_c - 2 - L ; \]

    • rows v, the T columns, the diffusion, the walls and the inner core: no \(\rho_0\) terms.

    The new labels pair bpo with lho (two profiles, plus ohr1 in the rows u). They vanish exactly for 'Boussinesq', whose matrices are those of steps 1 and 2 bitwise. tol_B0 does not cut these products (they converge slowly near the surface). submatrices.py counts odd profiles in both slots of a label for the parity at ricb = 0 (bitwise the same for every earlier label).

    Rows and implementation

    • Sections. f (rows \(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\) of the induction equation) and g (\(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\)), both in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis with two boundary-condition rows per block, are placed after the entropy section: ut.offset = {'u': 0, 'v': n, 'h': 2n, 'f': (2+thermal) n, 'g': (3+thermal) n} and sizmat = 2n + n*thermal + 2n*magnetic + 2*nic (nic for a conducting inner core, below). Non-magnetic matrices are unchanged.
    • Labels. decode_label gives sections f and g the row power \(2 - X\) and no \(\rho_0\). The profile codes are bpo (\(c_\mathrm{norm} h^{(k)}\)), bto (\(c_\mathrm{tor} k^{(j)}\)) and eta (\(\eta\)). The Lorentz labels carry ohr1 (one power of \(\rho_0\) less), so the momentum rows hold \(r^p\) times the force \(\rho_0 \mathbf F_L\). submatrices.py lists each label next to its conductive_IC name. tol_B0 (default 1e-8) is the relative cut of the Chebyshev series of \(r^p h^{(k)}\): it sets the band width of the magnetic blocks.
    • Operators (operators.lorentz, induction, magnetic_diffusion, b): the conductive_IC operators divided by \(L^3\) of the row, as every operator of this branch, with Hendrik and MagnetD for \((\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Le})^2\) and \(\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Em}\). The sign convention is the branch's: A holds the right-hand side, B is \(-\mathbf b\) in the induction rows (as \(-\)inertia in the momentum rows).
    • Toroidal field (step 4, operators.induction_tor, lorentz_tor). The rows were derived with derive.py (any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\): every coefficient is \(im\) (same degree) or the Coriolis factors \(C_- = \sqrt{l^2-m^2}/(2l-1)\), \(C_+ = \sqrt{(l+1)^2-m^2}/(2l+3)\) (columns \(l\mp1\)) times a polynomial in \(l\) (independent of \(m\)). Couplings: rows f from P at the same degree; rows g from P at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\) terms) and from T at \(l\); rows u from F at \(l\) (with \(\lambda_1\)) and G at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\)); rows v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\). Degree 2 (induction_tor2, lorentz_tor2): the quadrupole angular factors (operators.quad_factors, the same-degree terms with \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)) times polynomials in \(l\), couplings up to \(\Delta l = \pm2\): rows f from P at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); u from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); v from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\). operators.lorentz and induction add the poloidal and the toroidal parts (assemble.py loops over \(\Delta l = -2..2\) and skips absent couplings). Column offsets: operators.moff.
    • Poloidal field of degree 2 (step 5, operators.induction_pol2, lorentz_pol2, for 'Luo_S2' and 'FDM' with B0_l = 2). Derived with derive.py (\(b_0\) = ('pol', 2), any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\), \(m\) as for the toroidal field of degree 2: quad_factors times polynomials in \(l\) (the same-degree terms carry \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)), kept in two tables (row, column, \(\Delta l\)) of the label expressions. Couplings: rows f from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\pm1\) and T at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); u from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\); v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\). Two labels are added to those of the dipole (u2bpo2ohr1_D1, u2bpo2ohr1_D0). spin_doctor takes its Lorentz and induction powers from magnetic_powers_grid for these fields (utils4pp.use_grid).
    • Full sphere (step 5, ricb = 0). As for the flow, each block holds the Chebyshev polynomials of one parity on \([-1, 1]\) (ut.remroco): \(f\) has the vector parity vF = symmB0 * vP, \(g\) the opposite; \(h\) has the parity \((-1)^l\) of its degree and \(k\) that of \((-1)^n\) (utils.odd_profiles, both slots of a label). One boundary-condition row per block, the insulating condition at the outer wall (assemble.bc_b_spherical). The fields are regular at the centre: FDM \(j_l(\beta r)\) (analytic derivatives, background_field.jl_k), the polynomial fields, 'wall' \(= r^n(1 - r^2)\), 'Malkus' for \(n = 1\) only (\(k = r\) is not regular for \(n = 2\)), 'user' as \(f(\lvert r\rvert)\,\mathrm{sign}(r)^n\). cnorm and cnorm_tor are computed as with an inner core; spin_doctor's inner-wall terms are skipped.
    • Inviscid runs (step 5, ViscosD = 0, full sphere only). The Lorentz labels have \(D^3\) (rows u) and \(D^2\) (rows v), so the rows are in \(C^{(3)}\) (u) and \(C^{(2)}\) (v), with one boundary-condition row (\(u_r = 0\)) in u and none in v; without a field they stay in \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\). decode_label gives the u and v labels the row powers of the viscous rows. conductive_IC keeps \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\) and so converts these terms with the wrong matrices; its torsional test is barely affected (Checks).

    • Solver. solve.py writes real_magnetic.field, imag_magnetic.field and normalises the eigenvectors (over the shell, as conductive_IC) to

      \[ \mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{Hendrik}\int\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2dV = 1 . \]

    Couplings for any background

    bin/mag_algebra.py builds the couplings of the Lorentz force and of the induction for

    \[ \mathbf B_0 = \sum_n c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h_n Y_n^0\mathbf r) + \sum_n c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k_n Y_n^0\mathbf r), \]

    any degrees, from the recurrence algebra that the differential rotation uses, instead of the closed forms of operators.py (which exist for degrees 1 and 2 only). The background vectors are polynomials in \(x = \cos\theta\) times \(\hat{\mathbf r}\), \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\) or \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\); the products with the perturbation are sparse matrices on the degrees, times radial monomials; the rows take one radial derivative (product rule); each final monomial becomes a label with the profile codes hp + degree (\(c_\mathrm{norm}h_n\)) and kt + degree (\(c_\mathrm{tor}k_n\)). Derivation tools, part A.6, gives the method. mag_couplings = 'algebra' uses it for every type; 'closed' (default) keeps the closed forms, which 'general', 'ferraro' and B0_wound = 1 cannot use (they switch to the algebra by themselves). For the types that have closed forms both give the same eigenvalues to round-off.

    Boundary conditions

    Insulating walls (assemble.bc_b_spherical): \((l+1)f + rf' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the outer wall, \(lf - r f' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the inner one.

    Conducting inner core. innercore = 'conducting, Chebys' (eigenvalue problems, no-slip inner boundary bci = 1) adds the field inside the inner core to the unknowns, as conductive_IC does:

    • Unknowns. Sections fic, gic after f, g (ut.offset), with the degrees of f, g (up to lmax; conductive_IC's lmax_cic is not ported, as its ICB matching couples the degrees one by one). Each block is the Chebyshev series on \([-r_i, r_i]\) of one parity (that of the field at the centre), N_cic/2 coefficients per degree (ut.Nic, default N_cic = 64; conductive_IC found broken eigenvectors with 48 at sigma_i2o = \(10^{-2}\)); ut.nic per section.

    • Equation. With \(\mu_{i2o}\), \(\sigma_{i2o}\) (mu_i2o, sigma_i2o) the permeability and conductivity relative to the fluid,

      \[ \frac{\partial\mathbf b}{\partial t} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\eta_i\nabla^2\mathbf b, \qquad \eta_i = \frac{1}{\mu_{i2o}\sigma_{i2o}}, \]

      with rows \(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\) and \(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\) divided by \(L^3\) as in the fluid, in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis. Its matrices are built apart from the labels (submatrices.py: r0_D0f_ic, r2_D0f_ic, r1_D1f_ic, r2_D2f_ic and the same for g), with ut.Dlam and bc_variables.Tk on the inner core's domain (their optional domain arguments). - Matching at the ICB (assemble.bc_b_cic), one row in each section, the inner one of f, g and the only one of fic, gic: \(f_o = f_i\) (\([b_r]\)), \((g + rg')_o = \eta_i (g + rg')_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf E\)), \(\mu_{i2o}(f + rf')_o = (f + rf')_i\) and \(\mu_{i2o}\,g_o = g_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf H\)). The continuity of \(\eta\nabla^2_l f\) is not imposed: with \(b_r\) continuous it follows from the radial induction equation on both sides, since \(\mathbf u = 0\) at the no-slip ICB. - Output. real_magnetic_ic.field, imag_magnetic_ic.field. The eigenvectors are normalised with the fluid's energies only (the inner-core field is scaled along), as in conductive_IC.

    With a differential rotation

    With diff_rot = 1 the background flow

    \[ \mathbf U_0 = G\,\Delta\Omega\,r\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi} \]

    (\(G\) = Gaspard) also shears the field: the induction equation gains

    \[ \nabla\times(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b) = G\left[-im\,\Delta\Omega\,\mathbf b + \sin\theta\,(r\,\partial_r\Delta\Omega\,b_r + \partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\,b_\theta)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\right] \]

    (the \(\Omega\)-effect, and the Doppler shift of the field). It is built for any \(\Delta\Omega = \sum_n f_n P_n\) with the recurrence algebra of the momentum terms (diff_rot.induction_block; Derivation tools, part B): F→f and G→g couple at even \(\Delta l\) up to \(n\), F→g (a poloidal field sheared into a toroidal one) at odd \(\Delta l\) up to \(n + 1\). The Lorentz force needs nothing new: it does not involve \(\mathbf U_0\).

    Conducting inner core. It must co-rotate rigidly, \(\Delta\Omega(r_i, \theta) = c\) (\(f_n(r_i) = 0\) for \(n > 0\), diff_rot.rigid_ricb; else the run stops: a latitude-dependent rotation would shear a solid core). Its field then gains \(-imGc\,\mathbf b\). The matching conditions are unchanged, because only \(b_r\), which is continuous, enters the tangential part of \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\).

    The Ferraro condition

    For the background to be steady,

    \[ \nabla\times(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf B_0) = r\sin\theta\,(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi} \]

    must vanish: always for a toroidal \(\mathbf B_0\) (\(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\)), for a poloidal one only if \(\Delta\Omega\) is constant along its field lines (Ferraro's law of isorotation). The linear problem does not contain this term: it belongs to the background. background.ferraro_mismatch reports

    \[ \frac{\max\lvert\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert\,r}{\max\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert\,\max\lvert r\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert} \]

    over the fluid at the start of every run with diff_rot = 1 and a poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\), except with B0_wound = 1 (from the shapes \(f_n\), with a fourth-order difference in \(r\), and the poloidal profiles, on a grid). Above \(10^{-7}\) the parameter ferraro_policy decides: 'warn' (default: a warning, the background is assumed to be maintained) or 'refuse'. Consistent backgrounds: a toroidal field, a 'confined' field where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform, B0 = 'ferraro', diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', or any poloidal field with B0_wound = 1 (the winding balanced by diffusion; the run then reports that balance instead).

    The field from the rotation: B0 = 'ferraro'

    An axisymmetric poloidal field is \(\mathbf B_0 = \nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi\). With \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\) its field lines lie on the surfaces of constant rotation, so \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Omega = 0\) and the rotation and the field are a steady background together. In kore's form \(\psi = r\sum_n h_n(1 - x^2)P_n'(x)\), \(x = \cos\theta\), and the functions \((1 - x^2)P_n'\) are orthogonal with the weight \(1/(1 - x^2)\), so

    \[ h_n(r) = \frac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}\frac{1}{r}\int_{-1}^{1}\psi(r, x)\,P_n'(x)\,dx , \]

    computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature. \(\Delta\Omega\) has only even \(P_n\), so only odd \(n\) appear. \(G\) = B0_ferraro_G, a numpy expression of w = \(\Delta\Omega\) and p (B0_param); the derivatives of \(h_n\) come from Chebyshev interpolants of degree max(64, 2N). The assumptions, which background.ferraro_report prints at the start of a run (max \(\lvert\psi\rvert\) on the axis and at \(r = 1\), and the error of \(\psi\) rebuilt from the degrees):

    • \(\psi\) must vanish on the axis (it must be constant there for a regular field; for a rotation that varies along the axis, \(G\) has to vanish on the values \(\Delta\Omega\) takes there);
    • and at the outer wall if the field is to stay inside (otherwise it meets the insulating mantle with a surface current, which the linear problem allows);
    • the series is cut at B0_ferraro_nmax (odd, \(\ge 1\)), which sets the residual \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Omega\).

    A toroidal part (B0_tor, any type) can be added: it is consistent with any axisymmetric rotation, and it must have the field's equatorial symmetry (an even degree). Example: for a cylindrical rotation \(\Delta\Omega \propto s^2\), \(G(w) = w\) gives \(\psi \propto s^2\), the uniform axial field.

    The rotation from the field: diff_rot_type = 'ferraro'

    The reverse choice: the poloidal field is given (any type, 'general' included; e.g. a model of an observed or a dynamo field) and the rotation is deduced from it, \(\Delta\Omega = F(\psi)\), with \(\psi\) the field's own flux function. Since \(\Delta\Omega\) is constant on every field line, \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega = 0\) and the background is steady. Method: the flux function is built from the radial profiles of the field (background_field.pol_profiles),

    \[ \psi(r, x) = r\sum_n c\,h_n(1 - x^2)P_n'(x), \]

    \(F\) evaluated on a Gauss–Legendre grid in \(x\) and projected on the Legendre polynomials,

    \[ f_n(r) = \frac{2n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}F\big(\psi(r, x)\big)\,P_n(x)\,dx, \qquad n = 0, 2, \dots, \texttt{diff\_rot\_nmax}, \]

    and \(\Delta\Omega\) = diff_rot_ampl \(\cdot\sum_n f_n P_n\), as for the other rotation profiles: the force on the flow, the induction term and the thermal wind need nothing new (diff_rot._ferraro_shapes). \(F\) = diff_rot_F (default 'psi'), a numpy expression of psi and p (diff_rot_param). Rules, checked at the start of a run:

    • a poloidal B0 is needed (a toroidal field alone has no \(\psi\));
    • B0 = 'ferraro' is refused (each would be built from the other);
    • kore's \(\Delta\Omega\) has only even \(P_n\), so \(F(\psi)\) must be symmetric about the equator: any \(F\) for a dipolar field (\(\psi\) even in \(x\)), an even \(F\) (e.g. of psi**2) for a quadrupolar one (\(\psi\) odd), else the run stops;
    • with a conducting inner core \(\Delta\Omega(r_i, \theta)\) must be uniform, which in general means \(\psi\) constant on the inner core's surface;
    • diff_rot_nmax must be even.

    A toroidal part of B0 is left as it is. The series is cut at diff_rot_nmax: ferraro_mismatch then reports only the truncation, at round-off for fields of low degree with a polynomial \(F\). Example: the uniform axial field \(\psi = r^2\sin^2\theta/2\) with \(F(\psi) = \psi\) gives \(\Delta\Omega \propto s^2\), the cylindrical profile.

    The wound toroidal field: B0_wound = 1

    Motivation: in the solar tachocline both the rotation and a poloidal field are given, and they do not satisfy Ferraro's law. The shear winds the poloidal field into an azimuthal one, which grows until magnetic diffusion balances the winding. B0_wound = 1 adds that steady azimuthal field to \(\mathbf B_0\), so that the background is steady with both imposed.

    Method: the \(\phi\)-component of the steady induction equation for axisymmetric fields, with the background flow \(\mathbf U_0\) above (\(r\sin\theta = s\)) and a uniform diffusivity \(\eta\) = B0_wound_eta times MagnetD,

    \[ \eta\left(\nabla^2 - \frac{1}{s^2}\right)B_\phi = -G\,s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega , \]

    where \(\mathbf B_p\) is the poloidal B0 (any type, cnorm included). Written as kore's toroidal field, the operator acts degree by degree:

    \[ B_\phi = \sum_n k_n\sin\theta\,P_n'(\cos\theta), \qquad \left(\nabla^2 - \frac{1}{s^2}\right)B_\phi = \sum_n (D_n k_n)\,\sin\theta\,P_n', \qquad D_n k = k'' + \frac{2k'}{r} - \frac{n(n+1)k}{r^2} . \]

    The source is projected on \(\sin\theta\,P_n'\) by Gauss–Legendre quadrature in \(\cos\theta\), using

    \[ \int_{-1}^{1}(1 - x^2)P_n'P_m'\,dx = \delta_{nm}\,\frac{2n(n+1)}{2n+1}, \]

    which leaves one radial equation per degree, solved by Chebyshev collocation (background_field.wound_profiles). Boundary conditions:

    • \(k_n = 0\) at an insulating wall (\(B_\phi\) vanishes outside);
    • regular at \(r = 0\) in a full sphere (parity \((-1)^n\) on \([-1, 1]\));

    • with a conducting inner core, which must rotate rigidly: \(k = A r^n\) inside, and \(B_\phi/\mu\) and \(E_\theta\) continuous, which gives at \(r_i\) (\(\sigma\) = sigma_i2o)

      \[ r k_n' + \left(1 - \frac{n+1}{\sigma}\right)k_n = 0 . \]

    The degrees run up to (that of \(\mathbf B_p\)) + (that of \(\Delta\Omega\)) + 1, with the field's symmetry (even for a dipolar \(\mathbf B_p\), odd for a quadrupolar one). The \(k_n\) are added to any B0_tor, and the couplings come from mag_algebra.py as for 'general'.

    The size of the wound field is set by the physics, about \(G\,\Delta\Omega/\eta\) (times the length scale squared) relative to \(\mathbf B_p\). cnorm normalises the poloidal field alone, so the Lehnert or Elsasser number keeps its meaning for the field the user gives, and the wound part enters with its physical ratio. B0_wound_eta (default 1) is \(\eta_\mathrm{background}/\eta\): the diffusivity that balances the winding, in units of MagnetD, while the perturbation still diffuses with MagnetD. At the start of a run, instead of the Ferraro check, background.magnetic_diffrot prints the degrees and the residual of the steady balance on a grid off the collocation nodes,

    \[ \frac{\max\lvert\eta(\nabla^2 - 1/s^2)B_\phi + G s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert}{\max\lvert G s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert} . \]

    What is left out, so that the background is only approximately steady: the Ohmic decay of \(\mathbf B_p\) (the poloidal field is held fixed, as it is without rotation), and the Lorentz force of \(\mathbf B_0\) on the background flow (\(\Delta\Omega\) is imposed and not modified by the Lorentz force of \(\mathbf B_0\), which is then dominated by the wound \(B_\phi\), since it grows as \(G\,\Delta\Omega/\eta\)).

    When the ignored Lorentz force matters

    Orders of magnitude, in kore's units (time \(1/\Omega\), length the outer radius; timescale = 'rotation', so Gaspard = 1, Hendrik = \(\mathrm{Le}^2\), MagnetD = Em). cnorm normalises the poloidal field, so its maximum is of order 1. With \(\rho_0\) the background density (1 for 'Boussinesq') and \(\eta_w\) = B0_wound_eta, the Lorentz force per unit mass and the wound field are

    \[ \mathbf F_0 = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0}(\nabla\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf B_0, \qquad B_\phi \approx \frac{\Delta\Omega}{\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}\,B_p . \]

    The background's own force on \(\mathbf U_0\) has two parts.

    1. The azimuthal torque, \(\propto \mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla(s B_\phi)\): Ferraro's mechanism. It brakes \(\Delta\Omega\) along the field lines in a time of about \(1/(\Lambda_p\Omega)\), with \(\Lambda_p\) the Elsasser number of the poloidal field built on the background diffusivity. If viscous (or turbulent) stresses hold \(\Delta\Omega\), the torque compares with them through the Hartmann number \(Ha\) of the background values:

      \[ \Lambda_p = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}, \qquad Ha^2 = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w} \quad \left(\frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}} \text{ for } \rho_0 = \eta_w = 1\right). \]

    2. The meridional part, the hoop stress and magnetic pressure of \(B_\phi\), \(\propto B_\phi^2\): it changes the thermal-wind and entropy balance of the background and drives magnetic buoyancy. Relative to the background rotation it is \(\mathrm{Le}_\phi^2/\Delta\Omega\), with the Lehnert number of the wound field

      \[ \mathrm{Le}_\phi = \mathrm{Le}\,\frac{\max\lvert B_\phi\rvert}{\max\lvert\mathbf B_p\rvert} . \]
