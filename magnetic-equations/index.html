    Magnetic field equations

    This page gives the mathematics behind magnetic = 1, for readers who want to check the code or extend it: the equations, the formula of every background field, how the equations become matrix rows, the boundary conditions, what a differential rotation adds, the energy budgets, and the checks. How to set up a run is on Magnetic field; how the rows were derived and fitted is on Derivation tools, part A.

    Equations

    kore solves for a small perturbation \(\mathbf b\) of the imposed field \(\mathbf B_0\), together with the flow \(\mathbf u\). Two terms couple them: the flow induces \(\mathbf b\) by moving the field lines of \(\mathbf B_0\), and the Lorentz force of the two fields acts on the flow. Both terms are linear in the perturbation, because \(\mathbf B_0\) is fixed.

    In units of the time scale \(\tau\) (timescale), the induction equation and the Lorentz force per unit mass in the momentum equation (Anelastic equations) are

    \[ \frac{\partial \mathbf b}{\partial t} = \nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0) + \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla^2\mathbf b, \]
    \[ \mathbf F_L = \frac{\mathrm{Hendrik}}{\rho_0}\left[(\nabla\times\mathbf b)\times\mathbf B_0 + (\nabla\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf b\right]. \]

    The magnetic diffusivity \(\eta\) is uniform (background.diffusivity(..., 'eta') returns 1). The perturbation field is written with two scalars, as the flow is but without \(\rho_0\), since \(\mathbf b\) is solenoidal by itself:

    \[ \mathbf b = \nabla\times\nabla\times(f\,\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(g\,\mathbf r). \]

    Parameters. The factor Hendrik sets the strength of the Lorentz force and MagnetD that of the magnetic diffusion. set_scales() computes both from the Lehnert number and the magnetic Ekman number:

    factor definition set_scales()
    Hendrik \((\tau B_0/r_0)^2/(\rho_0\mu_0)\) \((\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Le})^2\)
    MagnetD \(\eta_0\tau/r_0^2\) \(\mathrm{Gaspard}\cdot\mathrm{Em}\)

    Both numbers are defined with the rotation rate, as Ek is:

    \[ \mathrm{Le} = \frac{B_0}{\Omega r_0\sqrt{\rho_0\mu_0}}, \qquad \mathrm{Em} = \frac{\eta_0}{\Omega r_0^2} = \frac{\mathrm{Ek}}{Pm}, \qquad \Lambda = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\mathrm{Em}} \]

    (\(\Lambda\) the Elsasser number, \(Pm\) the magnetic Prandtl number). Le and Em must be set before set_scales() is called. Besides 'rotation' and 'viscous', set_scales() knows two magnetic time scales: 'Alfven' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Le}\), so Hendrik \(= 1\)) and 'magnetic' (Gaspard \(= 1/\mathrm{Em}\), so MagnetD \(= 1\)).

    Equatorial symmetry. The symmetry of \(\mathbf B_0\) about the equator decides which degrees \(\mathbf b\) takes for a given flow. A poloidal field of degree \(n\) has the symmetry \((-1)^n\) and a toroidal one \((-1)^{n+1}\): the dipole is antisymmetric, a toroidal field of degree 1 (\(B_\phi\propto\sin\theta\)) is symmetric, one of degree 2 antisymmetric. \(\mathbf b\) has the symmetry of \(\mathbf u\) times that of \(\mathbf B_0\) (ut.bsymm = symm * symmB0): with an antisymmetric \(\mathbf B_0\), \(f\) takes the degrees of the toroidal flow and \(g\) those of the poloidal flow; with a symmetric one, \(f\) takes the degrees of the poloidal flow and \(g\) those of the toroidal flow. A poloidal and a toroidal part can be combined only if they have the same symmetry.

    Background fields

    The background field is steady and axisymmetric. It is the sum of a poloidal part (B0) and a toroidal part (B0_tor), each built from radial profiles times Legendre polynomials and scaled by cnorm or cnorm_tor. This section gives the profile of every option (bin/background_field.py) and how the amplitude is fixed.

    Poloidal fields

    Each poloidal option gives a radial profile \(h(r)\) of one degree \(l\) (several degrees for 'general' and 'ferraro'). The field follows from it by two curls,

    \[ \mathbf B_0 = c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h(r)\,Y_l^0\,\mathbf r), \]

    with \(Y_l^0\) the Schmidt harmonic \(P_l(\cos\theta)\). Its radial component and its current are

    \[ B_{0r} = c_\mathrm{norm}\,\frac{l(l+1)\,h}{r}\,Y_l^0, \qquad \nabla\times\mathbf B_0 = c_\mathrm{norm}(D_l h)\,\partial_\theta Y_l^0\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}, \qquad D_l h = h'' + \frac{2h'}{r} - \frac{l(l+1)h}{r^2} . \]
    B0 \(h(r)\) degree, symmetry
    'axial' \(r/2\): the uniform field \(c_\mathrm{norm}\hat{\mathbf z}\), no current \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'FDM' the poloidal free decay mode (equation below) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 (antisymmetric) or 2 (symmetric)
    'G21 dipole' \(r/6 - r^3/10\) (Gerick et al. 2021) \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S1' \(5r - 3r^3\) (Luo & Jackson 2022): 30 times the \(h\) of 'G21 dipole', so the same field with cnorm 'mag_energy', 'Schmitt2012' or 'rms_cmb', and 30 times stronger with a numerical cnorm \(l = 1\), antisymmetric
    'Luo_S2' \(157r^2 - 296r^4 + 143r^6\) (Luo & Jackson 2022) \(l = 2\), symmetric
    'confined' \(r^l(r_2^2 - r^2)^p\) (\(r_1 = 0\)) or \(r^l(r - r_1)^p(r_2 - r)^p\) in \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\), zero outside (\(r_1\), \(r_2\) = B0_conf_r1, B0_conf_r2, \(p\) = B0_conf_p, default 12) \(l\) = B0_l = 1 or 2
    'general' \(\sum_n h_n(r)\,Y_n^0\), B0_profiles = {n: 'numpy expression of r, p'} (\(p\) = B0_param) any degrees of one parity
    'ferraro' field lines on the surfaces of constant rotation, so that the rotation does not wind it up: the flux function is \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\) (below) odd degrees up to B0_ferraro_nmax, antisymmetric

    Free decay mode. 'FDM' is the field of degree \(l\) that decays most slowly when both walls are insulating. In a shell (Zhang & Fearn 1995) and in a full sphere (Gubbins & Roberts 1987) it is, with \(\beta\) the root that findbeta finds from the first guess beta (from the default, 3, the smallest root, that of the slowest mode, for degrees 1 and 2),

    \[ h = j_l(\beta r)\,y_{l-1}(\beta) - j_{l-1}(\beta)\,y_l(\beta r) \quad (r_i > 0), \qquad h = j_l(\beta r) \ \text{with}\ j_{l-1}(\beta) = 0 \quad (r_i = 0). \]

    At the outer wall. A field that does not match a potential field at the outer wall meets the insulating mantle with a surface current, which the linear problem allows. The FDM, 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' match one there (\(rh' + (l+1)h = 0\) at \(r = 1\)), and the FDM matches one at both walls, so it suits insulating walls. 'axial' is the uniform field, potential everywhere. 'confined' is zero at the wall. 'general' and 'ferraro' need not match.

    Full-sphere fields with an inner core. 'G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1' and 'Luo_S2' are defined for a full sphere, and B0_ic says how to continue them into an inner core. 'potential' (default) adds \(\beta_i r^{-(l+1)}\), \(\beta_i = r_i^{l+1}(r_i h' - l h)/(2l+1)\) at \(r_i\), so that \(rh' - lh = 0\) at the inner boundary: the field of the currents in the fluid, continued as a potential field into the core (conductive_IC's B0_beta_ic). 'frozen currents' (the name in conductive_IC; 'full sphere' before 2026-11-06) keeps \(h\) as it is, with the currents below \(r_i\) in the core. B0_ic has no effect on the other types or with ricb = 0.

    Confined field. 'confined' fills the layer \(r_1 \le r \le r_2\) and is zero outside it. Its purpose: a poloidal field in a layer where the rotation is uniform is not wound up, so it is a steady background with a differential rotation (The Ferraro condition). With an inner core the layer must lie in the fluid (\(r_1 \ge\) ricb). In a full sphere with \(r_1 > 0\), \(h\) is evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity \((-1)^l\).

    The field must vanish smoothly at the edges of the layer. \(h\) and its first \(p - 1\) derivatives vanish there, so there are no surface currents (\(B_r \propto h\) and \(B_\theta \propto h' + h/r\) are continuous for \(p \ge 2\)). The volume current, which involves \(h''\), is continuous for \(p \ge 3\), the smallest value accepted: the rows of the momentum equation hold \(h'''\), which would carry a delta at the edges for \(p = 2\). \(p = 4\) makes \(h'''\) continuous.

    The price of a field smooth only to order \(p\) is a slow convergence: its Chebyshev coefficients fall as \(k^{-(p+1)}\). With \(p = 12\) the budgets close to \(10^{-12}\) at N 96–128 in a shell, but a layer that reaches the centre of a full sphere needs N 192 (degree 1) to 384 (degree 2) for \(10^{-9}\). \(h\) is evaluated in factored form: expanded into monomials, a polynomial of degree \(2p + l\) loses all its digits to cancellation near the edges.

    Any degrees. 'general' takes one profile \(h_n\) per degree, given as expressions. Their derivatives come from Chebyshev interpolants (degree max(64, 2N)); in a full sphere they are evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity \((-1)^n\), and \(h_n\) should go as \(r^n\) at the centre (submatrices.py warns otherwise). The degrees must have one parity (one equatorial symmetry); the lowest sets it. cnorm = 'mag_energy' sums \(\int B_0^2\,dV\) over the degrees. The couplings come from the recurrence algebra (Couplings for any background).

    Toroidal fields

    A toroidal field is azimuthal: its field lines are circles around the axis. Each option gives a radial profile \(k(r)\) of degree \(n\) = B0_tor_l (several degrees for 'general'), scaled by \(c_\mathrm{tor}\) = cnorm_tor:

    \[ \mathbf B_0^{tor} = c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k(r)\,Y_n^0\,\mathbf r), \]
    \[ B_\phi = -c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\,\partial_\theta Y_n^0 = \begin{cases} c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta, & n = 1,\\ 3c_\mathrm{tor}\,k\sin\theta\cos\theta, & n = 2. \end{cases} \]
    B0_tor \(k(r)\)
    'Malkus' \(r\) for \(n = 1\), \(B_\phi = c_\mathrm{tor}\,s\) (Malkus 1967), with a uniform axial current \(2c_\mathrm{tor}\)
    'wall' \(r(r - r_i)(1 - r)/(1 - r_i)^2\); with ricb = 0, \(r^n(1 - r^2)\) zero at both walls
    'user' B0_tor_profile, a numpy expression of \(r\) and \(p\) = B0_tor_param its derivatives from a Chebyshev interpolant (degree max(64, 2N)); written to params.dat after #
    'general' \(\sum_n k_n Y_n^0\), B0_tor_profiles = {n: 'numpy expression of r, p'}, with \(p\) = B0_tor_param any degrees of one parity; derivatives as for the poloidal 'general'

    At the walls. A toroidal field that does not vanish at a wall ('Malkus') carries a surface current there. The linear problem is unaffected: the walls' conditions on \(\mathbf b\) do not involve \(\mathbf B_0\), and \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\) on a no-slip wall. Inside a conducting inner core \(\mathbf B_0\) does not enter either: there is no flow there, and \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\) at the no-slip inner boundary, so the matching conditions do not involve it. B0 = None gives a toroidal field alone.

    Normalisation

    The profiles have arbitrary scales, so the amplitude is set apart: cnorm multiplies the poloidal part and cnorm_tor the toroidal part. Each is a number, or a name that kore turns into a number as conductive_IC does (background_field.B0_norm, Gauss–Legendre quadrature with 240 nodes):

    • 'rms_cmb' (cnorm only): the rms of the radial field over the outer wall is 1, \(c_\mathrm{norm} = \sqrt{2l+1}/(L\,h(1))\), \(L = l(l+1)\). Single-degree types only: 'general' and 'ferraro' stop with a message, and so does 'confined', which is zero at the wall.
    • 'mag_energy': \(\int B_0^2\,dV = 1\) over the fluid (Luo & Jackson 2022).
    • 'Schmitt2012': \(\int B_0^2\,dV = 4\).

    For a poloidal field of one degree the integral is

    \[ \int B_0^2\,dV = \frac{4\pi L}{2l+1}\int\left[(L + 1)h^2 + 2rhh' + r^2h'^2\right]dr , \]

    summed over the degrees for 'general' and 'ferraro' (they are orthogonal), and taken over the layer only for 'confined'. For a toroidal field it is \(n_l L\int k^2 r^2\,dr\), with \(n_l = 4\pi/(2n+1)\) and \(L = n(n+1)\), summed over the degrees for 'general'.

    Variable density

    With a density that varies with radius, the flow is not solenoidal and the momentum equation carries \(1/\rho_0\), so the magnetic rows gain terms in the derivatives of \(\ln\rho_0\). This section lists them. They vanish for 'Boussinesq'.

    The flow is written as

    \[ \mathbf u = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P\,\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T\,\mathbf r), \]

    so its consoidal part is \(P' + P/r + \lambda_1 P\), with \(\lambda_1 = (\ln\rho_0)'\), \(\lambda_2 = (\ln\rho_0)''\), and \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf u = -\lambda_1 u_r\). The rows were derived from the definitions with tests/magnetic/derive.py (sympy, exact projection on the harmonics). For \(\mathbf B_0\) of degree 1:

    • rows f, columns P: every \(d/dr\) on \(P\) becomes \(d/dr + \lambda_1\) (label f1bpo0lho1_D0);

    • rows g, columns P, diagonal in \(l\) only (in the scale of conductive_IC's rows; zero for m = 0), from \(-\mathbf B_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf u\) and the \(\lambda_1 P\) above:

      \[ 2im\left[r h\lambda_1 P' + r h\lambda_2 P + \left(1 - \tfrac{L}{2}\right) r h'\lambda_1 P - \tfrac{L}{2}\,h\lambda_1 P\right]; \]

    • rows u, with \(\mathbf G\) the Lorentz force per volume:

      \[ \rho_0\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0^{-1}\mathbf G) = \hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times\mathbf G - \frac{\lambda_1}{r}\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf G_H . \]

      The extra term, \(\lambda_1 r^4\,\nabla_1\cdot\mathbf G_H\) projected on \(Y_l\), is, for the toroidal field of the same degree and for the poloidal field of degree \(l_c = l\pm1\) (operators.lorentz_rho),

      \[ -im\lambda_1\left[(L+2)r^2hg + Lr^3h'g + 2r^3hg'\right], \qquad \frac{y}{2}\lambda_1 r^3\left[2a\,h\,D_{l_c}f + L_c b\,(D_1 h) f\right], \]

      with \(D_l\) as above and

      \[ y = \langle Y_l\,\vert\,\cos\theta\,Y_{l_c}\rangle, \qquad a = 2 - L_c - L, \qquad b = L_c - 2 - L ; \]

    • rows v, the T columns, the diffusion, the walls and the inner core: no \(\rho_0\) terms.

    The new labels pair bpo with lho (two profiles, plus ohr1 in the rows u). They vanish exactly for 'Boussinesq', whose matrices are bitwise those of the uniform-density code. tol_B0 does not cut these products (they converge slowly near the surface). submatrices.py counts odd profiles in both slots of a label for the parity at ricb = 0 (bitwise the same for every earlier label).

    Rows and implementation

    This section says where the magnetic unknowns sit in the matrices and which code builds their rows. It matters only to change or extend the code.

    • Sections. The induction equation gives two sections of rows, f (rows \(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\) of the equation) and g (\(r^2\,\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\)), both in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis with two boundary-condition rows per block. They are placed after the entropy section: ut.offset = {'u': 0, 'v': n, 'h': 2n, 'f': (2+thermal) n, 'g': (3+thermal) n} and sizmat = 2n + n*thermal + 2n*magnetic + 2*nic (nic for a conducting inner core, below). Non-magnetic matrices are unchanged.
    • Labels. Each operator is a sum of labelled radial products. decode_label gives sections f and g the row power \(2 - X\) and no \(\rho_0\). The profile codes are bpo (\(c_\mathrm{norm} h^{(k)}\)), bto (\(c_\mathrm{tor} k^{(j)}\)) and eta (\(\eta\)). The Lorentz labels carry ohr1 (one power of \(\rho_0\) less), so the momentum rows hold \(r^p\) times the force \(\rho_0 \mathbf F_L\). submatrices.py lists each label next to its conductive_IC name. tol_B0 (default 1e-8) is the relative cut of the Chebyshev series of \(r^p h^{(k)}\): it sets the band width of the magnetic blocks.
    • Operators (operators.lorentz, induction, magnetic_diffusion, b). They are the conductive_IC operators divided by \(L^3\) of the row, as every operator of this branch, with Hendrik and MagnetD for \((\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Le})^2\) and \(\Omega\tau\,\mathrm{Em}\). The sign convention is the branch's: A holds the right-hand side, B is \(-\mathbf b\) in the induction rows (as \(-\)inertia in the momentum rows).
    • Toroidal field (operators.induction_tor, lorentz_tor). Closed forms for a toroidal field of degree 1 or 2. The rows were derived with derive.py (any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\): every coefficient is \(im\) (same degree) or the Coriolis factors \(C_- = \sqrt{l^2-m^2}/(2l-1)\), \(C_+ = \sqrt{(l+1)^2-m^2}/(2l+3)\) (columns \(l\mp1\)) times a polynomial in \(l\) (independent of \(m\)). Couplings: rows f from P at the same degree; rows g from P at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\) terms) and from T at \(l\); rows u from F at \(l\) (with \(\lambda_1\)) and G at \(l\pm1\) (with \(\lambda_1\)); rows v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\). Degree 2 (induction_tor2, lorentz_tor2): the quadrupole angular factors (operators.quad_factors, the same-degree terms with \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)) times polynomials in \(l\), couplings up to \(\Delta l = \pm2\): rows f from P at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); u from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); v from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\). operators.lorentz and induction add the poloidal and the toroidal parts (assemble.py loops over \(\Delta l = -2..2\) and skips absent couplings). Column offsets: operators.moff.
    • Poloidal field of degree 2 (operators.induction_pol2, lorentz_pol2, for 'Luo_S2' and 'FDM' with B0_l = 2). Closed forms, derived with derive.py (\(b_0\) = ('pol', 2), any \(\rho_0\)) and fitted in \(l\), \(m\) as for the toroidal field of degree 2: quad_factors times polynomials in \(l\) (the same-degree terms carry \(l(l+1) - 3m^2\)), kept in two tables (row, column, \(\Delta l\)) of the label expressions. Couplings: rows f from P at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and T at \(l\pm1\); g from P at \(l\pm1\) and T at \(l\), \(l\pm2\); u from F at \(l\), \(l\pm2\) and G at \(l\pm1\); v from F at \(l\pm1\) and G at \(l\), \(l\pm2\). Two labels are added to those of the dipole (u2bpo2ohr1_D1, u2bpo2ohr1_D0). spin_doctor takes its Lorentz and induction powers from magnetic_powers_grid for these fields (utils4pp.use_grid).
    • Full sphere (ricb = 0). As for the flow, each block holds the Chebyshev polynomials of one parity on \([-1, 1]\) (ut.remroco): \(f\) has the vector parity vF = symmB0 * vP, \(g\) the opposite; \(h\) has the parity \((-1)^l\) of its degree and \(k\) that of \((-1)^n\) (utils.odd_profiles, both slots of a label). There is one boundary-condition row per block, the insulating condition at the outer wall (assemble.bc_b_spherical). The fields are regular at the centre: FDM \(j_l(\beta r)\) (analytic derivatives, background_field.jl_k), the polynomial fields, 'wall' \(= r^n(1 - r^2)\), 'Malkus' for \(n = 1\) only (\(k = r\) is not regular for \(n = 2\)). 'general' and 'user' are evaluated as \(f(\lvert r\rvert)\,\mathrm{sign}(r)^n\), regular only if \(f\) goes as \(r^n\) times a series in \(r^2\); submatrices.py warns otherwise (Regularity at the centre). cnorm and cnorm_tor are computed as with an inner core; spin_doctor's inner-wall terms are skipped.
    • Inviscid runs (ViscosD = 0, full sphere only). The Lorentz labels have \(D^3\) (rows u) and \(D^2\) (rows v), so the rows are in \(C^{(3)}\) (u) and \(C^{(2)}\) (v), with one boundary-condition row (\(u_r = 0\)) in u and none in v; without a field they stay in \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\). decode_label gives the u and v labels the row powers of the viscous rows. conductive_IC keeps \(C^{(2)}\) and \(C^{(1)}\) and so converts these terms with the wrong matrices; its torsional test is barely affected (Checks).

    • Solver. solve.py writes real_magnetic.field, imag_magnetic.field and normalises the eigenvectors (over the shell, as conductive_IC) to

      \[ \mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{Hendrik}\int\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2dV = 1 , \]

      and without the flow (hydro = 0) to \(\int\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2dV = 1\).

    Couplings for any background

    The closed forms above exist only for fields of degree 1 and 2. For a background of any degrees, bin/mag_algebra.py builds the couplings of the Lorentz force and of the induction from the recurrence algebra that the differential rotation uses, for

    \[ \mathbf B_0 = \sum_n c_\mathrm{norm}\nabla\times\nabla\times(h_n Y_n^0\mathbf r) + \sum_n c_\mathrm{tor}\nabla\times(k_n Y_n^0\mathbf r). \]

    The background vectors are polynomials in \(x = \cos\theta\) times \(\hat{\mathbf r}\), \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\) or \(\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\); the products with the perturbation are sparse matrices on the degrees, times radial monomials; the rows take one radial derivative (product rule); each final monomial becomes a label with the profile codes hp + degree (\(c_\mathrm{norm}h_n\)) and kt + degree (\(c_\mathrm{tor}k_n\)). Derivation tools, part A.6, gives the method.

    mag_couplings = 'algebra' uses it for every type; 'closed' (default) keeps the closed forms. 'general' (poloidal or toroidal), 'ferraro' and B0_wound = 1 have no closed forms and switch to the algebra by themselves. For the types that have closed forms both give the same eigenvalues to round-off.

    Boundary conditions

    At each wall the perturbation field is matched to the field beyond it. The mantle is an insulator, possibly with a conducting layer at its base, thin (conditions at the wall) or of any thickness (its field solved for), so beyond the conducting parts the field is a potential field. The inner core is an insulator too (with or without a thin conducting layer), or a conductor whose field is solved for with the rest.

    Insulating walls (assemble.bc_b_spherical). Matching a potential field gives \((l+1)f + rf' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the outer wall, \(lf - r f' = 0\) and \(g = 0\) at the inner one.

    Conducting inner core. innercore = 'conducting, Chebys' (eigenvalue problems and forced runs, no-slip inner boundary bci = 1) adds the field inside the inner core to the unknowns, as conductive_IC does ('conducting, Bessel', for forced runs, solves it in closed form instead: Forced runs):

    • Unknowns. Sections fic, gic after f, g (ut.offset), with the degrees of f, g (up to lmax; conductive_IC's lmax_cic is not ported, as its ICB matching couples the degrees one by one). Each block is the Chebyshev series on \([-r_i, r_i]\) of one parity (that of the field at the centre), N_cic/2 coefficients per degree (ut.Nic, default N_cic = 64; conductive_IC found broken eigenvectors with 48 at sigma_i2o = \(10^{-2}\)); ut.nic per section.

    • Equation. The field diffuses (a core that a forcing moves also induces field: Forced runs), with \(\mu_{i2o}\), \(\sigma_{i2o}\) (mu_i2o, sigma_i2o) the permeability and conductivity relative to the fluid:

      \[ \frac{\partial\mathbf b}{\partial t} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\eta_i\nabla^2\mathbf b, \qquad \eta_i = \frac{1}{\mu_{i2o}\sigma_{i2o}}, \]

      with rows \(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\) and \(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\) divided by \(L^3\) as in the fluid, in the \(C^{(2)}\) basis. Its matrices are built apart from the labels (submatrices.py: r0_D0f_ic, r2_D0f_ic, r1_D1f_ic, r2_D2f_ic and the same for g), with ut.Dlam and bc_variables.Tk on the inner core's domain (their optional domain arguments).

    • Matching at the ICB (assemble.bc_b_cic), one row in each section, the inner one of f, g and the only one of fic, gic: \(f_o = f_i\) (\([b_r]\)), \((g + rg')_o = \eta_i (g + rg')_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf E\)), \(\mu_{i2o}(f + rf')_o = (f + rf')_i\) and \(\mu_{i2o}\,g_o = g_i\) (tangential \(\mathbf H\)). The continuity of \(\eta\nabla^2_l f\) is not imposed: with \(b_r\) continuous it follows from the radial induction equation on both sides, since \(\mathbf u = 0\) at the no-slip ICB (for a wall that the forcing moves, Forced runs).

    • Output. real_magnetic_ic.field, imag_magnetic_ic.field. The eigenvectors are normalised with the fluid's energies only (the inner-core field is scaled along), as in conductive_IC.

    Thin conducting layer

    A thin conducting layer between the fluid and an insulator carries currents that couple the field across it. The thin-wall approximation (Roberts, Glatzmaier & Clune 2010; Guervilly, Wood & Brummell 2013, appendix A) replaces the layer by conditions on the field at the wall, so the layer adds no unknowns. mantle = 'TWA' puts one at the base of the mantle, at the outer wall, innercore = 'TWA' one on the inner core, at the inner wall; the conditions are those of conductive_IC (bc_b_thinlayer, with its terms of first order in the thickness), in assemble.bc_b_twa.

    A layer of thickness \(h\), conductivity \(\sigma_w\) and permeability \(\mu_w\) at the radius \(r\) of the wall is described by

    \[ c = \frac{h\,\mu_w}{r\,\mu_f}, \qquad c_1 = \frac{h\,\sigma_w}{r\,\sigma_f}, \qquad p = \frac{\mu_w}{\mu_0}, \]

    (c_cmb, c1_cmb, muw_cmb or c_icb, c1_icb, muw_icb), with \(\mu_f = \mu\,\mu_0\) and \(\sigma_f\) the fluid's permeability and conductivity (mu); \(h/r = c\,\mu/p\). With \(F = rf\), \(G = rg\) and \(\mathcal D_l F = F'' - l(l+1)F/r^2 = r\nabla^2_l f\) the conditions are

    \[ \text{outer:}\quad \Big(\frac1\mu + l\,c\Big)F' + \frac lr F + \frac{c_1}{\mu}\,r\,\mathcal D_l F + \frac{c\,\mu\,l}{p\,r}\Big(\frac{l+1}{p} - 1\Big)F = 0, \qquad G + c_1\,r\,G' = 0, \]
    \[ \text{inner:}\quad \Big(\frac1\mu + (l+1)\,c\Big)F' - \frac{l+1}{r} F - \frac{c_1}{\mu}\,r\,\mathcal D_l F - \frac{c\,\mu\,(l+1)}{p\,r}\Big(\frac lp + 1\Big)F = 0, \qquad G - c_1\,r\,G' = 0. \]

    In kore's rows (on \(f\), \(g\)) the outer conditions are written as they are, the inner poloidal one times \(-1\) and the toroidal ones divided by \(r\), so that with \(c = c_1 = 0\) and \(\mu = 1\) they are the rows of the insulating walls.

    • The terms in \(p\) are of first order in \(h\): the curvature of the layer, the potential field matched at \(r \pm h\), and the \(l(l+1)/r^2\) of \(\nabla^2_l\) in the layer. They vanish for \(p = \infty\) (np.inf), the limit of Guervilly et al. (\(h \to 0\) with \(h\mu_w\) fixed). For \(p = 1\), a layer with the permeability of vacuum, and \(\mu = 1\) they cancel the \(c\) term for a steady field, which then crosses the layer as it would an insulator.
    • \(\mu\) enters only these conditions, so \(\mu \neq 1\) needs a thin layer or a conducting inner core at every wall.
    • \(\mathcal D_l F\) stands for the layer's \(\partial_t F/\eta\), taken from the fluid next to it (uniform diffusivity), and \(G'\) for the layer's tangential electric field. Both assume that the fluid moves with the solid at the wall: no slip, and no flow through it.

    • Validity. The approximation keeps the linear profile of the field across the layer. Inside it \(b'' = k^2 b\) with \(k^2 = \lambda/\eta_w\), and the first term left out, the layer's own induction, is a relative error

      \[ \varepsilon_\text{TWA} = \frac{\lvert kh\rvert^2}{3} = \frac23\Big(\frac{h}{\delta_w}\Big)^2, \qquad \Big(\frac{h}{\delta_w}\Big)^2 = \frac{c\,c_1\,r^2\,\lvert\lambda\rvert}{2\,\mathrm{MagnetD}} \]

      of the layer's effect (\(c\,c_1 = h^2\eta_f/(r^2\eta_w)\), any \(p\)), with \(\lambda\) the eigenvalue in the frame of the solid (utils4pp.twa_error; spin_doctor's εTWA). The conditions \(h \ll r\) and \(lh \ll r\) are not checked.

    A fluid slipping along a thin layer (bco = 0, bci = 0 or ViscosD = 0; utils.twa_slips). The conditions above take the tangential electric field of the fluid, \(\mathbf E = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla\times\mathbf b - \mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\), in the frame of the solid. kore continues it into the layer (condition C.I of Rekier, Triana, Trinh & Buffett 2023): a modelling choice, for a slip that stands for a viscous layer that is not resolved, valid while that layer is thin against the magnetic skin depth. With \(u_r = 0\) at the wall, \((\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0)_t = B_{0r}\,(\mathbf u_t\times\hat{\mathbf r})\), which enters through two projections on the degree \(l\): \(S_l\), the \(r\nabla Y_l\) part of \(B_{0r}(\mathbf u_t\times\hat{\mathbf r})\), and \(X_l = [\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0)]_l = -[\nabla\cdot(B_{0r}\mathbf u_t)]_l/r\), the radial induction of the slip. In the conditions

    \[ G' \to G' - \frac{r\,S_l}{\mathrm{MagnetD}}, \qquad \mathcal D_l F \to \mathcal D_l F + \frac{r^2 X_l}{l(l+1)\,\mathrm{MagnetD}}, \]

    the second because the layer's \(\partial_t F\) now includes the radial induction of the slip. assemble.bc_b_slip builds both for any poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\) with the recurrence algebra (angular.Algebra): \(B_{0r} = \sum_n n(n+1)h_n P_n/r\) over the degrees of the background, times \(\mathbf u_t = \sum_k s_k\,r\nabla Y_k + t_k\,r\nabla Y_k\times\hat{\mathbf r}\) with \(s_k = P_k' + P_k/r + (\ln\rho_0)'P_k\) and \(t_k = T_k\) at the wall; a toroidal background has no \(B_{0r}\). With the rotating solids the outer wall's frame turns with the mantle, and \(t_1 = T_1 - r\,\omega_M\) there, the slip relative to it (Rotating solids equations). conductive_IC has these terms for a single degree.

    The background field at a thin outer layer (B0_cmb). A steady \(\mathbf B_0\) drives no current in the layer, but it must meet the steady condition, \((1/\mu + lc)(rh)' + (l + e)h = 0\) at \(r = 1\) with \(e = c\mu l((l+1)/p - 1)/p\) (\(e = 0\) for \(p = \infty\)). With B0_cmb = 'wall' (default) the fields of internal currents ('G21 dipole', 'Luo_S1', 'Luo_S2', 'FDM') gain \(\alpha r^l\) in \(h\) (background_field.B0_alpha_cmb), with \(\alpha\) set by that condition. The added field is a potential field: it carries no current and keeps the matching at the inner wall. For \(p = 1\) and \(\mu = 1\) the condition is the insulating one, which these fields already meet. 'plain' leaves \(h\) unchanged; the other fields are used as given. At a thin layer on the inner core the background is not adjusted (for \(p = 1\) and \(\mu = 1\) a potential field below meets its condition).

    Resolved conducting mantle

    A layer that is not thin against its magnetic skin depth, or whose conductivity varies across it, cannot be reduced to conditions at the wall. mantle = 'resolved' (as conductive_IC, after PR #17 by A. Nuyt) adds the field of a solid conducting layer \(1 \le r \le r_s\) (r_surface) to the unknowns, with an insulator beyond. Its conductivity relative to the fluid's, \(\sigma(r)\) (sigma_mantle, a number or an expression of \(r\) and sigma_mantle_param), may vary with radius.

    • Unknowns. Sections fm, gm, last (after fic, gic; ut.offset), with the degrees of f, g. Each block is the Chebyshev series on \([1, r_s]\) (both parities), N_mantle coefficients per degree (ut.nman per section).

    • Equation. The layer is at rest (unless a forcing moves the outer wall: Forced runs) and its field diffuses, with the diffusivity \(\mathrm{MagnetD}/\sigma\):

      \[ \frac{\partial\mathbf b}{\partial t} = -\mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla\times\Big(\frac1\sigma\nabla\times\mathbf b\Big) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \sigma\,\partial_t f = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\nabla^2_l f, \qquad \sigma\,\partial_t g = \mathrm{MagnetD}\Big[\nabla^2_l g - \frac{(\ln\sigma)'}{r}(rg)'\Big]. \]

      The rows are those of the fluid (\(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\) and \(r^2\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\), the \(C^{(2)}\) basis) times \(\sigma\), so that \(\sigma\) enters only \(B\), through \(r^2\sigma\), and the toroidal rows through \((\ln\sigma)'\); \(A\) stays well scaled for a small \(\sigma\) or a large contrast. Their matrices are built apart from the labels (submatrices.py: r2s_D0_man, r0_D0_man, r1_D1_man, r2_D2_man, r1d_D0_man, r2d_D1_man), with \((\ln\sigma)'\) from a Chebyshev interpolant of \(\ln\sigma\) (background.dlnsigma_mantle). background.check_mantle stops the run if N_mantle polynomials do not resolve \(r^2\sigma\) and \(\ln\sigma\) (coefficients beyond N_mantle above \(10^{-6}\)): an unresolved \(\sigma\) in \(B\) can dip below 0 and give growing modes.

    • Matching at the outer wall (assemble.bc_b_mantle), the outer row of f, g and the first of fm, gm: \(\mathbf b\) continuous, \(f = f_m\), \((rf)' = (rf_m)'\), \(g = g_m\) (with \(\mu = 1\) on both sides), and the tangential electric field \(\eta\nabla\times\mathbf b\) continuous, \(\sigma(1)\,(rg)' = (rg_m)'\). Its other part, which drives \(b_r\), follows from the continuity of \(b_r\) and the radial induction equation on both sides.

    • The surface \(r = r_s\), the second row of fm, gm: \(r f_m' + (l+1) f_m = 0\) and \(g_m = 0\) (a potential field beyond).
    • A fluid slipping along the layer (bco = 0 or ViscosD = 0; utils.mantle_slips). The fluid's tangential electric field includes \(-\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\), continued into the layer as for a thin one (condition C.I): \(\sigma(1)\,[(rg)' - r\,S_l/\mathrm{MagnetD}] = (rg_m)'\), with the \(S_l\) of a thin layer (assemble.bc_b_slip, with \(\sigma(1)\) for \(c_1\)). The radial induction of the slip is in the fluid's own equation, and the poloidal matching needs nothing more.
    • Output. real_magnetic_mantle.field, imag_magnetic_mantle.field (fm then gm), scaled with the fluid's field.

    As \(r_s \to 1\) with \(\sigma\,(r_s - 1)\) fixed the layer tends to a thin layer ('TWA') with \(c = r_s - 1\) and \(c_1 = \sigma(r_s - 1)\), at first order in the thickness (Checks).

    Forced runs

    A forced run (forcing other than 0, as in conductive_IC) solves the equations at the one frequency \(w\) of the forcing (forcing_frequency), with every perturbation going as \(e^{iwt}\) and the forcing in the right-hand side:

    \[ (A - i\,w\,B)\,\mathbf x = \mathbf b . \]
    • Rows. The field enters as the flow does: \(A\) is the eigenproblem's, not normalised, and every row of the field, in the fluid, a conducting inner core and a resolved mantle, gets \(-i\,w\,B\), with \(B = -\mathbf b\) there (assemble.py). A frequency scan of forcing 11 shifts this matrix by \(-i\,(w' - w)\,B\) (B.npz, written without the normalisation), so it may be assembled at any forcing_frequency.
    • Kinematic induction (just_induction.py) in a forced run takes \(A\) as it is for its default \(\lambda = i\,w\). Another \(\lambda\) takes \(A - (\lambda - i\,w)B\), so it needs B.npz, which forcing 11 alone writes: in another forced run it stops.

    The Bessel inner core (innercore = 'conducting, Bessel', from conductive_IC). At one frequency the field of a uniform conducting inner core, regular at the centre, is known: each scalar goes as \(j_l(kr)\), with

    \[ k^2 = -\frac{i\,w}{\eta_i\,\mathrm{MagnetD}}, \qquad \eta_i = \frac{1}{\mu_{i2o}\,\sigma_{i2o}}, \]

    and \(w\) in the frame of the core: forcing_frequency, plus \(m\,\)Gaspard\(\,\Delta\Omega(r_i)\) with a differential rotation (utils.bessel_ic). The four matching conditions of 'conducting, Chebys', with the core's field eliminated, leave two rows at the inner wall (assemble.bc_b_bessel), with \(x_d = x\,j_l'(x)/j_l(x)\) at \(x = k\,r_i\):

    \[ \Big(\frac{1 + x_d}{\mu_{i2o}} - 1\Big)f - r_i f' = 0, \qquad g - \frac{\sigma_{i2o}}{1 + x_d}\,(g + r_i g') = 0, \]

    the rows of an insulating core for \(\sigma_{i2o} \to 0\) with \(\mu_{i2o} = 1\) (the poloidal one already for \(w \to 0\)). utils.xdlogjl computes \(x_d = l - x\,j_{l+1}(x)/j_l(x)\), from scipy's exponentially scaled Bessel functions where \(\lvert x\rvert > l\) and from the continued fraction of \(j_{l+1}/j_l\) (modified Lentz) elsewhere. These rows hold at that frequency only: the run stops in an eigenvalue problem and in a frequency scan, and just_induction.py with another \(\lambda\). spin_doctor takes the core's energy and Ohmic loss in closed form (utils4pp.bessel_budget): since \(k^2\) is imaginary,

    \[ \int_0^{r_i} r^2\Big\lvert\frac{j_l(kr)}{j_l(kr_i)}\Big\rvert^2dr = -\frac{r_i\,\mathrm{Im}\,x_d}{\mathrm{Im}\,k^2}, \qquad \int_0^{r_i}\big(l(l+1)\lvert f\rvert^2 + \lvert(rf)'\rvert^2\big)dr = r_i\,(1 + \mathrm{Re}\,x_d)\,\lvert f(r_i)\rvert^2 , \]

    the first \(r_i^3/(2l+3)\) at \(k = 0\). 'conducting, Bessel' writes no field files of the core.

    A boundary that moves (forced_walls). Forcings 6, 9, 10 and 14, and 7 with \(m\) = 2, stand for a boundary that moves or is not spherical: the wall stays at the sphere, and the forcing imposes \(u_r \neq 0\) there (background.moves_radially). The tangential electric field is continuous at the wall, and in a conducting solid it is \(\mathrm{MagnetD}\,\eta_s\nabla\times\mathbf b - \mathbf v_s\times\mathbf B_0\), with \(\mathbf v_s\) the velocity of the solid's material, so the matching depends on how the solid moves. Where a no-slip wall moves along itself, as in a libration, the solid turns with it, which induces nothing in the axisymmetric \(\mathbf B_0\). For the radial motion forced_walls chooses between two consistent pictures; None, the default, takes 'rigid' for forcings 6 and 7 and 'deforming' for 9, 10 and 14 (background.forced_walls).

    • 'rigid': the solid is at rest and only its shape moves past the sphere, so the flow passes through the wall. The fluid's tangential electric field, \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) included, is continued into the solid, as for a slipping fluid. With \(u_r \neq 0\), \((\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0)_t\) gains \(u_r\,\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf B_{0t}\), for a poloidal and a toroidal background. With \(S_l\) its part on \(r\nabla Y_l\) and \(X_l = [\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times(u_r\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf B_0)]_l = [\nabla_H\cdot(u_r\mathbf B_{0t})]_l\) its radial induction,

      \[ G' \to G' - \frac{r\,S_l}{\mathrm{MagnetD}}, \qquad \mathcal D_l F \to \mathcal D_l F + \frac{r^2 X_l}{l(l+1)\,\mathrm{MagnetD}} \]

      in the two conditions of a thin conducting layer, and the first in the electric-field row of a conducting inner core (both kinds) and of the resolved mantle (assemble.bc_b_radial, with \(u_r = l(l+1)P/r\) on the columns of the flow's \(P\) at the wall, from the recurrence algebra). There the toroidal part of the tangential electric field follows from \(b_r\) continuous and the radial induction on both sides, with \(u_r\) in the fluid and none in the solid: it matches a solid at rest too.

    • 'deforming': the solid deforms with the boundary. Its surface moves with the fluid, \(\mathbf v_s = \mathbf u\) at the wall, so \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) cancels in the matching, and the rows at the wall are those of a wall at rest without it, as in conductive_IC. Inside a thick conducting solid the motion induces a field too (below).

    A deforming solid. With 'deforming', the resolved mantle and 'conducting, Chebys' behind a wall that the forcing moves (background.deforms) move inside as well, across the background field, and the field that this motion induces is solved for with the rest:

    • Motion. The simplest one that matches the wall, \(\mathbf v_s = \nabla\times\nabla\times(P_s\,Y_l\,\mathbf r)\) with \(P_s = P(r_w)\,\phi_l(r)\), \(P(r_w)\) the flow's at the wall (background.deform_profile). In the mantle \(\phi = 1/r\): a radial motion, \(v_r = u_r(1)/r^2\), which keeps the volume of every shell. In the core \(\phi_l = \tfrac12\big[(l+3)\,x^l - (l+1)\,x^{l+2}\big]\) with \(x = r/r_i\): the polynomial of lowest degree that is regular at the centre with \(\phi_l = 1\) and \((r\phi_l)' = 0\) at \(r_i\). Both give the fluid's \(u_r\) at the wall and no motion along it, so \(\mathbf v_s = \mathbf u\) at a no-slip wall.
    • The background inside the solid (background_field.solid_profiles). Each degree of \(\mathbf B_0\) is continued from the wall so that it is continuous there, with \(x = r/r_w\): a poloidal \(h_n\), with \(h_n'\), as the potential field \(a\,x^n + b\,x^{-(n+1)}\) in the mantle and as \(a\,x^n + b\,x^{n+2}\) in the core (regular, and a potential field where \(\mathbf B_0\) meets the potential condition at the inner wall); a toroidal \(k_n\) as the steady field of a uniform conductor, \(a\,x^n + b\,x^{-(n+1)}\) with \(k_n = 0\) at \(r_s\) in the mantle, \(k_n(r_i)\,x^n\) in the core.

    • Rows (assemble.deform_rows). With \(\mathbf v_s\times\mathbf B_0 = \sum_l q_l\,Y_l\hat{\mathbf r} + s_l\,r\nabla Y_l + t_l\,r\nabla Y_l\times\hat{\mathbf r}\), from the recurrence algebra (mag_algebra.induction_monomials), the solid's equations gain

      \[ \partial_t f \mathrel{+}= t_l, \qquad \partial_t g \mathrel{+}= \frac{q_l - (r s_l)'}{r}, \]

      times \(\sigma\) in the mantle's rows. For each degree \(k\) of the flow this is a known function of \(r\) times \(P_k(r_w)\). Its Chebyshev series on the solid's interval, exact in the core where it is a polynomial, fills the solid's rows below the matching rows, on the columns of \(P_k\) through its Chebyshev polynomials at the wall; in the core it has the parity of the rows, which is checked.

    • Stop (background.check_forced_magnetic). 'conducting, Bessel' behind an inner wall that the forcing moves, since the field that the core's motion induces is not a Bessel function.

    A thin conducting layer under 'deforming'. The solid deforms with the boundary, and the thin conducting layer at its surface goes with it. The layer's conditions take \(\partial_t F\) and the tangential electric field from the fluid next to it, which holds that motion. One part is not there: a toroidal \(\mathbf B_0\) that is not zero at the wall falls to zero across the layer, toward the insulator beyond, so the layer's motion meets half of it on average (background.thin_drop). Its conditions gain half the 'rigid' terms of the toroidal part alone (\(S_l\) and \(X_l\) with half the toroidal \(\mathbf B_{0t}\) at the wall, assemble.bc_b_radial). This is the limit of a thin resolved mantle that deforms (Checks). A poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\) changes by \(O(h)\) across the layer and needs nothing more.

    Permeability at a wall that moves. A surface where the permeability jumps separates matter that \(\mathbf B_0\) magnetizes differently, so moving it moves magnetization through \(\mathbf B_0\), which changes the field. The rows above hold with the same permeability on both sides of every surface that moves. A wall that the forcing moves, where \(\mathbf B_0\) reaches it, therefore needs \(\mu = 1\), and \(\mu_{i2o} = 1\) with a conducting inner core, in both pictures. A thin layer holds \((p/\mu)\,\mathbf B_{0t}\) of the part of \(\mathbf B_0\) that enters it (\(\mathbf H_t\) is continuous), and its \(p\) must be 1 where that part reaches the wall:

    • the poloidal part, in a layer with a thickness (\(c \neq 0\)), and in a conducting layer at rest ('rigid'), whose current is driven by \(u_r\hat{\mathbf r}\times\) its own field, which its rows take as the fluid's;
    • the toroidal part, in a conducting layer (\(c_1 \neq 0\)): at rest for the same reason, and deforming because the layer then meets \(p/(2\mu)\) of it, not half.

    Elsewhere \(p\) takes any value: in an insulating layer (\(c = c_1 = 0\)), in a layer that does not conduct under a toroidal \(\mathbf B_0\) alone, which holds none of it, and in a conducting layer without a thickness (\(c = 0\)) that deforms with the boundary under a poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\), which carries the fluid's tangential electric field in its own frame whatever its permeability. The run stops otherwise (background.check_forced_magnetic). \(\mathbf B_0\) reaches the wall where \(h_n\) or \(h_n'\) (its poloidal part) or \(k_n\) (its toroidal part) of some degree is not zero there, to \(10^{-6}\) of the largest of them over the fluid; \(h_n'\) to \(10^{-12}\,n^2\,2/(1 - r_i)\) instead where that is larger, ten times the round-off of the slope of a profile interpolated at degree \(n = \max(64, 2N)\) (background.b0_at_wall: 'confined' never reaches a wall, the toroidal 'wall' vanishes at both; a field meant to vanish there needs an N that resolves it). Walls that stay in place take any permeability.

    Left out, in both pictures: the matching holds at the sphere, not at the displaced boundary, so the displacement \(\xi_r\) times the jump of \(\partial_r\mathbf B_0\) across the wall (the background's current there) is not in it.

    conductive_IC has the rows without \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) at every wall and no motion inside the solids, and it stops the resolved mantle behind a wall that moves. Its thin layers therefore follow 'deforming' with a poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\) (it stops a toroidal one in forced runs), while for a conducting inner core behind a wall that moves radially its solutions match neither picture: the core's surface moves with the fluid and its inside is at rest.

    With a differential rotation

    A differential rotation acts on magnetic field lines in two ways. It shears the perturbation field, which kore includes in the linear problem: this section gives that term. It also winds up the background field, which kore holds fixed: the subsections below give the condition under which nothing is wound up (the Ferraro condition), the set-ups that avoid the winding, the one that balances it, and what the background leaves out.

    With diff_rot = 1 the background flow is \(\mathbf U_0 = G\,\Delta\Omega\,r\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\) (\(G\) = Gaspard), and the induction equation gains

    \[ \nabla\times(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b) = G\left[-im\,\Delta\Omega\,\mathbf b + \sin\theta\,(r\,\partial_r\Delta\Omega\,b_r + \partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\,b_\theta)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\right] \]

    (the \(\Omega\)-effect, and the Doppler shift of the field). It is built for any \(\Delta\Omega = \sum_n f_n P_n\) with the recurrence algebra of the momentum terms (diff_rot.induction_block; Derivation tools, part B): F→f and G→g couple at even \(\Delta l\) up to \(n\), F→g (a poloidal field sheared into a toroidal one) at odd \(\Delta l\) up to \(n + 1\). The Lorentz force needs nothing new: it does not involve \(\mathbf U_0\).

    Conducting inner core. A solid core cannot be sheared, so it must rotate rigidly with the fluid at its surface: \(\Delta\Omega(r_i, \theta) = c\) (\(f_n(r_i) = 0\) for \(n > 0\), diff_rot.rigid_ricb), or the run stops. Its field then gains \(-imGc\,\mathbf b\). The matching conditions are unchanged, because only \(b_r\), which is continuous, enters the tangential part of \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\).

    A solid with a thin conducting layer turns with the fluid next to it in the same way, so \(\Delta\Omega\) must be uniform in latitude at each such wall (diff_rot.rigid_at), or the run stops. In the frame of the solid the layer's conditions are those above, and \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\) cancels there with the solid's own rotation. The resolved mantle turns with \(\Delta\Omega(1) = c\), uniform in latitude, and its field gains \(-imGc\,\mathbf b\) like a conducting inner core.

    The Ferraro condition

    The rotation winds up a poloidal field line unless the rotation rate is the same all along it. So nothing is wound up when the rotation rate is constant along every poloidal field line: Ferraro's law of isorotation. kore checks this at the start of a run.

    The winding is the induction of the background flow on the background field,

    \[ \nabla\times(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf B_0) = G\,r\sin\theta\,(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\,\hat{\boldsymbol\phi} . \]

    It vanishes always for a toroidal \(\mathbf B_0\) (\(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf B_0 = 0\)), and for a poloidal one only if \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega = 0\). The linear problem does not contain this term: it belongs to the background. background.ferraro_mismatch reports its relative size,

    \[ \frac{\max\lvert r\,\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert}{\max\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert\,\max\lvert r\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert} , \]

    over the fluid at the start of every run with diff_rot = 1 and a poloidal \(\mathbf B_0\), except with B0_wound = 1 (computed from the shapes \(f_n\), with a fourth-order difference in \(r\), and the poloidal profiles, on a grid). Above \(10^{-7}\) the parameter ferraro_policy decides: 'warn' (default) prints a warning and carries on, the background being assumed to be maintained by something outside the model; 'refuse' stops the run.

    The background is steady in two ways. Nothing is wound up with:

    • a toroidal field, of any shape;
    • a 'confined' field whose layer lies where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform (Poloidal fields);
    • B0 = 'ferraro', a poloidal field whose field lines lie on the surfaces of constant rotation;
    • diff_rot_type = 'ferraro', a rotation that is constant along the field lines.

    The winding goes on but is balanced by magnetic diffusion with any poloidal field and B0_wound = 1, which adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up (the run then reports that balance instead).

    Field lines on the surfaces of constant rotation: B0 = 'ferraro'

    A differential rotation winds up a poloidal field whose field lines cross the surfaces of constant rotation. B0 = 'ferraro' avoids the winding: kore uses a poloidal field whose field lines lie on the surfaces of constant rotation, so nothing is wound up, no azimuthal field is created, and the background is steady. The rotation is kept as given. Where the rotation is uniform the field vanishes, so this field lives only where the rotation varies. It is the opposite of B0_wound = 1 (below), which keeps a field that the shear crosses and adds the azimuthal field that the shear winds up from it.

    Method. An axisymmetric poloidal field can be written with a flux function \(\psi\), as \(\mathbf B_0 = \nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi\): its field lines are the lines of constant \(\psi\) in a meridional plane. kore takes \(\psi = G(\Delta\Omega)\), a function of the rotation rate. Every surface of constant rotation is then a surface of constant \(\psi\), so \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega = 0\); and where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform, \(\nabla\psi = G'\,\nabla\Delta\Omega = 0\), so the field vanishes. In kore's form \(\psi = r\sum_n h_n(1 - x^2)P_n'(x)\), \(x = \cos\theta\), and the functions \((1 - x^2)P_n'\) are orthogonal with the weight \(1/(1 - x^2)\), so the profiles are

    \[ h_n(r) = \frac{2n+1}{2n(n+1)}\frac{1}{r}\int_{-1}^{1}\psi(r, x)\,P_n'(x)\,dx , \]

    computed by Gauss–Legendre quadrature. \(\Delta\Omega\) has only even \(P_n\), so only odd \(n\) appear: the field is dipolar. \(G\) = B0_ferraro_G, a numpy expression of w = \(\Delta\Omega\) and p (B0_param); the derivatives of \(h_n\) come from Chebyshev interpolants of degree max(64, 2N).

    Conditions. background.ferraro_report prints at the start of a run how well they hold (max \(\lvert\psi\rvert\) on the axis and at \(r = 1\), and the error of \(\psi\) rebuilt from the degrees):

    • \(\psi\) must vanish on the axis (it must be constant there for a regular field; for a rotation that varies along the axis, \(G\) has to vanish on the values \(\Delta\Omega\) takes there);
    • and at the outer wall, if the field is to stay inside the fluid (wherever the field does not match a potential field at the wall, it meets the insulating mantle with a surface current, which the linear problem allows);
    • the series is cut at B0_ferraro_nmax (odd, \(\ge 1\)), which sets the residual \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\).

    A toroidal part (B0_tor, any type) can be added: it is consistent with any axisymmetric rotation, and it must have the field's equatorial symmetry (an even degree). Example: for a cylindrical rotation \(\Delta\Omega \propto s^2\), \(G(w) = w\) gives \(\psi \propto s^2\), the uniform axial field.

    Rotation constant along the field lines: diff_rot_type = 'ferraro'

    This set-up also avoids the winding, the other way round, for when the poloidal field is given (any type, 'general' included; e.g. a model of an observed or a dynamo field). kore keeps the field and replaces the rotation profile by one that is constant along every field line, \(\Delta\Omega = F(\psi)\), with \(\psi\) the field's own flux function. Then \(\mathbf B_0\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega = 0\), nothing is wound up, and the background is steady. The force of the rotation on the flow, its induction term and the thermal wind all use this profile.

    Method. The flux function is built from the radial profiles of the field (background_field.pol_profiles),

    \[ \psi(r, x) = r\sum_n c\,h_n(1 - x^2)P_n'(x). \]

    \(F\) is evaluated on a Gauss–Legendre grid in \(x\) and projected on the Legendre polynomials,

    \[ f_n(r) = \frac{2n+1}{2}\int_{-1}^{1}F\big(\psi(r, x)\big)\,P_n(x)\,dx, \qquad n = 0, 2, \dots, \texttt{diff\_rot\_nmax}, \]

    and \(\Delta\Omega\) = diff_rot_ampl \(\cdot\sum_n f_n P_n\), as for the other rotation profiles, so nothing else changes (diff_rot._ferraro_shapes). \(F\) = diff_rot_F (default 'psi'), a numpy expression of psi and p (diff_rot_param).

    Rules, checked at the start of a run:

    • magnetic = 1 and a poloidal B0 are needed (a toroidal field alone has no \(\psi\));
    • B0 = 'ferraro' is refused (the field would be set by the rotation and the rotation by the field);
    • kore's \(\Delta\Omega\) has only even \(P_n\), so \(F(\psi)\) must be symmetric about the equator: any \(F\) for a dipolar field (\(\psi\) even in \(x\)), an even \(F\) (e.g. of psi**2) for a quadrupolar one (\(\psi\) odd), else the run stops;
    • with a conducting inner core \(\Delta\Omega(r_i, \theta)\) must be uniform, which in general means \(\psi\) constant on the inner core's surface;
    • diff_rot_nmax must be even and not negative.

    A toroidal part of B0 is left as it is. The series is cut at diff_rot_nmax: ferraro_mismatch then reports only the truncation, at round-off for fields of low degree with a polynomial \(F\). Example: the uniform axial field \(\psi = r^2\sin^2\theta/2\) with \(F(\psi) = \psi\) gives \(\Delta\Omega \propto s^2\), the cylindrical profile.

    The wound toroidal field: B0_wound = 1

    When both the rotation and a poloidal field are given and they do not satisfy the Ferraro condition (for example in a model of the solar tachocline), the shear winds the poloidal field into an azimuthal one, which grows until magnetic diffusion balances the winding. B0_wound = 1 accepts the winding: it computes this steady azimuthal field and adds it to the background, on top of any B0_tor. The poloidal field and the rotation are kept as given, so the shear keeps crossing the field lines, and the winding is balanced by diffusion instead of avoided.

    Method. The steady balance is the \(\phi\)-component of the induction equation for axisymmetric fields, with the background flow \(\mathbf U_0\) above (\(r\sin\theta = s\)) and a uniform diffusivity \(\eta\) = B0_wound_eta times MagnetD,

    \[ \eta\left(\nabla^2 - \frac{1}{s^2}\right)B_\phi = -G\,s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega , \]

    where \(\mathbf B_p\) is the poloidal B0 (any type, cnorm included). Written as kore's toroidal field, the operator acts degree by degree:

    \[ B_\phi = \sum_n k_n\sin\theta\,P_n'(\cos\theta), \qquad \left(\nabla^2 - \frac{1}{s^2}\right)B_\phi = \sum_n (D_n k_n)\,\sin\theta\,P_n', \qquad D_n k = k'' + \frac{2k'}{r} - \frac{n(n+1)k}{r^2} . \]

    The source is projected on \(\sin\theta\,P_n'\) by Gauss–Legendre quadrature in \(\cos\theta\), using

    \[ \int_{-1}^{1}(1 - x^2)P_n'P_m'\,dx = \delta_{nm}\,\frac{2n(n+1)}{2n+1}, \]

    which leaves one radial equation per degree, solved by Chebyshev collocation (background_field.wound_profiles). Boundary conditions:

    • \(k_n = 0\) at an insulating wall (\(B_\phi\) vanishes outside);
    • regular at \(r = 0\) in a full sphere (parity \((-1)^n\) on \([-1, 1]\));

    • with a conducting inner core, which must rotate rigidly: \(k = A r^n\) inside, and \(B_\phi/\mu\) and \(E_\theta\) continuous, which gives at \(r_i\) (\(\sigma\) = sigma_i2o)

      \[ r k_n' + \left(1 - \frac{n+1}{\sigma}\right)k_n = 0 . \]

    The degrees run up to (that of \(\mathbf B_p\)) + (that of \(\Delta\Omega\)) + 1, with the field's symmetry (even for a dipolar \(\mathbf B_p\), odd for a quadrupolar one). The \(k_n\) are added to any B0_tor, and the couplings come from mag_algebra.py as for 'general'.

    Size. The physics, not cnorm, sets the size of the wound field: about \(G\,\Delta\Omega/\eta\) (times the length scale squared) relative to \(\mathbf B_p\). cnorm normalises the poloidal field alone, so the Lehnert or Elsasser number keeps its meaning for the field the user gives, and the wound part enters with its physical ratio. B0_wound_eta (default 1) is the diffusivity of this balance in units of MagnetD, while the perturbation still diffuses with MagnetD: a turbulent value above 1 gives a weaker wound field.

    Check. At the start of a run, instead of the Ferraro check, background.magnetic_diffrot prints the degrees and the residual of the steady balance on a grid off the collocation nodes,

    \[ \frac{\max\lvert\eta(\nabla^2 - 1/s^2)B_\phi + G s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert}{\max\lvert G s\,\mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega\rvert} . \]

    Left out. Two effects make the background only approximately steady: the Ohmic decay of \(\mathbf B_p\) (the poloidal field is held fixed, as it is without rotation), and the Lorentz force of \(\mathbf B_0\) on the background flow (\(\Delta\Omega\) is imposed). This force is dominated by the wound \(B_\phi\), which grows as \(G\,\Delta\Omega/\eta\). The next section says when it can be ignored.

    When the ignored Lorentz force matters

    kore imposes the rotation and leaves out the force of the background field on it. This section estimates that force and says when it is negligible. It has two parts: an azimuthal torque, which brakes the rotation, and a meridional part, which enters the thermal-wind balance. The conditions that follow are summarised on Magnetic field.

    The estimates are orders of magnitude, in kore's units (time \(1/\Omega\), length the outer radius; timescale = 'rotation', so Gaspard = 1, Hendrik = \(\mathrm{Le}^2\), MagnetD = Em). With cnorm = 'mag_energy' the maximum of the poloidal field is of order 1. With \(\rho_0\) the background density (1 for 'Boussinesq') and \(\eta_w\) = B0_wound_eta, the Lorentz force per unit mass and the wound field are

    \[ \mathbf F_0 = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0}(\nabla\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf B_0, \qquad B_\phi \approx \frac{\Delta\Omega}{\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}\,B_p . \]

    1. The azimuthal torque, \(\propto \mathbf B_p\cdot\nabla(s B_\phi)\): Ferraro's mechanism. It brakes \(\Delta\Omega\) along the field lines in a time of about \(1/(\Lambda_p\Omega)\), with \(\Lambda_p\) the Elsasser number of the poloidal field built on the background diffusivity. In a real system the wound field first has to build up, so the braking takes at least one Alfvén crossing time of the poloidal field. If viscous (or turbulent) stresses hold \(\Delta\Omega\), the torque compares with them through the Hartmann number \(Ha\) of the background values:

      \[ \Lambda_p = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w}, \qquad Ha^2 = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}\,\eta_w} \quad \left(\frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\mathrm{Ek}\,\mathrm{Em}} \text{ for } \rho_0 = \eta_w = 1\right). \]

    2. The meridional part, the hoop stress and magnetic pressure of \(B_\phi\), \(\propto B_\phi^2\): it changes the thermal-wind and entropy balance of the background and drives magnetic buoyancy. Relative to the rotation term of that balance it is about \(\mathrm{Le}_\phi^2/(2\rho_0\lvert\Delta\Omega\rvert)\), with the Lehnert number of the wound field

      \[ \mathrm{Le}_\phi = \mathrm{Le}\,\frac{\max\lvert B_\phi\rvert}{\max\lvert\mathbf B_p\rvert} . \]

    The thermal-wind balance with the field. The thermal wind sets the latitudinal entropy that balances the differential rotation (Differential rotation §2.4). With \(\mathbf F_0\) added to that balance, the \(\phi\)-component of its curl reads

    \[ \frac{\mathrm{Beyonce}\,g}{r}\,\frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta} = G^2 s\,\frac{\partial(1 + \Delta\Omega)^2}{\partial z} + \big[\nabla\times\mathbf F_0\big]_\phi , \]

    and kore keeps only the first term on the right. For a toroidal field alone the second is

    \[ \big[\nabla\times\mathbf F_0\big]_\phi = -\frac{\mathrm{Le}^2}{\rho_0\,s}\,\frac{\partial B_\phi^2}{\partial z} + \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2\rho_0'}{2\rho_0^2\,r\,s^2}\,\frac{\partial(s^2B_\phi^2)}{\partial\theta} , \]

    the hoop stress and, with a varying density, the magnetic pressure. It vanishes for a field without current in the fluid: a poloidal field with \(D_n h_n = 0\) for every degree, that is \(h_n = a\,r^n + b\,r^{-(n+1)}\), and no toroidal part. With a uniform density it also vanishes for the toroidal 'Malkus' field with B0_tor_l = 1, \(B_\phi \propto s\), whose force is then a gradient that pressure balances. For other fields, with the field and the rotation varying on the same scale, the ratio of the two terms is about

    \[ \epsilon = \frac{\mathrm{Le}^2\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2}{2\rho_0\lvert\Delta\Omega\rvert} , \]

    condition 1 of Magnetic field; item 2 above is the same estimate for the wound field. A field varying on a shorter scale \(\ell\) raises it by the ratio of the scales, or by its square for the tension of curved poloidal lines.

    In the mode frequencies. The thermal wind enters the frequency squared of a mode at order \(2\Delta\Omega\) (Differential rotation §1.3), so the error \(\epsilon\) moves it by about \(\mathrm{Le}^2\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2/\rho_0\), and the frequency \(\omega\) of a mode by about \(\mathrm{Le}^2\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2/2\rho_0\omega\), for modes that feel the baroclinicity and weighted by their share of the magnetised region. The field's direct force on a mode, which the linear problem includes, enters the frequency squared as \(\mathrm{Le}^2\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert^2k_\parallel^2/\rho_0\), with \(k_\parallel\) the mode's wavenumber along the field: larger by \((k_\parallel r)^2\).

    Uniform rotation. Where \(\Delta\Omega\) is uniform there is no thermal wind, and \(\mathbf F_0\) is balanced by an entropy change \(\delta S\) with \((\mathrm{Beyonce}\,g/r)\,\partial_\theta\delta S = [\nabla\times\mathbf F_0]_\phi\). Relative to the background stratification it is about \((v_A/N\ell)^2\), with \(v_A\) the Alfvén speed, \(N\) the buoyancy frequency and \(\ell\) the scale of the field: of order \(10^{-4}\) for a \(10^5\) G field on a tenth of the solar radius just below the tachocline (\(N \approx 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)), and larger nearer the convection zone, where \(N\) falls to zero.

    Energy budgets

    For each mode, spin_doctor checks that the energy equations close: the rate of change of each energy must equal the power that feeds it, so a large residual flags an unresolved or wrong mode. With a field, spin_doctor adds the power of the Lorentz force to the kinetic budget (residσ, residω), checks the budget of the induction equation in the fluid (resid𝐛), and checks the full budget including the field outside the fluid (resid𝐄). It prints resid𝐛, resid𝐄 and Mag/Kin and writes magnetic.dat:

    column name meaning
    1 ME magnetic energy in the fluid (without Hendrik, as conductive_IC; Mag/Kin is Hendrik ME/KE)
    2 Mdfs magnetic diffusion
    3 Indu induction work
    4 residb resid𝐛, the relative residual of the induction budget
    5, 6 Wlor, Wlor_im power of the Lorentz force, real and imaginary parts (in residσ, residω)
    7 MEic magnetic energy in the inner core (its field, or the potential field below an insulating one)
    8 MEext energy of the potential field outside the outer wall (beyond r_surface with a resolved mantle)
    9 Ohm_f Ohmic loss in the fluid
    10 Ohm_ic Ohmic loss in a conducting inner core
    11 residE resid𝐄, the full energy budget (nan with stress-free walls and centrifugal = 1, with thin layers and mu ≠ 1, and with rotating solids unless vtrq and mtrq are 1)
    12 MEc with centrifugal = 1, a thin layer or a resolved mantle: the energy that the deformation adds (0 without it)
    13, 14 E_w, Ohm_w with a thin layer or a resolved mantle: the magnetic energy stored in the thin layers or in the mantle, and their Ohmic loss
    15, 16 eps_twa_o, eps_twa_i with a thin layer or a resolved mantle: \(\varepsilon_\text{TWA}\) of the outer and of the inner thin layer (0 where there is none)

    with the integrals over the fluid unless stated, and with the Lorentz force per volume without Hendrik \(\mathbf G = (\nabla\times\mathbf b)\times\mathbf B_0 + (\nabla\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf b\),

    \[ \mathrm{ME} = \int\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2 dV, \qquad \mathrm{Mdfs} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\int\mathbf b\cdot\nabla^2\mathbf b\,dV, \qquad \mathrm{Indu} = \int\mathbf b\cdot\nabla\times(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0)\,dV, \]
    \[ \mathrm{resid}\mathbf b = \frac{\lvert 2\sigma\,\mathrm{ME} - \mathrm{Indu} - \mathrm{Mdfs}\rvert}{\max(\lvert 2\sigma\,\mathrm{ME}\rvert, \lvert\mathrm{Indu}\rvert, \lvert\mathrm{Mdfs}\rvert)}, \qquad \mathrm{Wlor} + i\,\mathrm{Wlor\_im} = \mathrm{Hendrik}\cdot 2\int\mathbf u^*\cdot\mathbf G\,dV, \]
    \[ \mathrm{MEic} = \tfrac12\int_{\mathrm{ic}}\frac{\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2}{\mu_{i2o}}\,dV, \qquad \mathrm{MEext} = \tfrac12\int_{r > 1}\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2dV, \qquad \mathrm{Ohm\_f} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\int\lvert\nabla\times\mathbf b\rvert^2dV, \qquad \mathrm{Ohm\_ic} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\,\eta_i\int_{\mathrm{ic}}\frac{\lvert\nabla\times\mathbf b\rvert^2}{\mu_{i2o}}\,dV . \]

    The full budget. resid𝐄 (conductive_IC's, utils4pp.em_budget) closes

    \[ 2\sigma[\mathrm{KE} + \mathrm{Hendrik}(\mathrm{ME} + \mathrm{MEic} + \mathrm{MEext})] = \mathrm{Dkin} + \mathrm{Wthm} + \mathrm{Wlor} + \mathrm{Hendrik}(\mathrm{Indu} - \mathrm{Ohm\_f} - \mathrm{Ohm\_ic}) : \]

    unlike resid𝐛 it sees the wall and ICB conditions. The \(1/\mu_{i2o}\) of the inner core's energy and Ohmic loss is not in conductive_IC's em_budget (there resid𝐄 is \(10^{-2}\) at mu_i2o = 2). Without the flow (hydro = 0) it is \(2\sigma(\mathrm{ME} + \mathrm{MEic} + \mathrm{MEext}) = \mathrm{Indu} - \mathrm{Ohm\_f} - \mathrm{Ohm\_ic}\), without Hendrik and for any walls (Indu is then the induction by a differential rotation, or 0).

    Slipping walls. Where the fluid slips along an impenetrable wall (bco or bci 0, or ViscosD = 0), \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) carries magnetic energy through the wall, into the field beyond it (and into a thin layer). resid𝐄 then uses the volume work of the induction alone, Indu minus its wall terms (utils4pp.INDU_WALLS, from the \((r, \theta)\) grid), as with a differential rotation (below).

    Thin layers (utils4pp.em_budget, twa_budget, as conductive_IC). The layers store \(E_w = \tfrac{cr}{2}\oint\lvert\mathbf b_t\rvert^2dS + \tfrac{h}{2p}\oint\lvert b_r\rvert^2dS\) (the poloidal tangential field: the toroidal condition has no \(c\) term; the second term only for finite \(p\)), and lose \(\mathrm{Ohm\_w} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\oint\lvert\mathbf K\rvert^2 dS/(c_1 r)\), with \(\mathbf K = \hat{\mathbf n}\times(\mathbf b_\text{beyond} - \mathbf b)\) the layer's current (\(\hat{\mathbf n}\) out of the fluid, \(\mathbf b_\text{beyond}\) the potential field beyond the layer): \(c_1\lvert r\nabla^2_l f\rvert^2\) from the poloidal condition (with the radial induction of a slip, and that of a radial boundary flow, utils4pp.radial_lapF: all of it where the solid is at rest, 'rigid', half its toroidal part where the layer deforms across a toroidal \(\mathbf B_0\), background.thin_drop) and \(\lvert g\rvert^2/c_1\) from the toroidal one. The field beyond the layer, which MEext and MEic count beyond \(r \pm h\), follows from its conditions. The full budget gains \(\mathrm{Hendrik}\,E_w\) on the left and \(-\mathrm{Hendrik}\,\mathrm{Ohm\_w}\) on the right. It closes exactly for \(c = 0\), and to \(O(c\,c_1)\) otherwise, the order of the approximation itself (resid𝐄 is then about \(\varepsilon_\text{TWA}\) or below); not with \(\mu \neq 1\).

    Resolved mantle (utils4pp.mantle_budget, as conductive_IC). The layer stores \(E_w = \tfrac12\int\lvert\mathbf b\rvert^2dV\) and loses \(\mathrm{Ohm\_w} = \mathrm{MagnetD}\int\lvert\nabla\times\mathbf b\rvert^2/\sigma\,dV\) over \(1 \le r \le r_s\) (Gauss-Legendre in \(r\)), and MEext is the potential field beyond \(r_s\). The full budget gains them as for thin layers, and closes exactly. A fluid slipping along the layer carries energy into it through \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\), as into a thin layer (INDU_WALLS, above).

    Moving walls (forced runs, and free walls bco or bci 2). Where a wall moves radially the pressure works on the fluid there. That work, Wp, enters residσ and the right-hand side of resid𝐄 with the magnetic part of the pressure, the consoidal part of the Lorentz force at the wall (utils4pp.wall_powers, lorentz_walls). In a forced run the motion of the boundary also works on the field, in a way that follows how the solid behind it moves (Forced runs):

    • Where the solid moves with the fluid at the wall: a no-slip wall that moves along itself (the libration), and a thin conducting layer under 'deforming'. The wall's velocity \(\mathbf V\) (\(\mathbf u_t\) at a no-slip wall, plus \(u_r\hat{\mathbf r}\) at a thin layer under 'deforming') carries the flux of \(\mathbf V\times\mathbf B_0\) out of the fluid, which resid𝐄 keeps in the induction work (INDU_ROT_SOLID). At a thin layer it is the work against the Lorentz force on its current \(\mathbf K\) (above), \(2\,\mathrm{Re}\oint(\mathbf V\times\mathbf B_0)\cdot\mathbf K^*dS\), with \(\mathbf B_0\) its mean across the layer (half the toroidal part, background.thin_drop). At a conducting inner core or the resolved mantle it is the flux of the jump of the tangential electric field, \(2\,\mathrm{Re}\oint\big((\mathbf V\times\mathbf B_0)\times\mathbf b_T^*\big)\cdot \mathbf n\,dS\), with \(\mathbf b_T\) the toroidal part of \(\mathbf b\) (the only part with which a consoidal field has a flux).
    • A thick solid that deforms ('deforming', background.deforms). Its surface moves with the fluid, so the tangential electric field is continuous at the wall and the fluxes through it cancel. The work of its motion inside, \(2\,\mathrm{Re}\int(\mathbf v_s\times\mathbf B_0)\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf b)^*dV\) over the solid, joins the induction work (utils4pp.deform_work, on a Gauss grid in \(r\) and \(\cos\theta\), apart from the rows).
    • 'rigid'. The flow passes through the wall into the solid at rest, whose rows take its \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\): its flux leaves the fluid for the solid's field, as at a slipping wall, and stays out of the induction work (INDU_WALLS).

    With a thin layer whose \(c\) is not zero this holds to the order of the approximation, as without the forcing.

    Rotating solids (rotdyn = 1). The solids store their rotational and gravitational energy, and exchange energy with the fluid through the torques: the full budget gains that energy on the left, and the power of the viscous torques and the inner core's relaxation loss on the right, while the \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) flux through the moving no-slip walls is minus the power of the magnetic torques and leaves the induction work (INDU_WALLS). Only with both torques on the solids (vtrq and mtrq 1); Rotating solids equations.

    How the terms are computed. spin_doctor's Lorentz force (utils4pp.lorentz4pp) acts per volume, and its power \(\int\mathbf u^*\cdot\mathbf G\,dV\) has no \(\rho_0\). Its induction term (induction4pp) takes the components of \(\mathbf u\) with their \(\lambda_1\) part, and holds for the non-solenoidal \(\mathbf u\) of a variable density (resid𝐛 closes to \(10^{-10}\) with 'Wu 2005', Checks). These spectral formulas are used for a poloidal field of degree 1 alone, with the closed-form couplings and no differential rotation. Otherwise (utils4pp.use_grid: a toroidal part, a poloidal field of degree other than 1, a differential rotation, the recurrence-algebra couplings, or KORE_MAG_GRID=1 in the environment) both come instead from utils4pp.magnetic_powers_grid, computed on an \((r,\theta)\) grid from the vector fields \(\mathbf u\), \(\mathbf b\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf b\), \(\mathbf B_0\), \(\nabla\times\mathbf B_0\) (with the wall terms of the induction work); for a poloidal field it agrees with the spectral formulas to the 10 printed digits.

    With a differential rotation. spin_doctor's induction work includes \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\) (on the \((r, \theta)\) grid). Because \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\) does not vanish at a wall where \(\Delta\Omega \ne 0\), it carries magnetic energy through the wall into the field outside: resid𝐛 (the fluid) uses the induction work with its wall terms, resid𝐄 (the full budget, which counts the energy outside) the volume work alone (utils4pp.INDU_WALLS), plus the power of the rotation's force on the flow. Where a conducting solid turns with the fluid (a conducting inner core, a thin layer, a resolved mantle), the flux of \(\mathbf U_0\times\mathbf b\) through its surface is the induction work of the solid's own rotation, \(\mathrm{Re}\int(\mathbf V\times\mathbf b)\cdot(\nabla\times\mathbf b)^*dV\) over it (\(\mathbf V\) rigid, the potential field beyond carries none), and resid𝐄 keeps it (utils4pp.INDU_ROT_SOLID). Without it resid𝐄 was about \(10^{-4}\) for a conducting inner core turning with \(\Delta\Omega(r_i) \neq 0\); with it the budget is the same in every frame.
