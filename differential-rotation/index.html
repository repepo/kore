    Differential rotation

    kore can compute the linear oscillation modes of a star or planet whose rotation rate varies with radius and latitude, not only of a rigidly rotating one. The background rotation is

    \[ \Omega(r,\theta) = \Omega_{\rm ref}\left[1 + \Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\right], \qquad \Delta\Omega = \sum_n f_n(r)\thinspace P_n(\cos\theta), \quad n = 0, 2, 4, \dots \]

    Here \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) is the rotation rate of the frame in which kore solves, and \(\Delta\Omega\) is the relative departure from it, written as a sum of Legendre polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) with radial coefficients \(f_n(r)\). Only even \(n\) appear, so the rotation is symmetric about the equator.

    This page is the user guide: what is modelled, the parameters, the built-in profiles and their limits, and two worked examples. The equations and how the code builds them are on the page Equations and implementation, the comparisons with published results on Benchmarks, and runs without thermal diffusion on ThermaD = 0.

    What is modelled

    kore solves in a frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\). In that frame the differential rotation is a steady azimuthal flow,

    \[ \mathbf{U}_ 0 = G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace r\sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf{e}}_ \phi , \]

    where \(G\) = Gaspard \(= \Omega_{\rm ref}\tau\) is the frame's rotation rate in kore's time unit \(\tau\) (1 with timescale = 'rotation'). Gaspard only sets the time unit; the frame itself is fixed by the profile, since \(\Delta\Omega\) is measured relative to it (diff_rot_frame for the solar profiles). The perturbation \(\mathbf u\) is advected and sheared by this flow. In the momentum equation this adds

    \[ G\left[\Delta\Omega\thinspace (i\thinspace m\thinspace \mathbf{u} + 2\hat{\mathbf{z}}\times\mathbf{u}) + r\sin\theta\thinspace (\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\Delta\Omega)\thinspace \hat{\mathbf{e}}_ \phi\right]. \]

    The first part is the advection by the local rotation and its Coriolis force, the second the transport of angular momentum by the perturbation across the shear. The density stratification is carried through the anelastic form of the velocity,

    \[ \mathbf{u} = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P\thinspace \mathbf{r}) + \nabla\times(T\thinspace \mathbf{r}), \]

    with \(P\) and \(T\) the poloidal and toroidal scalars that kore solves for.

    With thermal = 1 the entropy equation gains up to three terms,

    \[ i\thinspace m G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace s + u_r\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial r} + \frac{u_\theta}{r}\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta}. \]

    The first is the advection of the entropy perturbation \(s\) by \(\mathbf U_0\). The other two are the advection of a latitudinal background entropy \(S_{tw}(r,\theta)\) by the perturbation. \(S_{tw}\) is either the thermal wind that balances \(\Omega(r,\theta)\) (the latitudinal entropy a differentially rotating star needs to be in equilibrium; Bekki, Cameron & Gizon 2022, eq. 27) or prescribed. These thermal-wind terms are not small corrections: in a convection zone they act at the same order as the momentum terms (thermal-wind balance).

    Not modelled:

    • the background is fixed: nothing maintains or evolves \(\Omega\) and \(S_{tw}\) against diffusion;
    • \(\rho_0\), \(T_0\) and \(g\) stay spherical (no centrifugal distortion);
    • there is no magnetic field.

    Where it lives. Everything specific to the differential rotation (profiles, thermal wind, couplings, the terms of each row) is in bin/diff_rot.py, built on the angular algebra of bin/angular.py; the details are in differential_rotation_details.md. The rotation profiles do not depend on the background model (background.md).

    Frame and units

    \(\Delta\Omega\) is relative to the frame, and \(G\) converts it to the time unit, so any timescale works. Two things follow:

    • ViscosD, ThermaD and Beyonce must be computed for \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\). For 'solar' the frame must rotate at 435 nHz; for 'solar_P4' and 'tabulated' at diff_rot_frame (456 nHz by default). The other types are dimensionless.
    • With thermal_wind = 2 the eigenvalues do not depend on the choice of frame. With thermal_wind = 1 (linear in \(\Delta\Omega\)) they do, at second order in \(\Delta\Omega\): by 0.1–0.9 nHz for solar inertial modes.

    Eigenvalues are in the rotating frame of \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\), as for uniform rotation.

    Quick start

    par.diff_rot           = 1
par.diff_rot_type      = 'conical'   # see the table of profiles below
par.diff_rot_ampl = -0.25            # the factor of ΔΩ: ε for 'conical'
par.thermal_wind       = 1           # only read with thermal = 1; needs Beyonce > 0

    Then run submatrices.py, assemble.py and solve.py as usual. As for every run, lmax - m + 1 must be a multiple of 2*ncpus (assemble.py stops otherwise).

    Parameters

    parameter default meaning
    diff_rot 0 1 adds the differential-rotation terms
    diff_rot_type 'Y20' the profile, see below
    diff_rot_ampl 1.0 the factor of \(\Delta\Omega\), for every type (table below)
    diff_rot_param 0.0 the shape parameter: \(\sigma\) of 'shellular', p in 'user' expressions
    diff_rot_term_amp [1.0]*9 \(a_0, a_2, \dots, a_{16}\): factors of the \(P_0, P_2, \dots, P_{16}\) terms of \(\Delta\Omega\), any type, after diff_rot_ampl (missing entries are 1); e.g. [1, 1, 0] drops the \(P_4\) part of 'solar_P4'
    diff_rot_profiles {} 'user': {n: 'numpy expression'}, the shape \(f_n\) of each even degree (below)
    diff_rot_frame 456.0 'solar_P4', 'tabulated': \(\Omega_{\rm ref}/2\pi\) in nHz
    diff_rot_solar [464, -76, -50, 433.5, 0.71, 0.045] 'solar_P4': \(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{RZ}\) (nHz), \(r_{tc}\), \(d_{tc}\)
    diff_rot_file '' 'tabulated': the .npz file, absolute or relative to the run directory
    diff_rot_nmax 8 'tabulated': highest degree kept, even (up to 16 with thermal_wind = 2)
    diff_rot_rdeg 24 'tabulated': degree of the Chebyshev fit of each \(f_n\) in \(r\)
    diff_rot_window None 'tabulated': None fits over the shell if ricb > 0 and is 'sun' if ricb = 0; 'sun': one fit over the whole Sun, even in \(r\), the same for any ricb
    diff_rot_sun_deg 60 'sun': degree of that fit
    thermal_wind 1 heat-equation terms, see below
    thermal_wind_radial 1 0 drops \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\)
    thermal_wind_amplitude 1.0 factor on \(S_{tw}\) (thermal_wind 1, 2)
    dSdtheta 0.0 thermal_wind = 3: \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\)

    The background (structure, stratification, diffusivity, r_out) has its own page, background.md.

    Profiles

    \(a\) = diff_rot_ampl, \(p\) = diff_rot_param. For the solar types \(x\) is the fractional solar radius: \(x = r\times\)r_out with a solar structure (background.md), \(x = r\) otherwise.

    diff_rot_type \(\Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\) degrees notes
    'rigid' \(a\) \(P_0\) test profile: a uniform offset of the rotation rate, with an exact answer (below); \(a = 0\) gives uniform rotation with the diff-rot machinery on (needed for thermal_wind = 3)
    'Y20' \(a\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta)\) \(P_2\) \(P_2\) unnormalised, despite the name
    'Y20-wall-bounded' \(a\thinspace (1 - r)(r - r_i)\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta)\), \(r_i\) = ricb \(P_2\) not smooth at \(r = 0\): use ricb > 0
    'shellular' \(a\thinspace (r^\sigma - 1)\), \(\sigma = p\) \(P_0\) Baruteau & Rieutord 2013 (\(a = 1\)); not smooth at \(r = 0\) unless \(\sigma\) is even and positive
    'shellular_boussinesq' \(\tfrac12 N_0^2\thinspace (1 - r^2)\), \(N_0^2 = a\) \(P_0\) Mirouh et al. 2016; use thermal_wind = 0 (their background has no thermal wind), structure 'Boussinesq' and stratification 'linear'
    'cylindrical' \(\varepsilon\thinspace r^2\sin^2\theta\), \(\varepsilon = a\) \(P_0\), \(P_2\) Baruteau & Rieutord 2013; barotropic, \(S_{tw} = 0\)
    'conical' \(\varepsilon\sin^2\theta\), \(\varepsilon = a\) \(P_0\), \(P_2\) Guenel et al. 2016
    'solar' \(a\thinspace \times\) helioseismic fit, \(P_0 + P_2\), tanh step at \(x = 0.71\) of width 0.1 \(P_0\), \(P_2\) relative to 435 nHz; 4.5 nHz too fast at the equator and 12 nHz at the pole compared with the observations
    'solar_P4' \(a\thinspace (\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1)\), \(\Omega\) the fit below \(P_0\), \(P_2\), \(P_4\) diff_rot_solar, diff_rot_frame; defaults from Mukhopadhyay et al. 2026
    'tabulated' \(a\thinspace (\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1)\), \(\Omega\) from diff_rot_file \(P_0 \dots P_{n_{\max}}\) below
    'user' \(a\sum_n f_n(r) P_n(\cos\theta)\), \(f_n\) from diff_rot_profiles the keys of diff_rot_profiles below

    'rigid'. The eigenvalues are known exactly: with \(\lambda_0\) the diff_rot = 0 eigenvalue,

    \[ \lambda(a, G) = \lambda_0\big(G(1 + a)\big) - i\thinspace m G a . \]

    'solar_P4'. A rigid radiative zone joined by an erf step at the tachocline \(r_{tc}\) (width \(d_{tc}\)) to a convection zone whose rotation depends on latitude only:

    \[ \frac{\Omega}{2\pi} = \Omega_{RZ} + \tfrac12\left[\Omega_{CZ}(\theta) - \Omega_{RZ}\right]\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\thinspace \frac{x - r_{tc}}{d_{tc}}\right], \]
    \[ \Omega_{CZ} = c_0 + c_2\cos^2\theta + c_4\cos^4\theta , \]

    with diff_rot_solar = [\(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{RZ}\), \(r_{tc}\), \(d_{tc}\)] (nHz, and fractions of R☉).

    'user'. diff_rot_profiles gives the shape of each even degree as a numpy expression of r (kore radius), x (fractional solar radius) and p (diff_rot_param), with sin, cos, tan, arcsin, arccos, arctan, sinh, cosh, tanh, exp, log, log10, sqrt, abs, sign, pi, where, minimum, maximum, erf, erfc. For example, the cylindrical rotation as a 'user' profile (it gives the same matrices as 'cylindrical'):

    par.diff_rot_type     = 'user'
par.diff_rot_profiles = {0: '(2/3)*r**2', 2: '-(2/3)*r**2'}
par.diff_rot_ampl     = -0.25

    The degrees must be even, and the expressions must not contain #: spin_doctor writes them at the end of each params.dat row, after a #. Any degree up to 16 works with thermal_wind = 2 (32 with thermal_wind = 1).

    'tabulated'. The file holds ome[θ, r] (nHz), r (R☉) and theta (rad), the layout of data/diff_rot.npz of the Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 data set. tests/diff_rot/ls_to_tabulated.py converts the Larson & Schou 2018 table to it; bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz is the mean of the 83 JSOC HMI 72-day inversions (hmi.V_sht_2drls, NACOEFF 36) from 2010-04-30 to 2026-06-29 in this layout, on the 151 table radii. kore projects \(\Omega\) on \(P_0 \dots P_{n_{\max}}\) at each table radius, fits each \(f_n\) in \(r\) over the table radii within 0.01 of the shell, and prints the fit residuals and the truncation error in nHz (the largest difference between the table and its expansion up to \(P_{n_{\max}}\)); check that line. For the Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 profile the truncation error near the poles at the surface is up to 17 nHz at diff_rot_nmax = 8; 12 is the working choice.

    Heat equation (thermal = 1)

    thermal_wind chooses which entropy terms are added and where the latitudinal background entropy \(S_{tw}\) comes from. The advection of \(s\) by the differential rotation, the first term, is always included.

    thermal_wind terms added \(S_{tw}\)
    0 \(i\thinspace m G\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace s\) none (e.g. Mirouh et al. 2016)
    1 (default) the three terms thermal wind of \(\Omega\) to first order in \(\Delta\Omega\) (\(S_{tw} \propto G^2/\)Beyonce)
    2 the three terms thermal wind of the full \((1 + \Delta\Omega)^2\) balance; frame independent
    3 the three terms prescribed: \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\), uniform in \(r\) (Bekki et al. 2024)
    • thermal_wind 1 and 2 need Beyonce > 0. With timescale = 'rotation', set_scales() sets Beyonce = 0, so set it after calling set_scales().
    • dSdtheta is in the units of \(s\) (\(\delta s/c_p\)): dSdtheta \(= \Delta_\theta s/c_p\), with \(\Delta_\theta s\) in erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹. Bekki et al. use \(c_p\) = 4.17e8, Model S has about 3.46e8. Negative values give warm poles. The thermal-wind amplitude does not apply.
    • thermal_wind_radial = 0 drops \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\), as Bekki et al. 2022 and Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 do. Keeping it is the consistent choice, but it moves the solar columnar and high-frequency retrograde modes by 5–15 nHz.
    • With the thermal wind \(S_{tw}\) is the only latitudinal entropy gradient; in a convection zone with \(dS_0/dr = 0\) it is also the only background entropy gradient, and its effect on the modes is of the same order as the momentum terms.
    • ThermaD = 0 works with every option; ThermaD = 0 lists what to watch for.

    Resolution and cost

    • A differential rotation couples harmonic degrees \(l\) that uniform rotation keeps apart, so the matrices get wider. Let \(n_{\max}\) be the highest degree of \(\Delta\Omega\). The momentum rows then couple degrees that differ by up to \(n_{\max}\) (\(P \to P\), \(T \to T\)) or \(n_{\max} + 1\) (\(P \leftrightarrow T\)).
    • \(S_{tw}\) has degrees up to \(n_{\max} + 2\) (thermal_wind 1) or \(2n_{\max} + 2\) (thermal_wind 2), and the heat rows couple degrees that far apart. Matrices are therefore widest with 'tabulated' and a large diff_rot_nmax.
    • With ricb = 0 (a full sphere) the profiles are evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\), to keep them smooth at the centre. 'Y20-wall-bounded' and 'shellular' (except even \(\sigma > 0\)) have a kink at \(r = 0\) and submatrices.py prints a WARNING. The 'solar' tachocline step needs N ≈ 400 at ricb = 0.
    • Modes that reach into a radiative interior have short radial wavelengths. In a full sphere with the radiative interior of stratification 'solar_rz', solar inertial modes needed N = 768 to 1024 for the spin_doctor residuals, although their eigenvalues converge at a lower N. Solver has the memory this takes.
    • Check convergence with spin_doctor as for any run; its residuals include the diff-rot terms.

    Output

    spin_doctor adds the diff-rot powers to flow.dat (Wdr, Wdr_im, Ensdr) and thermal.dat (Wdr_thm), and writes the options to params.dat, after the general columns (the order is that of spin_doctor.param_table):

    diff_rot, diff_rot_ampl, thermal_wind, diff_rot_type, dSdtheta,
diff_rot_term_amp_0, _2, ..., _16, thermal_wind_amplitude, thermal_wind_radial,
diff_rot_frame, diff_rot_nmax, diff_rot_rdeg, diff_rot_generic, diff_rot_solar_0 ... _5,
structure, solar_density_deg, solar_density_window_lo, solar_density_window_hi,
solar_rz, solar_rz_dSdr, solar_rz_x1, solar_rz_x2, solar_rz_d, solar_rz_core, solar_rz_xos, solar_rz_dos,
diff_rot_window, diff_rot_hold, solar_rz_delta_cz, solar_rz_delta_sf, drop_tol, diff_rot_param,
stratification, diffusivity

    diff_rot_type is written as its index in diff_rot.TYPES ('Y20' 0, …, 'rigid' 7, 'solar_P4' 8, 'tabulated' 9, 'user' 10); the background columns are described in Background model. A row ends, after a # that np.loadtxt skips, with the 'user' profile definitions and, for 'tabulated', diff_rot_file with the first 12 hex digits of its sha256 (a row with neither has no #). diff_rot_window is 0 for the shell fit and 1 for the whole-Sun fit (with diff_rot_rdeg then the degree used, diff_rot_sun_deg). The columns diff_rot_generic and diff_rot_hold are not used (always 0 and nan); they keep the positions of the columns after them. Start a new params.dat rather than append to one written by another version of kore, which may have other columns.

    Examples

    The set-ups on the page Benchmarks are complete examples: conical and cylindrical rotation in an incompressible fluid, shellular rotation in a stratified Boussinesq fluid, and the solar convection zone with the observed rotation and its thermal wind, with and without a radiative interior. examples/smooth_sun/parameters.py is a full-sphere solar model with a radiative interior and the rotation of bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz.
