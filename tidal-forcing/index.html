    Tidal forcing

    Forcing 14 moves the walls radially in the pattern of a degree-2 tide, the way the tidal bulge raised by a companion moves the surface of a star or a planet. kore returns the flow this drives inside: tidally forced inertial and gravito-inertial waves, their dissipation, and the power that the tide puts in. Repeating the run over a range of forcing_frequency gives the frequency spectrum of the response.

    Two related tides are described at the end of this page:

    • Forcing 1 solves for the wave-like tide of an incompressible fluid, the part of the tide excited by rotation (The wave-like tide).
    • Forcing 2 moves the walls of the ocean of Enceladus with its eccentricity tide (The eccentricity tide of Enceladus).

    What it imposes

    At each wall the radial velocity is

    \[ u_r = A\thinspace Y_2^m(\theta)\thinspace e^{i(m\phi + wt)} + \text{c.c.}, \]

    with amplitude \(A\) = forcing_amplitude_cmb at the outer wall and forcing_amplitude_icb at the inner wall, and frequency \(w\) = forcing_frequency. Three tides are possible:

    tide m symm
    eccentricity tide, \((l, m) = (2, 0)\) 0 1
    obliquity tide, \((2, 1)\) 1 −1
    eccentricity and asynchronous tide, \((2, 2)\) 2 1

    The pattern rotates at the angular velocity \(-w/m\) in the rotating frame, so a negative \(w\) is prograde, as everywhere in kore. For a body spinning at the frame rate \(\Omega\) with a companion on a circular orbit of angular velocity \(n\), the (2, 2) tide has \(w = 2(\Omega - n)/\Omega\) with timescale = 'rotation'. If you know the tidal displacement of the wall rather than its velocity, set the amplitude to \(w\) times the displacement. The flow is proportional to the amplitude, and every power to its square.

    Requirements:

    • no-slip walls (bco = 1, and bci = 1 with an inner core), or an inviscid fluid in a full sphere;
    • one of the three (m, symm) pairs above.

    In a full sphere (ricb = 0) only the outer wall moves. The forcing works with any structure, with buoyancy, differential rotation, a background flow and a magnetic field (Magnetic field). Combining features lists what else it combines with.

    Example: the (2, 2) tide in a rotating shell

    Write in bin/parameters.py

    from params_default import default_params

par = default_params()

par.timescale = 'rotation'
par.Ek = 1e-3
par.set_scales()                  # Gaspard = 1, ViscosD = Ek

# incompressible fluid in a rotating shell, no-slip walls
par.structure = 'Boussinesq'
par.thermal = 0
par.ricb = 0.35
par.bci = 1
par.bco = 1

# the (2, 2) tide: m = 2, equatorially symmetric
par.m = 2
par.symm = 1

par.N = 96
par.lmax = 49
par.ncpus = 2

# radial velocity Y_2^2 at the outer wall, at the frequency w = 1 (retrograde in the frame)
par.forcing = 14
par.forcing_frequency = 1.0
par.forcing_amplitude_cmb = 1.0
par.forcing_amplitude_icb = 0.0

    and run

    ./bin/submatrices.py 2
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/assemble.py
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/solve.py
./bin/spin_doctor.py 2

    It takes a few seconds. spin_doctor prints the power the walls put in (P_in) and the viscous dissipation (Dν), and writes the whole energy budget to flow.dat (Output files):

    import numpy as np
f = np.loadtxt('flow.dat', ndmin=2)[-1]
P_in  = f[20] + f[21] + f[23] + f[24]   # work of the pressure and of the viscous stress at both walls
D_nu  = f[25]                           # viscous dissipation
print(P_in, D_nu)                       # 0.1416912 0.1416912

    With uniform rotation and no buoyancy, all the power that the tide puts in is dissipated by viscosity, so the two numbers agree.

    To compute a spectrum, loop over forcing_frequency. submatrices.py does not depend on it, but assemble.py and solve.py must be run again for each frequency.

    The energy of the response

    For a forced, periodic response, spin_doctor evaluates the energy budget of Energy balance:

    \[ P_{\rm in} = W_p + W_\nu^{\rm wall} = D_\nu - W_b - W_\Omega , \]
    quantity columns meaning
    \(P_{\rm in}\) flow.dat 21 + 22 + 24 + 25 the power the moving walls put into the fluid: the tidal power
    \(W_p\) flow.dat 21 (inner wall), 22 (outer wall) the part done by pressure
    \(W_\nu^{\rm wall}\) flow.dat 24, 25 the part done by viscous stress
    \(D_\nu\) flow.dat 26 the viscous dissipation, the heat released
    \(-W_b\) thermal.dat 5 (with the opposite sign) the power given to the entropy perturbation, released by thermal diffusion
    \(W_\Omega\) flow.dat 18 the power taken from the differential rotation

    Which one to report depends on the question:

    • Orbital and spin evolution depend on the tidal power \(P_{\rm in}\). It equals the viscous dissipation \(D_\nu\) only with uniform rotation and no buoyancy.
    • Tidal heating is \(D_\nu\), plus the thermal diffusion of the entropy perturbation with thermal = 1.
    • Exchange with the differential rotation, \(W_\Omega\), can have either sign. The waves can draw energy from the shear, so that the fluid dissipates more than the tide puts in, or give energy to it. kore keeps the background rotation fixed, so report \(W_\Omega\) together with the others.

    All powers are averages over one period of the oscillation.

    Interpreting the response

    • Unstable modes. The forced solve gives an answer even when the background has growing modes. The answer is the part of the flow at the forcing frequency, which is meaningful only if those instabilities are saturated by something else. It is not meaningful near a growing mode: run an eigenvalue solve (forcing = 0, itau = forcing_frequency) and check that no growing mode has a frequency close to the forcing.
    • Critical layers. With differential rotation, waves can meet critical layers, where the forcing frequency matches the local rotation. Only viscosity resolves them; check that the response converges with N and lmax.
    • No-slip walls. Forcing 14 imposes zero tangential velocity at the moving walls, which creates viscous boundary layers there. On the solid surface of a core this is physical. At the free surface of a star or a giant planet it is not. Compare runs at several Ekman numbers to tell the dissipation in the wall layers from that of the waves inside.
    • Resolution. The pressure work \(W_p\) converges more slowly with N than the kinetic energy, especially when the density varies strongly. Accept a response only when spin_doctor's residσ is below \(10^{-5}\).

    Parameters

    parameter meaning
    forcing 14
    forcing_frequency \(w\), in the rotating frame; negative is prograde
    forcing_amplitude_cmb amplitude of the radial velocity at the outer wall
    forcing_amplitude_icb amplitude at the inner wall (not used in a full sphere)
    m, symm (0, 1), (1, −1) or (2, 1)
    bci, bco bco = 1, and bci = 1 with an inner core

    The wave-like tide (forcing 1)

    In a fluid body the tide has two parts. The equilibrium tide is the large-scale flow that follows the tidal bulge as it moves. The wave-like tide is the response that the Coriolis force on that flow excites: inertial waves, whose dissipation depends strongly on frequency. Forcing 1 solves for the wave-like part alone, as Lin & Ogilvie (2018) do.

    The equilibrium tide of degree 2 in a shell is

    \[ \mathbf u_e = \nabla\big[X(r)\thinspace Y_2^m(\theta)\thinspace e^{i(m\phi + wt)}\big] + \text{c.c.}, \qquad X(r) = -\frac{3a}{4}\thinspace\frac{r^2 + \tfrac23 r_i^5 r^{-3}}{1 - r_i^5} , \]

    with \(a\) = forcing_amplitude_cmb, \(w\) = forcing_frequency and \(r_i\) = ricb. The Coriolis force on this flow acts as a body force on the wave-like tide, which kore solves for with walls at rest. The solution in flow.npy is the wave-like tide; add \(\mathbf u_e\) to get the whole tide.

    Requirements:

    • a uniform fluid without stratification or field: structure = 'Boussinesq', thermal = 0, compositional = 0, magnetic = 0;
    • (m, symm) = (0, 1), (1, −1) or (2, 1);
    • bco 0 or 1, and bci 0 or 1 with an inner core;
    • no drag at the outer wall;
    • with m = 2, an inner core (in a full sphere this tide drives nothing). With m = 0 or 1 a full sphere works, and the fluid may then be inviscid.

    Differential rotation and a background flow work.

    Example. In the example of forcing 14 above, set

    par.forcing = 1
par.forcing_frequency = 1.0
par.forcing_amplitude_cmb = 1.0

    and run the same four steps. The walls now do no work: the power comes from the body force (flow.dat column 42), and the viscosity dissipates it.

    The eccentricity tide of Enceladus (forcing 2)

    Enceladus has an ocean between an icy shell and a rocky core. On its eccentric orbit the tide stretches and squeezes both, so the walls of the ocean move, radially and along themselves. Forcing 2 moves the walls with the fits of Rekier et al. (2019, appendix A.1), which depend on the radius ratio ricb, and kore returns the flow in the ocean, with its inertial waves and their dissipation.

    forcing_amplitude_cmb scales the motion at both walls (1: the fits as they are). The tide of a moon in synchronous rotation has three parts, each a separate run whose powers add: m = 0, and m = 2 with forcing_frequency equal to −Gaspard (prograde) and to Gaspard (retrograde).

    Requirements:

    • ricb between 16/21 and 1, where the fits hold (Enceladus has about 0.88);
    • (m, symm) = (0, 1) or (2, 1);
    • no-slip walls (bci = bco = 1) and a viscous fluid.

    Any structure, buoyancy, differential rotation and a background flow work, as does a magnetic field with insulating walls. kore stops with a conducting solid behind a wall (Magnetic field). The power is read as for forcing 14 (The energy of the response).
