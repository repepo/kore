    Background flow

    With bgflow = 1 the fluid carries a steady, axisymmetric background flow, and kore computes the waves and instabilities on top of it. Differential rotation (diff_rot = 1, Background model) is the special case of a purely azimuthal flow. A background flow can also circulate in the meridional plane (up along the axis and back near the walls, or the reverse), and its rotation can have shapes that diff_rot cannot describe.

    The main use is the flow driven by turning walls, computed by kore itself in a Spherical Couette flow run. You can also give the flow as expressions of the radius.

    What it adds

    The background flow carries the perturbations and is distorted by them. kore adds both effects to the momentum, heat, composition and induction equations. The background may also have its own entropy and composition, which the perturbation flow then carries too.

    The background flow is written as

    \[ \mathbf U = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\sum_n\left[\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r)\right], \]

    where \(Y_n\) is the Legendre polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\cos\theta\), \(P_n(r)\) and \(T_n(r)\) are the poloidal and toroidal radial functions, and Ro = bgflow_Ro is the strength of the flow relative to the rotation rate of the frame. The equations are in Background flow equations.

    The background flow must:

    • be symmetric about the equator;
    • not cross the walls;
    • turn rigidly at a conducting solid (a conducting inner core, thin conducting layer or resolved mantle), so that the solid can turn with the fluid.

    It works with any structure, in a shell or a full sphere, with differential rotation, a magnetic field, conducting solids, the constant drag at the outer wall, the forcings and the free decay. It does not work with the centrifugal deformation, the rotating solids or the topographic drag.

    With a magnetic field, the background field is in general not steady under the background flow. submatrices.py prints how far it is from steady and warns when the mismatch is significant: the linear problem assumes that something maintains the background.

    A background from turning walls

    Run a Spherical Couette flow (forcing 12), then, in its folder,

    python3 bin/bgflow.py 12

    This writes bgflow.npz, holding the flow up to Legendre degree 12, normalized to Ro = 1. Copy it to the folder of the run that uses it, and set bgflow = 1 and the Ro you want. A maximum degree of about 12 is usually enough; thinner boundary layers, at lower Ekman numbers, need more.

    This linear flow is accurate only for small Ro. At larger Ro the flow carries its own momentum, which the linear equations leave out. With

    python3 bin/bgflow.py 12 nonlinear

    kore solves the full steady equations of the axisymmetric flow at the Ro of the run (forcing_amplitude_icb over Gaspard) by Newton's method (Background flow equations). The file is then valid only at that Ro, and a run that reads it must set bgflow_Ro to that value.

    The file also records the density, ricb and which wall turned, and kore stops if a run reads it with incompatible parameters. With thermal = 1 or compositional = 1 it also holds the background entropy and composition that the flow produced. bgflow.py does not support runs with a magnetic field.

    Example: waves on the flow of an inner core that turns

    The flow of Spherical Couette flow at Ek \(10^{-3}\), nonlinear at Ro = 1 (the inner core turning at twice the rate of the frame), and the waves of \(m = 2\) on it. First the flow: write in bin/parameters.py

    from params_default import default_params

par = default_params()

par.timescale = 'rotation'
par.Ek = 1e-3
par.set_scales()

par.structure = 'Boussinesq'
par.ricb = 0.35
par.bci = 1
par.bco = 1

par.m = 0
par.symm = 1
par.N = 48
par.lmax = 31
par.ncpus = 2

par.forcing = 12
par.forcing_frequency = 0.0
par.forcing_amplitude_icb = 1.0
par.forcing_amplitude_cmb = 0.0

    and run

    ./bin/submatrices.py 2
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/assemble.py
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/solve.py
python3 bin/bgflow.py 12 nonlinear

    Then, in a new folder with that bgflow.npz, use the same parameters with

    par.m = 2
par.lmax = 29
par.forcing = 0
par.itau = 1.0

par.bgflow = 1
par.bgflow_Ro = 1.0

    and run submatrices.py, assemble.py, solve.py and spin_doctor.py. The eigenvalue nearest \(1i\) is \(-0.1818 + 1.1437i\) with the flow, compared with \(-0.2687 + 0.9803i\) without it. The example takes a couple of minutes, most of it in Newton's method.

    A background from expressions

    With bgflow_profiles the background is given as numpy expressions of r (and ricb), one per radial function and degree, normalized to Ro = 1:

    par.bgflow = 1
par.bgflow_Ro = 0.3
par.bgflow_profiles = {'P2': '0.2*(r-ricb)**2*(1-r)**2',   # a meridional cell in each hemisphere
                       'T1': '0.5*r',                      # a rotation
                       'T3': '(r-ricb)*(1-r)*r',            # and its shear
                       'S2': '0.05*(r-ricb)*(1-r)'}         # an entropy of degree 2 (thermal = 1)

    Each key is a letter and a degree: P and T for the poloidal and toroidal flow, S for the entropy, C for the composition. Poloidal, entropy and composition terms take even degrees, toroidal terms odd degrees. In this example the flow does not cross the walls and turns rigidly there, so the walls may be conducting. In a full sphere the expressions must be smooth at the centre (Regularity at the centre).

    Resolution

    A background flow can make the waves much harder to resolve than in a fluid at rest:

    • The flow strains the waves. A meridional circulation can squeeze the waves into thin layers, for instance at the centre of a full sphere, and the radial resolution N must be raised until the spin_doctor residuals are small.
    • Without diffusion, the flow creates ever finer structure. Without thermal or compositional diffusion, or without viscosity, the background carries the perturbation along its streamlines with nothing to smooth it. This gives a band of frequencies where modes do not converge. Put the target outside that band, and keep only the modes that stay put when N and lmax are raised. For forced runs without diffusion kore stops in such cases (Combining features).

    As with any run, compare two resolutions and check spin_doctor's residuals (Numerical method).

    Output

    spin_doctor.py adds the power exchanged with the background flow to the budgets: in flow.dat the power that the perturbation draws from the background, Wbg (columns 46–48), and in thermal.dat and composition.dat the work of its advection (column 8). A growing mode takes its energy from there. params.dat records bgflow and bgflow_Ro, and the expressions or the name of the file (Output files).

    Parameters

    parameter meaning
    bgflow 1 for a background flow
    bgflow_Ro its strength Ro, relative to the rotation rate of the frame; a nonlinear file is valid only at its own Ro
    bgflow_file the file of the background, 'bgflow.npz' by default, read when bgflow_profiles is empty
    bgflow_profiles the background as expressions, {'P2': '...', 'T1': '...', ...}, normalized to Ro = 1; no # in them
