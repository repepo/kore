    Background flow

    With bgflow = 1 the fluid carries a steady, axisymmetric background flow, and kore solves the waves and instabilities of the fluid on top of it. A differential rotation (diff_rot = 1, Background model) is the special case of a flow along the azimuth. The flow that turning walls drive also turns in the meridional planes (up along the axis and back near the walls, or the reverse), and its rotation has a shape that no rotation profile of diff_rot holds. That flow, written by kore from its own run of turning walls (Spherical Couette flow), is the main use; a flow given as expressions of \(r\) is the other.

    What it adds

    The background flow carries the perturbations and is distorted by them. kore adds both effects to each equation it solves: to the momentum equation the advection of the flow by the background and of the background by the flow, to the heat and composition equations the advection of the entropy and the composition, and to the induction equation the field that the background flow induces from the perturbed field. The background may also carry its own entropy and composition, the ones that the flow brought about in the run that made it; the flow of the perturbation then carries those too.

    The background is

    \[ \mathbf U = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\sum_n\left[\rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r)\right], \]

    with \(Y_n\) the Legendre polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\cos\theta\) and \(\mathrm{Ro}\) = bgflow_Ro, the rate of the flow in units of the rotation rate of the frame (with Gaspard = 0, a frame at rest, in units of \(1/\tau\)). It carries no mass across a sphere: \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf U) = 0\). Its entropy and composition are \(\mathrm{Ro}\sum_n\sigma_n Y_n\) and \(\mathrm{Ro}\sum_n\gamma_n Y_n\), used with thermal = 1 and compositional = 1. The radial functions \(P_n\), \(T_n\), \(\sigma_n\), \(\gamma_n\) (per unit Ro) come from a file or from expressions. The terms are in Background flow equations.

    It works with any structure, in a shell or a full sphere, with or without the diffusion of the entropy and of the composition (a forced run without it has a condition of its own, below), without viscosity in a full sphere, with a differential rotation, a magnetic field, conducting solids, the constant drag at the outer wall and the forcings, in eigenvalue and forced problems, and in the free decay (hydro = 0), where it carries the entropy, the composition and the field. The background must

    • be symmetric about the equator, as kore's perturbations are (symm): \(P_n\), \(\sigma_n\), \(\gamma_n\) of even \(n\) and \(T_n\) of odd \(n\);
    • not cross the walls: \(P_n = 0\) there;
    • turn rigidly at a conducting solid (a conducting inner core, a thin layer that conducts, c1_cmb or c1_icb not 0, a resolved mantle): \(P_n\), \(P_n'\) and every \(T_n\) but \(T_1\) vanish at its wall, and the solid turns with the fluid there, at \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace T_1/r\), so that its field is carried along.

    With a background field (magnetic = 1), submatrices.py prints how far the field is from steady under the background flow, \(\lvert\nabla\times(\mathbf U\times\mathbf B_0)\rvert\thinspace r\) relative to \(\lvert\mathbf U\rvert\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert\), and warns above \(10^{-7}\) (it stops with ferraro_policy = 'refuse'): the linear problem assumes that something maintains the background. A uniform axial field under a rotation about the axis is steady; most other pairs are not. kore stops with centrifugal = 1, rotdyn = 1 and topo_drag = 'rekier'.

    A background from turning walls

    A forcing-12 run of kore gives the steady flow of walls that turn about the axis at their own rates. In its folder,

    python3 bin/bgflow.py 12

    writes bgflow.npz with the degrees \(n \le 12\) of that flow, per unit Ro: the flow of the linear equations, which is proportional to the rates. Ro is the rate of the inner wall over Gaspard (of the outer wall when the inner one is at rest), and the other wall turns at the same fraction of it as in the run. Copy the file to the folder of the run that uses it, with bgflow = 1 and the Ro you want: any Ro gives the linear flow at that rate. The degrees of the run that wrote it go up to its lmax and fall off quickly: in the example below the degrees above 12 move the eigenvalues by about \(10^{-4}\), those above 6 by \(5\times10^{-3}\). Thinner layers, at lower Ek, need more.

    The linear flow holds for small Ro. At larger Ro the flow carries itself along: it moves its own momentum, which the linear equations leave out. With

    python3 bin/bgflow.py 12 nonlinear

    kore solves the full steady equations of the axisymmetric flow at the Ro of the run (forcing_amplitude_icb over Gaspard) by Newton's method, starting from the linear flow and raising Ro in steps of 0.15. The file then holds that Ro, and a run that reads it needs bgflow_Ro at that value: the flow at another Ro is not this one scaled. Each step prints how far the iteration still moves, and it converges in a few iterations, the error squaring at each. The method is on Background flow equations.

    The file also holds the density of the run (the poloidal flow is that density's), the ricb, and which wall turned. kore stops when a run reads it with another ricb, with another density where it has a poloidal flow, or, for a nonlinear file, with another bgflow_Ro. With entropy or composition (thermal, compositional = 1), the file holds the parts of the entropy and of the composition that the flow brought about, \(\sigma_n\) and \(\gamma_n\). bgflow.py does not write the flow of a run with a background field: the steady state of such a run has a field that the flow induces from \(\mathbf B_0\), which the file would leave out. The files that the conductive_IC branch writes are read as they are.

    Example: waves on the flow of an inner core that turns

    The flow of Spherical Couette flow at Ek \(10^{-3}\), nonlinear at Ro = 1 (the inner core turning at twice the rate of the frame), and the waves of \(m = 2\) on it. First the flow: write in bin/parameters.py

    from params_default import default_params

par = default_params()

par.timescale = 'rotation'
par.Ek = 1e-3
par.set_scales()

par.structure = 'Boussinesq'
par.ricb = 0.35
par.bci = 1
par.bco = 1

par.m = 0
par.symm = 1
par.N = 48
par.lmax = 31
par.ncpus = 2

par.forcing = 12
par.forcing_frequency = 0.0
par.forcing_amplitude_icb = 1.0
par.forcing_amplitude_cmb = 0.0

    and run

    ./bin/submatrices.py 2
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/assemble.py
mpiexec -n 2 ./bin/solve.py
python3 bin/bgflow.py 12 nonlinear

    Then, in a new folder with that bgflow.npz, the same parameters with

    par.m = 2
par.lmax = 29
par.forcing = 0
par.itau = 1.0

par.bgflow = 1
par.bgflow_Ro = 1.0

    and run submatrices.py, assemble.py, solve.py and spin_doctor.py. The eigenvalue nearest \(1i\) is \(-0.1818 + 1.1437i\) on the flow, and \(-0.2687 + 0.9803i\) without it. The nonlinear flow differs from the linear flow at Ro = 1 by 6%, and the linear flow gives \(-0.1811 + 1.1491i\): at this rate the nonlinear part moves the eigenvalues by about 0.005. The eigenvalues hold to 8 digits at N 64 and lmax 41, and the conductive_IC branch gives the same ones from its own matrices (tests/bgflow). The whole example takes about two minutes on two cores, most of it in Newton's method.

    A background from expressions

    With bgflow_profiles the background is given as numpy expressions of \(r\) (and ricb, with the functions of diff_rot_profiles), one per radial function and degree, per unit Ro:

    par.bgflow = 1
par.bgflow_Ro = 0.3
par.bgflow_profiles = {'P2': '0.2*(r-ricb)**2*(1-r)**2',   # a meridional cell in each hemisphere
                       'T1': '0.5*r',                      # a rotation
                       'T3': '(r-ricb)*(1-r)*r',            # and its shear
                       'S2': '0.05*(r-ricb)*(1-r)'}         # an entropy of degree 2 (thermal = 1)

    The keys are the kind (P, T, S for \(\sigma_n\), C for \(\gamma_n\)) and the degree. bgflow_file is then not read. This \(P_2\) vanishes at both walls with its derivative, and \(T_3\) vanishes there, so the flow is a rigid rotation at each wall and the walls may be conducting. In a full sphere an expression is evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with the parity \((-1)^n\) of its degree; a smooth flow goes as \(r^n\) times a series in \(r^2\), as 'P2': 'r**2*(1-r**2)**2' (Regularity at the centre).

    Resolution

    A background flow can make the waves much harder to resolve than the same waves in a fluid at rest, in two ways.

    • The flow strains the waves. In a full sphere a meridional circulation of degree 2 is a straining flow at the centre, which presses the waves into thin layers there, where the Chebyshev points are furthest apart. The budgets and irreg₀ then fail, and they converge as N grows: with the circulation \(P_2 = 2r^2(1-r^2)^2\) at Ro = 0.1 and Ek \(10^{-3}\), residσ is \(3\times10^{-1}\) at N 64, \(3\times10^{-4}\) at N 96, \(10^{-8}\) at N 128 and \(10^{-12}\) at N 160.
    • The flow carries what nothing smooths. Without the diffusion of the entropy or of the composition, or without viscosity, the background carries the perturbation along its streamlines with nothing to limit how fine it gets. The frequencies of that transport, \(m\) times the angular velocities of the flow and the rates of its meridional circulation, form a continuous band, and a target in that band gives modes that do not converge. Target outside it, and keep the modes that stay put when N and lmax are raised. A forced run has no target to move: without the diffusion of the entropy or of the composition kore stops it if the background has a meridional circulation, on whose closed streamlines the scalar resonates, or if \(w + m\Omega\) vanishes somewhere in the fluid, \(w\) being the frequency of the run and \(\Omega\) the angular velocity of the background and of a differential rotation (Combining features).

    As with any run, compare two resolutions; spin_doctor's residuals tell when the budget, and so the mode, is resolved (Numerical method).

    Output

    spin_doctor.py adds the power of the background's terms to the budgets: in flow.dat the power of its force on the flow, Wbg, its imaginary part and its enstrophy term (columns 46–48), in thermal.dat and composition.dat the work of its advection (column 8). They enter residσ, residω, residens, resid𝑠, resid𝑐 and resid𝐄. The power Wbg is what the perturbation draws from the shear of the background: a growing mode takes its energy from there. The background puts no torque in the fluid, so resid𝐋 holds as without it, axial and equatorial. params.dat gets bgflow and bgflow_Ro (columns 155–156), and after the # the expressions of the background or the name of its file with the first 12 hexadecimal digits of its sha256 (Output files).

    Parameters

    parameter meaning
    bgflow 1 for a background flow
    bgflow_Ro its rate Ro, in units of the frame's rotation rate (of \(1/\tau\) with Gaspard = 0); a nonlinear file holds at its own Ro only
    bgflow_file the file of the background, 'bgflow.npz' by default, read when bgflow_profiles is empty
    bgflow_profiles the background as expressions, {'P2': '...', 'T1': '...', ...}, per unit Ro; no # in them (they are written to params.dat)

    tests/bgflow checks the background against the conductive_IC branch, its terms against a differential rotation, a change of frame and a computation on a grid, and the budgets (Modifying kore).
