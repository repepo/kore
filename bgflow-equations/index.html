    Background flow equations

    This page gives the background flow of bgflow = 1 (Background flow), the terms it adds to the perturbation equations, how they become rows, what happens at the walls, the powers that spin_doctor reports, and the Newton's method that gives the nonlinear flow of turning walls. They hold for any density, in a shell or a full sphere, with the entropy, the composition and the magnetic field (bin/bgflow.py).

    The background

    The background is written as the perturbations are (Numerical method), with a poloidal and a toroidal scalar for each degree, so that it carries no mass across a sphere whatever its scalars:

    \[ \mathbf U = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\sum_n\left[\frac{1}{\rho_0}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T_n Y_n\thinspace\mathbf r)\right], \qquad S_U = \mathrm{Ro}\sum_n\sigma_n Y_n, \qquad C_U = \mathrm{Ro}\sum_n\gamma_n Y_n , \]

    with \(Y_n\) the Legendre polynomial of degree \(n\) in \(\cos\theta\), \(\mathrm{Ro}\) = bgflow_Ro, \(G\) = Gaspard (1 when Gaspard = 0), and \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf U) = 0\). \(S_U\) and \(C_U\) are the entropy and the composition that the flow brought about in the run that made it. With \(\lambda_1 = (\ln\rho_0)'\) and a prime for \(d/dr\), the components are

    \[ U_r = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\sum_n\frac{n(n+1)P_n}{r}Y_n, \qquad U_\theta = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\sum_n\left(P_n' + \frac{P_n}{r} + \lambda_1 P_n\right)\partial_\theta Y_n, \qquad U_\phi = -\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\sum_n T_n\thinspace\partial_\theta Y_n . \]

    \(T_1 = r\) is a rotation at the rate \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\), and at Ro = 1 \(T_1 = (f_0 - f_2/5)\thinspace r\), \(T_3 = f_2\thinspace r/5\) is the differential rotation \(\Delta\Omega = f_0 + f_2 Y_2\) of diff_rot. The poloidal scalars make the meridional circulation. The vorticity \(\boldsymbol\Omega_U = \nabla\times\mathbf U\) follows degree by degree: \(\Omega_\phi = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\left[(rs_n)'/r - n(n+1)P_n/r^2\right]\partial_\theta Y_n\) from \(P_n\), with \(s_n = P_n' + P_n/r + \lambda_1 P_n\), and \(\Omega_r = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace n(n+1)T_n Y_n/r\), \(\Omega_\theta = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace(rT_n)'\thinspace\partial_\theta Y_n/r\) from \(T_n\).

    Like the perturbations, the background is symmetric about the equator: \(P_n\), \(\sigma_n\), \(\gamma_n\) of even \(n\) and \(T_n\) of odd \(n\).

    The terms

    The perturbation equations, linearised about \(\mathbf U\), \(S_U\) and \(C_U\), gain the advection of the perturbation by the background and of the background by the perturbation. The momentum equation gains

    \[ (\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U = \nabla(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf u) - \mathbf U\times\boldsymbol\omega - \mathbf u\times\boldsymbol\Omega_U , \qquad \boldsymbol\omega = \nabla\times\mathbf u , \]

    on the side of the inertia; the gradient joins the pressure and leaves the rows, which take the curl and the double curl of the equation. The heat and the composition equations gain

    \[ p_0\left(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla s + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_U\right), \qquad \rho_0\left(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla c + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla C_U\right) \]

    on the side of \(\partial_t\), and the induction equation gains \(\nabla\times(\mathbf U\times\mathbf b)\) on the side of the induction. The background itself is held fixed: what keeps \(\mathbf U\), \(S_U\) and \(C_U\) steady (the walls, the buoyancy of \(S_U\) and \(C_U\), the advection of \(\mathbf U\) by itself) belongs to the equations of the background, not to those of the perturbation.

    Rows and implementation

    This section says how the terms become radial operators and where they sit in the matrices. It matters only to change or extend the code.

    • Angular couplings. The background vectors are polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) times \(\hat{\mathbf r}\), \(\sin\theta\thinspace\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}\) or \(\sin\theta\thinspace\hat{\boldsymbol\phi}\), and their products with the perturbation are sparse matrices over the degrees, built exactly with the recurrence algebra of angular.py, as for a background field (Magnetic field equations): a degree \(n\) of \(\mathbf U\) couples the degree \(l\) of a row to \(l'\) with \(\lvert l - l'\rvert \le n + 1\). bgflow.build writes each term as a sum of radial monomials times these matrices, in kore's rows: \(r^5\rho_0\) and \(r^3\rho_0\) times \(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\nabla\times\) and \(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\nabla\times\) of the momentum equation (sections u, v), \(r^2\) times the heat and composition equations (h, c), and the rows f, g of the induction.

    • Labels and profile codes. A monomial \(r^p\thinspace w_B^{(j)}\thinspace X^{(d)}\) of a row is the label {row}{q}w{B}{n}{j}[lho{k} | pss0 | dns0]_D{d}, with \(q\) the power of the row minus \(p\), \(w_B\) a profile code of degree \(n\) and its \(j\)-th derivative, lho{k} a factor \((\ln\rho_0)^{(k)}\), pss0 and dns0 a factor \(p_0\) or \(\rho_0\), and \(X^{(d)}\) the \(d\)-th derivative of the unknown. For example v2wt10_D1 is \(r\thinspace\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace T_1 P_l'\) in the rows of \(T_l\), times its angular coefficient. The profile codes, per degree \(n\) (one character, 0–9 then a–z: degrees up to 35), are

      code profile
      wq \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace n(n+1)P_n/r\)
      ws \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\left(P_n' + P_n/r + \lambda_1 P_n\right)\)
      wt \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace T_n\)
      wh, wd \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace p_0\sigma_n\), \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace p_0\sigma_n'\) (without \(p_0\) where the heat rows are divided by it)
      wc, we \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace\rho_0\gamma_n\), \(\mathrm{Ro}\thinspace\rho_0\gamma_n'\) (without \(\rho_0\) where the rows of the composition are divided by it)

    • Assembly. assemble.bgflow_blocks places the blocks of each row degree for every \(\Delta l\) up to the largest degree of the background plus one, in the rows u, v (columns \(P\), \(T\)), h (\(s\), \(P\), \(T\)), c (\(c\), \(P\), \(T\)) and f, g (columns of the field). operators.background_flow returns each block as the sum of the angular coefficients times the radial operators, divided by \(\mathcal L^3\) of the row as every operator.

    • Without diffusion. With ThermaD = 0 the heat rows are \(r^2(\text{eq})/p_0\) in the \(C^{(1)}\) basis, as with the thermal wind (Differential rotation): \(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_U\) holds \(u_\theta\), which holds \(P_l'\), and \(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla s\) holds \(U_\theta\partial_\theta s/r\). With CompoD = 0 the rows of the composition are \(r^2(\text{eq})/\rho_0\) in \(C^{(1)}\) in the same way.
    • Inviscid runs (ViscosD = 0, full sphere). The terms hold \(P_l'''\) in the rows u and \(P_l''\) in the rows v, so these rows are in \(C^{(3)}\) and \(C^{(2)}\) and are \(r^5\rho_0^2\) and \(r^3\rho_0\) times the equations, as with a magnetic field.
    • Full sphere. Each scalar has the parity \((-1)^n\) of its degree, and the codes wq, ws, wd, we the opposite one. An expression is evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\) with that parity and interpolated at the Chebyshev–Lobatto points of \([-1, 1]\) (of \([r_i, 1]\) in a shell), so that a scalar that vanishes at the wall vanishes there to round-off. A scalar of degree \(n\) is smooth at the centre if it goes as \(r^n\) times a series in \(r^2\); submatrices.py warns for each one that does not (Regularity at the centre).

    The walls

    kore's walls are impenetrable, so the background must not cross them: \(P_n = 0\) at each wall, which submatrices.py checks to \(10^{-10}\) of the largest \(\lvert P_n\rvert\), \(\lvert T_n\rvert\). A conducting solid at a wall (an inner core, a thin conducting layer, a resolved mantle) carries its field along as a solid body, so the background must turn rigidly there: \(P_n = P_n' = 0\), and \(T_n = 0\) for \(n \neq 1\). The solid then turns with the fluid at the wall, at

    \[ \Omega_w = \mathrm{Ro}\thinspace G\thinspace\frac{T_1(r_w)}{r_w} \]

    relative to the frame, and the equation of its field gains the advection by that rotation, \(-i\thinspace m\thinspace \Omega_w\thinspace\mathbf b\) (bgflow.rigid_rate, added as for a differential rotation). The Bessel inner core and spin_doctor's energies of the solids use the frequency in the frame of the solid.

    With a background field \(\mathbf B_0\) the linear problem also assumes that the field is steady under the background flow. bgflow.steadiness reports

    \[ \max\lvert\nabla\times(\mathbf U\times\mathbf B_0)\rvert\thinspace r \Big/ \left(\max\lvert\mathbf U\rvert\max\lvert\mathbf B_0\rvert\right) \]

    on a grid of the fluid (with the flow of a differential rotation added), and warns above \(10^{-7}\), or stops with ferraro_policy = 'refuse'. A rotation about the axis keeps an axisymmetric field steady when the rotation rate is constant along the field lines (Magnetic field equations), so a rigid rotation keeps any of them.

    Energy budgets

    spin_doctor adds the background's terms to the budgets of Energy balance, computed on a grid of Gauss–Legendre points in \(\cos\theta\) that makes the angular products exact (utils4pp.bgflow_setup):

    \[ W_\mathrm{bg} = -2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace\mathbf u^*\cdot\left[(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U\right] dV , \]

    its imaginary part, and the enstrophy term of the same force. The part of \((\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\) has no real part, \(\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace\mathbf u^*\cdot(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u\thinspace dV = \tfrac12\int\rho_0\thinspace\mathbf U\cdot\nabla\lvert\mathbf u\rvert^2\thinspace dV = 0\), since \(\nabla\cdot(\rho_0\mathbf U) = 0\) and \(\mathbf U\) runs along the walls: \(W_\mathrm{bg}\) is the power that the perturbation draws from the shear of the background through \((\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U\). In the same way \(\mathbf r\times[(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)\mathbf u + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)\mathbf U] = (\mathbf U\cdot\nabla)(\mathbf r\times\mathbf u) + (\mathbf u\cdot\nabla)(\mathbf r\times\mathbf U)\), whose integral with \(\rho_0\) is a flux through the walls, zero when neither flow crosses them: the background puts no torque in the fluid, and the budget of its angular momentum (resid𝐋, axial and equatorial) holds as without it. The heat and composition budgets gain

    \[ W_\mathrm{bg}^{s} = -2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int p_0\thinspace s^*\left(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla s + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_U\right) dV , \qquad W_\mathrm{bg}^{c} = -2\thinspace\mathrm{Re}\int\rho_0\thinspace c^*\left(\mathbf U\cdot\nabla c + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla C_U\right) dV . \]

    The force is computed with its gradient \(\nabla(\mathbf U\cdot\mathbf u)\), so that the pressure that spin_doctor derives from the momentum equation, and its power at the walls, are the physical ones. The columns are in Output files.

    Newton's method

    The nonlinear flow of turning walls is the steady solution of the full axisymmetric equations, in which the flow also carries itself and its entropy and composition along. bgflow.py finds it with kore's own matrices. For \(m = 0\) the fields of a forced run are \(\mathbf x + \mathrm{c.c.} = 2\mathbf x\), so the steady problem reads

    \[ A\mathbf x + \tfrac12 B(2\mathbf x)\thinspace\mathbf x = \mathbf b , \]

    with \(A\mathbf x = \mathbf b\) the linear problem of forcing 12 and \(B(\mathbf V)\) the matrix of the terms of a background \(\mathbf V\). \(B(\mathbf V)\mathbf w\) is linear and symmetric in its two flows, so \(B(2\mathbf x)\) is also the Jacobian of the quadratic term, and a Newton step is

    \[ \left(A + B_k\right)\mathbf x_{k+1} = \mathbf b + \tfrac12 B_k\thinspace\mathbf x_k, \qquad B_k = B(2\mathbf x_k), \]

    with every degree of \(\mathbf x_k\) in \(B_k\). \(B_k\) is the matrix of a run with bgflow = 1 and the background \(2\mathbf x_k\) minus that of the same run at bgflow_Ro = 0, so that both have the same rows and bases. Ro rises from 0 in steps of at most 0.15, each starting from the flow of the last step with its part of order \(\mathrm{Ro}^2\) scaled to the new Ro, and the iterations of a step stop when one moves \(\mathbf x\) by less than \(10^{-9}\) of itself. The matrices of \(m = 0\) couple every degree of the background with every other, so the solves are dense. At Ek \(10^{-3}\) and Ro 1 (the example), each step takes three or four iterations, the change squaring at each, and the flow differs from Ro times the linear flow by 6%.

    The file

    bgflow.py nmax [nonlinear] writes bgflow.npz:

    key content
    nP, P; nT, T the degrees \(n \le\) nmax and the Chebyshev coefficients of \(P_n\), \(T_n\) per unit Ro, one row per degree, on \([r_i, 1]\) (on \([-1, 1]\), of parity \((-1)^n\), in a full sphere)
    nS, S; nC, C the same for \(\sigma_n\) and \(\gamma_n\), with thermal = 1 and compositional = 1
    ricb the radius of the inner wall
    rho_r, rho the density at 8 radii: the poloidal flow is that density's
    wall, ratio the wall that turned ('inner' or 'outer'), and the rate of the other relative to it
    Ro for a nonlinear flow, the Ro it holds at

    P and T are the scalars of the run's flow divided by the rate of the wall in units of \(1/\tau\), and \(\sigma_n\), \(\gamma_n\) those of its entropy and composition divided by that rate over \(G\).
