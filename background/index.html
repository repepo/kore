    Background model

    kore computes small perturbations of a steady background state. You describe that background in the parameter file, in seven independent parts:

    part parameter what it sets
    structure structure density, pressure and gravity as functions of radius
    stratification stratification the radial entropy gradient (only with thermal = 1)
    diffusivity diffusivity how viscosity and thermal diffusivity vary with radius
    composition comp_stratification the gradient of a second scalar, the composition (only with compositional = 1)
    rotation diff_rot, diff_rot_type uniform or differential rotation
    shape centrifugal, centrifugal_shape a sphere, or a body flattened by its rotation
    flow bgflow a steady axisymmetric flow, see Background flow

    The equations where these enter are on Anelastic equations.

    Units

    kore works with dimensionless quantities (Non-dimensionalization). The unit of length is the radius of the outer wall. The parameter r_out (default 1) gives that radius as a fraction of the stellar radius; the solar profiles below are written in terms of this fractional stellar radius, called \(x\).

    The helper module bin/units.py converts physical values into kore's parameters, using \(1/\Omega\) as the time unit. In a parameter file, after setting r_out:

    import units
units.set_physical(par, frame_nHz=456.0, nu=1e12, kappa=1e12)   # sets Gaspard, Beyonce, ViscosD, ThermaD, diff_rot_frame
units.set_target(par, omega=-271.2, sigma=-33.2)                 # sets the solver target from a frequency and a damping rate in nHz

    set_physical takes the viscosity and thermal diffusivity in cm²/s, and uses the solar radius and surface gravity unless you pass radius and gsurf (in cgs units). Running python3 bin/units.py in a run directory prints the computed eigenvalues as frequency and damping rate in nHz.

    Quick start

    The background of the smooth Sun example (examples/smooth_sun/parameters.py), a full sphere with the observed solar rotation:

    par.thermal        = 1
par.r_out          = 0.985                 # outer wall in solar radii
par.structure      = 'model S hydrostatic'
par.stratification = 'solar_rz'            # a radiative interior below a slightly subadiabatic convection zone
par.solar_rz_delta_cz = -4e-7
par.diffusivity    = 'solar_rz'            # viscosity and diffusivity drop to visc0, therm0 below 0.70 R☉
par.visc0, par.therm0 = 0.01, 0.04
par.diff_rot       = 1
par.diff_rot_type  = 'tabulated'           # rotation rate read from a table
par.diff_rot_file  = 'bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz'
par.diff_rot_nmax  = 4
par.thermal_wind   = 2                     # with its exact thermal wind
par.thermal_wind_radial = 0
units.set_physical(par, frame_nHz=456.0, nu=1e12, kappa=1e12)

    The default bin/parameters.py uses structure = 'Wu 2005' and stratification = 'top hat' with uniform rotation. Getting started uses an incompressible fluid ('Boussinesq', thermal = 0).

    Structure

    structure sets the density, pressure and gravity. It has no default.

    structure description parameters
    'model S hydrostatic' the Sun: a smooth fit to the density of solar Model S, with gravity and pressure consistent with it. Recommended for solar work structure_deg (30)
    'Wu 2005' a polytrope-like density \(\rho_0 = (1 - x^2)^2\) (Wu 2005) needs r_out < 1, so that the density is not zero at the wall
    'Boussinesq' uniform density: the Boussinesq (incompressible) equations –
    '8-term fit' legacy solar fit, kept to reproduce old runs –
    'old model S prototype' legacy solar fit, kept to reproduce old runs solar_density_deg, solar_density_window, solar_density_sun_deg
    • Only the shape of the density and pressure profiles matters, not their overall scale.
    • 'model S hydrostatic' matches Model S to within about 1 % at the default degree 30. Use structure_deg = 50 to compare closely with work that uses Model S itself; this needs a slightly higher lmax.

    Stratification

    With thermal = 1, stratification sets the background entropy gradient \(dS/dr\), which controls buoyancy. A positive gradient is stable (radiative), zero is neutral (convective). There is no default.

    stratification profile parameters
    'top hat' a stable layer between two smooth steps aux1…aux5: position and width of each step, then the amplitude
    'odd top hat' the same, but well behaved at the centre of a full sphere aux1…aux5
    'solar_rz' the solar radiative interior of Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b (below) solar_rz_*
    'linear' proportional to \(r\) –
    'inverse square' proportional to \(1/r^2\) (conduction between the walls) needs ricb > 0
    'two zone' a nearly neutral inner zone below a stable outer zone two_zone = [transition radius, width] ([0.7, 0.1])
    'user' your own expression (below) stratification_profile, stratification_param
    • In a full sphere the gradient must go to zero smoothly at the centre. Use 'odd top hat' rather than 'top hat' unless the first step is far from the centre.
    • In a shell without thermal diffusion, keep the gradient zero at the inner wall (see ThermaD = 0).
    • 'odd top hat' with aux1…aux5 = 0, 0.03, 0.68, 0.06, 2.6 approximates the entropy gradient of solar Model S.

    'solar_rz'. A stable radiative interior joined to a convection zone at about 0.7 solar radii, following Mukhopadhyay et al. (2025b) and Bekki et al. (2024):

    \[ \frac{dS}{dr} = \frac{A}{4}\left[1 - \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_1}{d}\right]\left[1 - \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_2}{d}\right]\mathrm{erf}\frac{x}{w_0} + \delta(x)\thinspace \frac{d\ln p_0}{dr}\thinspace \frac{1}{2}\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_{OS}}{d_{OS}}\right]. \]

    The first term is the radiative interior, the second the convection zone. The parameters and their defaults:

    parameter meaning default
    solar_rz_dSdr amplitude \(A\) of the radiative gradient 2.615
    solar_rz_steps \([x_1, x_2, d]\), the steps that shape it [0.61, 0.68, 0.04]
    solar_rz_core \(w_0\), makes the profile smooth at the centre 0.05
    solar_rz_os \([x_{OS}, d_{OS}]\), base and width of the convection zone [0.70, 0.04]
    solar_rz_delta_cz superadiabaticity \(\delta\) of the convection zone (negative is stable) 0
    solar_rz_delta_sf \(\delta\) just below the surface 0

    A slightly negative solar_rz_delta_cz, such as the \(-4\times10^{-7}\) of the smooth Sun example, noticeably changes the frequencies of the high-frequency retrograde modes.

    Diffusivity

    The viscosity is ViscosD times a radial profile, and the thermal diffusivity is ThermaD times another. diffusivity chooses those profiles (and the composition's, with comp0 in place of therm0).

    diffusivity profile parameters
    'uniform' (default) constant –
    'step' visc0, therm0 below radius aux3, 1 above, joined by a smooth step of width aux4 visc0, therm0, aux3, aux4
    'solar_rz' visc0, therm0 in the radiative interior, 1 in the convection zone (Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b) visc0, therm0, solar_rz_os
    'user' your own expressions (below) diffusivity_profiles, diffusivity_param
    • With 'step' and 'solar_rz', ViscosD and ThermaD are the values in the outer (convective) layer.
    • visc_pow, therm_pow and comp_pow (default 0) multiply the profile by a power of the density. With 1, the dynamic diffusivities (density times diffusivity) follow the profile instead of the kinematic ones.
    • Convective forcing uses only the turbulent part of the viscosity, above a floor rsf_nu_floor.

    ThermaD = 0

    ThermaD = 0 turns thermal diffusion off: the motion is adiabatic and the thermal boundary conditions are not used. It works with every structure, stratification and rotation profile. Things to know:

    • Prefer ThermaD = 0 to a very small ThermaD. A very small diffusivity creates thin thermal boundary layers at the walls that need much more radial resolution. The eigenvalues approach the ThermaD = 0 ones smoothly anyway.
    • Keep the entropy gradient zero at the inner wall. Otherwise spurious modes appear there. They move when you change N, and spin_doctor flags them. If the gradient must reach the wall, ask for one or two more eigenvalues (nev), since the spurious modes take some of the slots.
    • With differential rotation, expect many spurious eigenvalues with near-zero damping. They come from the entropy being carried by the rotation without diffusion. They change with lmax and fail the ℓ-convergence check of spin_doctor; discard them. Keep the solver target away from that band.
    • Check convergence in N. Runs without diffusion usually need more radial resolution.
    • Forced runs. Without diffusion, the entropy is only carried by the flow. If the forcing frequency, seen from the rotating fluid, is zero somewhere in the domain, the problem has no well-behaved solution and kore stops (Combining features).

    Composition

    A fluid can be stratified by composition as well as by entropy: helium below the convection zone of a star, or light elements in a planet's core. With compositional = 1 kore adds a second scalar, the composition, that is carried by the flow, diffuses, and adds its own buoyancy. It can be used alone (thermal = 0) or together with the entropy, for a double-diffusive fluid. Its equation is on Anelastic equations.

    par.compositional = 1
par.comp_stratification = 'linear'      # background gradient proportional to r
par.BeyonceC = 10.                      # strength of its buoyancy; set after set_scales()
par.CompoD = 1e-3                       # its diffusivity
par.bci_compositional = 0               # at each wall, 0: fixed composition, 1: no flux
par.bco_compositional = 0

    comp_stratification takes the values 'linear', 'inverse square', 'two zone' (with comp_two_zone) and 'user' (with comp_stratification_profile), as for the entropy. It has no default. BeyonceC sets the strength of the compositional buoyancy.

    • A higher composition is lighter, as for the entropy: the layer is stable where BeyonceC times the gradient is positive.
    • CompoD = 0 turns its diffusion off; the advice of ThermaD = 0 then applies to it.
    • units.set_physical(par, ..., D=...), with \(D\) in cm²/s, also sets CompoD and BeyonceC.
    • The composition works with nearly every other feature. The tidal forcing 1 needs compositional = 0 (Combining features).
    • Its results are in composition.dat (Output files).

    Rotation

    With diff_rot = 0 (the default) the fluid rotates uniformly with the frame. With diff_rot = 1 it rotates differentially, at

    \[ \Omega(r,\theta) = \Omega_{\rm ref}\left[1 + \Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\right], \]

    where \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) is the rotation rate of the frame and \(\Delta\Omega\) the relative departure from it. kore expands \(\Delta\Omega\) in even Legendre polynomials in latitude, so the rotation is always symmetric about the equator. Eigenvalues are given in the frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\).

    With thermal = 1, the background also includes the latitudinal entropy variation that balances the differential rotation, the thermal wind (see below).

    Parameters

    parameter default meaning
    diff_rot 0 1 turns on differential rotation
    diff_rot_type 'Y20' the profile, see below
    diff_rot_ampl 1.0 a factor that multiplies \(\Delta\Omega\)
    diff_rot_param 0.0 shape parameter of some profiles
    diff_rot_term_amp [1.0]*9 factors on each Legendre term; e.g. [1, 1, 0] drops the third term
    diff_rot_profiles {} 'user': one expression per Legendre degree (below)
    diff_rot_frame 456.0 'solar_P4', 'tabulated': frame rotation rate in nHz
    diff_rot_solar [464, -76, -50, 433.5, 0.71, 0.045] 'solar_P4': coefficients of the fit below
    diff_rot_file '' 'tabulated': the table file, absolute or relative to the run directory
    diff_rot_nmax 8 'tabulated', 'ferraro': highest Legendre degree kept (even)
    diff_rot_F 'psi' 'ferraro': the rotation as a function of the magnetic flux function
    diff_rot_rdeg, diff_rot_window, diff_rot_sun_deg 24, None, 60 'tabulated': settings of the radial fit
    thermal_wind 1 the thermal wind, see below
    thermal_wind_radial 1 0 drops the radial advection of the thermal-wind entropy
    thermal_wind_amplitude 1.0 a factor on the thermal-wind entropy
    dSdtheta 0.0 thermal_wind = 3: the prescribed latitudinal entropy gradient

    If diff_rot is used with physical units ('solar', 'solar_P4', 'tabulated'), compute ViscosD, ThermaD and Beyonce for the frame rate: 435 nHz for 'solar', diff_rot_frame for the other two. units.set_physical does this.

    Profiles

    Below, \(a\) is diff_rot_ampl and \(p\) is diff_rot_param.

    diff_rot_type rotation notes
    'rigid' uniform offset \(a\) for tests: the exact answer is known
    'Y20' \(a\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta)\), latitudinal only use ricb > 0
    'Y20-wall-bounded' the same, vanishing at both walls use ricb > 0
    'shellular' \(a\thinspace (r^p - 1)\), radial only Baruteau & Rieutord 2013
    'shellular_boussinesq' radial only, proportional to \(1 - r^2\) Mirouh et al. 2016; use with thermal_wind = 0, 'Boussinesq' and 'linear'
    'cylindrical' \(a\thinspace r^2\sin^2\theta\), constant on cylinders Baruteau & Rieutord 2013
    'conical' \(a\sin^2\theta\), constant on cones Guenel et al. 2016; use ricb > 0
    'solar' an older helioseismic fit, relative to 435 nHz less accurate than 'solar_P4' or 'tabulated'
    'solar_P4' a fit of the solar rotation, see below Mukhopadhyay et al. 2026
    'tabulated' read from a table of observations see below
    'user' your own expressions see below
    'ferraro' constant along the field lines of a poloidal magnetic field needs magnetic = 1 (Magnetic field)

    'solar_P4'. A uniformly rotating radiative zone, joined at the tachocline (radius \(r_{tc}\), width \(d_{tc}\)) to a convection zone whose rotation depends only on latitude:

    \[ \frac{\Omega}{2\pi} = \Omega_{RZ} + \tfrac12\left[\Omega_{CZ}(\theta) - \Omega_{RZ}\right]\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\thinspace \frac{x - r_{tc}}{d_{tc}}\right], \qquad \Omega_{CZ} = c_0 + c_2\cos^2\theta + c_4\cos^4\theta , \]

    with diff_rot_solar = [\(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{RZ}\), \(r_{tc}\), \(d_{tc}\)], rates in nHz and radii in solar radii.

    'tabulated'. The rotation rate comes from an .npz file with the arrays ome[θ, r] (nHz), r (solar radii) and theta (radians). bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz is the mean solar rotation measured by HMI from 2010 to 2026, and tests/diff_rot/ls_to_tabulated.py converts the Larson & Schou (2018) table to this format. kore expands the table up to Legendre degree diff_rot_nmax and prints how well the expansion fits the table: check that line. Near the surface at high latitude the solar profile needs a degree of about 12 for good accuracy.

    'user'. diff_rot_profiles gives the radial shape of each even Legendre degree as an expression (below). For example, the cylindrical profile written by hand:

    par.diff_rot_type     = 'user'
par.diff_rot_profiles = {0: '(2/3)*r**2', 2: '-(2/3)*r**2'}
par.diff_rot_ampl     = -0.25

    Thermal wind

    In a stratified fluid, differential rotation must be balanced by a latitudinal variation of the background entropy, the thermal wind. With thermal = 1, thermal_wind chooses how kore treats it:

    thermal_wind background entropy
    0 no thermal wind; the rotation only carries the entropy perturbation (as in Mirouh et al. 2016)
    1 (default) the thermal wind to first order in the differential rotation
    2 the exact thermal wind; the eigenvalues then do not depend on the choice of frame
    3 a prescribed latitudinal entropy gradient, dSdtheta (as in Bekki et al. 2024)
    • Options 1 and 2 need a non-zero Beyonce. set_scales() with timescale = 'rotation' sets Beyonce = 0, so set it after calling set_scales().
    • thermal_wind_radial = 0 drops one term of the thermal wind, as Bekki et al. 2022 and Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 do. Keeping it is more consistent, but use 0 to compare with those papers.
    • With a magnetic field, the thermal wind ignores the force of the background field (Magnetic field).

    Resolution and cost

    • Differential rotation couples spherical-harmonic degrees that uniform rotation keeps separate, so the matrices are larger and the runs slower. The cost grows with the highest Legendre degree of the rotation, so keep diff_rot_nmax as low as the problem allows.
    • In a full sphere, some profiles are not smooth at the centre ('Y20', 'conical', most 'shellular'). submatrices.py prints a warning for each.
    • Modes that reach into a radiative interior have short radial wavelengths and can need N of 700 or more. See Solver for the memory this takes.
    • Check every solution with spin_doctor, as for any run.

    Centrifugal deformation

    A rotating star or planet is flattened at the poles. With centrifugal = 1, kore gives the background this shape: the walls and the surfaces of constant density become oblate spheroids, and every term of the equations feels it. kore keeps its spherical grid and solves in coordinates that follow the flattened surfaces. The equations are on Centrifugal deformation.

    The deformation is accurate to first order in the flattening, which is enough for most stars and planets. 'spheroid' treats a uniform-density body exactly, at any flattening.

    Flattening

    parameter default meaning
    centrifugal 0 1 flattens the body
    centrifugal_shape 'clairaut' how the flattening varies with radius (below)
    centrifugal_q 0.0 the ratio of centrifugal to gravitational acceleration at the equator
    centrifugal_f None the flattening of the outer wall, (equatorial − polar radius) / equatorial radius
    centrifugal_profile, centrifugal_param 'user': the flattening as an expression (below)
    centrifugal_shape flattening set by
    'clairaut' the hydrostatic shape of the chosen structure centrifugal_q or centrifugal_f
    'uniform' the same at every radius centrifugal_f
    'user' any profile centrifugal_profile
    'spheroid' the same at every radius, exact at any flattening centrifugal_f
    • 'spheroid' suits a uniform-density fluid in a fast-rotating or rigid container, such as a planetary core inside a rigid mantle. It needs 'Boussinesq', uniform diffusivities and no differential rotation. A negative flattening gives an elongated body.
    • submatrices.py prints the flattening at the walls, and warns above 0.1, where the first-order treatment becomes inaccurate.

    Typical values:

    body centrifugal_q surface flattening
    Earth 3.5e-3 3.4e-3 (core-mantle boundary: 2.5e-3)
    Jupiter 0.089 0.065
    Saturn 0.155 0.098
    Sun 2e-5 8e-6

    A Jupiter-like body:

    par.structure         = 'Wu 2005'
par.r_out             = 0.99
par.centrifugal       = 1
par.centrifugal_shape = 'clairaut'
par.centrifugal_q     = 0.089

    The Earth's core, whose shape is set by the whole planet, so give the flattening of the core-mantle boundary directly:

    par.structure         = 'Boussinesq'
par.centrifugal       = 1
par.centrifugal_shape = 'uniform'
par.centrifugal_f     = 2.5e-3

    Limitations

    • The deformation works with buoyancy, viscosity, thermal and compositional diffusion, differential rotation and a magnetic field (insulating walls or a conducting inner core), in shells and full spheres.
    • It does not work with forced runs, walls of zero radial stress (bci or bco = 2), the rotating solids, the drag at the outer wall, thin conducting layers, a resolved mantle or a background flow. kore stops and says why (Combining features).
    • A viscous flow in a full sphere with m ≠ 0 needs rpower_v of at least 4; kore stops and says so.
    • With the thermal wind, the background entropy ignores the flattening. submatrices.py warns about this.
    • In spin_doctor, the total energy budget closes only approximately (to second order in the flattening), except with 'spheroid'.

    User profiles

    The 'user' options take a numpy expression as a string. It may use r (kore's radius), x (the stellar radius with a solar structure, otherwise equal to r), p (the matching ..._param parameter), and the usual functions: sin, cos, tan, arcsin, arccos, arctan, sinh, cosh, tanh, exp, log, log10, sqrt, abs, sign, pi, where, minimum, maximum, erf, erfc. For example:

    par.stratification = 'user'
par.stratification_profile = '2.5*exp(-((r - 0.75)/0.06)**2)'
par.diffusivity = 'user'
par.diffusivity_profiles = {'nu': '0.01 + 0.99*0.5*(1 + tanh((x - 0.70)/0.015))', 'kappa': '1'}

    In a full sphere, profiles must be smooth at the centre; submatrices.py warns if they are not (Regularity at the centre). Expressions must not contain #.

    Checks

    kore checks the background before building anything, and stops with an explanation if something cannot work: an unknown type, a parameter name from an older version of kore (the message gives the new name), or a combination that is not supported. submatrices.py also warns about any parameter name it does not recognize, so a misspelled name is not silently ignored.
