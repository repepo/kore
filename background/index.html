    Background model

    kore solves for small perturbations of a background. The background has four parts, each chosen by name in the parameter file:

    part parameter what it sets
    structure structure the density \(\rho_0\), \(p_0 = \rho_0 T_0\) and gravity \(g\), functions of \(r\)
    stratification stratification the entropy gradient \(dS/dr\), with \(S = \delta s/c_p\) (only with thermal = 1)
    diffusivity diffusivity the radial profiles of the viscosity \(\nu\) and the thermal diffusivity \(\kappa\)
    rotation diff_rot, diff_rot_type a uniform or a differential rotation \(\Omega(r,\theta)\), and the latitudinal entropy that balances it

    The equations in which they enter are on the page Anelastic equations. The code is in bin/background.py (the choice, the checks, the stratification and diffusivity types), bin/radial_profiles.py (one class per structure) and bin/diff_rot.py (the rotation).

    Units

    kore is dimensionless (Non-dimensionalization). The unit of length is the radius of the outer wall, \(r = 1\). r_out is that radius in star radii (1 by default), so \(x = r\cdot\)r_out is the radius in star radii, the coordinate of the solar profiles below. The only physical unit kore reads is that of the solar rotation data, in nHz.

    bin/units.py turns physical values into the parameters, with the time unit \(1/\Omega\); kore itself never imports it. In a parameter file, after r_out is set:

    import units
units.set_physical(par, frame_nHz=456.0, nu=1e12, kappa=1e12)   # Gaspard, Beyonce, ViscosD, ThermaD, diff_rot_frame
units.set_target(par, omega=-271.2, sigma=-33.2)                 # itau, rtau from ω, σ in nHz

    set_physical takes \(\nu\) and \(\kappa\) in cm²/s and uses the solar radius and surface gravity unless radius and gsurf (cgs) are given. For the Sun with r_out = 0.985 in a frame rotating at 456 nHz it gives Beyonce \(= 4.86\times10^4\) and, for \(\nu = 10^{12}\) cm²/s, ViscosD \(= 7.4\times10^{-5}\). In a run directory, python3 bin/units.py prints the eigenvalues as \(\omega\) and \(\sigma\) in nHz, \(\omega = -\mathrm{Im}\thinspace\lambda\cdot F/\mathrm{Gaspard}\) and \(\sigma = \mathrm{Re}\thinspace\lambda\cdot F/\mathrm{Gaspard}\), with \(F\) = diff_rot_frame the frame rate in nHz.

    Quick start

    The background of the smooth Sun example (examples/smooth_sun/parameters.py), a full sphere with the observed solar rotation:

    par.thermal        = 1
par.r_out          = 0.985                 # outer wall in solar radii
par.structure      = 'model S hydrostatic'
par.stratification = 'solar_rz'            # a radiative interior below a slightly subadiabatic convection zone
par.solar_rz_delta_cz = -4e-7
par.diffusivity    = 'solar_rz'            # ν, κ drop to visc0, therm0 below 0.70 R☉
par.visc0, par.therm0 = 0.01, 0.04
par.diff_rot       = 1
par.diff_rot_type  = 'tabulated'           # Ω(r, θ) from a table
par.diff_rot_file  = 'bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz'
par.diff_rot_nmax  = 4
par.thermal_wind   = 2                     # with its exact thermal wind
par.thermal_wind_radial = 0
units.set_physical(par, frame_nHz=456.0, nu=1e12, kappa=1e12)

    The checked-in bin/parameters.py, a gravito-inertial mode, uses structure = 'Wu 2005' with r_out = 0.99 and stratification = 'top hat', with uniform rotation. Getting started uses an incompressible fluid ('Boussinesq', thermal = 0) with a shellular rotation.

    Structure

    structure has no default.

    structure \(\rho_0\), \(p_0\), \(g\) parameters
    'model S hydrostatic' the Sun, for new runs: an even polynomial fit of \(\ln\rho\) of Model S (bin/model_s.at) over the whole Sun, \(g\) from its mass, \(p_0\) from hydrostatic balance structure_deg (30)
    'Wu 2005' \(\rho_0 = (1 - x^2)^2\) (Wu 2005, \(\beta = 2\)) with its \(g\) and \(p_0\) r_out < 1, so that \(\rho_0 > 0\) at the wall
    'Boussinesq' \(\rho_0 = p_0 = 1\), \(g = r\): the Boussinesq equations –
    '8-term fit' (legacy) the smooth fit of bin/smoothed_sun.py, up to 48 % off Model S in \(\rho\) near the surface, which moves solar inertial modes by 1.5–8 nHz –
    'old model S prototype' (legacy) Chebyshev fits of \(\ln\rho\) and \(\ln p\) of Model S, with the \(g\) of the 8-term fit (not hydrostatic) solar_density_deg, solar_density_window, solar_density_sun_deg
    • Only ratios of \(\rho_0\) and of \(p_0\) enter the equations, so their scale does not matter. \(g\) is 1 at the surface (\(x = 1\)) in the solar structures and in 'Wu 2005'.
    • The three solar structures (the first and the last two rows) also make \(x = r\cdot\)r_out the radius of the solar rotation profiles (diff_rot.solar_x).
    • 'model S hydrostatic' is within 1.0 %, 0.5 % and 0.75 % of Model S in \(\rho\), \(p\) and \(g\) at degree 30, and within 0.14 %, 0.06 % and 0.10 % at degree 50. Use 50 to compare with papers that use Model S itself; degrees of 40 and above need about 16 more lmax for the same \(\ell\)-convergence of the HFR modes.
    • The two legacy structures are kept so that runs made before 'model S hydrostatic' give the same results. For 'old model S prototype', solar_density_window None fits the shell \([\)ricb\(\cdot\)r_out \(- 0.01\), r_out \(+ 0.005]\) when ricb > 0, and is 'sun' when ricb = 0: one even fit over the whole Sun of degree solar_density_sun_deg (60).

    Stratification

    With thermal = 1, stratification sets \(dS/dr\) for \(r \ge 0\), in units of \(1/L\); it has no default then. In a full sphere (ricb = 0) kore uses it as an odd function of \(r\), so it should go to zero smoothly at the centre.

    stratification \(dS/dr\) parameters
    'top hat' \(\tfrac{A}{2}\left[\mathrm{erf}\frac{r - x_1}{w_1} - \mathrm{erf}\frac{r - x_2}{w_2}\right]\) aux1…aux5 = \(x_1\), \(w_1\), \(x_2\), \(w_2\), \(A\)
    'odd top hat' the same made odd in \(r\): with \(x_1 = 0\) it rises as \(A\thinspace\mathrm{erf}(r/w_1)\) from the centre aux1…aux5
    'solar_rz' the radiative interior of Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b, below solar_rz_dSdr, solar_rz_steps, solar_rz_core, solar_rz_delta_cz, solar_rz_delta_sf, solar_rz_os
    'linear' \(r\); with 'Boussinesq', \(N^2\) = Beyonce \(r^2\) (Rieutord 2006, Mirouh et al. 2016) –
    'inverse square' \(1/r^2\), conduction between the walls needs ricb > 0
    'user' stratification_profile, a numpy expression (below) stratification_param (\(p\))
    • 'top hat' in a full sphere needs \(x_1 \gtrsim 6 w_1\), so that \(dS/dr \to 0\) at the centre; otherwise use 'odd top hat'. With ricb > 0 and ThermaD = 0, a \(dS/dr \neq 0\) at the inner wall gives spurious modes trapped there (below).
    • 'odd top hat' with aux1…aux5 = 0, 0.03, 0.68, 0.06, 2.6 fits the \(dS/dr\) of Model S to 10 % rms.

    'solar_rz'. With \(x = r\cdot\)r_out in solar radii (their eq. 10, plus the convection zone of Bekki et al. 2024, eq. 2):

    \[ \begin{aligned} \frac{dS}{dr} &= \frac{A}{4}\left[1 - \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_1}{d}\right]\left[1 - \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_2}{d}\right]\mathrm{erf}\frac{x}{w_0}\cr &\quad + \delta(x)\thinspace \frac{d\ln p_0}{dr}\thinspace \frac{1}{2}\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\frac{x - x_{OS}}{d_{OS}}\right], \end{aligned} \]
    \[ \delta(x) = \delta_{cz} + (\delta_{sf} - \delta_{cz})\thinspace e^{-((x - 0.985)/0.015)^2}, \]

    with \(A\) = solar_rz_dSdr (2.615), \([x_1, x_2, d]\) = solar_rz_steps ([0.61, 0.68, 0.04]), \(w_0\) = solar_rz_core (0.05: makes \(dS/dr\) odd and smooth at \(r = 0\), no effect above \(x \approx 3w_0\)), \([x_{OS}, d_{OS}]\) = solar_rz_os ([0.70, 0.04]) and \(\delta_{cz}\), \(\delta_{sf}\) = solar_rz_delta_cz, solar_rz_delta_sf (0). \(A\) is their \((\partial s_0/\partial r)_{RZ}\) = 1.32e-2 erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹ cm⁻¹ times \(L/c_p\), with \(c_p\) = 3.46e8 and r_out = 0.985, so it scales with r_out. \(\delta < 0\) is subadiabatic; kore stops if \(\delta\) is set with another stratification. In the smooth Sun example, \(\delta_{cz} = -4\times10^{-7}\) makes the HFR \(m = 8\) mode 34 nHz more retrograde than \(\delta_{cz} = 0\).

    Diffusivity

    \(\nu\) = ViscosD \(\hat\nu(r)\) and \(\kappa\) = ThermaD \(\hat\kappa(r)\); diffusivity sets \(\hat\nu\) and \(\hat\kappa\).

    diffusivity \(\hat\nu\), \(\hat\kappa\) parameters
    'uniform' (default) 1 –
    'step' visc0, therm0 below \(r\) = aux3, 1 above, joined by an erf of width aux4 visc0, therm0, aux3, aux4
    'solar_rz' visc0, therm0 below \(x_{OS}\), 1 above, as \(\tfrac12[1 + \mathrm{erf}((x - x_{OS})/d_{OS})]\) (Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b, eqs. 8–9) visc0, therm0, solar_rz_os
    'user' diffusivity_profiles = {'nu': '...', 'kappa': '...'}, numpy expressions (below) diffusivity_param (\(p\))
    • With 'step' and 'solar_rz', ViscosD and ThermaD are the values above the step (the convection zone of the Sun). Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b use visc0 = 0.01 and therm0 = 0.04.
    • Any type is multiplied by \((\rho_0(x_2)/\rho_0)^p\), \(x_2\) = aux3, with \(p\) = visc_pow for \(\hat\nu\) and therm_pow for \(\hat\kappa\): 0 (the default) keeps the kinematic diffusivities as set, 1 makes \(\rho_0\nu\) and \(\rho_0\kappa\) follow the profile instead.
    • Forcing 11 (random Reynolds stresses) uses the turbulent part of the viscosity, ViscosD \((\hat\nu - \nu_f)\): the floor \(\nu_f\) is visc0 with 'step' and 'solar_rz' when visc0 < 1 and 0 otherwise, unless rsf_nu_floor is set.

    ThermaD = 0

    ThermaD = 0 removes the thermal diffusion: the motion is adiabatic, and bci_thermal, bco_thermal are not used (Without thermal diffusion). It works with every structure, stratification and rotation option. What to watch for:

    • Prefer ThermaD = 0 to a very small ThermaD. As ThermaD → 0 the eigenvalues of a resolved diffusive run approach the ThermaD = 0 ones linearly. With the thermal wind and walls that are both stress-free and at fixed entropy (bci_thermal = bco_thermal = 0), a small ThermaD forms a thermal boundary layer of width about \((\mathrm{ThermaD}/\lvert\lambda\rvert)^{1/2}\) at the walls, which below ThermaD ≈ \(10^{-7}\) needs far more radial resolution than the bulk. The ThermaD = 0 run has no such layer, and neither have zero-flux or no-slip walls.
    • Let \(dS/dr\) vanish at the inner wall. In a shell, a \(dS/dr \neq 0\) at the inner wall gives spurious modes trapped there. Their eigenvalues move with N, and spin_doctor flags them (residσ ≈ 1). For an erf top hat, take \(x_1 \ge\) ricb \(+ 3w_1\). If the gradient must reach the wall, raise nev by one or two, because the spurious modes take some of the slots.
    • With a differential rotation, expect a band of eigenvalues near \(\sigma = 0\). Without diffusion, the advection of \(s\) by the rotation has a continuous spectrum, \(\lambda = -i\thinspace m\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\), which the discretization shows as many eigenvalues near \(\sigma = 0\) with frequencies in the range of \(m\thinspace\mathrm{Gaspard}\thinspace\Delta\Omega\). With the thermal wind some of them grow slowly. They peak at \(l \approx\) lmax and change with lmax: discard any mode that fails the ℓ-convergence check of spin_doctor. They disappear with thermal_wind = 0 or with a moderate diffusivity (ThermaD ≈ \(10^{-3}\)). Put the target away from the band, where the solver converges slowly.
    • Check the convergence in N. In a full sphere, eigenfunctions with ThermaD = 0 need more radial resolution than diffusive ones (N = 200 instead of 120 for a smooth solar model). Where \(\rho_0\) nearly vanishes at the outer wall (structure 'Wu 2005' with r_out close to 1), the thermal-wind rows converge slowly in N.

    Rotation

    With diff_rot = 0 (the default) the background rotates uniformly at the rate of the frame. With diff_rot = 1 it rotates at

    \[ \Omega(r,\theta) = \Omega_{\rm ref}\left[1 + \Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\right], \qquad \Delta\Omega = \sum_n f_n(r)\thinspace P_n(\cos\theta), \quad n = 0, 2, 4, \dots \]

    where \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\) is the rotation rate of the frame in which kore solves, and \(\Delta\Omega\) the relative departure from it, a sum of Legendre polynomials in \(\cos\theta\) with radial coefficients \(f_n(r)\). Only even \(n\) appear, so the rotation is symmetric about the equator. The profiles do not depend on the structure. With thermal = 1, the latitudinal entropy that balances the rotation, the thermal wind, is part of the background too (thermal_wind, below). The terms this adds to the equations are in Anelastic equations.

    Frame and units

    \(\Delta\Omega\) is relative to the frame, and Gaspard converts it to the time unit, so any timescale works. Two things follow:

    • ViscosD, ThermaD and Beyonce must be computed for \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\). For 'solar' the frame must rotate at 435 nHz; for 'solar_P4' and 'tabulated' at diff_rot_frame (456 nHz by default). The other types are dimensionless.
    • With thermal_wind = 2 the eigenvalues do not depend on the choice of frame. With thermal_wind = 1 (linear in \(\Delta\Omega\)) they do, at second order in \(\Delta\Omega\): by 0.1–0.9 nHz for solar inertial modes.

    Eigenvalues are in the frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\), as for uniform rotation.

    Parameters

    parameter default meaning
    diff_rot 0 1 adds the differential rotation
    diff_rot_type 'Y20' the profile, see below
    diff_rot_ampl 1.0 the factor of \(\Delta\Omega\), for every type (table below)
    diff_rot_param 0.0 the shape parameter: \(\sigma\) of 'shellular', p in 'user' expressions
    diff_rot_term_amp [1.0]*9 \(a_0, a_2, \dots, a_{16}\): factors of the \(P_0, P_2, \dots, P_{16}\) terms of \(\Delta\Omega\), any type, after diff_rot_ampl (missing entries are 1); e.g. [1, 1, 0] drops the \(P_4\) part of 'solar_P4'
    diff_rot_profiles {} 'user': {n: 'numpy expression'}, the shape \(f_n\) of each even degree (below)
    diff_rot_frame 456.0 'solar_P4', 'tabulated': \(\Omega_{\rm ref}/2\pi\) in nHz
    diff_rot_solar [464, -76, -50, 433.5, 0.71, 0.045] 'solar_P4': \(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{RZ}\) (nHz), \(r_{tc}\), \(d_{tc}\)
    diff_rot_file '' 'tabulated': the .npz file, absolute or relative to the run directory
    diff_rot_nmax 8 'tabulated': highest degree kept, even (up to 16 with thermal_wind = 2)
    diff_rot_rdeg 24 'tabulated': degree of the Chebyshev fit of each \(f_n\) in \(r\)
    diff_rot_window None 'tabulated': None fits over the shell if ricb > 0 and is 'sun' if ricb = 0; 'sun': one fit over the whole Sun, even in \(r\), the same for any ricb
    diff_rot_sun_deg 60 'sun': degree of that fit
    thermal_wind 1 the latitudinal background entropy, see below
    thermal_wind_radial 1 0 drops \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\)
    thermal_wind_amplitude 1.0 factor on \(S_{tw}\) (thermal_wind 1, 2)
    dSdtheta 0.0 thermal_wind = 3: \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\)

    Profiles

    \(a\) = diff_rot_ampl, \(p\) = diff_rot_param. For the solar types \(x\) is the fractional solar radius: \(x = r\times\)r_out with a solar structure, \(x = r\) otherwise.

    diff_rot_type \(\Delta\Omega(r,\theta)\) degrees notes
    'rigid' \(a\) \(P_0\) test profile: a uniform offset of the rotation rate, with an exact answer (below); \(a = 0\) gives uniform rotation with the diff-rot machinery on (needed for thermal_wind = 3)
    'Y20' \(a\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta)\) \(P_2\) \(P_2\) unnormalised, despite the name
    'Y20-wall-bounded' \(a\thinspace (1 - r)(r - r_i)\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta)\), \(r_i\) = ricb \(P_2\) not smooth at \(r = 0\): use ricb > 0
    'shellular' \(a\thinspace (r^\sigma - 1)\), \(\sigma = p\) \(P_0\) Baruteau & Rieutord 2013 (\(a = 1\)); not smooth at \(r = 0\) unless \(\sigma\) is even and positive
    'shellular_boussinesq' \(\tfrac12 N_0^2\thinspace (1 - r^2)\), \(N_0^2 = a\) \(P_0\) Mirouh et al. 2016; use thermal_wind = 0 (their background has no thermal wind), structure 'Boussinesq' and stratification 'linear'
    'cylindrical' \(\varepsilon\thinspace r^2\sin^2\theta\), \(\varepsilon = a\) \(P_0\), \(P_2\) Baruteau & Rieutord 2013; barotropic, \(S_{tw} = 0\)
    'conical' \(\varepsilon\sin^2\theta\), \(\varepsilon = a\) \(P_0\), \(P_2\) Guenel et al. 2016
    'solar' \(a\thinspace \times\) helioseismic fit, \(P_0 + P_2\), tanh step at \(x = 0.71\) of width 0.1 \(P_0\), \(P_2\) relative to 435 nHz; 4.5 nHz too fast at the equator and 12 nHz at the pole compared with the observations
    'solar_P4' \(a\thinspace (\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1)\), \(\Omega\) the fit below \(P_0\), \(P_2\), \(P_4\) diff_rot_solar, diff_rot_frame; defaults from Mukhopadhyay et al. 2026
    'tabulated' \(a\thinspace (\Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1)\), \(\Omega\) from diff_rot_file \(P_0 \dots P_{n_{\max}}\) below
    'user' \(a\sum_n f_n(r) P_n(\cos\theta)\), \(f_n\) from diff_rot_profiles the keys of diff_rot_profiles below

    'rigid'. The eigenvalues are known exactly: with \(\lambda_0\) the diff_rot = 0 eigenvalue,

    \[ \lambda(a, G) = \lambda_0\big(G(1 + a)\big) - i\thinspace m G a , \]

    with \(G\) = Gaspard.

    'solar_P4'. A rigid radiative zone joined by an erf step at the tachocline \(r_{tc}\) (width \(d_{tc}\)) to a convection zone whose rotation depends on latitude only:

    \[ \frac{\Omega}{2\pi} = \Omega_{RZ} + \tfrac12\left[\Omega_{CZ}(\theta) - \Omega_{RZ}\right]\left[1 + \mathrm{erf}\thinspace \frac{x - r_{tc}}{d_{tc}}\right], \]
    \[ \Omega_{CZ} = c_0 + c_2\cos^2\theta + c_4\cos^4\theta , \]

    with diff_rot_solar = [\(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{RZ}\), \(r_{tc}\), \(d_{tc}\)] (nHz, and fractions of R☉).

    'user'. diff_rot_profiles gives the shape of each even degree as a numpy expression (below). For example, the cylindrical rotation as a 'user' profile (it gives the same matrices as 'cylindrical'):

    par.diff_rot_type     = 'user'
par.diff_rot_profiles = {0: '(2/3)*r**2', 2: '-(2/3)*r**2'}
par.diff_rot_ampl     = -0.25

    The degrees must be even. Any degree up to 16 works with thermal_wind = 2 (32 with thermal_wind = 1).

    'tabulated'. The file holds ome[θ, r] (nHz), r (R☉) and theta (rad), the layout of data/diff_rot.npz of the Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 data set. tests/diff_rot/ls_to_tabulated.py converts the Larson & Schou 2018 table to it; bin/jsoc_hmi_2010_2026.npz is the mean of the 83 JSOC HMI 72-day inversions (hmi.V_sht_2drls, NACOEFF 36) from 2010-04-30 to 2026-06-29 in this layout, on the 151 table radii. kore projects \(\Omega\) on \(P_0 \dots P_{n_{\max}}\) at each table radius, fits each \(f_n\) in \(r\) over the table radii within 0.01 of the shell, and prints the fit residuals and the truncation error in nHz (the largest difference between the table and its expansion up to \(P_{n_{\max}}\)); check that line. For the Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 profile the truncation error near the poles at the surface is up to 17 nHz at diff_rot_nmax = 8; 12 is the working choice.

    Thermal wind

    With thermal = 1, thermal_wind chooses the latitudinal background entropy \(S_{tw}(r,\theta)\) and the terms the differential rotation adds to the heat equation. The advection of \(s\) by the rotation is always included.

    thermal_wind heat-equation terms \(S_{tw}\)
    0 the advection of \(s\) only none (e.g. Mirouh et al. 2016)
    1 (default) and the advection of \(S_{tw}\) by the perturbation thermal wind of \(\Omega\) to first order in \(\Delta\Omega\) (\(S_{tw} \propto G^2/\)Beyonce)
    2 the same thermal wind of the full \((1 + \Delta\Omega)^2\) balance; frame independent
    3 the same prescribed: \(\partial_\theta S_{tw}\) = dSdtheta \(\sin 2\theta\), uniform in \(r\) (Bekki et al. 2024)
    • thermal_wind 1 and 2 need Beyonce > 0. With timescale = 'rotation', set_scales() sets Beyonce = 0, so set it after calling set_scales().
    • dSdtheta is in the units of \(s\) (\(\delta s/c_p\)): dSdtheta \(= \Delta_\theta s/c_p\), with \(\Delta_\theta s\) in erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹. Bekki et al. use \(c_p\) = 4.17e8, Model S has about 3.46e8. Negative values give warm poles. The thermal-wind amplitude does not apply.
    • thermal_wind_radial = 0 drops \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\), as Bekki et al. 2022 and Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 do. Keeping it is the consistent choice, but it moves the solar columnar and high-frequency retrograde modes by 5–15 nHz.
    • With the thermal wind \(S_{tw}\) is the only latitudinal entropy gradient; in a convection zone with \(dS_0/dr = 0\) it is also the only background entropy gradient, and its effect on the modes is of the same order as the momentum terms.
    • With diff_rot = 1 the default thermal_wind = 1 includes the thermal wind. For the advection of \(s\) by the rotation alone, set thermal_wind = 0.

    Resolution and cost

    • A differential rotation couples harmonic degrees \(l\) that uniform rotation keeps apart, so the matrices get wider. Let \(n_{\max}\) be the highest degree of \(\Delta\Omega\). The momentum rows then couple degrees that differ by up to \(n_{\max}\) (\(P \to P\), \(T \to T\)) or \(n_{\max} + 1\) (\(P \leftrightarrow T\)).
    • \(S_{tw}\) has degrees up to \(n_{\max} + 2\) (thermal_wind 1) or \(2n_{\max} + 2\) (thermal_wind 2), and the heat rows couple degrees that far apart. Matrices are therefore widest with 'tabulated' and a large diff_rot_nmax.
    • With ricb = 0 (a full sphere) the profiles are evaluated at \(\lvert r\rvert\), to keep them smooth at the centre. 'Y20-wall-bounded' and 'shellular' (except even \(\sigma > 0\)) have a kink at \(r = 0\) and submatrices.py prints a WARNING. The 'solar' tachocline step needs N ≈ 400 at ricb = 0.
    • Modes that reach into a radiative interior have short radial wavelengths. In a full sphere with the radiative interior of stratification 'solar_rz', solar inertial modes needed N = 768 to 1024 for the spin_doctor residuals, although their eigenvalues converge at a lower N. Solver has the memory this takes.
    • Check convergence with spin_doctor as for any run; its residuals include the terms of the differential rotation.

    'user' profiles

    stratification_profile, the two diffusivity_profiles and the diff_rot_profiles are numpy expressions of r (the kore radius), x (\(r\cdot\)r_out with a solar structure, \(r\) otherwise) and p (stratification_param, diffusivity_param or diff_rot_param), with sin, cos, tan, arcsin, arccos, arctan, sinh, cosh, tanh, exp, log, log10, sqrt, abs, sign, pi, where, minimum, maximum, erf, erfc. For example:

    par.stratification = 'user'
par.stratification_profile = '2.5*exp(-((r - 0.75)/0.06)**2)'      # dS/dr in 1/L for r >= 0
par.diffusivity = 'user'
par.diffusivity_profiles = {'nu': '0.01 + 0.99*0.5*(1 + tanh((x - 0.70)/0.015))', 'kappa': '1'}

    kore evaluates them at \(\lvert r\rvert\), so in a full sphere \(dS/dr\) is odd and \(\hat\nu\), \(\hat\kappa\) and the \(f_n\) are even whatever the expression; for a profile that is smooth at the centre, \(dS/dr\) should still vanish there. The expressions must not contain #: spin_doctor writes them at the end of each params.dat row, after a #.

    Checks

    Before anything is built, kore stops on a background that cannot work: an unknown type, a parameter name of an older version of kore (the message gives the replacement), 'Wu 2005' without \(0 <\) r_out \(< 1\), 'inverse square' with ricb = 0, \(\delta\) without 'solar_rz', or a 'user' expression that does not evaluate. submatrices.py also warns about any name in the parameter file that kore does not define, so a misspelt name is not ignored without notice. spin_doctor records the background of every solution in params.dat (Output files).
