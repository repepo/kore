    Differential rotation in the heat equation: derivation, implementation, benchmarks

    kore has long included a differential rotation of the background in the momentum equation. This page explains why it must also enter the heat (entropy) equation, and records how that was derived, coded and tested. If you only want to run the code, start with the user guide, differential-rotation.md, which lists the parameters, profiles and examples. This page is the reference behind it.

    The physics in two sentences. A perturbation is carried around by the mean flow, so its entropy is advected by the differential rotation. And a star whose rotation rate varies with latitude and depth is only in equilibrium if its entropy also varies with latitude (thermal-wind balance); the flow of a perturbation across that latitudinal entropy gradient changes its entropy, which feeds back on the motion through buoyancy.

    How the page is organised:

    • Notation and code names defines the symbols and the kore jargon used below.
    • §0 states the result: three new terms in the heat equation.
    • §1 derives the background entropy \(S_{tw}\) that thermal-wind balance requires.
    • §2 writes the three terms in kore's spherical-harmonic form.
    • §3 explains how they map onto kore's matrices and operator labels.
    • §4 lists the published benchmarks and which terms each needs.
    • §5 records the implementation and its tests.
    • §6 compares with the solar inertial-mode calculations of Bekki et al. 2022 and Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025.
    • §7 lists what is still open.
    • §8 generalises the rotation profile to any Legendre degree ('solar_P4', 'tabulated') and repeats the solar benchmark like for like.
    • §9 adds the radiative interior of Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025b, fits of Model S and of a tabulated \(\Omega\) over the whole Sun, and follows the solar inertial modes as the inner boundary moves from 0.71 R☉ to the centre.

    Notation and code names

    Everything is dimensionless, in kore units: the length unit \(R\) is the outer radius of the shell, and the time unit is \(1/\Omega_0\) unless stated otherwise (see Gaspard below).

    Code concepts used on this page:

    • Sections. kore stacks its unknowns in blocks: u (poloidal scalar \(P\), from the radial component of the curl of the curl of the momentum equation), v (toroidal scalar \(T\), from the radial component of the curl) and h (the entropy \(s\), from the heat equation). "Section h" means the matrix rows that hold the heat equation.
    • Eigenproblem. kore solves \(A\mathbf x = \lambda B\mathbf x\). The real part of \(\lambda\) is the growth rate, and the frequency is minus its imaginary part.
    • Row weights. Before projection each equation is multiplied by a power of \(r\) (and in section h by \(p_0\)), so that its radial coefficients are polynomials and the matrices are banded. Every operator is also divided by \(L^3\), a normalisation common to all of kore's operators.
    • Gegenbauer bases. The rows are projected on Gegenbauer polynomials \(C^{(k)}\) (gebasis in submatrices.py). A row with radial derivatives up to order \(k\) needs at least the \(C^{(k)}\) basis; section h uses \(C^{(2)}\) by default.
    • Operator labels. Each radial operator is named by a short string, read by utils.decode_label. For example h1tw20_D0 means: section h; 1 lowers the row's power of \(r\) by one; the profile tw2 with derivative order 0; and _D0, no radial derivative of the unknown. A second profile can follow the first (h1tw20lho1_D0 is \(r\thinspace p_0\sigma_2\thinspace h'\) acting on \(P\)).
    • Parity in a full sphere. With no inner core (ricb = 0) kore expands each radial function in Chebyshev polynomials of one parity in \(r\). Every profile must then be even or odd in \(r\), and the odd ones are listed in the adj lists of submatrices.py.
    • Symmetry. symm = ±1 selects the equatorial symmetry of the mode; it fixes which degrees \(l\) appear for each unknown (ut.ell).
    • LBR. The Lantz–Braginsky–Roberts form of the anelastic approximation, in which the buoyancy depends on the entropy perturbation only.

    0. Result in brief

    kore's heat equation is the set of rows of section h, built by operators.entropy, thermal_advection and thermal_diffusion:

    \[ p_0\thinspace \frac{\partial s}{\partial t} = -p_0\thinspace u_r S_0' + \mathrm{ThermaD}\thinspace \nabla\cdot(\kappa\thinspace p_0\nabla s), \qquad p_0 = \rho_0 T_0. \]

    In words: the entropy perturbation changes because the radial flow carries fluid across the background entropy gradient, and it diffuses.

    The rows are multiplied by \(r^2\). Without thermal diffusion (ThermaD = 0) they are also divided by \(p_0\), and the weight is \(r\) instead of \(r^2\) unless the thermal wind is on (§3 and thermad0.md).

    Now let the background rotate differentially,

    \[ \Omega = \Omega_0\thinspace [1 + \Delta\Omega(r, \theta)], \qquad \Delta\Omega = f_0(r) + f_2(r)\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta). \]

    (§0 to §7 use this \(P_0 + P_2\) form; §8 extends it to any even degree.) Two things change.

    • The perturbation is advected by the mean flow of the differential rotation,
    \[ \mathbf U_0 = \Delta\Omega\thinspace r\sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi . \]
    • The background entropy acquires a latitudinal part \(S_{tw}(r, \theta)\), fixed by thermal-wind balance (§1).

    Linearising

    \[ \frac{\partial s}{\partial t} + \mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla s + \mathbf u\cdot\nabla(S_0 + S_{tw}) = \text{diffusion} \]

    adds three terms to the left-hand side of the heat equation:

    \[ p_0\left[\thinspace i\thinspace m\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace s + u_r\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial r} + \frac{u_\theta}{r}\thinspace \frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta} \right]. \qquad (1) \]

    They are, in order:

    • (a) advection. The perturbation is carried around at the local rotation rate. On its own this only shifts frequencies (it is the Doppler shift of a rigid rotation, made local).
    • (b) radial. The radial velocity moves fluid across the radial gradient of \(S_{tw}\).
    • (c) latitudinal. The meridional velocity moves fluid across the latitudinal gradient of \(S_{tw}\). This is the baroclinic coupling. In §2 it splits into (c), from \(P\), and (c′), from \(T\).

    The first term follows from the linearisation alone. The other two come from the background entropy, which in kore's framework is the thermal wind of §1 (kore's background is spherical in \(\rho_0\), \(T_0\) and \(g\), has no mean centrifugal term, and uses LBR buoyancy). Terms (b) and (c) must be switchable (thermal_wind), because the Boussinesq benchmark of Mirouh et al. 2016 imposes \(\Omega(r)\) with a purely radial \(T_0\) (§4).

    Nothing else changes:

    • The momentum equation keeps its present diff-rot term. The background pressure is a gradient and drops out of the curled equations, and the buoyancy stays \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ r\).
    • The diffusion acts on \(s\) only: \(S_{tw}\) is assumed to be maintained, like \(S_0\).
    • The mean flow does not advect \(S_{tw}\), because \(S_{tw}\) does not depend on \(\phi\): \(\mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla S_{tw} = 0\).
    • There is no viscous heating in kore.

    §2 gives the terms in kore's harmonic form, §3 what they need in the code.

    1. Thermal-wind entropy S_tw

    A rotation rate that varies along the rotation axis cannot be balanced by pressure alone. The centrifugal and Coriolis forces of the differential rotation then have a curl, and in a stratified fluid only a buoyancy force that varies with latitude can balance it. This is thermal-wind balance. This section computes the latitudinal entropy \(S_{tw}\) that kore's background must therefore have, and estimates its size.

    The balance

    Consider the steady background in the rotating frame, in kore units: time \(1/\Omega_0\), length \(R\), and buoyancy \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g(r)\thinspace s\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ r\), so that

    \[ N^2 = \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace \frac{dS}{dr}. \]

    The momentum balance of the mean flow is

    \[ \mathbf U_0\cdot\nabla\mathbf U_0 + 2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf U_0 = -\nabla p_b + \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace S_{tw}\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ r . \]

    Its left side, the centrifugal and Coriolis forces of the differential rotation, is

    \[ -\left[(1+\Delta\Omega)^2 - 1\right] r\sin\theta\thinspace \hat{\mathbf e}_ s , \]

    where \(\hat{\mathbf e}_ s\) is the cylindrical radial unit vector. Taking the curl removes the pressure, and its \(\phi\)-component gives

    \[ \frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial S_{tw}}{\partial\theta} = \frac{r\sin\theta}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g(r)}\thinspace \frac{\partial (1+\Delta\Omega)^2}{\partial z} \qquad (2) \]

    where \(z\) is the coordinate along the rotation axis:

    \[ \frac{\partial}{\partial z} = \cos\theta\thinspace \frac{\partial}{\partial r} - \frac{\sin\theta}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}. \]

    Equation (2) is the thermal-wind equation in kore's variables. In dimensional form it reads

    \[ r\sin\theta\thinspace \frac{\partial\Omega^2}{\partial z} = \frac{g}{c_p\thinspace r}\thinspace \frac{\partial S}{\partial\theta}, \]

    which is eq. (27) of Bekki, Cameron & Gizon 2022 (A&A 662, A16). The \(l = 0\) part of \(S_{tw}\) is absorbed in \(S_0(r)\).

    Linear and exact forms

    To first order in \(\Delta\Omega\), replace \((1+\Delta\Omega)^2\) by \(1 + 2\Delta\Omega\). Then (2) integrates in closed form:

    \[ \begin{aligned} S_{tw} &= \sigma_2(r)\thinspace P_2(\cos\theta) + \sigma_4(r)\thinspace P_4(\cos\theta),\cr \sigma_2 &= -\frac{2r^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[\frac{f_0'}{3} + \frac{f_2'}{21} + \frac{4}{7}\frac{f_2}{r}\right],\cr \sigma_4 &= -\frac{6r^2}{35\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[f_2' - \frac{2f_2}{r}\right]. \end{aligned} \qquad (3) \]

    This linear form is thermal_wind = 1.

    Keeping the \(\Delta\Omega^2\) term gives the exact form, thermal_wind = 2. It changes \(\sigma_2\) and \(\sigma_4\) by products of the profiles and adds a \(P_6\) component. In addition, when kore's time unit \(\tau\) is not \(1/\Omega_{\rm ref}\), so that \(G\) = Gaspard = \(\Omega_{\rm ref}\tau \neq 1\), the right side of (2) gets a factor \(G^2\). With \(e = 0\) for thermal_wind = 1 and \(e = 1\) for thermal_wind = 2, the closed forms are (checked by tests/heat_dr/tw_sympy.py):

    \[ \begin{aligned} \sigma_2 &= -\frac{2r^2G^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[(1+e f_0)\left(\frac{f_0'}{3} + \frac{f_2'}{21} + \frac{4}{7}\frac{f_2}{r}\right) + \frac{e\thinspace f_2\thinspace (f_0' + f_2')}{21}\right],\cr \sigma_4 &= -\frac{6r^2G^2}{35\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[(1+e f_0)\left(f_2' - \frac{2f_2}{r}\right) + e\left(f_2 f_0' + \frac{4}{11} f_2 f_2' + \frac{14}{11}\frac{f_2^2}{r}\right)\right],\cr \sigma_6 &= -e\thinspace \frac{4r^2G^2}{77\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\thinspace f_2\left(f_2' - \frac{2f_2}{r}\right). \end{aligned} \qquad (3') \]

    How much the exact form matters. For the conical profile below, it multiplies \(\sigma_2\) by \(1 + 2\varepsilon/3\) and \(\sigma_4\) by \(1 + 12\varepsilon/11\): 17 % and 27 % for \(\varepsilon = -0.25\). The \(\sigma_6\) term needs

    \[ P_6(X) = \frac{231X^6 - 315X^4 + 105X^2 - 5}{16} \]

    and couplings \(\Delta l = \pm 6\) (for \(P\)) and \(\pm 5\) (for \(T\)) in §2 and §3.

    Frame independence. Only the exact form is independent of the frame: a run in a frame rotating at \(\Omega_{\rm ref}(1 + c)\), with \(f_0\) shifted by \(-c\), gives the same modes. With the linear form the choice of frame moves the modes slightly (§6).

    Checks on simple profiles

    • Cylindrical rotation, constant on cylinders: \(\Delta\Omega = \varepsilon\thinspace r^2\sin^2\theta\), that is \(f_0 = (2/3)\thinspace \varepsilon r^2\) and \(f_2 = -(2/3)\thinspace \varepsilon r^2\). It gives \(\sigma_2 = \sigma_4 = 0\) in both versions, as it must: a barotropic rotation needs no thermal wind.
    • Conical rotation, constant on radial lines: \(\Delta\Omega = \varepsilon\sin^2\theta\). It gives the values below. For \(\varepsilon > 0\) (equator faster, as in the Sun's convection zone) the poles are warmer than the equator, the known solar sense.
    \[ \sigma_2 = \frac{16\thinspace \varepsilon r}{21\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}, \qquad \sigma_4 = -\frac{8\thinspace \varepsilon r}{35\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}, \qquad S_{tw}(\text{pole}) - S_{tw}(\text{equator}) = \frac{\varepsilon r}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g} > 0 . \]
    • The 'solar' fit, a tanh step at the tachocline radius rtc. Here \(f_0'\) and \(f_2'\) are localised at the tachocline. The \((4/7)\thinspace f_2/r\) and \(-(12/35)\thinspace f_2/r\) parts of (3) fill the convection zone, where kore's \(S_0'\) is zero, so there \(S_{tw}\) is the only background entropy gradient.

    How large is the thermal wind

    Take the solar case: Beyonce = 4.9e4, \(f_2 = -0.198\), \(r \approx 0.9\), \(g \approx 1\). Then \(\sigma_2 \approx 4\times10^{-6}\) and \(\sigma_4 \approx -10^{-6}\). The pole-minus-equator contrast of \(S_{tw}\) is about \(5\times10^{-6}\) in units of \(c_p\) (kore's \(s\) is \(\delta s/c_p\)). With \(c_p \approx 3.5\times10^{8}\) that is \(\approx 1.9\times10^{3}\) erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹, the thermal-wind value at the top of the range Bekki et al. scan.

    The small number is misleading. \(S_{tw}\) scales as \(1/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\), while the buoyancy it produces is \(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g\thinspace s\). So in the convection zone, where \(S_0' = 0\), the entropy perturbation driven by term (c) feeds back on the momentum equation at order \(\Delta\Omega\) relative to the Coriolis force, independently of Beyonce. That is the same order as the momentum diff-rot term. The thermal-wind terms are therefore leading order for convection-zone modes, not a correction: this is the baroclinic coupling that destabilises the high-latitude modes in Bekki et al.

    Prescribed latitudinal entropy (thermal_wind = 3)

    Bekki et al. 2024 (and Bekki et al. 2022, eq. 26) impose a latitudinal entropy gradient that is independent of \(\Omega\) and uniform in \(r\):

    \[ \frac{\partial s}{\partial\theta} = \Delta_\theta s\thinspace \sin 2\theta . \]

    Since \(\sin 2\theta = -(2/3)\thinspace dP_2/d\theta\), this is \(S_{tw} = \sigma_2 P_2\) with a constant \(\sigma_2\) and nothing else:

    \[ \sigma_2 = -\tfrac{2}{3}\thinspace \mathtt{dSdtheta}, \qquad \sigma_4 = \sigma_6 = 0, \qquad \mathtt{dSdtheta} = \Delta_\theta s/c_p . \]

    kore's \(s\) is \(\delta s/c_p\), and \(\Delta_\theta s < 0\) gives warm poles. Because \(\sigma_2\) is constant in \(r\), term (b) vanishes and only (c) and (c′) act. \(\Omega\) still enters through (a) and the momentum equation. For uniform rotation use diff_rot_type = 'rigid' with diff_rot_ampl = 0.

    What is neglected

    \(S_{tw}\) is the thermal wind consistent with LBR. The full anelastic balance would add

    \[ \frac{S_0'}{\rho_0\thinspace g}\thinspace p_b \]

    to \(S_{tw}\). This is the same \(p_1 S_0'\) term that kore drops for the perturbations, so it matters only where \(S_0' \neq 0\) (the radiative zone).

    Also neglected, consistently with kore's spherical background, are three effects of relative size \(\Delta\Omega/(\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g)\) in the anelastic case, negligible for the Sun (Beyonce ≈ 5e4):

    • the perturbation density in the background centrifugal acceleration;
    • the \(\theta\)-dependence of \(\rho_0 T_0\) and \(g\) induced by the thermal wind;
    • the thermal-wind density \(\rho_{tw}\) in the mass flux.

    2. The three terms in kore's harmonic form

    kore expands every field in spherical harmonics of one azimuthal order \(m\) and solves for a radial function per degree \(l\). Multiplying by a function of latitude, such as \(\Delta\Omega\) or \(S_{tw}\), mixes neighbouring degrees. This section writes the three terms as such couplings. All of them are built from two small matrices, \(X\) and \(D\).

    Fields

    The entropy and the velocity are

    \[ s = \sum_l s_l(r)\thinspace S_l^m, \qquad \mathbf u = \rho_0^{-1}\nabla\times\nabla\times(\rho_0 P\thinspace \mathbf r) + \nabla\times(T\thinspace \mathbf r), \]

    with \(S_l^m\) the Schmidt harmonics of ut.Ylm, \(L = l(l+1)\) and \(h = \ln\rho_0\). The terms need two velocity components, for one degree:

    \[ u_r = \frac{L P}{r}\thinspace S_l, \qquad u_\theta = \frac{1}{r}\left[\partial_r(rP) + r h' P\right]\partial_\theta S_l + \frac{i\thinspace m T}{\sin\theta}\thinspace S_l . \]

    Angular coupling matrices

    With \(x = \cos\theta\), two recurrences define matrices on the degree index at fixed \(m\) (row: target degree \(l'\); column: source degree \(l\)):

    \[ \begin{aligned} x\thinspace S_l &= p_l\thinspace S_{l+1} + q_l\thinspace S_{l-1} &&\to X,\cr (1 - x^2)\thinspace \frac{dS_l}{dx} &= (l+1)\thinspace q_l\thinspace S_{l-1} - l\thinspace p_l\thinspace S_{l+1} &&\to D,\cr p_l &= \frac{\sqrt{(l+1)^2 - m^2}}{2l+1}, \qquad q_l = \frac{\sqrt{l^2 - m^2}}{2l+1}. \end{aligned} \]

    Multiplying by a polynomial in \(\cos\theta\) is then the same polynomial of the matrix \(X\):

    \[ P_2(X) = \frac{3X^2 - 1}{2}, \qquad P_4(X) = \frac{35X^4 - 30X^2 + 3}{8}, \qquad P_2'(X) = 3X, \qquad P_4'(X) = \frac{35X^3 - 15X}{2}. \]

    For instance, the non-zero entries of \(P_2(X)\) in column \(l\) are

    \[ P_2(X)_ {l,l} = \tfrac12\left[3(p_l\thinspace q_{l+1} + q_l\thinspace p_{l-1}) - 1\right], \qquad P_2(X)_ {l+2,l} = \tfrac32\thinspace p_{l+1}\thinspace p_l, \qquad P_2(X)_ {l-2,l} = \tfrac32\thinspace q_{l-1}\thinspace q_l . \]

    In general they are Gaunt coefficients,

    \[ X_{l'l} = \sqrt{\frac{2l'+1}{2l+1}}\thinspace \sqrt{\frac{4\pi}{3}}\thinspace (-1)^m\thinspace \mathrm{gaunt}(l',1,l,-m,0,m), \]

    and the same with \(n = 2, 4\) in place of 1 for \(P_n(X)\).

    The terms

    Row \(l'\) of the heat equation gains the following terms, before the \(r^2 p_0\) row weight. \(L_l\) is that of the source degree \(l\).

    \[ \begin{aligned} \text{(a) advection:}\quad & i\thinspace m G\thinspace \left[f_0\thinspace \delta_{l'l} + f_2\thinspace P_2(X)_ {l'l}\right] s_l ,\cr \text{(b) radial:}\quad & \frac{L_l P_l}{r}\left[\sigma_2'\thinspace P_2(X)_ {l'l} + \sigma_4'\thinspace P_4(X)_ {l'l}\right],\cr \text{(c) latitudinal, from } P\text{:}\quad & \frac{P_l + r P_l' + r h' P_l}{r^2}\left[\sigma_2\thinspace (3XD)_ {l'l} + \sigma_4\left(\tfrac12(35X^3 - 15X)D\right)_ {l'l}\right],\cr \text{(c}'\text{) latitudinal, from } T\text{:}\quad & -\frac{i\thinspace m T_l}{r}\left[\sigma_2\thinspace (3X)_ {l'l} + \sigma_4\left(\tfrac12(35X^3 - 15X)\right)_ {l'l}\right]. \end{aligned} \]
    term couples \(\Delta l\)
    (a) advection \(s \to s\) \(0, \pm 2\)
    (b) radial \(P \to s\) \(0, \pm 2, \pm 4\)
    (c) latitudinal, \(P\) \(P \to s\) \(0, \pm 2, \pm 4\)
    (c′) latitudinal, \(T\) \(T \to s\) \(\pm 1, \pm 3\)

    Remarks:

    • With thermal_wind = 2 the sums run to \(\sigma_6\), and \(\Delta l\) goes up to \(\pm 6\) for \(P\) and \(\pm 5\) for \(T\). The extra polynomial is given below.
    • The even \(\Delta l\) for \(P\) and odd \(\Delta l\) for \(T\) match the equatorial-symmetry classes of ut.ell (section h uses the poloidal set of degrees). So the block structure is that of the momentum diff-rot blocks, with \(\Delta l = \pm 4\) added.
    • For \(m = 0\) the terms (a) and (c′) vanish. The couplings into \(l = 0\), which is absent from kore's basis, are harmless: nothing feeds back from \(s_0\).
    • Build \(P_2(X)\), \(P_4(X)\) and \(P_n'(X)D\) from \(X\) and \(D\) with about five degrees more than lmax, and cut afterwards. Formed at lmax they are wrong in the top degrees (errors of order one in the entries).
    • Term (a) is anti-Hermitian. In the entropy budget it only shifts the frequency, by an amount set by \(\int p_0\thinspace \Delta\Omega\thinspace \lvert s\rvert^2\thinspace dV\), while (b) and (c) exchange entropy "energy" with the flow.

    The polynomial needed for \(\sigma_6\) is

    \[ P_6'(X) = \frac{1386X^5 - 1260X^3 + 210X}{16}. \]

    sympy, 0 failures; tests/heat_dr/tw_sympy.py and heat_check.py repeat the main checks:

    • (3) against direct integration of (2);
    • the curl of the background balance vanishes with \(S_{tw}\) in place (linear and exact);
    • the barotropic check of §1;
    • the matrices \(X\), \(D\), \(P_2(X)\), \(P_4(X)\), \(P'(X)D\) against quadrature projections for \(m = 0\ldots 3\);
    • (b), (c), (c′) against the full 3-D \(\mathbf u\cdot\nabla S_{tw}\) built from \(P\), \(T\) and \(\ln\rho_0\) with sympy and projected on \(S_{l'}\), for \(m = 0, 1, 2\) and every \(\Delta l\).

    Independent readers redid the thermal wind with full spherical vector calculus, the linearisation, the couplings with Wigner 3j symbols, and the velocity sign conventions against kore's assembled Coriolis operator. None found a discrepancy.

    3. What kore needs

    This section maps the terms of §2 onto kore's building blocks: radial profiles (functions of \(r\) known on the radial grid), operator labels, and the assembly loops.

    Radial profiles

    • \(f_0\) and \(f_2\) exist already (abu, aub).
    • New are \(\sigma_2\) and \(\sigma_4\) from (3). They need \(g\), \(g'\), \(f'\) and \(f''\): gra, and spv/svp and psl/pls, which give \(r f'\) and \(r^2 f''\).
    • Following pdS = \(p_0\thinspace dS/dr\) and kps = \(\kappa p_0\), the code uses the products tw2 = \(p_0\sigma_2\), tw4 = \(p_0\sigma_4\), tp2 = \(p_0\sigma_2'\) and tp4 = \(p_0\sigma_4'\). Separate tp profiles are needed because a label such as h1tw21_D0 would mean \(r\thinspace (p_0\sigma_2)'\thinspace P\), the derivative of the product, which is not (b).

    Parity in a full sphere

    At ricb = 0, \(\sigma_2\) and \(\sigma_4\) are even in \(r\) (\(f\) is even, \(f'\) and \(g\) are odd). So tw2 and tw4 are even, like all other profiles. But tp2 and tp4 are odd. They must be added to the adj lists of submatrices.py (lines 286 and 340; also tests/heat_dr/ebal.py and ru_insane.row_parity), next to gra, pdS and dSd. Otherwise term (b) is silently dropped in full-sphere runs. (The 'solar' profile's parity defect in \(r\) is fixed by evaluating the profiles at \(\lvert r\rvert\), §5.)

    Operators and labels

    Like the other section-h operators, the rows are multiplied by \(r^2 p_0\) and divided by \(L_{l'}^3\):

    All twelve labels parse with utils.decode_label (the 13-character two-function form for the first and last of (c)).

    Signs and factors

    The section-h rows of \(A\) hold the right-hand side of the heat equation: thermal_advection enters as \(-r p_0 S_0' u_r\), with \(B\) = +entropy. So the three left-hand-side terms of (1) go into \(A\) with a minus sign. This is opposite to the momentum rows, where \(B\) = −inertia and the diff-rot block is added with +.

    • (a) carries the factor Gaspard, like the momentum diff-rot operator. Both had none before the second round, so only timescale = 'rotation' was right.
    • (b), (c) and (c′) carry \(G^2\) through \(\sigma_n\) (but not with thermal_wind = 3).
    • thermal_wind = 0/1/2/3 selects whether (b), (c) and (c′) are included, and in which form; (a) always is.

    Assembly

    • In submatrices.py the \(T\)-column labels are registered with vector parity vT, the others with vP.
    • assemble.py section h used to call only diagonal operators. It needs an \(s\)-column loop over \(\Delta l \in \lbrace -2, 0, 2\rbrace\), a \(P\)-column loop over \(\lbrace -4, \ldots, 4\rbrace\) and a \(T\)-column loop over \(\lbrace -3, -1, 1, 3\rbrace\). These use the offd of operators.differential_rotation: for \(P\) and \(s\), offd = \(\Delta l/2\); for \(T\), the symm-dependent offsets of its 'utor' branches.
    • The thermal boundary rows and forced runs need no change: every 'h' label carries the bc rows, and −i wf entropy is already added.

    Without thermal diffusion (ThermaD = 0)

    With ThermaD = 0 the section-h rows are \(r \times (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) in a \(C^{(0)}\) basis. Term (a) then becomes h0abu0_D0 and h0aub0_D0 (no \(p_0\)). Terms (b), (c) and (c′) do not fit there: the \(r\) row weight makes the \(P/r\) part of (c) non-polynomial, and the basis has no \(C^{(1)}\) for the \(P'\) term.

    So with ThermaD = 0 and thermal_wind ≥ 1 (decided only in utils.adiab_tw), the section-h rows are \(r^2 \times (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) in a \(C^{(1)}\) basis: gebasis[2] = 1, still no bc rows, chop = 0. The labels are the same (decode_label gives section h the \(r^2\) weight), and tw2/4/6, tp2/4/6 are \(\sigma_n\) and \(\sigma_n'\) without \(p_0\). With ThermaD = 0 and thermal_wind = 0 or diff_rot = 0 nothing changes (\(r \times (\mathrm{eq})/p_0\) in \(C^{(0)}\)). Details and tests are in thermad0.md.

    submatrices.py raises an error if a label's derivative order is above its section's basis. Before, G[gbx][gbx-dx] with dx > gbx indexed from the end of the list, which silently picked a wrong basis.

    Diagnostics

    The new powers couple different \(l\). utils4pp (used by spin_doctor) therefore builds the diff-rot force and heat terms on an \((r, \cos\theta)\) grid once per mode (diff_rot_setup) and projects them back on the harmonics, so that each per-\(l\) worker can pair them with its own \(l\) (§5). The matrix-level budget, heat_blocks() in tests/heat_dr/ebal.py, is the independent check. It verifies the entropy balance

    \[ \lambda\langle E\rangle - \langle A\rangle - \langle D\rangle - \langle H\rangle = 0 . \]

    4. Benchmarks and which terms they need

    Mirouh et al. 2016 (JFM 800, 213; kore shellular_boussinesq). Boussinesq, with the rotation profile imposed from Rieutord 2006's baroclinic flow and a radial \(T_0\) only:

    \[ \Omega(r) = 1 + \frac{N^2}{2}\thinspace (1 - r^2). \]

    Their heat equation (2.9) is

    \[ (\lambda + i\thinspace m\Omega)\thinspace T + r v_r = \frac{E}{P}\thinspace \Delta T . \]

With (3) switched on, \(\sigma_2 = 2N^2r^2/(3\thinspace \mathrm{Beyonce})\) would be as large as \(S_0'\), and the comparison would test the wrong model. kore runs it with structure 'Boussinesq', stratification 'linear' and thermal_wind = 0 (differential_rotation_details.md §11).

    Bekki et al. 2022. Fully compressible, solar \(\rho_0\), helioseismic \(\Omega\), and \(S_{tw}\) from (2) with the full \(\Omega^2\), with \(s_0\) uniform in \(r\). It needs (a), (c) and (c′). kore's \(S_{tw}\) also has (b), which they drop by construction. The momentum terms of their eq. (1),

    \[ -(\Omega-\Omega_0)\thinspace \partial_\phi\mathbf v - 2\Omega\thinspace \hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf v - r\sin\theta\thinspace (\mathbf v\cdot\nabla\Omega), \]

    are kore's existing diff-rot operator.

    Baruteau & Rieutord 2013, Guenel et al. 2016, Astoul et al. 2021. thermal = 0, so unaffected.

    6. Benchmark: solar inertial modes (Bekki et al. 2022, Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025)

    The best target is Mukhopadhyay, Bekki, Zhu & Gizon 2025 (A&A, arXiv 2501.16797). It uses the set-up of Bekki, Cameron & Gizon 2022 with Model S, and tabulates modes to 1e-4 nHz for compressible, anelastic and Boussinesq models. Their anelastic model is kore's.

    The set-up is that of tests/diff_rot/mk_bekki.py with drtype=Y20: N 64, lmax \(m + 63\), \(\nu = \kappa = 10^{12}\) cm² s⁻¹, \(\delta = 0\) (adiabatic background, \(S_0' = 0\)). This section uses kore's \(P_0 + P_2\) rotation profile; §8 repeats the comparison with their full \(\Omega(r, \theta)\).

    Uniform rotation. kore matches their anelastic Tables C.1–C.4 to 0.006 nHz or better for 27 modes (\(n = 0\) and 1 Rossby, high-frequency retrograde (HFR), columnar; \(m = 1\ldots 16\)). This needs the Model S density: the stock 'smoothed sun' fit is up to 48 % off near the surface, which moves the \(n = 1\), HFR and columnar modes by 1.5–8 nHz (18 at \(m = 16\)).

    Time unit (Gaspard). The same problem solved with time unit \(1/\Omega_0\) (\(G = 1\)) and with time unit \(1/\Omega_{\rm Carrington}\) (\(G \neq 1\)), in the same frame, agrees to 2e-6 nHz.

    Frame. Changing the frame itself (426.86 vs 456 nHz, with \(\Delta\Omega\) redefined relative to it) does not matter with thermal_wind = 2 (2e-6 nHz); with thermal_wind = 1 it moves modes by 0.1–0.9 nHz.

    Differential rotation (their Tables C.5–C.9, thermal_wind = 2 without \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\), as in the papers). kore minus target, in nHz:

    • \(n = 0\) Rossby: within 0.8;
    • \(n = 1\) Rossby: within 1.1–2.7;
    • high-latitude, m 1 and 2: within 0.2–1.5;
    • HFR: 2.7–6.2;
    • columnar: 3.4–9.2, growing with \(m\).

    The remaining difference is the rotation profile. kore's \(\Omega\) here has \(P_0 + P_2\) only (4.5 nHz too fast at the equator, 12 at the pole); theirs has \(P_4\) and radial shear.

    Term (b). Keeping \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\) (kore always did before §8 added the switch) moves columnar modes by 5–15 nHz and HFR modes by 8–10 nHz. At \(\delta = 0\) it also makes the low latitudes superadiabatic: \(m = 1\) symm +1 then has a weakly growing mode (12.4 nHz, growth 1.8 nHz).

    Not reproduced with the \(P_0 + P_2\) profile:

    • the growing \(m = 1\) high-latitude mode of Bekki et al. 2022 (−90.9 nHz, growth 14.1 nHz); kore's nearest modes are damped by 3 nHz;
    • the baroclinic onset of Bekki et al. 2024; kore needs a 4–8 times larger \(\Delta_\theta s\).

    Both were caused by the \(P_2\)-only rotation profile. With the observed \(\Omega(r, \theta)\), kore has the growing high-latitude mode (+11.3 nHz; Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025: +11.7) and the onset near 500 (§8).

    The \(\Delta_\theta s\) runs used a run-local edit of \(\sigma_2\), \(\sigma_4\). With thermal_wind = 3 and dSdtheta = \(\Delta_\theta s/c_p\), the warm-poles case at 4000 erg g⁻¹ K⁻¹ gives \(A\) and \(B\) bitwise equal to that run (−101.42 nHz, growth +7.30 nHz).

    8. Generic differential rotation: any Pₙ, 'solar_P4', 'tabulated' (2026-10-13)

    Commit 92a0d020. The rotation profile is no longer limited to \(P_0 + P_2\). It can be any sum of even Legendre polynomials, each with an amplitude factor \(a_n\):

    \[ \Delta\Omega = \sum_{n\ \text{even}} a_n\thinspace f_n(r)\thinspace P_n(\cos\theta). \]

    This makes the observed solar rotation usable, either as a fit ('solar_P4') or as a helioseismic table ('tabulated').

    Momentum couplings

    \(n = 0\) and 2 keep the closed forms of diff_rot_coefficients.py. For \(n \ge 4\) the new bin/diff_rot_generic.py projects the diff-rot force of each source degree on the target degrees:

    \[ \mathbf W = G\left[\Delta\Omega\thinspace (i\thinspace m\thinspace \mathbf u + 2\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf u) + \sin\theta\thinspace \left(r\thinspace \partial_r\Delta\Omega\thinspace u_r + \partial_\theta\Delta\Omega\thinspace u_\theta\right)\hat{\mathbf e}_ \phi\right]. \]
    • The projection uses Gauss–Legendre quadrature in \(\cos\theta\), exact for these polynomial integrands.
    • It turns the 12 primitive projections into the 18 coefficients of the existing labels, in the naming and normalisation of diff_rot_coefficients.py. They are cached per \((n, m)\) and built to \(\mathrm{lmax} + n + 6\).
    • The radial profiles \(f_n\), \(r f_n'\) and \(r^2 f_n''\) have the codes abN, svN, plN (N = 4, 6, 8, then a, c, e, g for 10–16).
    • Couplings: \(P \to P\) and \(T \to T\) have \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le n\), and \(P \leftrightarrow T\) have \(\lvert\Delta l\rvert \le n + 1\), so assemble.py loops over \(\Delta l\) up to utils.dr_nmax (+1).
    • diff_rot_generic = 1 builds \(n = 0, 2\) with the quadrature too, as a self-check (agrees to ~1e-12).

    Thermal-wind entropy

    For thermal_wind = 1 or 2 with degrees above 2, radial_profiles.sigma_all computes the Legendre coefficients of

    \[ \frac{\partial S}{\partial x} = -\frac{r^2G^2}{\mathrm{Beyonce}\thinspace g}\left[x\thinspace \partial_r F + \frac{(1 - x^2)\thinspace \partial_x F}{r}\right], \qquad F = 2\Delta\Omega \ \ \text{or}\ \ (1 + \Delta\Omega)^2 - 1, \]

    by quadrature on the Chebyshev nodes, and integrates in \(x\). \(\sigma_n'\) comes from Chebyshev differentiation.

    • \(S_{tw}\) has degrees \(2\ldots\mathrm{nmax} + 2\) (thermal_wind 1) or \(2\ldots 2\thinspace \mathrm{nmax} + 2\) (thermal_wind 2).
    • Profiles: tw2…tw8, twa, twc, …, twy (\(n = 34\)) for \(\sigma_n\), and tp* for \(\sigma_n'\).
    • heat_dr_coefficients.py builds \(P_n(X)\), \(P_n'(X)\) and \(P_n'(X)D\) for any \(n\) by the Legendre recurrence.
    • thermal_wind = 3 is unchanged (\(\sigma_2\) only).

    Profiles

    'solar_P4' is an analytic fit with \(P_0\), \(P_2\) and \(P_4\), with an erf step at the tachocline:

    \[ \Omega = \Omega_{\rm RZ} + \left[\Omega_{\rm CZ}(\theta) - \Omega_{\rm RZ}\right]\frac{1 + \mathrm{erf}\big((x - \mathrm{rtc})/\mathrm{dtc}\big)}{2}, \qquad \Omega_{\rm CZ} = c_0 + c_2\cos^2\theta + c_4\cos^4\theta . \]

    diff_rot_solar = [\(c_0\), \(c_2\), \(c_4\), \(\Omega_{\rm RZ}\), rtc, dtc] = [464, −76, −50, 433.5, 0.71, 0.045] (nHz and \(R_\odot\); Mukhopadhyay et al. 2026).

    'tabulated' reads \(\Omega(r, \theta)\) from diff_rot_file, an .npz with ome[θ, r] (nHz), r (R☉) and theta (rad). This is the layout of the file data/diff_rot.npz of Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025; tests/diff_rot/ls_to_tabulated.py converts the Larson & Schou 2018 table. At each table radius the profile is projected on \(P_0\ldots P_{\mathrm{nmax}}\) (diff_rot_nmax, 4–16), then fitted over the shell by a Chebyshev series of degree diff_rot_rdeg (24). The fit and truncation errors are printed.

    Both use \(\Delta\Omega = \Omega/\Omega_{\rm ref} - 1\), with \(\Omega_{\rm ref}/2\pi\) = diff_rot_frame (456 nHz), and \(x = r\cdot\)r_out for a solar structure. For the Mukhopadhyay et al. 2025 profile the truncation error at nmax 8 is up to 17 nHz near the poles at the surface; nmax 12 is the working choice.

    Amplitude controls

    These apply to every profile type:

    • diff_rot_term_amp = \([a_0, a_2, \ldots, a_{16}]\) multiplies each \(P_n\) term of \(\Delta\Omega\) (missing entries are 1).
    • diff_rot_ampl multiplies the whole of \(\Delta\Omega\), for every type. (Until 2026-10-23 it was diff_rot_amplitude, which was the exponent of 'shellular' and \(N_0^2\) of 'shellular_boussinesq'.)
    • thermal_wind_amplitude multiplies \(S_{tw}\), which is otherwise the thermal wind of the scaled \(\Delta\Omega\).
    • thermal_wind_radial = 0 drops \(u_r\thinspace \partial_r S_{tw}\) (term (b)), as the papers do; 1 (default) keeps it.

    Model S density
