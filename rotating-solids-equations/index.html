    Rotating solids equations

    This page gives the equations of the rotating solids (rotdyn = 1, Rotating solids), the torques that couple them to the fluid, and the angular momentum balance that spin_doctor checks (resid𝐋). They are those of the conductive_IC branch (its rows in assemble.py, utils.gamma_visc, gamma_magnetic, utils4pp.angrymom and torque_walls), written for any density profile and any poloidal background field in bin/rotdyn.py.

    Equations

    The mantle and the inner core turn about the rotation axis at the rates \(\omega_M\) and \(\omega_{IC}\) relative to the frame, and \(\varphi\) is the angle of the inner core relative to the mantle. Each solid's angular momentum changes by the torques on it. For a mode \(e^{\lambda t}\)

    \[ \lambda I_M\,\omega_M = \Gamma_M + K\varphi, \qquad \lambda I_{IC}\,\omega_{IC} = \Gamma_{IC} - K\varphi, \qquad \lambda\varphi = \omega_{IC} - \omega_M - c\,\varphi , \]

    with \(I_M\), \(I_{IC}\) = MoIZ_M, MoIZ_IC, \(K\) = gTorque Gaspard² and \(c\) = Gaspard/OmgtauIC in the units of the run. \(\Gamma_M\) and \(\Gamma_{IC}\) are the torques of the fluid on the mantle and on the inner core (below): the viscous ones times vtrq, the magnetic ones times mtrq. These are the last three rows of \(A\) and \(B\), after all the fields (utils.offset['rot']).

    The walls. The fluid follows the solids through the no-slip walls. A rigid rotation \(\omega\) about the axis is the toroidal flow of degree 1 with \(T = \omega r\) (\(u_\phi = T\sin\theta\), kore's \(Y_1^0\) being \(\cos\theta\)), so the no-slip rows of that degree become

    \[ T(1) = \omega_M, \qquad T(r_i) = r_i\,\omega_{IC} , \]

    and every other wall condition is unchanged. At a stress-free outer wall (bco = 0) the row of \(T_1\) stays the stress-free one, and a drag acts there on the slip relative to the mantle, \(T_1(1) - \omega_M\) (Drag at the outer wall equations); without a drag only the field couples the fluid to the mantle. A rigid rotation carries an axisymmetric field without changing it, so the rotating solids leave the conditions on the field, and the field of a conducting inner core or mantle, as they are for solids at rest, but for a fluid slipping along a conducting outer wall, whose electric field enters the wall through the slip relative to the mantle (assemble.bc_b_slip).

    Torques

    The torques are the moments of the stresses on each wall. With \(T\) the toroidal flow of degree 1, the axial angular momentum of the fluid is

    \[ L = \int\rho_0\,s\,u_\phi\,dV = \frac{8\pi}{3}\int_{r_i}^{1}\rho_0\,r^3\,T\,dr . \]

    Viscous. The shear stress on a sphere is \(\sigma_{r\phi} = c_\nu\,(T' - T/r)\sin\theta\) with \(c_\nu = \rho_0\,\nu\,\)ViscosD, so that

    \[ \Gamma^\nu_M = -\frac{8\pi}{3}\,c_\nu\,r^2\left(rT' - T\right)\Big|_{r=1}, \qquad \Gamma^\nu_{IC} = \frac{8\pi}{3}\,c_\nu\,r^2\left(rT' - T\right)\Big|_{r=r_i} . \]

    Magnetic. The magnetic stress on the fluid's side of each wall, with \(B_{0\phi} = 0\) there:

    \[ \Gamma^B_M = -\mathrm{Hendrik}\oint s\,B_{0r}\,b_\phi\,dS\,\Big|_{r=1}, \qquad \Gamma^B_{IC} = \mathrm{Hendrik}\oint s\,B_{0r}\,b_\phi\,dS\,\Big|_{r=r_i} . \]

    It acts on everything beyond the wall: a thin conducting layer, a resolved mantle or a conducting inner core (at an insulating wall \(b_\phi = 0\)). With \(b_\phi = -\sum_l g_l\,\partial_\theta Y_l\) and \(B_{0r} = \sum_n n(n+1)\,h_n Y_n/r\) (the degrees \(n\) of the poloidal background, cnorm included),

    \[ \oint s\,B_{0r}\,b_\phi\,dS = \sum_l C_l\,g_l, \qquad C_l = 2\pi r^2\sum_n n(n+1)\,h_n\,\frac{2l(l+1)}{(2l+1)(2n+1)}\left(\delta_{n,l-1} - \delta_{n,l+1}\right), \]

    which is \(\tfrac{16\pi}{5}\,r^2 h_1 g_2\) for a dipole (conductive_IC's gamma_magnetic).

    Gravitational. \(K\varphi\) on the mantle and \(-K\varphi\) on the inner core, which also relaxes towards alignment at the rate \(c\).

    The angular momentum balance: resid𝐋

    The fluid's angular momentum changes only through the torques on it. spin_doctor computes them from the fields, apart from the matrices, and checks

    \[ \lambda L = -\Gamma_M - \Gamma_{IC} , \]

    with the viscous torques from \(T\) at the walls and the magnetic ones from the stress \(\mathrm{Hendrik}\,(B_{0r}\,\mathbf b + b_r\,\mathbf B_0)\) on a grid in \(\theta\) at each wall, for any poloidal and toroidal background (rotdyn.viscous_torques, wall_torques), whatever vtrq and mtrq. The other forces exert no net axial torque: the pressure is normal to the walls, the buoyancy radial, and for \(m = 0\) neither the Coriolis force nor the advection by a differential rotation \(\Delta\Omega\) has one, since \(\int\rho_0\,\mathbf u\cdot\nabla(s^2\Delta\Omega)\,dV = 0\) between impenetrable walls.

    Equatorial. For \(m = 1\), symm = -1 the same balance holds for the equatorial angular momentum, with

    \[ L = \frac{16\pi}{3}\int_{r_i}^{1}\rho_0\,r^3\,T\,dr = \frac{L_x - iL_y}{N} , \]

    \(N = -1/\sqrt2\) the normalisation of kore's \(Y_1^1 = N\sin\theta\,e^{i\phi}\), the torques in the same combination (the viscous ones with \(16\pi/3\) for \(8\pi/3\)), and the Coriolis torque \(-\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf L\), which is \(i\,\mathrm{Gaspard}\,L\):

    \[ \lambda L = -\Gamma_M - \Gamma_{IC} + i\,\mathrm{Gaspard}\,L . \]

    A differential rotation would add a torque here, so this case needs diff_rot = 0.

    resid𝐋 is the residual of the balance relative to the largest of \(\lvert\lambda\rvert L_{\rm abs}\), the torques, and Gaspard \(L_{\rm abs}\) for \(m = 1\), with \(L_{\rm abs}\) the same integral of \(\lvert T\rvert\): a mode on which no torque acts (stress-free walls) is then checked to carry no angular momentum.

    Energy

    With a field, resid𝐄 (Magnetic field equations) counts the solids too. In the units of spin_doctor's kinetic energy a rigid rotation \(\omega\) of a body of moment of inertia \(I\) has the energy \(I\lvert\omega\rvert^2\), and the equations above give

    \[ 2\sigma\left(I_M\lvert\omega_M\rvert^2 + I_{IC}\lvert\omega_{IC}\rvert^2 + K\lvert\varphi\rvert^2\right) = 2\,\mathrm{Re}\left(\omega_M^*\,\Gamma_M + \omega_{IC}^*\,\Gamma_{IC}\right) - 2Kc\,\lvert\varphi\rvert^2 . \]

    The power of the viscous torques is minus the work of the moving walls on the fluid, which the viscous power of the fluid includes; at a stress-free outer wall with a drag the two differ by the power the drag dissipates (Drag at the outer wall equations). The power of the magnetic torques moves energy between the solids and the field: it is minus the flux of \(\mathbf v\times\mathbf B_0\) through the moving walls (\(\mathbf v\) their velocity), which resid𝐄 leaves out of the induction work with the rest of the flux of \(\mathbf u\times\mathbf B_0\) through the walls (utils4pp.INDU_WALLS). So resid𝐄 adds the energy of the solids to the left-hand side, and the power of the viscous torques and the loss \(-2Kc\lvert\varphi\rvert^2\) to the right. It does so when both torques act on the solids (vtrq and mtrq 1).

    Checks (2026-11-06)

    tests/rotdyn/rotdyn_check.py (run_all.sh, about 1 min on 2 ranks):

    • Decoupled solids. With vtrq = 0 and no field the solids feel only each other: two modes are the roots of \(\lambda^2 + c\lambda + K(1/I_M + 1/I_{IC}) = 0\) (\(3\times10^{-15}\)), the others those of fixed walls (\(10^{-14}\)).
    • Heavy solids. Moments of inertia of \(10^{12}\) give the eigenvalues of fixed walls (\(10^{-12}\)).
    • Against conductive_IC (2d84351), five runs: without a field (two sets of solids, one of them Earth-like), thin conducting layers with a dipole, a conducting inner core and a resolved mantle with a field of degree 2, an axial field with an exponential resolved mantle and a thin layer on the inner core. Eigenvalues to \(10^{-14}\), and the rotation of the solids and the torques on the mantle in rotdyn.dat to \(10^{-10}\), its precision.
    • Conservation with 'Wu 2005' (r_out 0.9, N 64): every mode but \(\lambda = 0\) carries no total angular momentum (\(10^{-9}\)) and closes resid𝐋 (\(10^{-9}\)); without a field the energy budget of the fluid and the solids closes (\(10^{-10}\)), with a dipole, a conducting inner core and a resolved mantle resid𝐄 does (\(10^{-10}\)).
    • Equatorial resid𝐋 without the solids: \(10^{-15}\) without a field, \(10^{-14}\) to \(10^{-11}\) with a field (a conducting inner core and a resolved mantle, thin layers, a toroidal field that does not vanish at the walls), and \(10^{-9}\) in a full sphere.
    • Stops of background.check_rotdyn.

    Also checked once: the equatorial balance with the opposite sign of the Coriolis torque fails by O(1); the axial resid𝐋 is \(10^{-14}\) with a differential rotation ('conical', 'Wu 2005'); the solids work with buoyancy (thermal = 1, resid𝐋 \(10^{-13}\), total angular momentum \(10^{-10}\)).
